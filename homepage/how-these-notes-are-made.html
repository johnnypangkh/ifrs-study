How these notes are made

  1. Sources

    These notes start from sources that can be named.

    • Official Standard first — IFRS / IAS text for that pack, including Basis for Conclusions and Illustrative Examples where the chapter uses them.
    • Firm manuals actually used for that pack (PwC, Deloitte / DTT, EY, KPMG where on disk and scanned for the chapter). A firm that was not used is not listed as if it were.
    • Firm FAQs / alerts only as support that densifies a manual view — never as a substitute for the Official text or for a manual that was never opened.
    • Every pack lists its real sources on the References page. Cite chips in the chapter point back to those same works.

  2. Read

    Against those sources, the notes separate what the Standard requires from how a firm explains or illustrates it.

    • Requirements and definitions stay tied to Official locators.
    • Firm views, examples, and FAQs keep their own house tags — they are not rewritten as if they were the Standard.
    • When sources treat one point differently, the page shows both side by side and does not reconcile them into a third “in practice” answer.
    • Case facts and numbers come from Official or pack Big4 examples. Invented companies or CU amounts are not kept.

  3. Shape

    What each chapter page is built to show:

    • Concept Map — a teaching story in space and a jump board into the chapter. One glance should show how the parts relate; section chips jump to the matching body section. The map does not re-teach the full text.
    • Sections — the deep study surface: definitions, tests, tables, and callouts in technical IFRS wording, detail-first so the reader does not have to reopen the PDFs for every point the pack covers.
    • Cite chips — on every sourced point: firm (or Official) + work + locator inside the chip (no bare house name, no § in chips).
    • Illustrations — worked examples taken from Official or pack Big4 named examples / case facts (Facts → Assessment / Conclusion). Plain firm prose without case facts stays ordinary teaching text, not an Illustration.
    • Pack chrome the reader also sees: Abbreviations (short forms used in that standard), References, bottom Disclaimer, and Last update on every pack page.

  4. Check

    Before a point stays on the page, it has to survive a source check.

    • A cite has to match a source that was actually used for that pack.
    • Invented requirements, fake examples, and unsupported tips are removed.
    • Structure alone is not enough: thin placeholders that only restate a heading are not treated as finished teaching.
    • What the page promises the reader is traceability — every teaching claim can be walked back to Official or a named firm work — not that every PDF on earth was scanned.
