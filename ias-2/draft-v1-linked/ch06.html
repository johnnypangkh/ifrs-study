6.0 Disclosure requirementstop

The financial statements disclose the accounting policies adopted in measuring inventories, the carrying amounts of inventories and the amounts recognised in profit or loss listed in paragraph 36; paragraphs 37 to 39 deal with the classifications of inventories and with how the amount recognised as an expense is presented. Disclosure opens with the paragraph 36 list, notes that presentation and disclosure still apply where measurement is carved out, and flags cryptocurrencies held for sale in the ordinary course of business. 6.1 Accounting policies, including the cost formula usedtop

The financial statements disclose the accounting policies adopted in measuring inventories, including the cost formula used. Entities disclose the accounting policies used to measure inventories, including the cost formula as a matter of policy; the permitted formulas are specific identification, FIFO or weighted average; and practice examples show how those policies are worded. IAS 2 · para 36(a) DTT A11 2022 · 4.2.1 PwC MOA 2020 · 25.47

  • Accounting policies. the accounting policies adopted in measuring inventories, including the cost formula used. IAS 2 · para 36(a) DTT A11 2022 · 4.2.1
  • Cost formula. The requirement to disclose the accounting policies used for measuring inventories “including the cost formula used” reinforces the view that the cost formula selected is a matter of accounting policy. A change from one cost formula to another is therefore a change in accounting policy, whereas other changes in the way inventories are measured — for example, the basis for allocating overheads or other costs of conversion — are likely to be changes in estimate, the effect of which is separately disclosed when material (Chapter 3 · change of cost formula). DTT A11 2022 · 3.2.8.6-1 IAS 2 · para 36(a) IAS 8 · para 39
  • Cost formulas. The cost of items that are not ordinarily interchangeable is assigned by specific identification; the cost of other inventories by the first-in, first-out (FIFO) or weighted average cost formula, with the same cost formula for all inventories having a similar nature and use to the entity (Chapter 3 · 3.10). The last-in, first-out (LIFO) formula is not permitted (Chapter 3 · prohibition of LIFO). IAS 2 · para 23 IAS 2 · para 25 IAS 2 · para 26

6.2 Total carrying amount and classifications of inventoriestop

The financial statements disclose the total carrying amount of inventories and the carrying amount in classifications appropriate to the entity. The total carrying amount is analysed in classifications appropriate to the entity, using common classes such as merchandise, production supplies, materials, work in progress and finished goods, with specialist industries choosing classes that fit their operations and service providers normally describing inventories as work in progress. IAS 2 · para 36(b) DTT A11 2022 · 4.2.2 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.8.400.40

  • Analysed in a manner appropriate to the entity. the total carrying amount of inventories and the carrying amount in classifications appropriate to the entity. The total carrying amount of inventories is disclosed, together with an analysis of the carrying amount in a manner appropriate to the entity; the carrying amount of each class of inventory is disclosed separately. IAS 2 · para 36(b) DTT A11 2022 · 4.2.2 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.8.400.40
  • Common classifications Information about the carrying amounts held in different classifications of inventories and the extent of the changes in these assets is useful to financial statement users. Common classifications of inventories are merchandise, production supplies, materials, work in progress and finished goods. The classifications chosen are applied consistently. IAS 2 · para 37 PwC MOA 2020 · 25.48 DTT A11 2022 · 4.2.2 IAS 1 · para 78
  • Specialist industries. Entities in specialist industries use a classification that is meaningful in the context of their operations; for example, property development entities could analyse their development portfolio as land held for development, properties under construction and completed properties held for sale. DTT A11 2022 · 4.2.2
  • Service providers. Classes of inventories include raw materials, work in progress and finished goods; the inventories of a service provider are normally described as work in progress. KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.8.400.40

Presentation in the statement of financial position

When material, inventories are presented as a separate line item; they are current assets with a split disclosed if recovery straddles twelve months; and advance payments for purchases are not classified as inventory.

  • Separate line item. When material, the total carrying amount of inventories — other than those included in disposal groups classified as held for sale — is presented as a separate item in the statement of financial position. DTT A11 2022 · 4.1 IAS 1 · para 54
  • Current assets. Inventories are current assets. However, for each asset that combines amounts expected to be recovered both within 12 months of and after 12 months from the end of the reporting period, an entity discloses the amount expected to be recovered after more than 12 months; it is appropriate for an entity to disclose the amount of inventories expected to be recovered after more than one year from the end of the reporting period. KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.8.400.10 DTT A11 2022 · 4.2.3 IAS 1 · para 61 IAS 1 · para 65
  • Advance payments.
    • Advance payments made in respect of purchases of inventories are not classified as inventory; generally such payments are either a right to receive inventory or a refund of cash — the EY illustration presents them differently (Illustration 6.2.1 · sources differ).
    • Advance payments received from customers in respect of goods to be sold are not netted against inventories; instead the entity recognises a contract liability for its obligation to transfer goods or services to a customer.

6.3 Inventories carried at fair value less costs to selltop

The financial statements disclose the carrying amount of inventories carried at fair value less costs to sell. Fair value less costs to sell disclosure applies to commodity broker-traders, inventories that remain measured under IAS 2 inside a disposal group, and cryptocurrencies measured at fair value. IAS 2 · para 36(c) IAS 2 · para IN16 DTT A11 2022 · 4.2.4

  • Commodity broker-traders. the carrying amount of inventories carried at fair value less costs to sell. The Standard requires disclosure of the carrying amount of inventories carried at fair value less costs to sell; in practice this requirement applies only to certain inventories held by commodity broker-traders (Chapter 1 · 1.3). IAS 2 · para 36(c) IAS 2 · para IN16 DTT A11 2022 · 4.2.4 EY IGAAP 2026 · Ch23 · section 6.1
  • Still measured under IAS 2. When inventories are classified as held for sale as part of a disposal group, they continue to be measured in accordance with IAS 2; the disposal group itself may then be written down to fair value less costs to sell, but not the inventories per se. DTT A11 2022 · 4.2.4
  • Cryptocurrencies. If an entity measures holdings of cryptocurrencies at fair value, the disclosures specified in IFRS 13 · para 91–99 are provided (Illustration 1.1.2). DTT A11 2022 · 2.1-3

6.4 Amounts recognised in profit or losstop

Amounts disclosed. The financial statements disclose:

  • The amount of inventories recognised as an expense during the period
  • The amount of any write-down recognised as an expense
  • The amount of any reversal of a write-down recognised as a reduction in that expense
  • The circumstances or events that led to the reversal

  • Retained in IAS 2. IAS 1 does not specifically require it when expenses are analysed by function. The Exposure Draft proposed deleting the paragraphs of the previous version of IAS 2 that required disclosure of the cost of inventories recognised as an expense in the period, because this disclosure is required in IAS 1. Some respondents observed that IAS 1 does not specifically require that disclosure when presenting an analysis of expenses using a classification based on their function, and argued that the information is important to understand the financial statements; the Board therefore decided to require the disclosure specifically in IAS 2. 6.5 Analysis of expenses by function or by naturetop

Under a nature-of-expense format, entities disclose amounts other than cost of sales; two approaches satisfy the requirement to disclose inventories recognised as an expense.

  • Nature of expense Some entities adopt a format for profit or loss that results in amounts being disclosed other than the cost of inventories recognised as an expense during the period. Under this format, an entity presents an analysis of expenses using a classification based on the nature of expenses. In this case, the entity discloses the costs recognised as an expense for raw materials and consumables, labour costs and other costs together with the amount of the net change in inventories for the period. IAS 2 · para 39 PwC MOA 2020 · 25.49 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.8.400.60
  • Two ways to satisfy the requirement to disclose the amount of inventories recognised as an expense. Some entities adopt a classification of expenses according to the nature of the expense, and others present expenses based on their function. An entity that adopts the ‘nature of expense’ approach discloses wages of factory employees as staff costs; one that adopts the ‘function of expense’ approach discloses such wages as part of cost of sales. An entity that allocates expenses by function discloses the cost of inventories sold during the period (6.4). PwC MOA 2020 · 25.49 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.8.400.60 IAS 2 · para 36(d)

6.6 Inventories pledged as security for liabilitiestop

The financial statements disclose the carrying amount of inventories pledged as security for liabilities. Pledging inventory as security is one way to obtain its benefits, but inventory is derecognised only when control passes to the customer. IAS 2 · para 36(h) DTT A11 2022 · 4.2.6 EY IGAAP 2026 · Ch23 · section 6.1

  • Pledging and control. Pledging the asset to secure a loan is one of the ways in which the benefits of an asset can be obtained; inventory is derecognised only when control of the asset is transferred to the customer — control and the benefits of an asset: Chapter 5 · 5.1. PwC MOA 2020 · 25.45 IFRS 15 · para 33

Key terms

  • Accounting policies, including the cost formula used — disclosed under IAS 2 · para 36(a); the cost formula selected is a matter of accounting policy
  • Classifications of inventories — classifications appropriate to the entity; commonly merchandise, production supplies, materials, work in progress and finished goods (IAS 2 · para 36(b) IAS 2 · para 37)
  • Inventories carried at fair value less costs to sell — carrying amount disclosed separately; in practice certain inventories of commodity broker-traders (IAS 2 · para 36(c))
  • Amount of inventories recognised as an expense — often referred to as cost of sales; costs previously included in inventory now sold, unallocated production overheads and abnormal amounts of production costs (IAS 2 · para 36(d) IAS 2 · para 38)
  • Write-down and reversal of a write-down — amounts disclosed separately, with the circumstances or events that led to the reversal (IAS 2 · para 36(e)–(g))
  • Analysis of expenses by nature — raw materials and consumables, labour costs and other costs, with the net change in inventories (IAS 2 · para 39)
  • Inventories pledged as security — carrying amount pledged as security for liabilities (IAS 2 · para 36(h))
