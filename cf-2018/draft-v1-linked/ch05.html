Chapter 5 — Recognition & derecognition

Concept Map

5.0Scope of this chaptertop

Chapter 5 sets out when an item that meets an element definition is captured in the statements, and when a recognised asset or liability is removed. Recognition depicts the item in the statement of financial position or the statement(s) of financial performance — alone or aggregated, in words and by a monetary amount — and includes that amount in one or more totals; derecognition removes all or part of a recognised asset or liability from the statement of financial position. Only items that meet a definition can be recognised, but not every such item is. Meeting the definition of an asset, a liability or equity (or of income or expenses) makes the item a candidate for recognition; it is not yet depicted. 5.1Recognition process — capture, link, no matching objectivetop

Recognition captures a defined element in structured summaries. The amount at which an asset, a liability or equity is recognised in the statement of financial position is its carrying amount. The statement of financial position and the statement(s) of financial performance are structured summaries designed to make financial information comparable and understandable; amounts recognised in a statement are included in the totals and, if applicable, subtotals that link the items in it. Matching follows from asset and liability changes — it is not an objective. Initial recognition of assets or liabilities may bring simultaneous recognition of income and related expenses — a sale of goods for cash recognises income (the cash, an asset recognised) and an expense (the goods sold, an asset derecognised). That pairing is sometimes called matching of costs with income; the Framework produces it when it arises from recognising changes in assets and liabilities, but matching is not an objective, and items that do not meet the definition of an asset, a liability or equity cannot be recognised in the statement of financial position. The asset-liability approach has replaced the matching concept as the driver of recognition, while information about income and expenses remains as important as information about assets and liabilities. 5.2Recognition criteria — relevance, faithful representation, costtop

Only defined items are candidates for recognition and not every one is recognised, non-recognition has a cost too, and an item is recognised only if the information is useful.

  • Only defined items are candidates, and not every defined item is recognised. Only items that meet the definition of an asset, a liability or equity are recognised in the statement of financial position, and only items that meet the definition of income or expenses in the statement(s) of financial performance. CF 2018 · para 5.6PwC MOA 2020 · 1.74EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 8.2
  • Non-recognition has a cost too. Not recognising an item that meets a definition makes the statements less complete and can exclude useful information; in some circumstances, though, recognising some items that meet a definition would not provide useful information. CF 2018 · para 5.7EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 8.2
  • Recognise only if the information is useful. An asset or liability is recognised only if recognition of that asset or liability and of any resulting income, expenses or changes in equity provides users of financial statements with information that is useful — (a) relevant information about the asset or liability and any resulting income, expenses or changes in equity (paragraphs 5.12–5.17), and (b) a faithful representation of them (paragraphs 5.18–5.25). 5.3Why the 2018 shape — probability and “reliable measurement” removedtop

The 2010 criteria were probable flow plus reliable measurement — and both caused problems. The 2010 Framework recognised an item meeting an element definition if it was probable that any future economic benefit would flow to or from the entity, and the item had a cost or value that could be measured with reliability. Standards used the probability criterion inconsistently (“probable”, “more likely than not”, “virtually certain”, “reasonably possible”). Applying it could lose relevant information or mislead — it could prevent recognising some derivatives, and if an entity received cash for a liability to pay a fixed amount on an unlikely future event and did not recognise that liability, it would recognise an immediate gain when no economic gain had occurred; some Standards, such as IFRS 9, therefore applied no probability criterion. “Reliability” was no longer a defined qualitative characteristic, so a reliable-measurement criterion could be read as prohibiting recognition of any item with high measurement uncertainty, even when recognition would be useful. CF 2018 · para BC5.1–BC5.2

Same objective, different means. The 2010 Framework set practical but subjective filters referring to probability and reliability; the 2018 Framework refers directly to the qualitative characteristics and then explains when recognition might lack them — including some, but not necessarily all, cases where the 2010 filters would have said a flow is not probable or reliable measurement is not possible. The Board did not aim to increase or decrease the range of recognised assets and liabilities. CF 2018 · para BC5.3–BC5.4CF 2018 · para BC5.9–BC5.10DTT A2 2022 · 8.1

5.4Relevance — existence uncertainty and low probability as factorstop

Relevant in general, but not always for a particular item. Information about assets, liabilities, equity, income and expenses is relevant, but recognition of a particular asset or liability may not always provide relevant information — for example when it is uncertain whether the asset or liability exists, or when it exists but the probability of an inflow or outflow of economic benefits is low. Neither factor leads automatically to a conclusion that recognition lacks relevance; other factors may also matter, and it may be a combination rather than any single factor that decides. CF 2018 · para 5.12–5.13CF 2018 · para BC5.12PwC MOA 2020 · 1.76EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 8.2.1

Existence uncertainty is a separate kind of uncertainty. It is conceptually different from outcome uncertainty and measurement uncertainty, though it may contribute to both, and could affect recognition decisions differently; distinguishing the types makes it easier to decide what information is most relevant and how to depict it faithfully. Existence uncertainty, possibly combined with a low probability of flows and an exceptionally wide range of possible outcomes, may mean that recognition at a single amount would not provide relevant information. Whether or not the item is recognised, explanatory information about the uncertainties may be needed. The Framework gives no detailed guidance on existence uncertainty because the approach depends on facts and circumstances. CF 2018 · para 5.14CF 2018 · para BC5.13–BC5.14CF 2018 · para 4.13CF 2018 · para 4.35DTT A2 2022 · 8.1EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 7.2.1EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 7.3.1

Low probability does not stop an item existing. An asset or liability can exist even if the probability of an inflow or outflow of economic benefits is low. When probability is low, the most relevant information may be the magnitude of the possible flows, their possible timing and the factors affecting their probability — typically given in the notes. A low probability of existence or of related benefits does not, of itself, preclude recognition; it is among the considerations for recognition, measurement and the information given about the uncertainties. 5.5Faithful representation — measurement uncertainty and related depictiontop

Recognition must also faithfully represent the item and its effects. Recognition of a particular asset or liability is appropriate only if it provides not only relevant information but also a faithful representation of the asset or liability and of any resulting income, expenses or changes in equity; that may be affected by measurement uncertainty or by other factors. CF 2018 · para 5.18PwC MOA 2020 · 1.77EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 8.2.2

Measurement uncertainty is normal. To be recognised an item must be measured, and many measures must be estimated. Reasonable estimates are an essential part of preparing financial information and do not undermine usefulness if clearly and accurately described and explained; even a high level of measurement uncertainty does not necessarily prevent an estimate from providing useful information. CF 2018 · para 5.19CF 2018 · para 2.19CF 2018 · para BC5.21EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 8.2.2.A

When uncertainty may be too high. The level may be so high that it is questionable whether the estimate gives a sufficiently faithful representation — for example, when the only way to estimate the measure is a cash-flow-based technique and, in addition, one or more of the following exists. CF 2018 · para 5.20PwC MOA 2020 · 1.77EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 8.2.2.A Those circumstances are an exceptionally wide range of outcomes, exceptional sensitivity to small changes in probabilities, and exceptionally difficult allocations of cash flows.

5.6Derecognition — twin aims and conflict casestop

Derecognition normally follows loss of the definition. Derecognition is the removal of all or part of a recognised asset or liability from an entity’s statement of financial position. It normally occurs when the item no longer meets the definition — for an asset, when the entity loses control of all or part of it; for a liability, when the entity no longer has a present obligation for all or part of it. CF 2018 · para 5.26PwC MOA 2020 · 1.83DTT A2 2022 · 8.2EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 8.3

Accounting requirements for derecognition aim to faithfully represent both: CF 2018 · para 5.27EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 8.3

  1. Any assets and liabilities retained after the transaction or other event that led to derecognition (including any acquired, incurred or created as part of it); and CF 2018 · para 5.27(a)EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 8.3
  2. The change in the entity’s assets and liabilities as a result of that transaction or other event. CF 2018 · para 5.27(b)EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 8.3

The aims are normally achieved in three steps. CF 2018 · para 5.28CF 2018 · para BC5.29EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 8.3 An entity derecognises the transferred component, continues to recognise the retained component and adds presentation or explanation if needed, and splitting an asset into rights bears on derecognition.

  • Derecognise the transferred component. Assets or liabilities that have expired or been consumed, collected, fulfilled or transferred are derecognised and any resulting income and expenses recognised; together they are the “transferred component”. CF 2018 · para 5.28(a)EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 8.3
  • Continue to recognise the retained component. Any assets or liabilities retained become a unit of account separate from the transferred component, so no income or expenses arise on the retained component from derecognising the transferred one — unless derecognition changes the measurement requirements that apply to it. CF 2018 · para 5.28(b)EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 8.3
  • Add presentation or explanation if needed. Present any retained component separately in the statement of financial position; present separately the income and expenses from derecognising the transferred component; or provide explanatory information. CF 2018 · para 5.28(c)EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 8.3
  • Splitting an asset into rights bears on derecognition. A physical object can be “sliced and diced” into different rights; the challenge is determining how far an asset can be split and the effect on recognition and derecognition, including what to do when the entity retains some rights after a transfer. When an apparent transfer is not a transfer. An entity may appear to transfer an asset or liability that nevertheless remains its own: retained exposure to significant positive or negative variations in the benefits the asset may produce sometimes indicates continued control, and an asset transferred to another party holding it as the entity’s agent is still controlled by the transferor. Derecognition is then not appropriate, because it would achieve neither aim. When derecognition overstates the change. If the entity no longer has the transferred component, derecognition faithfully represents that fact — but it may still suggest the financial position changed more than it did, even with the extra procedures: for example, a transfer together with a present right or obligation to reacquire the asset (a forward contract, written put option or purchased call option), or retained exposure to significant variations in a transferred component the entity no longer controls. If derecognition is not sufficient to achieve both aims, they might sometimes be achieved by continuing to recognise the transferred component: no income or expenses arise on either component, the proceeds are treated as a loan received (or given), and separate presentation or explanation shows that the entity no longer has rights or obligations from the transferred component. Assessment — what depicts an apparent transfer faithfully?

Test

Does the entity still have the transferred component — and if not, does derecognition, with any separate presentation or explanation, depict both what is retained and the change? CF 2018 · para 5.27CF 2018 · para 5.29–5.32

Controls

Outcome · still the entity’s asset

Do not derecognise — for example, retained exposure to significant variations that indicates continued control, or a transfer to an agent of the entity. CF 2018 · para 5.29–5.30

Aims met

Outcome · derecognition depicts both aims

Derecognise the transferred component and recognise the resulting income and expenses; keep any retained component as a separate unit of account, with separate presentation or explanation if needed. CF 2018 · para 5.28

Not met

Outcome · derecognition overstates the change

Why the Framework names two aims rather than one approach. The 2010 Framework did not define derecognition. Discussions usually contrasted two approaches; with an entire transfer and no retained exposure both give the same outcome and both aims are easy to achieve, but with a partial transfer or retained exposure the outcomes can differ and the two aims can conflict. The Board views both aims as valid. CF 2018 · para BC5.23–BC5.28 The control approach mirrors recognition and the risks-and-rewards approach lets exposure decide, while relevance is implicit in the aims.

  • Control approach — the mirror image of recognition. An entity derecognises an asset or liability when it no longer meets the criteria for recognition (or no longer exists, or is no longer the entity’s asset or liability); it focuses more on representing what is retained. CF 2018 · para BC5.24(a)CF 2018 · para BC5.27
  • Risks-and-rewards approach — exposure decides. An entity continues to recognise an asset or liability until it is no longer exposed to most of the risks and rewards it generates — even if the retained component would not qualify for recognition had it been acquired alone at the disposal date; it focuses more on representing the change. CF 2018 · para BC5.24(b)CF 2018 · para BC5.27
  • Relevance is implicit in the aims. The aims identify which economic phenomena need to be faithfully represented, and information about those phenomena is what is relevant, so the Board added no explicit reference to relevance. CF 2018 · para BC5.30

5.7Contract modifications — which unit of accounttop

A modification that reduces or eliminates rights or obligations raises a derecognition question. Deciding how to account for it means considering which unit of account gives the most useful information about the assets and liabilities retained after the modification and about how the modification changed them. CF 2018 · para 5.33EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 8.3

Assessment — what does the contract modification do?

Test

Does the modification only eliminate existing rights or obligations, only add new ones, or both? CF 2018 · para 5.33

Removes

Outcome · only eliminates

Apply the derecognition discussion to decide whether to derecognise those rights or obligations. CF 2018 · para 5.33(a)CF 2018 · para 5.26–5.32

Adds

Outcome · only adds

Decide the unit of account — a separate asset or liability, or part of the same unit of account as the existing rights and obligations. CF 2018 · para 5.33(b)CF 2018 · para 4.48–4.55

Both

Outcome · eliminates and adds

Consider the separate and combined effect — if the modification in substance replaces the old asset or liability, the entity may need to derecognise the original and recognise the new one. CF 2018 · para 5.33(c)
