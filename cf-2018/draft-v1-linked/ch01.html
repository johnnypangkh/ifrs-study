Chapter 1 — Objective & users

Concept Map

1.0Scope of this chaptertop

The objective of general purpose financial reporting is the foundation of the Conceptual Framework; every other aspect flows from it. Other aspects of the Conceptual Framework—the qualitative characteristics of, and the cost constraint on, useful financial information, a reporting entity concept, elements of financial statements, recognition and derecognition, measurement, presentation and disclosure—flow logically from the objective. CF 2018 · para 1.1EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4 Chapter 1 carries over the 2010 text largely as it was, but now gives more prominence to the information users need to assess management’s stewardship.

  • 2018 Change — stewardship reinstated in an otherwise largely unchanged chapter. Chapter 1 is largely unchanged from the 2010 Framework. The Board reintroduced the term stewardship and gave more prominence to the information users need to assess management’s stewardship (see 1.4). DTT A2 2022 · 4CF 2018 · para BC1.32–BC1.33
Pitfall: quoting the objective sentence without its three decision types and its two assessments. CF 2018 · para 1.2–1.4

1.1Structure of the objective — users, decisions, assessments, informationtop

The objective links four ideas: who the primary users are, which decisions they make, which assessments those decisions depend on, and what information those assessments need. Later chapters (qualitative characteristics through presentation and disclosure) are justified by usefulness for these decisions. CF 2018 · para 1.1–1.4EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.1.1

Objective

Useful financial information about the reporting entity for decisions relating to providing resources to the entity. CF 2018 · para 1.2EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.1.1

Primary users

Existing and potential investors, lenders and other creditors who must rely on general purpose financial reports. CF 2018 · para 1.2CF 2018 · para 1.5EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.1.1

Decisions

Buy, sell or hold instruments; provide or settle credit; vote on or otherwise influence management’s use of resources. CF 2018 · para 1.2(a)–(c)EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.1.1

Assessments → information

Cash-flow prospects and management’s stewardship → information about resources, claims and changes, and about how efficiently and effectively management used resources. CF 2018 · para 1.3–1.4EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.1.1

1.2Objective — useful financial information for resource-provision decisionstop

The objective names the users and three decision types; expected returns tie those decisions to two assessments, and the assessments set the information needed. CF 2018 · para 1.2–1.4EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.1.1 The objective is followed by the terms defined for use throughout the Framework, the two assessments on which expected returns depend, and the information needed for those assessments.

  • Objective The objective of general purpose financial reporting is to provide financial information about the reporting entity that is useful to existing and potential investors, lenders and other creditors in making decisions relating to providing resources to the entity. Those decisions involve decisions about:
    • (a) Buying, selling or holding equity and debt instruments;
    • (b) Providing or settling loans and other forms of credit; or
    • (c) Exercising rights to vote on, or otherwise influence, management’s actions that affect the use of the entity’s economic resources. CF 2018 · para 1.2PwC MOA 2020 · 1.19EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.1.1KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 1.2.20
  • Defined terms used throughout the Framework. Unless specifically indicated otherwise:
    • Financial reports and financial reporting mean general purpose financial reports and general purpose financial reporting
    • Entity means the reporting entity
    • Management means management and the governing board
    • Primary users and users mean existing and potential investors, lenders and other creditors who must rely on general purpose financial reports for much of the financial information they need
    CF 2018 · para 1.2 fn1–2CF 2018 · para 1.4 fn3CF 2018 · para 1.5 fn4
  • Expected returns depend on two assessments. The decisions depend on the returns users expect — for example, dividends, principal and interest payments or market price increases. Expectations about returns depend on users’ assessment of the amount, timing and uncertainty of (the prospects for) future net cash inflows to the entity and on their assessment of management’s stewardship of the entity’s economic resources; users need information to help them make both assessments. CF 2018 · para 1.3PwC MOA 2020 · 1.20EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.1.1
  • Information needed for the two assessments. (a) The entity’s economic resources, claims against the entity and changes in those resources and claims (see 1.5); and (b) how efficiently and effectively management and the governing board have discharged their responsibilities to use the entity’s economic resources (see 1.4). CF 2018 · para 1.4PwC MOA 2020 · 1.21EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.1.1

Resource-provision decisions — full scope

All three decision types (a)–(c), including decisions made while holding an investment — for example, decisions to reappoint or replace management, to assess the adequacy of management’s remuneration or to approve a business strategy proposed by management. CF 2018 · para 1.2(a)–(c)CF 2018 · para BC1.36–BC1.37EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.1.1

Narrow reading — buy, sell or hold only

Excludes decisions made while holding. Feedback on the 2015 Exposure Draft showed some respondents read resource allocation decisions as buying, selling or holding only; the Board did not intend that narrow reading and so expanded the explanation of the decisions in 1.2. CF 2018 · para BC1.36–BC1.37

1.3Primary userstop

Primary users are defined by reliance on general purpose financial reports, not by whether a party finds them useful. The points are the definition, why this group is the primary user group, common information needs and subset extras, who is not a primary user (management and other parties), further other parties (employees, suppliers, customers and governments), and uses left outside the objective (corporation tax and distributions). CF 2018 · para 1.5CF 2018 · para 1.10EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.1.1

  • Definition Many existing and potential investors, lenders and other creditors cannot require reporting entities to provide information directly to them and must rely on general purpose financial reports for much of the financial information they need. Consequently, they are the primary users to whom general purpose financial reports are directed. CF 2018 · para 1.5CF 2018 · para 1.5 fn4PwC MOA 2020 · 1.22EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.1.1
  • Why this group is the primary user group.
    • (a) They have the most critical and immediate need for the information in financial reports, and many cannot require the entity to provide it directly.
    • (b) The Board’s and the FASB’s responsibilities require a focus on participants in capital markets — existing and potential investors and existing and potential lenders and other creditors.
    • (c) Information that meets their needs is likely to meet users’ needs both under shareholder-based and under stakeholder-based corporate governance models. CF 2018 · para BC1.16
  • Common information needs; subset extras allowed. Individual primary users have different, and possibly conflicting, information needs and desires. The Board, in developing Standards, seeks the information set that meets the needs of the maximum number of primary users; focusing on common needs does not prevent the entity from including additional information most useful to a particular subset. The Board kept the primary user description unchanged in 2018 for these reasons. CF 2018 · para 1.8CF 2018 · para BC1.19EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.1.2
  • Not primary users — management and other parties. Management is interested in financial information about the entity but need not rely on general purpose financial reports because it can obtain the information internally. Other parties may also find the reports useful, but the reports are not primarily directed to them — the Framework names regulators and members of the public other than investors, lenders and other creditors. CF 2018 · para 1.9–1.10PwC MOA 2020 · 1.18EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.1.1EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.1.2
  • Further other parties — employees, suppliers, customers, governments. PwC also lists employees, suppliers, customers, and governments and their agencies. PwC MOA 2020 · 1.18
  • Uses left outside the objective — corporation tax and distributions. EY observes that restricting the objective to the information needs of those providing resources excludes other common uses of financial information, notably the levying of corporation taxes and restrictions on distribution to members. Both typically take reported profit as a starting point, so if those uses are not considered in developing accounting requirements there is a risk that financial statements will be less suitable for them. EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.1.1

1.4Management accountability (stewardship) — an input to resource-provision decisionstop

Users assess management accountability (the Framework’s term is stewardship) alongside cash-flow prospects to make resource-provision decisions. Expectations about returns depend on two assessments — the prospects for future net cash inflows and management’s stewardship of the entity’s economic resources. CF 2018 · para 1.3–1.4EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.1.1

The 2018 Framework gives stewardship more prominence to highlight management’s accountability for the resources entrusted to their care. CF 2018 · para BC1.33 Information about the use of resources serves both assessments and supports voting and influence decisions, and other types of information also feed the stewardship assessment. Stewardship carries its ordinary meaning, its purpose is to hold management to account, and the label was reinstated although the need is not new.

  • Use-of-resources information serves both assessments. Information about how efficiently and effectively the reporting entity’s management has discharged its responsibilities to use the entity’s economic resources helps users to assess management’s stewardship of those resources. It also helps predict how efficiently and effectively management will use the resources in future periods, and so helps assess prospects for future net cash inflows. CF 2018 · para 1.4(b)CF 2018 · para 1.22PwC MOA 2020 · 1.29EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.1.1EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.2.3
  • Voting and influence decisions rely on it. Information about management’s discharge of its responsibilities helps existing investors, lenders and other creditors who have the right to vote on or influence management’s actions to make those decisions — decision type (c), which covers decisions made while holding an investment. PwC MOA 2020 · FAQ 1.21.1CF 2018 · para 1.2(c)CF 2018 · para BC1.36EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.1.1
  • Other information types also feed the stewardship assessment. Information about resources and claims, about the return produced on resources, about financial performance during a period and about cash flows each also helps users assess management’s stewardship; users must be able to separate performance changes from other changes such as issuing debt or equity. CF 2018 · para 1.13CF 2018 · para 1.15–1.16CF 2018 · para 1.18CF 2018 · para 1.20PwC MOA 2020 · 1.27–1.28EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.2.1EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.2.2
  • Meaning — ordinary sense of the word. The Board uses stewardship in its ordinary meaning: the careful and responsible management of something entrusted to one’s care. CF 2018 · para BC1.41
  • Purpose — holding management to account. Users need stewardship information to hold management to account for the resources entrusted to their care; that assessment in turn helps decisions about providing resources. DTT A2 2022 · 4CF 2018 · para BC1.41
  • History — label reinstated, need not new. Pre-2010 versions of the Framework used the term stewardship; the 2010 Framework described the same need (how efficiently and effectively management discharged its responsibilities) without the label. The 2018 Framework reintroduces the term; information needed to assess stewardship may be partly different from information needed to assess cash-flow prospects, and both are needed. DTT A2 2022 · 4CF 2018 · para BC1.32–BC1.34

Examples of management’s responsibilities to use the entity’s economic resources

Examples of management’s responsibilities to use the entity’s economic resources include protecting those resources from unfavourable effects of economic factors, such as price and technological changes, and ensuring that the entity complies with applicable laws, regulations and contractual provisions. CF 2018 · para 1.23EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.2.3

Stewardship assessment — an input under one objective

Not an end in itself. Assessing management’s stewardship is an input needed in making resource allocation decisions — for example, a conclusion that stewardship is unsatisfactory may lead to a decision to replace management with the aim of increasing future returns. CF 2018 · para 1.3CF 2018 · para BC1.35(a)DTT A2 2022 · 4EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.1.1

Rejected — a separate or equally prominent objective

Considered and rejected by the Board. Some stakeholders proposed making stewardship part of the objective or an additional, equally prominent objective; the Board rejected this because assessing stewardship is not an end in itself but an input to resource allocation decisions, and an additional objective could be confusing. CF 2018 · para BC1.35

1.5Information about resources, claims and changestop

General purpose financial reports provide information about the financial position of a reporting entity, which is information about the entity’s economic resources and the claims against the reporting entity. Financial reports also provide information about the effects of transactions and other events that change a reporting entity’s economic resources and claims. CF 2018 · para 1.12EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.1.2

InformationWhat it helps users assess
Economic resources and claims
  • Nature and amounts →
    • Financial strengths and weaknesses
    • Liquidity and solvency
    • Needs for additional financing and how successful the entity is likely to be in obtaining it
    • Also management’s stewardship
  • Priorities and payment requirements of existing claims → how future cash flows will be distributed among those with a claim.
CF 2018 · para 1.13PwC MOA 2020 · 1.27PwC MOA 2020 · FAQ 1.21.1
Two types of change Changes from financial performance vs changes from other events or transactions such as issuing debt or equity instruments — users must be able to identify both types to assess cash-flow prospects and stewardship. CF 2018 · para 1.15PwC MOA 2020 · 1.28PwC MOA 2020 · FAQ 1.21.1
Return on resources
  • Performance shows the return produced on economic resources and helps assess stewardship
  • The variability and components of that return matter especially for the uncertainty of future cash flows
  • Past performance and stewardship usually help predict future returns
CF 2018 · para 1.16PwC MOA 2020 · 1.28PwC MOA 2020 · FAQ 1.21.1
Accrual performance and past cash flows Two complementary views of financial performance — see 1.6. CF 2018 · para 1.17–1.20
Changes not from performance Changes such as issuing debt or equity instruments — needed for a complete understanding of why resources and claims changed and the implications for future financial performance. CF 2018 · para 1.21
Use of resources How efficiently and effectively management discharged its responsibilities to use the resources — see 1.4. CF 2018 · para 1.22–1.23

Different types of economic resources

Different types of economic resources affect a user’s assessment of the reporting entity’s prospects for future cash flows differently. Some future cash flows result directly from existing economic resources, such as accounts receivable. Other cash flows result from using several resources in combination to produce and market goods or services to customers. Although those cash flows cannot be identified with individual resources (or claims), users still need to know the nature and amount of the resources available for use in the entity’s operations. CF 2018 · para 1.14EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.2.1

Pitfall: teaching financial position only — users also need information about changes (performance and other), cash flows and the use of resources. CF 2018 · para 1.12–1.23

1.6Financial performance — accrual accounting and past cash flowstop

Accrual-basis performance and past cash flows are complementary: accrual is the better basis for assessing performance, and cash-flow information still adds its own insight. CF 2018 · para 1.17CF 2018 · para 1.20EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.2.2

Accrual-basis performance

Effects in the periods in which they occur, even if the cash receipts and payments occur in a different period — a better basis for assessing past and future performance than cash receipts and payments alone. Shows the entity’s capacity to generate net cash inflows through its operations rather than by obtaining resources directly from investors and creditors, and may show how changes in market prices or interest rates affected resources and claims; also helps assess stewardship. CF 2018 · para 1.17–1.19PwC MOA 2020 · 1.25EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.2.2

Past cash flows

How the entity obtains and spends cash — borrowing and repayment of debt, cash dividends or other distributions to investors, and other factors affecting liquidity or solvency. Helps users understand operations, evaluate financing and investing activities, assess liquidity or solvency and interpret other performance information; also helps assess future net cash inflows and stewardship. CF 2018 · para 1.20PwC MOA 2020 · FAQ 1.21.1EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.2.2

Income and expenses recognised together arise from changes in the same assets and liabilities, so items that are not assets, liabilities or equity are never put on the balance sheet just to match costs with income.

  • Accrual timing is not a matching objective. Income and expenses may be recognised at the same time when they arise from the same changes in assets and liabilities — for example, a sale of goods for cash recognises income (the cash received) and an expense (the goods derecognised). That “matching” of costs with income is not an objective of the Framework, and items that do not meet the definition of an asset, a liability or equity are not recognised in the balance sheet. PwC MOA 2020 · 1.25–1.26CF 2018 · para 5.5EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 8.1

1.7Limitations of general purpose financial reportstop

General purpose financial reports are necessary for primary users but not sufficient, do not show the entity’s value, and rest largely on estimates. CF 2018 · para 1.6–1.7CF 2018 · para 1.11EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.1.2 General purpose financial reports do not give all the information users need, are not designed to show the entity’s value, and rest largely on estimates, judgements and models.

  • Not all the information users need. …general purpose financial reports do not and cannot provide all of the information that existing and potential investors, lenders and other creditors need. Users also need to consider information from other sources — general economic conditions and expectations, political events and political climate, and industry and company outlooks. CF 2018 · para 1.6EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.1.2
  • Not designed to show the entity’s value. General purpose financial reports are not designed to show the value of a reporting entity; but they provide information to help existing and potential investors, lenders and other creditors to estimate the value of the reporting entity. CF 2018 · para 1.7PwC MOA 2020 · 1.23EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.1.2
  • Based largely on estimates, judgements and models. Financial reports are, to a large extent, based on estimates, judgements and models rather than exact depictions; the Framework establishes the concepts that underlie them. Those concepts are the goal towards which the Board and preparers strive. The Framework’s vision of ideal financial reporting is unlikely to be achieved in full, at least not in the short term, because new ways of analysing transactions and other events take time to understand, accept and implement — but a goal is essential if financial reporting is to evolve so as to improve its usefulness. CF 2018 · para 1.11PwC MOA 2020 · 1.24EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 4.1.2
Pitfall: treating an item missing from general purpose financial reports as a failure of the Framework — the reports do not and cannot provide all the information users need. CF 2018 · para 1.6
