3.0

Financial statements

A particular form of general purpose financial reports — resources, claims and changes that meet the element definitions.

3.2

Objective

Information about the reporting entity’s assets, liabilities, equity, income and expenses.

Useful for assessing future net cash inflows and stewardship of the entity’s economic resources

Statement of financial position

Recognised assets, liabilities and equity.

Statement(s) of financial performance

Recognised income and expenses.

Other statements and notes

  • Nature and risks of recognised and unrecognised items
  • Cash flows — important, not elements
  • Contributions from holders of equity claims, and distributions to them
  • Methods, assumptions and judgements

Reporting period

Prepared for a specified period.

Preceding

Comparatives for at least one preceding period — changes and trends.

This period

Assets and liabilities — including unrecognised — and equity, at the end or during the period. Income and expenses for the period.

After period-end

Events included only when needed to meet the objective.

Forward-looking information only when it relates to those elements and is useful — for example, estimated future cash flows used to measure an asset or liability. Not management’s expectations and strategies.

3.3Entity perspective

How is the whole viewed?

Transactions and events from the reporting entity as a whole — not from any one group of investors, lenders or other creditors. The entity is separate from its capital providers.

Non-controlling interests are equity of that whole.

3.4Going concern

Will it keep operating?

Normally assume the entity continues in operation for the foreseeable future. If it intends or needs to liquidate or cease trading, the statements may be prepared on a different basis — and they describe that basis.

Reporting entity

An entity that is required, or chooses, to prepare financial statements. A single entity, a portion of an entity, or more than one — not necessarily a legal entity.

3.6Consolidated and unconsolidated

Consolidated

Parent and subsidiaries as one reporting entity. Distributions to the parent depend on cash inflows to the subsidiaries. Not a separate picture of any one subsidiary.

See IFRS 10 control

Unconsolidated

The parent alone. A claim against the parent typically gives no claim against the subsidiaries.

See IAS 27 “separate” — label only

When consolidated statements are required, unconsolidated statements are not a substitute. A parent may still prepare them in addition.

Harder boundary

Not a legal entity, and not only legal entities linked as parent–subsidiary.

The boundary follows primary users’ needs: relevant information that faithfully represents —

  • not an arbitrary or incomplete set
  • neutral
  • described: how drawn, what it comprises

3.7 Combined financial statements. Two or more entities not all linked by a parent–subsidiary relationship. Acknowledged — the Framework does not say when or how.
