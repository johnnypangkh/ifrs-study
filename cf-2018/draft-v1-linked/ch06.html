Chapter 6 — Measurement

Concept Map

6.0Scope of this chaptertop

Measurement quantifies recognised elements in monetary terms by applying a measurement basis. A measurement basis is an identified feature — for example historical cost, fair value or fulfilment value — of the item being measured; applying it to an asset or liability creates a measure for that item and for the related income and expenses. The Framework does not prescribe specific bases: it describes them and expects that the qualitative characteristics and the cost constraint will lead to different bases for different assets, liabilities, income and expenses. CF 2018 · para 6.1–6.2PwC MOA 2020 · 1.84EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9

Recognition — whether to capture

Recognition decides whether a defined item is captured in the statements; it is the Chapter 5 decision on relevance, faithful representation and cost. CF 2018 · para 5.6–5.7CF 2018 · para 6.1EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 8.2EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9

Measurement — how to quantify

Measurement is not a recognition test. The Framework favours neither family, and current value is not fair value only — value in use, fulfilment value and current cost are siblings. CF 2018 · para 6.2CF 2018 · para 6.11CF 2018 · para BC6.10–BC6.11DTT A2 2022 · 9EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 1.2.170

Standards say how to implement the basis they select, and the 2018 chapter filled a gap because the 2010 Framework gave little guidance on measurement. It describes bases and discusses factors without detailed “when to use” guidance or a separate objective, and measurement gives a list of choices rather than direction.

  • Standards say how to implement the basis they select. A Standard may specify techniques that may or must be used to estimate a measure; a simplified approach likely to give information similar to a preferred basis; or how to modify a basis — for example, excluding own credit risk from the fulfilment value of a liability. The Framework does not override Standards. CF 2018 · para 6.3CF 2018 · para BC6.15CF 2018 · para SP1.2EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9
  • 2018 Filled a gap — the 2010 Framework gave little guidance on measurement. The chapter discusses historical cost and current value and how the Board selects a basis; high measurement uncertainty does not render a basis irrelevant. The Board rejected delaying the revision, issuing it without a measurement section, or interim guidance. CF 2018 · para BC6.2–BC6.3DTT A2 2022 · 1.2DTT A2 2022 · 9
  • Describe bases and discuss factors — no detailed “when to use” guidance and no separate objective. Suitability varies with facts and circumstances, so the Framework describes the bases and the information they provide and discusses the factors to consider; a separate measurement objective was judged unlikely to add useful guidance — measurement serves the objective of general purpose financial statements. CF 2018 · para BC6.1CF 2018 · para BC6.4CF 2018 · para 6.45EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.3
  • Measurement gives a list of choices rather than direction. Some chapters — such as measurement — only highlight a list of choices for the Board to apply when setting standards, whereas chapters such as assets and liabilities give more direction on how to make those choices. KPMG New Foundation 2018 · p1

6.1Measurement bases — two familiestop

Two categories of measurement basis: historical cost and current value. Current value is a family — fair value, value in use for assets and fulfilment value for liabilities, and current cost — not a synonym for fair value. CF 2018 · para 6.4CF 2018 · para 6.10–6.11CF 2018 · para BC6.12PwC MOA 2020 · 1.85DTT A2 2022 · 9.1EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.1.1EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.1.2

Historical cost

Monetary information derived, at least in part, from the price of the transaction or other event that gave rise to the item. Does not reflect value changes except impairment or a liability becoming onerous. CF 2018 · para 6.4EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.1.1

Current value

Monetary information updated to reflect conditions at the measurement date — not derived, even in part, from the originating transaction price. CF 2018 · para 6.10–6.11EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.1.2

Current value measurement bases include three siblings of equal rank. CF 2018 · para 6.11EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.1.2

Fair value

Price received to sell an asset, or paid to transfer a liability, in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date. CF 2018 · para 6.12–6.16EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.1.2.A

Value in use · fulfilment value

Entity-specific present value of cash flows from using and ultimately disposing of an asset (value in use), or of the resources transferred to fulfil a liability (fulfilment value). CF 2018 · para 6.17–6.20EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.1.2.B

Current cost

Cost of an equivalent asset, or consideration for an equivalent liability, at the measurement date — an entry value. CF 2018 · para 6.21–6.22EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.1.2.C

Why more than one basis? Different bases provide different information, and in different circumstances a basis may be easier to understand and implement, more verifiable or less uncertain, or less costly than another — so the qualitative characteristics and the cost constraint are likely to lead to different bases for different items. CF 2018 · para 6.2CF 2018 · para 6.23CF 2018 · para BC6.10–BC6.11EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.2 A single basis was considered and rejected, there is no default basis and no bias, and entry versus exit is not how bases are categorised. The Framework leaves some bases undescribed, “value” means economic value, and in practice most items are at historical cost.

  • A single basis was considered and rejected. A single basis would let amounts be added, subtracted and compared more meaningfully and make statements less complex; it would also be needed to measure a concept of wealth or capital, which the Board decided not to seek. Nearly all respondents supported mixed measurement; a few proposed historical cost, fair value, current entry value or deprival (relief) value, and most of them conceded a single basis was not achievable in practice. CF 2018 · para BC6.5–BC6.9
  • No default basis and no bias. Some respondents asked for a default basis with any departure explained; others saw a bias against historical cost or against current values. The Board sought a balanced description and did not intend to favour one basis over the others. CF 2018 · para BC6.9CF 2018 · para BC6.17
  • Entry vs exit is not how bases are categorised. The Board rejected grouping bases by inputs (entry values such as historical cost and current cost) versus outputs (exit values such as fair value, value in use and fulfilment value), because entry and exit values in the same market often differ little except for transaction costs. CF 2018 · para BC6.14
  • Bases the Framework does not describe. Deprival value (assets) and relief value (liabilities) — more complex and used in few jurisdictions; net realisable value — derived from another current measure; cost of release — obtaining release from a liability by negotiation is relatively unusual. CF 2018 · para BC6.29
  • “Value” means economic value. In the chapter, “value” refers to an economic value of an item, not its carrying amount and not a specific current value such as fair value. CF 2018 · para BC6.18
  • In practice most items are at historical cost. Some Standards use fair values (for example IAS 19, IFRS 9, IAS 40, IAS 41 and IFRS 2), but property, plant and equipment, intangibles and inventories are generally at historical cost, as are most liabilities; financial institutions use fair value for a substantial proportion of assets, while for many entities it is confined to some financial assets. PwC MOA 2020 · 1.98
Pitfall: Do not skip “what information does this basis provide?” and jump straight to fair value. CF 2018 · para 6.23CF 2018 · para BC6.16

6.2Historical costtop

Historical cost uses the price of the originating transaction or event. It provides monetary information about assets, liabilities and related income and expenses using information derived, at least in part, from that price. Unlike current value, it does not reflect changes in values — except impairment of an asset or a liability becoming onerous. CF 2018 · para 6.4CF 2018 · para BC6.21PwC MOA 2020 · 1.94EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.1.1

Initial historical cost includes transaction costs. An asset’s historical cost is the consideration paid to acquire or create it plus transaction costs; a liability’s is the consideration received to incur or take on it minus transaction costs. When the event is not a transaction on market terms, a cost may not be identifiable or relevant — a current value may then be used as a deemed cost on initial recognition and as the starting point for subsequent historical cost. When developing Standards, the Board decides whether to specify how initial values are determined. CF 2018 · para 6.5–6.6CF 2018 · para BC6.19PwC MOA 2020 · 1.94EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.1.1

Under historical cost, consumption is derecognition, impairment and onerous updates keep cost relevant, and amortised cost is one form of the basis.

  • Consumption is derecognition. Consuming part of an asset derecognises that part, reflected at historical cost through depreciation or amortisation; fulfilling part of a liability derecognises that part. CF 2018 · para BC6.20
  • Impairment and onerous updates keep cost relevant. An impaired asset’s or onerous liability’s initial cost is unlikely to give relevant information if not updated; historical cost still does not reflect value changes of an asset that is not impaired or a liability that is not onerous. CF 2018 · para BC6.21
  • Amortised cost is a form of historical cost. One way to apply historical cost to financial assets and liabilities: estimated future cash flows discounted at a rate set at initial recognition (updated only for variable-rate instruments), updated for interest accrual, impairment and receipts or payments. For a loan with regular interest, amortised cost typically approximates the amount originally paid or received. CF 2018 · para 6.9CF 2018 · para BC6.22EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.1.1
Pitfall: An updated carrying amount is not a current value — amortisation and impairment stay within the historical cost family. CF 2018 · para 6.4CF 2018 · para 6.7–6.9

6.3Current value — fair value, value in use / fulfilment value, current costtop

Current value is updated to conditions at the measurement date. Because of the updating, current values reflect changes since the previous measurement date in estimates of cash flows and other factors; unlike historical cost, a current value is not derived, even in part, from the price of the originating transaction. CF 2018 · para 6.10–6.11PwC MOA 2020 · 1.95EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.1.2

Fair value takes the market participant’s view. It is the price that would be received to sell an asset, or paid to transfer a liability, in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date, using the assumptions those participants — in a market the entity can access — would use when acting in their economic best interest. It may be observed directly in an active market, or estimated with techniques such as cash-flow-based measurement reflecting the factors below. CF 2018 · para 6.12–6.14PwC MOA 2020 · 1.96EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.1.2.A A cash-flow-based estimate of fair value reflects estimates of future cash flows, possible variations in their amount or timing, the time value of money, the price for bearing uncertainty, and other factors such as liquidity.

  • Estimates of future cash flows. CF 2018 · para 6.14(a)EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.1.2.A
  • Possible variations in the estimated amount or timing of those cash flows, caused by their inherent uncertainty. CF 2018 · para 6.14(b)EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.1.2.A
  • The time value of money. CF 2018 · para 6.14(c)EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.1.2.A
  • The price for bearing uncertainty — a risk premium or discount: investors generally pay less for an asset (and require more for a liability) with uncertain cash flows. CF 2018 · para 6.14(d)EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.1.2.A
  • Other factors such as liquidity, if market participants would take them into account. Variations and the risk price include counterparty credit risk and the entity’s own credit risk. CF 2018 · para 6.14(e)CF 2018 · para 6.15EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.1.2.A

Fair value ignores transaction costs on both sides. It is not increased by acquisition costs or decreased by costs of incurring a liability, and does not reflect costs of ultimate disposal, transfer or settlement. The description is consistent with IFRS 13. CF 2018 · para 6.16CF 2018 · para BC6.24PwC MOA 2020 · 1.96EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.1.2.A

Value in use and fulfilment value take the entity’s view. Value in use is the present value of the cash flows, or other economic benefits, an entity expects from using an asset and from its ultimate disposal; fulfilment value is the present value of the cash or other resources it expects to be obliged to transfer to fulfil a liability — including amounts to other parties that enable it to fulfil. Both reflect the same factors as fair value from an entity-specific perspective (in practice there may be little difference), cannot be observed directly, and exclude acquisition transaction costs but include the present value of expected disposal or fulfilment transaction costs. CF 2018 · para 6.17–6.20PwC MOA 2020 · 1.97EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.1.2.B Value in use and fulfilment value derive from IAS 36, form a separate basis rather than only an impairment input, and include a risk premium.

  • Derived from IAS 36. The descriptions come from the IAS 36 value in use definition — the most explicit entity-specific value in earlier Standards. CF 2018 · para BC6.24
  • A separate basis, not only an impairment input. Some Standards use value in use only to determine recoverable amount; the Framework still identifies it separately because it differs conceptually from historical cost, and the Board might choose an entity-specific current value instead of fair value in some circumstances. CF 2018 · para BC6.25
  • Includes a risk premium. Value in use and fulfilment value reflect the price for bearing uncertainty, which reflects the economic difference between items with different levels of uncertainty. CF 2018 · para BC6.26–BC6.27

Current cost is a current entry value. The current cost of an asset is the cost of an equivalent asset at the measurement date (consideration plus transaction costs); of a liability, the consideration that would be received for an equivalent liability minus transaction costs. Like historical cost it is an entry value; unlike historical cost it reflects conditions at the measurement date — whereas fair value, value in use and fulfilment value are exit values. If prices exist only for new assets, a used asset’s current cost may be estimated by adjusting for age and condition. CF 2018 · para 6.21–6.22PwC MOA 2020 · 1.99EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.1.2.C

Why the Framework describes current cost. Although current cost is not widely used in IFRS Standards, a significant body of academic literature advocates its use in financial reporting, so the 2018 Framework describes it. CF 2018 · para BC6.28

Transaction costs by basis. Acquisition costs are a feature of the past transaction; disposal costs are a feature of a possible future transaction. Defining transaction costs is left to Standards (normally incremental costs that would not otherwise have been incurred). CF 2018 · para BC6.30–BC6.33

  • Historical cost — recovery and margins. After a recent market-terms acquisition an entity expects at least to recover the cost of an asset, and expects a liability’s obligation to be no more than the consideration received less transaction costs; because cost is reduced for consumption and impairment (and increased when onerous), recoverable amounts are at least the carrying amount. Sale or consumption gives an expense at historical cost, recognised with the related income — the difference is the margin; liabilities fulfilled give income at the consideration received. CF 2018 · para 6.24–6.29EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.2.1
  • Historical cost — predictive, confirmatory and stewardship value. Cost of goods and services sold or consumed, and consideration received, can feed predictions of future margins over many periods — including on goods not yet held — give feedback on earlier predictions, and help assess how efficiently and effectively management used the entity’s resources; interest at amortised cost can likewise have predictive and confirmatory value.   • Fair value — market expectations. It reflects market participants’ current expectations of the amount, timing and uncertainty of cash flows, priced at their current risk preferences, giving predictive and confirmatory value; related income and expenses can feed predictions and help assess stewardship. Separately identifying changes from factors with different characteristics can be useful. CF 2018 · para 6.32–6.34EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.2.2
  • Fair value — day-one and exit effects. If an asset is bought in one market and measured using prices in the market where it would be sold, the price difference is income when fair value is first determined; a later sale or transfer in the source market would normally give small net income or expenses unless transaction costs are significant. Value in use gives the present value of cash flows from use and disposal; fulfilment value the present value of cash flows to fulfil a liability — predictive especially if the liability will be fulfilled rather than transferred or settled by negotiation; updated estimates give feedback on earlier ones. CF 2018 · para 6.37–6.39EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.2.2

It reflects what an equivalent asset would cost (or a liability would bring) now; margins at current cost may predict future margins better than historical cost when price changes are significant. Reporting current cost of consumption requires splitting the change in carrying amount into that cost and the effect of price changes — a holding gain or loss. 6.5Selecting a measurement basis — factors weighed togethertop

The selected basis must give useful information — relevant and faithfully represented, and as far as possible comparable, verifiable, timely and understandable. In most cases no single factor determines the basis; the relative importance of each depends on facts and circumstances. The discussion focuses on recognised items but may also apply to measures given in the notes. CF 2018 · para 6.43–6.45CF 2018 · para 6.47CF 2018 · para BC6.34PwC MOA 2020 · 1.86–1.87DTT A2 2022 · 9.2EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.3

No hierarchy or decision tree of factors. The Board considered prescribing an order — a hierarchy or decision tree — and concluded it would be neither possible nor desirable, because the relative importance of factors depends on facts and circumstances and several often matter together. CF 2018 · para BC6.1CF 2018 · para BC6.35EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9

Most relevant first, then faithful representation. As with the qualitative characteristics generally, identify the most relevant information; if it is not available or cannot be faithfully represented, consider the next most relevant. Enhancing characteristics and cost then constrain the choice. Choose looking at both initial and subsequent measurement (6.6). Relevance — characteristics

Cash-flow variability; sensitivity to market or other risks. For market-sensitive items historical cost may diverge sharply and delay depicting changes (amortised cost cannot give relevant information about a derivative). CF 2018 · para 6.49–6.53EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.3.1.A

Relevance — cash-flow contribution

Used in combination → historical cost or current cost often relevant (margins). Producing cash directly → current value often most relevant. Managing contractual cash flows → amortised cost may fit. CF 2018 · para 6.54–6.57EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.3.1.B

Faithful representation

Measurement inconsistency between related items. High measurement uncertainty does not automatically bar a relevant basis — extreme uncertainty may point to another relevant basis. CF 2018 · para 6.58–6.62EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.3.2

Enhancing characteristics + cost

Comparability, verifiability, understandability; benefits vs costs. More bases make totals less informative — sometimes still justified. CF 2018 · para 6.63–6.68EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.3.3

Relevance depends on the item’s characteristics. In particular, the variability of its cash flows and whether its value is sensitive to market factors or other risks. Faithful representation — related items and measurement uncertainty. CF 2018 · para 6.58–6.62CF 2018 · para BC6.43EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.3.2 Related items measured on different bases can create a measurement inconsistency, free from error is not perfect accuracy, and measurement uncertainty arises when a measure must be estimated. Measurement uncertainty is one of three kinds of uncertainty, “reliability” was not reintroduced, and tolerance is not asymmetric.

  • Measurement inconsistency. Different bases for related assets and liabilities can create a measurement inconsistency (accounting mismatch); using the same basis may give more useful information, particularly when cash flows of one item are directly linked to another’s. CF 2018 · para 6.58EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.3.2
  • Free from error is not perfect accuracy. A faithful representation does not require measures to be perfectly accurate in all respects. CF 2018 · para 6.59CF 2018 · para 2.13CF 2018 · para 2.18EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.1.2EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.3.2
  • Measurement uncertainty arises when a measure must be estimated. High uncertainty does not necessarily prevent use of a basis that gives relevant information, but a balance between relevance and faithful representation is needed. CF 2018 · para 6.60DTT A2 2022 · 9.2.3EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.3.2
  • Three kinds of uncertainty. Outcome uncertainty (amount or timing of flows) and existence uncertainty (whether the item exists) differ from measurement uncertainty; they may contribute to it but need not — a fair value observed in an active market carries no measurement uncertainty even if the cash it will produce is uncertain. CF 2018 · para 6.61–6.62EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.3.2
  • “Reliability” was not reintroduced, and tolerance is not asymmetric. Some respondents called measurement uncertainty “reliability”; the Board did not reintroduce the term. Others said prudence means a higher tolerable uncertainty for liabilities than assets; the Board concluded the tolerable level depends on facts and circumstances and can be decided only when developing Standards. CF 2018 · para BC6.44–BC6.45

6.7Measurement of equity and transactions with holders of equity claimstop

Total equity is not measured directly — it is a residual. It equals the total carrying amounts of recognised assets less those of recognised liabilities. CF 2018 · para 6.87PwC MOA 2020 · 1.88EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.4 Total equity is not the value of the entity, some classes and components can be measured directly but not all, direct measurement does not contradict the residual definition or the entity perspective, and equity can be negative.

  • Not the value of the entity. General purpose financial statements are not designed to show an entity’s value, so total equity will not generally equal the aggregate market value of equity claims, the amount from selling the entity as a whole on a going concern basis, or the amount from selling all assets and settling all liabilities. CF 2018 · para 6.88EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.4
  • Some classes and components can be measured directly — not all. Some classes and components of equity may be measured directly, but because total equity is a residual, at least one class and at least one component cannot be. CF 2018 · para 6.89CF 2018 · para 4.65–4.66CF 2018 · para BC6.52EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 7.4EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.4
  • Direct measurement does not contradict the residual definition or the entity perspective. Some respondents objected; the Board noted that equity is a type of claim, total equity still equals assets minus liabilities, and direct measures reflect equity claims against the entity from the entity’s perspective, not a particular claimholder’s. CF 2018 · para BC6.53–BC6.55
  • Equity can be negative. A class or component, and total equity, is normally positive but can be negative depending on which items are recognised and how they are measured. CF 2018 · para 6.90EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.4

Transactions with holders of equity claims on non-market terms. When the price differs from market terms because of the relationship, the substance may include a contribution from or a distribution to holders of equity claims, recognised in equity rather than as income or expenses. CF 2018 · para 6.80(a)CF 2018 · para 6.82CF 2018 · para 4.68–4.70EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 7.5EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.3.4

Facts

  • Variant A: Company B sells inventory at fair value to a shareholder.
  • Variant B: B gives the inventory to a shareholder without consideration.
  • Variant C: shareholder pays considerably more than fair value for the inventory.

Assessment

  • Variant A: on-market terms indicate the shareholder is not acting in capacity as shareholder → recognise in profit or loss.
  • Variant B: benefit received in capacity as shareholder → recognise directly in equity as a distribution (absent other facts).
  • Variant C: split — proceeds equal to FV in P&L; excess as contribution in equity.
  • Distributions and contributions sit in equity: CF 6.82 frames owner transactions that change equity without changing the definitional income/expense path when they are distributions/contributions.

Conclusion

  • Price vs FV is the capacity signal — third-party equivalent vs owner capacity.
  • Do not force all shareholder inventory movements through P&L when the facts show a distribution/contribution.
  • Attribution of shareholder-on-behalf transactions generally needs judgement; IFRS 2 may require attribution for certain SBPs.
Pitfall: The carrying amount of equity is not what the company is worth. CF 2018 · para 6.88

6.8More than one measurement basis — P&L and OCItop

Sometimes more than one basis is needed to give relevant information that faithfully represents both financial position and financial performance. CF 2018 · para 6.83CF 2018 · para BC6.50EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.3.5 Usually one basis is used in the statements and another in the notes, but sometimes current value is used in the statement of financial position and a different basis in profit or loss. The OCI mechanics then put the rest of the change in current value in OCI, and the Board would require this only in exceptional circumstances.

  • Usually: one basis in the statements, another in the notes. In most cases the most understandable way is a single basis for the item and related income and expenses, with additional information on a different basis in the notes; using the same basis in both statements normally gives the most useful information. CF 2018 · para 6.84DTT A2 2022 · 9.2.4EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.3.5
  • Sometimes: current value in the statement of financial position, a different basis in profit or loss. In some cases that is more relevant or more faithful; the factors in 6.43–6.76 still apply. CF 2018 · para 6.85EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.3.5
  • The OCI mechanics. Total income or expenses from the change in current value is separated: profit or loss includes the amount on the basis selected for that statement, and OCI the rest — so accumulated OCI for the item equals its carrying amount less the carrying amount on the profit-or-loss basis. CF 2018 · para 6.86EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.3.5
  • Only in exceptional circumstances. The Board would require this only in exceptional circumstances, and only if it makes profit or loss more relevant or more faithful. CF 2018 · para BC6.51CF 2018 · para 7.17EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 10.2.3.A

6.9Cash-flow-based measurement techniquestop

Techniques, not measurement bases. When a measure cannot be observed directly, one way to estimate it is a cash-flow-based technique. Such techniques are used in applying a basis, so it is necessary to identify the basis and how far the technique reflects that basis’s factors — for fair value, those in 6.14. The 2013 Discussion Paper treated cash-flow-based measurements as a separate category; the 2018 Framework does not. CF 2018 · para 6.91CF 2018 · para BC6.13PwC MOA 2020 · 1.89EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.5 Cash-flow-based techniques can apply modified bases or central estimates, but a central estimate carries no price for uncertainty and range information may still be needed.

  • Modified bases. Techniques can apply a modified basis — for example, fulfilment value excluding own credit risk — which may be more relevant or less costly but harder to understand. CF 2018 · para 6.92EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.5
  • Central estimates. Possible variations in amount or timing are considered in selecting a single amount from the range; the most relevant is usually from the central part of the range, and different central estimates give different information. CF 2018 · para 6.93EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.5
  • No price for uncertainty in a central estimate. A central estimate does not capture the price for bearing the uncertainty that the outcome may differ from it. CF 2018 · para 6.94EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.5
  • Range information may still be needed. No central estimate gives complete information about the range of possible outcomes. CF 2018 · para 6.95EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9.5
