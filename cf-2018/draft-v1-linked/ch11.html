Chapter 11 — Status & use with Standards

Where the Conceptual Framework stands against IFRS Standards — three purposes, a status that never overrides a Standard, and the IAS 8 route that leads preparers to it when no Standard applies. CF 2018 · para SP1.1–SP1.2DTT A2 2022 · 2

11.0Scope of this chaptertop

The Framework opens with a section on its status and purpose, ahead of its eight chapters and an appendix of defined terms. That section states what the Framework is for, that it is not a Standard and overrides none, how the Board departs from it or revises it, and the outcomes that Standards built on it serve. 11.1Purpose — three main usestop

The Framework describes the objective of, and the concepts for, general purpose financial reporting. CF 2018 · para SP1.1EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 3.2 The Framework serves the Board, preparers and all other parties, each in a different way.

  • Its purpose is to assist three groups. The Framework is to:
    • (a) Assist the Board to develop IFRS Standards based on consistent concepts;
    • (b) Assist preparers to develop consistent accounting policies when no Standard applies to a particular transaction or other event, or when a Standard allows a choice of accounting policy; and
    • (c) Assist all parties to understand and interpret the Standards.
    CF 2018 · para SP1.1DTT A2 2022 · 2PwC MOA 2020 · 1.16EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 3.2

Board

Develop Standards on consistent concepts. CF 2018 · para SP1.1(a)

Preparers

Develop consistent policies when no Standard applies, or when a Standard allows a choice. CF 2018 · para SP1.1(b)

All parties

Understand and interpret the Standards. CF 2018 · para SP1.1(c)

The 2018 Framework cut a long list of uses down to three, the Board kept all three uses rather than naming only its own, and a few aspects of the Framework can be applied only by the Board.

  • The 2018 Framework cut a long list of uses down to three. The 2010 Conceptual Framework listed many possible uses; in 2018 the Board identified three main ones — developing Standards, developing accounting policies when no Standard applies or a Standard allows a choice, and understanding and interpreting Standards. CF 2018 · para BC0.18
  • The Board kept all three uses rather than naming only its own. It considered stating that the primary purpose was only to assist the Board in developing Standards, and rejected that: acknowledging the help the Framework gives other parties does not stop the Board from developing focused and consistent concepts. CF 2018 · para BC0.19
  • A few aspects of the Framework can be applied only by the Board. Preparers apply the Framework when no Standard applies or a Standard allows a choice, but in some places the Framework says the Board may make particular decisions in developing Standards — for example, paragraph 7.17 on including income or expenses in other comprehensive income. 11.2Status — the Framework is not a Standard; Standards prevailtop

Nothing in the Framework overrides any Standard or any requirement in a Standard. The Conceptual Framework is not a Standard. CF 2018 · para SP1.2PwC MOA 2020 · 1.17EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 3.2 The Framework’s status has not changed since 1989, the explicit no-override statement stops inappropriate overrides, and Framework principles cannot justify contravening a Standard.

  • The status has not changed since 1989. The 1989 Framework and the 2010 Conceptual Framework both stated that the Framework is not a Standard and does not override any specific Standard; the Board reconfirmed that status in 2018. CF 2018 · para BC0.21
  • The explicit no-override statement stops inappropriate overrides. The status has worked well in practice, and stating that the Framework does not override any requirement in a Standard prevents entities from overriding Standards they view as contradicting the Framework. CF 2018 · para BC0.22
  • Framework principles cannot justify contravening a Standard. The concepts do not override any requirement in an IFRS Standard, so they cannot be used to justify a treatment that contravenes a Standard’s specific requirements. CF 2018 · para SP1.2DTT A2 2022 · 2

A policy choice within a Standard is subject to fair presentation. If a Standard permits a choice of accounting policy, entities select a policy subject to the overall requirement in IAS 1 that financial statements provide a fair presentation of financial position, financial performance and cash flows; paragraph 15 of IAS 1 describes the link between fair presentation and the Framework’s concepts. 11.3IAS 8 gap-fill — similar Standards first, the Framework secondtop

When no Standard or Interpretation specifically applies, IAS 8 requires management to use judgement to develop and apply an accounting policy that results in relevant and reliable information. DTT A2 2022 · 2

IAS 8 sends preparers to the Framework for a transaction with no specifically applicable Standard. Paragraph 11 of IAS 8 requires entities to consider the Framework in developing and applying an accounting policy for a transaction, other event or condition to which no Standard specifically applies. CF 2018 · para BC0.28 fnIAS 8 · para 11

  1. First — Standards and Interpretations dealing with similar and related issues: management considers their requirements and guidance. IAS 8 · para 11DTT A2 2022 · 2

The preparer purpose covers gaps and policy choices, and Framework changes reach most entities only through Standards.

  • The preparer purpose covers gaps and policy choices. Preparers use the Framework to develop consistent accounting policies when no Standard applies to a particular transaction or other event, or when a Standard allows a choice of accounting policy. CF 2018 · para SP1.1(b)EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 3.2
  • Framework changes reach most entities only through Standards. Changes to the Framework do not automatically change existing Standards, so they have no immediate effect on the financial statements of most reporting entities; preparers are directly affected only if they use the Framework to develop a policy when no Standard applies or a Standard allows a choice. 11.4Departures and revisionstop

The Board may depart from aspects of the Framework when needed to meet the objective of general purpose financial reporting, and explains any departure in the Basis for Conclusions on that Standard. The points are when a departure is appropriate, that revising the Framework does not automatically change Standards, that the revised definitions were tested against future and existing Standards, that inconsistencies compete with other priorities, that the Interpretations Committee refers inconsistencies to the Board, that the impact on standard setting takes time to show, and that a framework does not remove the need for judgement. CF 2018 · para SP1.3EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 3.2

  • Departures are appropriate only when needed to meet the objective. Some stakeholders said the Board should never develop Standards that depart from the Framework; the Board disagreed — conceptual thinking or the economic environment may change, and new or revised Standards might need to reflect those changes. CF 2018 · para BC0.23
  • Revising the Framework does not automatically change Standards. The Framework may be revised in light of the Board’s experience of working with it; any decision to amend a Standard requires the Board’s due process for adding a project to its agenda and developing an amendment. CF 2018 · para SP1.4EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 3.2
  • The revised definitions were tested against future and existing Standards. Responding to concerns about the 2015 Exposure Draft, the Board tested the revised asset and liability definitions and supporting guidance so that it and stakeholders could assess the implications for future Standards, and tested for inconsistencies between the revised concepts and existing Standards. See also 4.1.2 CF 2018 · para BC0.24
  • Inconsistencies compete with other priorities. The tests were not meant to identify Standards to amend: changes to Standards address deficiencies in financial reporting, and inconsistencies highlighted by Framework changes are weighed against other priorities when the Board develops its work plan. CF 2018 · para BC0.25
  • The Interpretations Committee refers inconsistencies to the Board. If, when developing a draft Interpretation, the Committee faces an inconsistency between a Standard — including one developed on the 1989 or 2010 Framework — and the 2018 concepts, it refers the issue to the Board, as the Due Process Handbook requires. CF 2018 · para BC0.27EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 2
  • The impact on standard setting takes time to show. The Framework does not override the requirements of individual IFRSs, and inconsistencies with the revised Framework go through the usual due process, so the overall impact on standard setting may take some time to crystallise. CF 2018 · para SP1.4CF 2018 · para BC0.25KPMG New Foundation 2018 · p2
  • A framework does not remove the need for judgement. An agreed framework is not a panacea for all accounting problems; it provides a framework within which judgements can be made, and its principles are frequently referred to in IFRSs and during their development. EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 1.2

11.5Foundation — transparency, accountability, economic efficiencytop

The Framework provides the foundation for Standards that serve three outcomes. CF 2018 · para SP1.5EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 3.2

Transparency

Comparable, higher-quality information lets investors and other market participants make informed economic decisions. CF 2018 · para SP1.5(a)

Accountability

A smaller information gap between providers of capital and the people entrusted with their money; information to hold management to account, and of vital importance to regulators. CF 2018 · para SP1.5(b)

Economic efficiency

Better capital allocation as investors identify opportunities and risks; a single, trusted accounting language lowers the cost of capital and reduces reporting costs. CF 2018 · para SP1.5(c)

Standards built on the Framework are meant to bring transparency, accountability and efficiency to financial markets, which is how the Board says its work serves the public interest.

  • The three outcomes echo the Board’s public-interest mission. The Board states that its work serves the public interest by fostering trust, growth and long-term financial stability in the global economy, and that the Framework contributes to Standards that bring transparency, accountability and efficiency to financial markets; its stated objective is to develop IFRS standards that bring those qualities to financial markets around the world. EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 3.2PwC MOA 2020 · 1.6

11.6References in Standards and the 2018 effective datetop

The Board and the Interpretations Committee started using the 2018 Framework as soon as it was issued; preparers that use the Framework moved to it through the Amendments to References. The Board issued Amendments to References to the Conceptual Framework in IFRS Standards in 2018; where appropriate, that document replaces references to the 1989 Framework with references to the 2018 Framework and updates related quotations. CF 2018 · para BC0.27–BC0.28EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 2

11.7IFRS for SMEs — a separate concepts sectiontop

Entities applying the IFRS for SMEs Standard use that Standard’s own concepts section, so the Framework is not directly relevant to them. The SMEs Standard includes a section setting out its underlying concepts and pervasive principles. DTT A2 2022 · 3 The IFRS for SMEs concepts section has moved from the 1989 basis to the 2018 Framework, and the SMEs Standard is a separate standard for entities without public accountability.

  • The SMEs concepts section has moved from the 1989 basis to the 2018 Framework. The section was based on the 1989 Framework, and the Board said it would consider amending it at the next review of that Standard; in 2025 the Board issued the third edition of the IFRS for SMEs Accounting Standard and revised the section, which is now based on the 2018 Framework. CF 2018 · para BC0.26CF 2018 · para BC0.26 fn
  • The SMEs Standard is a separate standard for entities without public accountability. It can be applied to the general purpose financial statements of small and medium-sized entities that have no public accountability and publish general purpose financial statements for external users; it omits some standards (such as earnings per share, segment reporting and interim reporting), simplifies measurement in some areas, reduces disclosures and simplifies language. PwC MOA 2020 · 1.10–1.11
