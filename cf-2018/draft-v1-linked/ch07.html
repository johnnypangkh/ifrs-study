Chapter 7 — Presentation & disclosure

Concept Map

7.0Scope of this chaptertop

Presentation and disclosure are how a reporting entity communicates information about its assets, liabilities, equity, income and expenses in its financial statements. Effective communication makes that information more relevant and contributes to a faithful representation; it also enhances understandability and comparability. The Framework does not override Standards. CF 2018 · para 7.1–7.2CF 2018 · para SP1.2PwC MOA 2020 · 1.100EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 10

Recognition and measurement — what is captured, at what amount

Recognition captures an item that meets an element definition, and measurement quantifies it in monetary terms by applying a measurement basis. CF 2018 · para 5.1CF 2018 · para 6.1EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 8.1EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 9

Presentation and disclosure — how it is communicated

Presentation and disclosure communicate what has been recognised and measured by classifying and aggregating it on the face of the statements and in the notes. CF 2018 · para 7.1–7.2CF 2018 · para 7.7CF 2018 · para 7.20–7.22EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 10.2EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 10.3

The topic of presentation and disclosure is new in 2018, three things are introduced for the first time, and the chapter gives high-level concepts with principles on OCI. Standard-setting projects build on these concepts, and presentation and disclosure give a list of choices rather than direction.

  • The topic is new in 2018. The 2010 Framework did not address presentation and disclosure. Respondents to the Board’s 2011 agenda consultation identified it as a priority — in particular information about financial performance, including the use of other comprehensive income. CF 2018 · para BC7.1
  • Three things introduced for the first time.
    • (a) Concepts describing how information should be presented and disclosed — they guide the Board in setting requirements in Standards and may guide entities;
    • (b) Guidance for the Board on whether income and expenses are included in the statement of profit or loss or in other comprehensive income; and
    • (c) Guidance for the Board on whether and when amounts in other comprehensive income are reclassified into profit or loss. CF 2018 · para BC7.2CF 2018 · para 7.15–7.19DTT A2 2022 · 10.1
    EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 10.2.3
  • High-level concepts, with principles on OCI. The chapter gives high-level presentation and disclosure concepts. The statement of profit or loss is the primary source of information about performance and the default location for income and expenses; only in exceptional circumstances may the Board, when developing Standards, decide that income or expenses arising from a change in the current value of an asset or liability are included in other comprehensive income — when that makes profit or loss more relevant or more faithful for the period. CF 2018 · para 7.16–7.17CF 2018 · para BC7.24DTT A2 2022 · 1.2DTT A2 2022 · 10EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 10
  • Standard-setting projects build on these concepts. CF 2018 · para BC7.3

Some chapters — such as presentation and disclosures — only highlight a list of choices for the Board to apply when setting standards, whereas chapters such as assets and liabilities give more direction on how to make those choices. 7.1Presentation and disclosure as communication toolstop

Effective communication requires three things.

  • (a) Focusing on presentation and disclosure objectives and principles rather than on rules;
  • (b) Classifying information so that similar items are grouped and dissimilar items are separated; and
  • (c) Aggregating information so that it is not obscured either by unnecessary detail or by excessive aggregation. CF 2018 · para 7.2PwC MOA 2020 · 1.100
EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 10

Objectives and principles

Objectives and principles rather than rules alone guide what is useful and how to communicate it. CF 2018 · para 7.2(a)CF 2018 · para 7.4–7.6EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 10.1

Classification

Sort on shared characteristics — nature, role (or function), how measured — so similar items are together and dissimilar items apart. CF 2018 · para 7.2(b)CF 2018 · para 7.7–7.8EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 10.2

Aggregation

Add together items with shared characteristics in the same classification, without unnecessary detail or excessive aggregation. CF 2018 · para 7.2(c)CF 2018 · para 7.20–7.21EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 10.3

Presentation and disclosure

The face of the statements typically gives summarised information and the notes more detail — different levels of aggregation, one communication goal. CF 2018 · para 7.1CF 2018 · para 7.22EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 10

Cost constrains presentation and disclosure decisions too. As with other financial reporting decisions, it is important to consider whether the benefits to users of presenting or disclosing particular information are likely to justify the costs of providing and using it. CF 2018 · para 7.3CF 2018 · para 2.39PwC MOA 2020 · 1.101EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 10

7.2Objectives and principlestop

Standards balance flexibility against comparability. When developing presentation and disclosure requirements, a balance is needed between (a) giving entities the flexibility to provide relevant information that faithfully represents their assets, liabilities, equity, income and expenses, and (b) requiring information that is comparable, both from period to period for one reporting entity and across entities in a single reporting period. CF 2018 · para 7.4PwC MOA 2020 · 1.102DTT A2 2022 · 10.2EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 10.1

Objectives in Standards help entities decide what to communicate and how. Including presentation and disclosure objectives in Standards supports effective communication because such objectives help entities identify useful information and decide how to communicate it in the most effective manner. CF 2018 · para 7.5PwC MOA 2020 · 1.102DTT A2 2022 · 10.2EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 10.1

7.3Classificationtop

Classification sorts items on shared characteristics for presentation and disclosure purposes. The characteristics include — but are not limited to — the nature of the item, its role (or function) within the entity’s business activities, and how it is measured. CF 2018 · para 7.7PwC MOA 2020 · 1.62PwC MOA 2020 · 1.103EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 10.2

Classifying dissimilar items together can mislead. It can obscure relevant information, reduce understandability and comparability, and may not faithfully represent what it purports to represent. CF 2018 · para 7.8PwC MOA 2020 · 1.103EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 10.2

Offsetting is generally not appropriate. Offsetting occurs when an entity recognises and measures an asset and a liability as separate units of account but groups them into a single net amount in the statement of financial position. It classifies dissimilar items together. Treating a set of rights and obligations as a single unit of account is different. 7.4Aggregationtop

Aggregation adds together items that share characteristics and sit in the same classification. CF 2018 · para 7.20PwC MOA 2020 · 1.100EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 10.3

Aggregation summarises but also conceals. It makes information more useful by summarising a large volume of detail, but it conceals some of that detail. A balance is needed so that relevant information is obscured neither by a large amount of insignificant detail nor by excessive aggregation. CF 2018 · para 7.21CF 2018 · para 7.2(c)PwC MOA 2020 · 1.100EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 10.3

Different parts of the statements may need different levels. Typically the statement of financial position and the statement(s) of financial performance give summarised information, and more detailed information is given in the notes. CF 2018 · para 7.22EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 10 The unit of account and the level of presentation are separate choices, and obscuring can make information material.

  • Unit of account and presentation level are separate choices. One unit of account may be selected for recognition and another for measurement — for example, contracts recognised individually but measured as a portfolio — and for presentation and disclosure items may need to be aggregated or separated into components, with related information in the notes. CF 2018 · para 4.49PwC MOA 2020 · 1.69EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 7.1.1
  • Obscuring can make information material. Information is material if omitting, misstating or obscuring it could reasonably be expected to influence the decisions primary users make. CF 2018 · para 2.11EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.1.1

7.5Profit or loss and other comprehensive incometop

Income and expenses go either in the statement of profit or loss or, outside it, in other comprehensive income. CF 2018 · para 7.15PwC MOA 2020 · 1.106EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 10.2.3.A

The statement of profit or loss is the primary source of information about performance for the period. Its total for profit or loss is a highly summarised depiction of performance, and many users incorporate that total in their analysis as a starting point or as the main indicator. Understanding performance still requires analysing all recognised income and expenses — including those in other comprehensive income — and other information in the financial statements. CF 2018 · para 7.16CF 2018 · para BC7.15–BC7.16DTT A2 2022 · 10.3EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 10.2.3.A The Framework does not specify one statement or two, its terms cover either layout with the choice left to Standards, and two terms are kept for clarity.

  • One statement or two is not specified by the Framework. The Framework does not specify whether the statement(s) of financial performance are a single statement or two. CF 2018 · para 7.15 fn11EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 6.1.1
  • Terms cover either layout; the choice is a Standards decision. “Statement of profit or loss” covers a separate statement or a section of a single statement, and “total for profit or loss” covers a total or a subtotal. The Board views one statement or two as a decision for developing Standards; since 2007 that decision has been set out in IAS 1. CF 2018 · para 7.15 fn11CF 2018 · para BC7.11
  • Two terms kept for clarity. “Statement(s) of financial performance” covers the profit or loss statement or section together with the other comprehensive income statement or section — consistent with “statement of financial position” and clearer than “statement of comprehensive income”. “Other comprehensive income”, introduced in 2007, means income and expenses not included in profit or loss; some find it neither descriptive nor well understood, but changing it could confuse. CF 2018 · para BC7.6–BC7.8

Decision — statement of profit or loss or other comprehensive income?

Test

No

Outcome · No

Profit or loss — the default location. All income and expenses are in principle included there. Income and expenses on a historical cost basis are included, also when separately identified within a change in current value — for example, interest income on a financial asset measured at current value. CF 2018 · para 7.17–7.18CF 2018 · para BC7.24

Yes

Outcome · Yes

The Board may, in exceptional circumstances, include it in other comprehensive income when developing Standards, explaining its reasons in the bases for conclusions; entities cannot take that decision. CF 2018 · para 7.17CF 2018 · para BC7.25DTT A2 2022 · 10.3

No definition of profit or loss was possible. Earlier decisions to put items outside profit or loss were made for particular reasons in particular projects, and the 1989 and 2010 Frameworks did not mention other comprehensive income. No single characteristic, or small number of characteristics, is shared by all items in profit or loss but not by items better placed in other comprehensive income, so a robust conceptual definition of either is not possible. A prescriptive list of profit or loss categories could never be complete and would push items into other comprehensive income; stakeholder proposals reached no consensus; and a 2013 approach defining a few categories for other comprehensive income lacked support. The Board instead built on the description of the statement as the primary source: excluding items without compelling reasons could make it less useful. CF 2018 · para BC7.9–BC7.10CF 2018 · para BC7.17–BC7.23 The Board treated the lack of guidance on reporting financial performance as a significant gap and left room for further work.

  • Guidance included despite calls for more. Many respondents to the 2013 Discussion Paper and 2015 Exposure Draft found the proposals insufficient and asked for further work on reporting financial performance. The Board judged the missing guidance a significant gap, included high-level guidance it saw as a significant improvement, and noted that this does not preclude further work. CF 2018 · para BC7.13–BC7.14CF 2018 · para BC7.20

Reclassification (“recycling”) now follows one principle. Earlier Standards required or prohibited it, reflecting approaches that changed over time — one view treated the statement(s) of financial performance as a single statement in which each item appears once; another wanted all income and expenses in profit or loss at some point. To stop decisions fluctuating, the Board set one principle: if profit or loss is the primary source, cumulative amounts in it over time should be as complete as possible, so items are permanently excluded only for a compelling reason. CF 2018 · para BC7.26–BC7.29EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 10.2.3.A

Where profit or loss and the statement of financial position use different measurement bases, reclassification is what makes the two agree over the holding period.

When one measurement basis is used in the statement of financial position and another in profit or loss (Ch6 6.8), reclassification is the only way to make cumulative profit or loss over the holding period equal the amount on the basis selected for that statement. The Board — when developing Standards

Using other comprehensive income and reclassifying from it are standard-setting decisions. The Board takes them when developing Standards and explains its reasons in the bases for conclusions. CF 2018 · para BC7.2(b)–(c)CF 2018 · para BC7.25CF 2018 · para BC7.33

Entities — applying Standards

Entities cannot take those decisions. Income and expenses go to profit or loss unless a Standard requires or permits otherwise; the Framework cannot be used to justify putting an item in other comprehensive income. CF 2018 · para BC7.25CF 2018 · para SP1.2DTT A2 2022 · 10.3

Pitfall: Using the Framework alone to put an item in other comprehensive income — that is a Board decision in a Standard. CF 2018 · para BC7.25DTT A2 2022 · 10.3
