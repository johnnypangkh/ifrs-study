Chapter 2 — Qualitative characteristics

Concept Map

2.0Scope of this chaptertop

The qualitative characteristics identify the types of information likely to be most useful to primary users for decisions about the reporting entity. They cover information in the entity’s financial report (financial information); throughout the Framework, qualitative characteristics and cost constraint mean the qualitative characteristics of, and the cost constraint on, useful financial information. CF 2018 · para 2.1CF 2018 · para 2.3 fn5EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5 The qualitative characteristics apply to financial statements and to financial information provided in other ways, and the 2018 changes left the chapter largely unchanged apart from reinstating substance over form and prudence.

  • Apply to financial statements and to financial information provided in other ways. Financial reports provide information about economic resources, claims and the effects of transactions and other events and conditions that change them (economic phenomena); some reports also include explanatory material about management’s expectations and strategies and other forward-looking information. The qualitative characteristics and the cost constraint apply to all of it, but how they are applied may differ by type of information — for example, forward-looking information versus information about existing resources and claims. CF 2018 · para 2.2–2.3EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5
  • 2018 Change — largely unchanged chapter; substance over form and prudence reinstated. Detail and reasons in 2.4. DTT A2 2022 · 5CF 2018 · para BC2.21
Pitfall: limiting the qualitative characteristics to the financial statements — forward-looking and other explanatory information is assessed under the same characteristics. CF 2018 · para 2.2–2.3

2.1Structure of the qualitative characteristics — fundamental, enhancing, costtop

Useful information must be relevant and faithfully represented; four enhancing characteristics improve it; cost constrains all of it. If financial information is to be useful, it must be relevant and faithfully represent what it purports to represent. The usefulness of financial information is enhanced if it is comparable, verifiable, timely and understandable. CF 2018 · para 2.4PwC MOA 2020 · 1.30EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.1

Fundamental — both required

Relevance and faithful representation. Neither a faithful representation of an irrelevant phenomenon nor an unfaithful representation of a relevant phenomenon helps users make good decisions. CF 2018 · para 2.5CF 2018 · para 2.20EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.1.3

Enhancing

Comparability, verifiability, timeliness, understandability — enhance information that is already relevant and faithfully represented; cannot make other information useful. CF 2018 · para 2.23CF 2018 · para 2.37EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.2.5

Cost constraint

Pervasive — the benefits of reporting information should justify the costs of providing and using it. CF 2018 · para 2.39CF 2018 · para 2.42EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.3

2.2Fundamental qualitative characteristic — relevance (including materiality)top

Relevance, including materiality as its entity-specific aspect, covers information capable of making a difference even if unused or already known, predictive value and confirmatory value, materiality, immaterial items to which Standards need not be applied, nature alone making an item material, and immaterial information that need not be reported and can obscure. CF 2018 · para 2.6CF 2018 · para 2.11EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.1.1 Relevant information can make a difference even if unused or already known and has predictive value, confirmatory value or both, and materiality is then defined. Standards need not be applied to immaterial items, nature alone can make an item material, and immaterial information need not be reported and can obscure relevant information.

  • Capable of making a difference — even if unused or already known. Relevant financial information is capable of making a difference in the decisions made by users. Information may be capable of making a difference even if some users choose not to take advantage of it or already know it from other sources. CF 2018 · para 2.6PwC MOA 2020 · 1.31EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.1.1
  • Predictive value, confirmatory value, or both. Predictive value: the information can be used as an input to users’ own processes for predicting future outcomes — it need not itself be a prediction or forecast. Confirmatory value: it provides feedback that confirms or changes previous evaluations. The two are interrelated — for example, current-year revenue can be used to predict future revenue and can also be compared with revenue predictions made in past years, helping users correct and improve their prediction processes. CF 2018 · para 2.7–2.10PwC MOA 2020 · 1.31EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.1.1
  • Materiality Information is material if omitting, misstating or obscuring it could reasonably be expected to influence decisions that the primary users of general purpose financial reports (see paragraph 1.5) make on the basis of those reports, which provide financial information about a specific reporting entity. Materiality is based on the nature or magnitude, or both, of the items in the context of an individual entity’s report, so the Board cannot specify a uniform quantitative threshold or predetermine what could be material in a particular situation. CF 2018 · para 2.11PwC MOA 2020 · 1.32EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.1.1
  • Immaterial items — Standards need not be applied. Materiality is an entity-specific aspect of relevance; IFRS requirements need not be applied to immaterial items. The definition was amended together with IAS 1 / IAS 8 (“could reasonably be expected to influence”). CF 2018 · para 2.11CF 2018 · para BC2.20AKPMG Insights 2019/20 · 1.2.40
  • Nature alone can make an item material. The nature and materiality of information affect its relevance, and nature alone can be enough; whether information is material is a matter of professional judgement. Items that often qualify as material regardless of their individual size:
    • Related party transactions;
    • A transaction or adjustment that changes a profit to a loss (or vice versa), or takes the entity from net current assets to net current liabilities (or vice versa);
    • A transaction or adjustment that affects the entity’s ability to meet analysts’ consensus expectations, or masks a change in earnings or other trends;
    • A transaction or adjustment concerning a segment or other portion of the business identified as playing a significant role in the entity’s operations or profitability;
    • A transaction or adjustment that affects compliance with loan covenants or other contractual requirements, or increases management’s compensation (for example, by satisfying bonus-award requirements);
    • Changes in laws and regulations, non-compliance with laws and regulations, fines against the entity, and legal cases;
    • Deterioration in relationships with individual or groups of key suppliers, customers or employees, and dependency on a particular supplier, customer or employee. PwC MOA 2020 · FAQ 1.51.1
  • Immaterial information need not be reported — and can obscure. An item that is not material is not relevant, cannot influence users’ decisions and need not be reported. Reporting immaterial items can interfere with decision-making because excessive detail may obscure relevant information; the October 2018 IAS 1 / IAS 8 amendments clarify that obscuring covers situations whose effect is similar to omitting or misstating information (annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2020, earlier application permitted). Fixed monetary limits or rules would be inappropriate — materiality remains professional judgement. PwC MOA 2020 · FAQ 1.51.2CF 2018 · para 2.11EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.1.1

2.3Fundamental qualitative characteristic — faithful representationtop

Faithful representation depicts the substance of a phenomenon; a perfectly faithful depiction would be complete, neutral and free from error, with neutrality supported by prudence. CF 2018 · para 2.12–2.16EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.1.2 Faithful representation depicts substance, not merely legal form, and aims to maximise completeness, neutrality and freedom from error, which are seldom perfect. Completeness and neutrality are defined, prudence supports neutrality without any asymmetry requirement although Standards may contain asymmetric requirements, free from error does not mean perfectly accurate, and reasonable estimates are essential under measurement uncertainty.

  • Substance, not merely legal form. To be useful, financial information must not only represent relevant phenomena, but it must also faithfully represent the substance of the phenomena that it purports to represent. Substance and legal form are often the same; if they are not, information only about the legal form would not faithfully represent the phenomenon (applied to contractual rights and obligations in Ch4 · 4.8). CF 2018 · para 2.12CF 2018 · para 4.59–4.62PwC MOA 2020 · 1.33DTT A2 2022 · 5EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.1.2EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 7.1.3
  • Complete, neutral, free from error — maximised, seldom perfect. A perfectly faithful depiction would have all three characteristics; perfection is seldom, if ever, achievable, so the Board’s objective is to maximise them to the extent possible. CF 2018 · para 2.13PwC MOA 2020 · 1.34EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.1.2
  • Complete — everything a user needs to understand the phenomenon. Includes all necessary descriptions and explanations — for a group of assets, at a minimum a description of their nature, a numerical depiction of all the assets, and what that depiction represents (for example, historical cost or fair value). For some items it also covers significant facts about their quality and nature, factors that might affect them, and the process used to determine the numerical depiction. CF 2018 · para 2.14PwC MOA 2020 · 1.35EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.1.2
  • Neutral — without bias in selection or presentation. A neutral depiction is not slanted, weighted, emphasised, de-emphasised or otherwise manipulated to increase the probability that financial information will be received favourably or unfavourably by users. Neutral does not mean without purpose or influence — relevant information is, by definition, capable of making a difference; but information biased to encourage users to take or avoid predetermined actions is not neutral. CF 2018 · para 2.15CF 2018 · para BC2.36PwC MOA 2020 · 1.36EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.1.2
  • Prudence supports neutrality — caution that works both ways. Neutrality is supported by the exercise of prudence. Prudence is the exercise of caution when making judgements under conditions of uncertainty. Assets and income are not overstated and liabilities and expenses are not understated; equally, prudence does not allow understating assets or income or overstating liabilities or expenses — such misstatements can lead to over- or understating income or expenses in future periods. CF 2018 · para 2.16PwC MOA 2020 · 1.37DTT A2 2022 · 5EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.1.2
  • No asymmetry requirement — but Standards may contain asymmetric requirements. Prudence does not imply a systematic need for more persuasive evidence to recognise assets or income than liabilities or expenses; such asymmetry is not a qualitative characteristic. Particular Standards may contain asymmetric requirements as a consequence of selecting the most relevant, faithfully representative information — for example, IAS 37 sets a higher recognition threshold for contingent assets than for contingent liabilities, which the Board views as that kind of choice rather than deliberate asymmetric prudence. CF 2018 · para 2.17CF 2018 · para BC2.45PwC MOA 2020 · 1.37DTT A2 2022 · 5EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.1.2
  • Free from error — not perfectly accurate. No errors or omissions in describing the phenomenon, and the process has been selected and applied without error. An estimate of an unobservable price or value cannot be called accurate or inaccurate, but its representation can be faithful if it is clearly described as an estimate, the nature and limitations of the estimating process are explained, and no errors were made in selecting and applying an appropriate process. CF 2018 · para 2.18PwC MOA 2020 · 1.38EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.1.2
  • Measurement uncertainty — reasonable estimates are essential. Measurement uncertainty arises when monetary amounts must be estimated rather than observed. Reasonable estimates do not undermine usefulness if clearly and accurately described and explained; even a high level of measurement uncertainty does not necessarily prevent an estimate from providing useful information (trade-off in 2.5). CF 2018 · para 2.19PwC MOA 2020 · 1.39DTT A2 2022 · 5EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5

Cautious prudence — in the 2018 Framework

Caution without more caution for income or assets than for expenses or liabilities. Helps achieve neutrality in applying accounting policies: it helps preparers, auditors and regulators counter a natural management bias towards optimism (for example, care in selecting inputs for a measure that cannot be observed directly) and helps the Board develop rigorous Standards that reduce the risk of management bias. CF 2018 · para 2.16CF 2018 · para BC2.37(a)CF 2018 · para BC2.39EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.1.2

Asymmetric prudence — not a qualitative characteristic

Systematic asymmetry — expenses recognised earlier than income. Rejected because a systematic requirement could conflict with relevance and faithful representation — for example, prohibiting recognition of all unrealised gains (needed for many financial instruments), or permitting an asset to be measured below an unbiased estimate or a liability above one. CF 2018 · para 2.17CF 2018 · para BC2.37(b)CF 2018 · para BC2.41–BC2.42EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.1.2

2.4Changes from 2010 — substance over form, prudence, measurement uncertaintytop

Chapter 2 is largely unchanged from 2010; the 2018 changes sit inside faithful representation. The Board reintroduced explicit references to substance over form and prudence, and added a section on measurement uncertainty under faithful representation. DTT A2 2022 · 1.2DTT A2 2022 · 5EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 1.2.30 In 2010 reliability became faithful representation, substance over form was removed until its reinstatement in 2018, and prudence was excluded until 2018 brought it back with a two-way definition. The 2018 Framework also added a section on measurement uncertainty under faithful representation and clarified that materiality refers to primary users, with the definition amended in October 2018.

  • Reliability became faithful representation (2010). Concepts Statement 2 and the 1989 Framework used reliability for what is now called faithful representation. Substance over form, prudence (conservatism) and verifiability had been aspects of reliability; the 2010 Framework did not treat them as aspects of faithful representation, and since 2010 verifiability has been an enhancing characteristic. CF 2018 · para BC2.21–BC2.22
  • Substance over form — removed as redundant in 2010, reinstated in 2018. In 2010 the Board considered a separate reference redundant, because faithful representation already means depicting substance rather than merely legal form. Some stakeholders inferred the Board was no longer committed to depicting substance; to avoid that misunderstanding the 2018 Framework reinstated an explicit reference in 2.12 and explained the substance of contractual rights and obligations in 4.59–4.62. CF 2018 · para BC2.32–BC2.33DTT A2 2022 · 5
  • Prudence — excluded in 2010, reintroduced with a two-way definition in 2018. In 2010 the Board excluded prudence and conservatism as inconsistent with neutrality. Removing the term led to confusion and more diversity in its use, and some stakeholders said IFRS information was therefore imprudent. The Board reintroduced it, defined as caution under uncertainty that works both ways, so assets and liabilities are neither overstated nor understated. CF 2018 · para BC2.34CF 2018 · para BC2.40DTT A2 2022 · 5
  • Measurement uncertainty — new section under faithful representation. Respondents said the 2010 Framework did not convey that a high level of measurement uncertainty can make financial information less useful; the 2018 Framework addresses this in 2.19 and the trade-off in 2.22. CF 2018 · para BC2.46CF 2018 · para 2.19CF 2018 · para 2.22DTT A2 2022 · 5EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.1.3
  • Materiality — users clarified as primary users (2018); definition amended (October 2018). The 2018 revision kept the 2010 description except to clarify that the users are the primary users in 1.5, so materiality reflects their needs and not those of any other group; the later amendment is covered in 2.2. CF 2018 · para BC2.20–BC2.20A

2.5Applying the fundamental qualitative characteristics — process and trade-offtop

Information must be both relevant and faithfully represented; the Framework describes the usual process for achieving both and the trade-off when the most relevant measure is highly uncertain. Information must both be relevant and provide a faithful representation of what it purports to represent if it is to be useful. CF 2018 · para 2.20PwC MOA 2020 · 1.30EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.1.3 Applying the two fundamental characteristics follows a set order, starting from an economic phenomenon whose information could be useful to users.

  • Usual process — most relevant information first, then faithful representation. The most efficient and effective process would usually be (ignoring, for this description, enhancing characteristics and the cost constraint):
    • First, identify an economic phenomenon, information about which is capable of being useful to users
    • Second, identify the type of information about it that would be most relevant
    • Third, test that information as shown below

Measurement uncertainty affects faithful representation rather than relevance, can reduce usefulness when it is large even if the estimate is faithful, and carries into recognition and measurement.

  • Measurement uncertainty affects faithful representation, not relevance. Relevance concerns which information is capable of being useful; faithful representation concerns whether it can be faithfully provided. A highly uncertain measure can still be relevant — sometimes it is the only relevant information about a phenomenon subject to significant risks and uncertainties. CF 2018 · para BC2.48
  • Large uncertainty can reduce usefulness even when the estimate is faithful. An impairment estimate can be a faithful representation if an appropriate process was properly applied, the estimate described and significant uncertainties explained; but if the uncertainty is large enough the estimate will not be particularly useful — though, without a more faithful alternative, it may still be the best available information. CF 2018 · para BC2.47
  • Carries into recognition and measurement. Measurement uncertainty affects whether an item is recognised (Ch5 · 5.5) and the selection of a measurement basis (Ch6 · 6.5). DTT A2 2022 · 5
Pitfall: discarding the most relevant measure only because it is highly uncertain — the Framework first considers that estimate with a description and explanation of its uncertainties. CF 2018 · para 2.19CF 2018 · para 2.22

2.6Enhancing qualitative characteristicstop

Comparability, verifiability, timeliness and understandability enhance information that is already relevant and faithfully represented; they can also decide between two equally relevant and faithful depictions. CF 2018 · para 2.23PwC MOA 2020 · 1.42EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5 Comparability makes like things look alike and different things look different, and the Framework gives no primacy between comparability within and between entities although IFRS 1 makes that distinction. Verifiability, timeliness and understandability are then defined, and applying these characteristics is iterative, follows no prescribed order and sometimes involves trade-offs.

  • Comparability — like things look alike, different things look different. Users choose between alternatives (selling or holding; investing in one entity or another), so information is more useful if it can be compared with other entities and with the same entity for another period or date. Comparability lets users identify and understand similarities and differences, and needs at least two items. Consistency — the same methods for the same items over time or across entities — helps achieve comparability but is not the same:
    • Comparability is the goal; consistency helps to achieve that goal. Comparability is not uniformity
    • Some comparability follows from meeting the fundamental characteristics
    • Permitting alternative methods for the same phenomenon diminishes it
    CF 2018 · para 2.24–2.29PwC MOA 2020 · 1.43PwC MOA 2020 · 1.45EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.2.1
  • No primacy between comparability within and between entities — IFRS 1 makes the distinction. The Framework gives neither primacy; IFRS 1 does, for first-time adoption: comparability within the entity’s first IFRS financial statements is more important than comparability with entities already using IFRS. PwC MOA 2020 · 1.44
  • Verifiability — knowledgeable, independent observers could reach consensus. Consensus, not necessarily complete agreement, that a depiction is faithful. Quantified information need not be a single point estimate — a range of amounts and related probabilities can be verified. Direct verification observes the amount (for example, counting cash); indirect verification checks a model’s inputs and recalculates the output (for example, inventory quantities and costs recalculated using the same first-in, first-out assumption). Some explanations and forward-looking information may not be verifiable until a future period, if at all, so underlying assumptions, compilation methods and supporting factors would normally be disclosed. CF 2018 · para 2.30–2.32PwC MOA 2020 · 1.46EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.2.2
  • Timeliness — available in time to influence decisions. Generally, older information is less useful, but some information stays timely long after the period end — for example, for users identifying and assessing trends. CF 2018 · para 2.33PwC MOA 2020 · 1.47EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.2.3
  • Understandability — clear and concise, without leaving out complex phenomena. Classifying, characterising and presenting information clearly and concisely makes it understandable. Excluding inherently complex phenomena might make reports easier to understand, but they would be incomplete and possibly misleading. Reports are prepared for users with reasonable knowledge of business and economic activities who review the information diligently; even they may need an adviser for complex phenomena. CF 2018 · para 2.34–2.36PwC MOA 2020 · 1.48EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.2.4
  • Applying them — iterative, no prescribed order, sometimes traded off. Enhancing characteristics should be maximised to the extent possible. One may be diminished to maximise another — for example, a temporary reduction in comparability from prospectively applying a new Standard may be worthwhile to improve relevance or faithful representation in the longer term; appropriate disclosures may partly compensate. CF 2018 · para 2.37–2.38PwC MOA 2020 · 1.49EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.2.5

2.7Cost constrainttop

Cost is a pervasive constraint: the costs of reporting information should be justified by the benefits of reporting it. Cost is a pervasive constraint on the information that can be provided by financial reporting. There are several types of costs and benefits to consider. CF 2018 · para 2.39PwC MOA 2020 · 1.50EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5

Costs — who bears them

Providers spend most of the effort collecting, processing, verifying and disseminating information, but users ultimately bear those costs through reduced returns. Users also incur costs of analysing and interpreting the information, and of obtaining or estimating needed information that is not provided. CF 2018 · para 2.40EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.3

Benefits — who receives them

Relevant, faithfully represented information lets users decide with more confidence — more efficient capital markets and a lower cost of capital for the economy as a whole; individual investors, lenders and other creditors make better-informed decisions. General purpose financial reports still cannot provide all the information every user finds relevant. CF 2018 · para 2.41EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.3

The Board applies the cost constraint by assessing whether benefits justify costs, and it makes that assessment for financial reporting generally rather than entity by entity.

  • How the Board applies the constraint. The Board assesses whether the benefits of reporting particular information are likely to justify the costs of providing and using it, seeking information from providers, users, auditors, academics and others about the expected benefits and costs of a proposed Standard; assessments usually combine quantitative and qualitative information. CF 2018 · para 2.42EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.3
  • Assessed for financial reporting generally, not entity by entity. Because cost-benefit assessments are subjective and vary between individuals, the Board considers costs and benefits for financial reporting generally rather than for individual reporting entities. That does not mean the same requirements suit all entities — differences may be appropriate for entity size, ways of raising capital (publicly or privately), users’ needs or other factors. CF 2018 · para 2.43PwC MOA 2020 · 1.50EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 5.3
