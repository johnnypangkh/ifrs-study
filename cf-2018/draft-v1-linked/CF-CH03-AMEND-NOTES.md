## Story

Financial statements are a particular form of general purpose financial report, and their objective lives inside them: information about the reporting entity’s assets, liabilities, equity, income and expenses that is useful for assessing future net cash inflows and for assessing stewardship. That information has places — the statement of financial position, the performance statement(s), and other statements and notes, where cash flows matter but are not elements — and the statements are prepared for a specified reporting period, with comparatives, admitting forward-looking or post-period information only when it serves the objective. Entity perspective and going concern are two readings of those same statements; they sit side by side and do not produce each other or the boundary. The statements are about a reporting entity — a single entity, a portion, or more than one, not necessarily a legal entity — and where a parent controls subsidiaries that boundary is read as consolidated (parent and subsidiaries as one) or unconsolidated (the parent alone). A harder boundary test applies when the entity is not a legal entity and not a parent–subsidiary set; combined financial statements are only acknowledged.

Not the story: a Board pipeline from perspective to going concern to boundary; combined statements as a third reading beside consolidated and unconsolidated; cash flows as elements; an IFRS 10 control test.

## Visit path

1. Enter the financial-statements plate at its title: a particular form of general purpose financial report.
2. Inside the same plate, read the objective — the five elements, then the two assessments side by side (“and”, not an arrow).
3. Move left to right across the three places. Cash flows sit in the third place, marked as not elements.
4. The reporting-period axis is fixed under those three places: preceding comparatives, this period (the stronger mark), and events after period-end, with the forward-looking limit on the same bar.
5. A hairline, then two equal cards — entity perspective and going concern. Their borders close. Neither card has a stem.
6. The only stem leaves the bottom edge of the statements plate, labelled “are about”, and the arrowhead enters the top edge of the reporting entity.
7. Inside the entity: the boundary (single, portion, or more than one). Then the dashed consolidated plate — parent controls each subsidiary — beside the consolidated and unconsolidated readings, with “not a substitute” under that pair.
8. The harder boundary is a lighter rail beside that usual picture. Combined statements are a one-line note under a hairline at the foot: a stop, not a third reading.

## Relationship

One sentence: these financial statements are about a reporting entity. Primary backbone is that relationship scene (two objects, one labelled stem). Nested inside the statements: a placement row, a time axis under it, and two closed readings. Nested inside the entity: an org chart beside two boundary readings, a side rail for the harder boundary, and a foot note for combined statements.

## How A–H are handled

### A. Scope / financial statements vs general purpose financial reports (§3.1)

On the map. Chip **3.0** → `#s-3-0`. The plate title is the object: financial statements as a particular form of general purpose financial reports, depicting resources, claims and changes that meet the element definitions. Chapters 1–2 versus 3–8 stay in the body; the map does not become a chapter index.

### B. Objective of financial statements (§3.2–3.3)

On the map, inside the statements plate. Chip **3.2** → `#s-3-2` (this chip also covers the reporting period, because that is where the site teaches it). The objective names the five elements and sets the two assessments side by side. Three place cards say where the information appears. Cash flows are in the third card, in stronger ink, as important and not elements. Recognition versus other statements is the card split (position and performance recognise elements; the third card is presentation and disclosure). Risk, contributions and distributions, and methods sit in that third card. The longer “why the Board wrote it this way” (IAS 1 comparison, BC3.3–BC3.8) stays in the body.

### C. Reporting period (§3.4–3.7) — placed on the map

On the map, as a **time axis under the three places**, still inside the 3.2 group (left rule runs from the objective through the axis). It is not a fourth lens and not a step after going concern.

The site has no separate anchor for the period: official §3.4–3.7 are taught inside `#s-3-2`. A second circle would repeat 3.2, so the period has a heading of the same weight as Objective and no chip of its own. The 3.2 chip above it is the jump.

What the axis holds:

- Specified period (lead line).
- This period, the stronger mark: end-of-period or during-period position, including unrecognised assets and liabilities, and income and expenses for the period.
- Preceding: comparatives for at least one period, so users can see changes and trends.
- After period-end: events only when needed to meet the objective.
- Forward-looking limit on the same bar: only when it relates to those elements and is useful (measurement estimates as the example). Strategy narrative is named as what the statements do not usually contain.

### D. Entity perspective (§3.8)

On the map. Chip **3.3** → `#s-3-3`. Equal card beside going concern, inside the statements plate, closed bottom. Whole-entity view, not any one investor, lender or creditor group; the entity is separate from its capital providers. One consequence line: non-controlling interests are equity of that whole. No IFRS 10 mechanics. The proprietary-viewpoint contrast stays in the body.

### E. Going concern (§3.9)

On the map. Chip **3.4** → `#s-3-4`. Same size and format as perspective. Normally continue; if there is an intention or need to liquidate or cease trading, a different basis may be used and must be described. No stem out of this card, so it does not look caused by, or causative of, the boundary. The worked example and the IAS 1 / IAS 10 alignment stay in the body.

### F. Reporting entity and boundary (§3.10–3.14)

On the map. Chip **3.5** → `#s-3-5` on the entity plate the stem enters. Definition: required or chooses to prepare financial statements; single, portion, or more than one; not necessarily a legal entity. The dashed plate is the usual case (a legal entity, or legal entities linked by control), with the fork labelled “controls” and a leg to each subsidiary. The harder boundary is a lighter side rail, not a third step and not a yes/no gate: both conditions (not a legal entity, and not only legal entities linked as parent–subsidiary), then primary users’ needs — not an arbitrary or incomplete set, neutral, described. Joint control and significant influence are not on the map. The branch worked example stays in the body.

### G. Consolidated vs unconsolidated (§3.15–3.18)

On the map. One chip **3.6** → `#s-3-6` on the pair, not a chip on each card. They sit beside the org chart, not under the subsidiary row. Dashed consolidated card matches the group plate; unconsolidated fill matches the parent. Consolidated: one reporting entity; distributions to the parent depend on the subsidiaries; not a separate picture of one subsidiary. Unconsolidated: the parent alone; a claim on the parent typically gives no claim on the subsidiaries. Shared line under the pair: when consolidated statements are required, unconsolidated statements are not a substitute; a parent may still prepare them in addition. Dashed see-also on the card it qualifies: IFRS 10 for control, IAS 27 for the “separate” label. Distributable reserves, and parent-alone figures in the consolidated notes, stay in the body.

### H. Combined financial statements (§3.12)

On the map only as a foot note inside the entity plate. Chip **3.7** → `#s-3-7`. No card matching the consolidated / unconsolidated pair. Wording: two or more entities not all linked by a parent–subsidiary relationship; acknowledged; the Framework does not say when or how.

## What was rejected from cfch3-opus2

- The three lenses were the whole map. Scope, the objective, where information appears, cash flows as non-elements, the reporting period, the “not a substitute” rule, the non-controlling-interest consequence, and combined statements were absent.
- The stem left Boundary only (“draws the reporting entity”). That made perspective and going concern look like unused steps, and it made the boundary the cause of the entity. Going concern is not caused by drawing the boundary. The new stem leaves the statements plate as a whole and says “are about”.
- Chip 3.6 was captioned “Financial statements” on the consolidated pair. That name belongs to the chapter’s first object. The pair is now “Consolidated and unconsolidated”.
- No Board order was added to replace that stem. Perspective and going concern do not arrow into the entity. There is no lenses → org → gate flow.
- Combined statements were already off the opus2 map. They stay off the peer row; the foot note is a pointer, not a promotion.
