Chapter 9 — Combined financial statements

Financial statements of a reporting entity that comprises two or more entities not all linked by a parent–subsidiary relationship. The Framework acknowledges them but does not say when or how to prepare them. CF 2018 · para 3.12CF 2018 · para BC3.21

Concept Map

9.0Scope of this chaptertop

Combined financial statements are the financial statements of a reporting entity that comprises two or more entities not all linked by a parent–subsidiary relationship. The points are that they are prepared for a transaction and for a limited time, that no IFRS Standard governs their preparation, and that Standards still prevail. The Framework acknowledges them because they can give useful information in some circumstances, but does not discuss when or how entities could prepare them. CF 2018 · para 3.12CF 2018 · para BC3.21DTT A2 2022 · 6.2EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 6.2

  • Prepared for a transaction, for a limited time. Financial information may be needed for parts of a group under common control that are not a legal sub-group — usually for disposals, distributions, business combinations, spin-offs and IPOs. Combined statements are inherently temporary: prepared for a specific objective at a point in time for a set number of periods, after which the reporting entity prepares consolidated statements or is subsumed into a larger group. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.2–A2.3PwC MOA 2020 · A2.26
  • No IFRS Standard governs their preparation. IFRS has no specific guidance on preparing combined statements, although the Framework and other Standards contain relevant information; firm guidance does not override the Standards or regulatory requirements. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.8PwC MOA 2020 · A2.10CF 2018 · para BC3.21
  • Standards still prevail for recognition and measurement of items inside the reporting entity once the boundary is set. CF 2018 · para SP1.2IAS 8 · para 11
See also 3.5 Reporting entity3.6 Reporting entity boundary3.7 Combined financial statementsCh10 Carve-out

9.1Reporting entity and the combined labeltop

The label follows the composition of the reporting entity, not its legal form. CF 2018 · para 3.10–3.12EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 6.2 A reporting entity can be one entity, a portion of an entity or several entities, parent–subsidiary paths lead to consolidated or unconsolidated FS, and the combined FS label covers entities not all linked by a parent–subsidiary relationship, with sibling subsidiaries as an example.

  • Reporting entity. A reporting entity is an entity that is required, or chooses, to prepare financial statements. A reporting entity can be a single entity or a portion of an entity or can comprise more than one entity. A reporting entity is not necessarily a legal entity. CF 2018 · para 3.10EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 6.2KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 1.2.90.10
  • Parent–subsidiary paths. Parent + subsidiaries → consolidated FS; parent alone → unconsolidated FS. CF 2018 · para 3.11EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 6.2.1
  • Combined FS label. If a reporting entity comprises two or more entities that are not all linked by a parent-subsidiary relationship, the reporting entity’s financial statements are referred to as ‘combined financial statements’.   • Sibling subsidiaries are an example. The combined financial statements of sibling subsidiaries within a group illustrate the label; the Board concluded the concept would be best developed as a separate standards-level project rather than in the Framework. DTT A2 2022 · 6.2

9.2Difficult boundaries — primary-user teststop

When the reporting entity is not a legal entity and not only legal entities linked by parent–subsidiary, set the boundary from primary users’ information needs. The points are the trigger, the driver, what faithful representation requires, and that a business without legal boundaries can be a reporting entity. CF 2018 · para 3.13–3.14EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 6.2

  • Trigger. Determining the appropriate boundary can be difficult if the reporting entity (a) is not a legal entity; and (b) does not comprise only legal entities linked by a parent–subsidiary relationship. CF 2018 · para 3.13PwC MOA 2020 · A2.38
  • Driver. Boundary is driven by the information needs of the primary users of that reporting entity’s financial statements — they need relevant information that faithfully represents what it purports to represent. CF 2018 · para 3.14EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 6.2
  • Faithful representation requires that:
    • The boundary does not contain an arbitrary or incomplete set of economic activities;
    • Including that set within the boundary results in neutral information; and
    • A description is provided of how the boundary was determined and of what constitutes the reporting entity.
    CF 2018 · para 3.14KPMG Combined/Carve 2022 · 1.2KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 1.2.90.30–40
  • A business without legal boundaries can be a reporting entity. A vehicle without separate legal personality can be one if it is a separately identifiable financial structure — for example some partnerships, branches or segments. Its boundary should let users obtain relevant financial information that faithfully represents its economic activities. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.39–A2.41CF 2018 · para 3.10

Test

Is the set of economic activities inside the boundary complete and non-arbitrary, and does including it give neutral information? CF 2018 · para 3.14

Yes

Outcome · Yes

9.3Combined vs consolidated — firm definitionstop

Consolidated — parent and all subsidiaries

Historical financial information of a legal group, requiring consolidation of the parent and all of its subsidiaries. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.15CF 2018 · para 3.11

Combined — specific economic activities

Historical financial information of specific economic activities bound together but not a legal group — usually aggregated from segments, separate entities or components that fail the IFRS 10 definition of a group. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.4PwC MOA 2020 · A2.15CF 2018 · para 3.12

In the firm definitions the label turns on the links between entities, the new reporting entity is the economic activity behind the combined statements, and several kinds of entity can be a reporting entity in practice.

  • The label turns on the links between entities. Multiple entities not all linked by parent–subsidiary → combined financial statements. KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 1.2.90.20CF 2018 · para 3.12
  • New reporting entity. The economic activity, usually part of an existing group, that is the basis of the combined financial statements. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.6
  • Who can be a reporting entity in practice. An operating segment of a larger group, legally unconnected entities operating together under common control to achieve a single business objective, or entities bound together by contract could each be a reporting entity in appropriate circumstances. In a typical capital-market transaction the reporting entity is a group headed by a legal entity, but for combined statements that legal structure is seldom in place throughout the track-record period. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.34PwC MOA 2020 · A2.37

9.4Why combined FS are preparedtop

Combined FS are driven by transactions, still serve the CF objective and may be required or shaped by regulation, while their purpose and users shape preparation and presentation and any specific regulatory requirements must be followed.

  • Transaction drivers. IPO of a line of business; spin-off; SPAC target; private M&A (asset or share deal) — historical FS of the existing legal group may not represent the undertaking being offered. KPMG Combined/Carve 2022 · 1.4PwC MOA 2020 · A2.2–A2.3
  • Objective still CF objective. Primary users need relevant, faithfully representative information about the reporting entity’s resources, claims and changes — stewardship + cash-flow prospects remain the assessments. CF 2018 · para 1.2CF 2018 · para 3.2KPMG Combined/Carve 2022 · 1.2PwC MOA 2020 · A2.25
  • Regulation may require or shape the pack (EU prospectus complex history; SEC Topic 1.B; local practices differ). Regulatory basis may sit alongside an IFRS compliance statement. KPMG Combined/Carve 2022 · 1.4.10PwC MOA 2020 · A2.20–A2.23
  • Purpose and users shape preparation and presentation. Combined statements are usually prepared for a contemplated transaction — an IPO through a spin-off of a division or major line of business, a planned distribution to shareholders, or as the basis for final purchase-price discussions in an M&A deal. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.18–A2.19
  • Specific regulatory requirements must be followed. Few countries have them; where they exist, the result might or might not comply with IFRS. Information sources include UK SIR 2000, US SEC Staff Accounting Bulletin Topic 1.B, Australian guidance on non-IFRS financial information, a Hong Kong auditing guideline, and Accountancy Europe’s analysis of common practices. Where a jurisdiction has no guidance, voluntarily following a regulator’s guidance can help users. Management should consult its auditors and advisers and understand the regulator’s views early. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.13PwC MOA 2020 · A2.20–A2.24PwC MOA 2020 · Exhibit A2.1

In accordance with IFRS — fair presentation

Many combined statements of a new reporting entity assert IFRS compliance and receive a normal audit opinion referring to a true and fair view or fair presentation in accordance with IFRS. Some regulators — for example the Hong Kong Stock Exchange — require opinions referring to full compliance with IFRS. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.27–A2.28PwC MOA 2020 · A2.32

Basis of preparation — not general purpose

The more complex the pre-transaction structure and the more adjustments and allocations, the further the information moves towards a basis of preparation — described as drawn up in accordance with the basis in a note, not general purpose financial statements in accordance with IFRS; assurance then refers to that basis. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.29–A2.32

Auditors often warn readers that the new reporting entity has not operated on its own, so its combined results may not show how it would have performed stand-alone.

  • Emphasis of matter is common. Audit opinions on IFRS combined statements often explain that the new reporting entity has not operated as a separate entity, or that the statements are not necessarily indicative of results had it been a stand-alone entity. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.33

A binding element must hold the economic activities together throughout the period. It is the characteristic on which the activities are bound — for example legal ownership or common control; it is not necessarily direct legal ownership, although common control is almost always present. Common control exists where control lies with the same ultimate party, or parties bound by a contract. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.16PwC MOA 2020 · A2.35 Two considerations decide whether a reporting entity exists, and common control gives one governing body to assess.

  • Two considerations decide whether a reporting entity exists. (a) Whether the assets and liabilities are under common control or common management — usually common control; and (b) whether the economic activity is or will be bound together for a purpose — by a legal re-organisation after the reporting date but before publication, a re-organisation that will happen with a proposed IPO, disposal or similar transaction, or an agreement in place throughout the periods covered. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.42
  • Common control gives one governing body to assess. Users can assess whether that single governing body made efficient and effective use of resources. Combined statements can only be prepared for the period of common control; an entity acquired from a third party during the track-record period is accounted for by acquisition accounting from the date control was obtained. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.44–A2.45

9.6Preparation approach — steps, accounting policies and IFRS 1top

There is no set IFRS method and a common set of questions decides, preparation follows five key steps, statements claiming IFRS compliance must pass the IFRS claim test, and a re-organisation often forms the new reporting entity.

  • No set IFRS method — a common set of questions decides. Preparation can require considerable judgement: the nature of the transaction, the boundary, the quality of the accounting records, the parent’s past practices and policies, and the regulator’s views all matter. The answers determine whether IFRS combined statements can be prepared, how, and what disclosures are needed. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.7PwC MOA 2020 · A2.9PwC MOA 2020 · A2.11CF 2018 · para BC3.21
  • Five key steps.
    • (1) Determine the purpose of the combined statements and understand the relevant regulatory requirements;
    • (2) Determine the new reporting entity;
    • (3) Consider whether the combined statements can comply with IFRS;
    • (4) Deal with the practical issues of preparation;
    • (5) Make clear disclosures
    PwC MOA 2020 · A2.14
  • IFRS claim test. Combined statements that claim IFRS compliance must follow all recognition, measurement and disclosure requirements of IFRS; the basic objectives still apply — assessing management stewardship, assessing the ability to generate cash flows, and comparability over time. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.50
  • A re-organisation often forms the new reporting entity. A new entity might be formed to acquire only a portion of the existing legal group as the listing business. In one demerger basis-of-preparation extract, the company was established on 29 November 20X6; until then its business was performed by entities controlled by another group, and a demerger split that group in two and transferred certain assets to the company. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.51PwC MOA 2020 · Exhibit A2.6

Accounting policies must suit the purpose of the combined statements. The accounting information previously recorded for the economic activities is the starting point, adjusted to the chosen policies. Applying different policies does not in itself cause an IFRS compliance issue — management must select policies that give relevant and reliable information. The overall approach starts with whether the entity is a first-time adopter and then chooses top-down or bottom-up, and IFRS 1 applies to a new reporting entity.

  • Overall approach — first-time adoption, then top-down or bottom-up. Two linked decisions: (a) whether the combined/carved-out RE is a first-time adopter of IFRS Standards (IFRS 1 — jurisdiction diversity; Ex 3A–3B inventory only, not CF Illus); (b) top-down extraction from larger consolidated FS vs bottom-up build from component FS. CF does not prescribe either. KPMG Combined/Carve 2022 · 3.1
  • IFRS 1 applies to a new reporting entity. Paragraph D16 gives a subsidiary — including a carve-out entity — that adopts IFRS later than its parent two options: carrying amounts that would be in the parent’s consolidated statements, based on the parent’s transition date, or carrying amounts required by IFRS 1, based on its own transition date. A new reporting entity is unlikely to have previous financial statements, so no previous GAAP reconciliation can be prepared; one spun off from an IFRS reporter has no reconciling items. An opening balance sheet is still required. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.134–A2.137

Facts

  • Group X (consumer markets) plans an IPO of clothing operations held partly in two subsidiaries and partly in a non-legal-entity division.
  • Listing documents require carve-out FS for clothing.
  • X decides to prepare by extracting clothing amounts from its consolidated FS (top-down).

Top-down flow

  • SourceLarger Group X consolidated FS (include clothing)
  • ExtractClothing carrying amounts recognised by the larger RE
  • IntoClothing carve-out / IPO pack
  • CF still doesSet + describe clothing RE boundary (3.13–3.14) — does not pick the approach

Assessment

  • Top-down: carrying amounts recognised by the larger RE for clothing operations flow into the carve-out pack.
  • CF role: still set and describe the clothing reporting-entity boundary (3.13–3.14); CF does not choose top-down vs bottom-up.
  • Overlap: same clothing IPO shape as Illus 9.5.2 — here the teaching point is the preparation approach, not the label.

Conclusion

  • Top-down is a firm overall approach, not an Official CF measurement basis.
  • Disclose overall approach; IFRS claim still hinges on purpose, boundary, and historical (not pro forma) content.

Illustration: 9.6.2 — Bottom-up approach (build from component FS)KPMG Combined/Carve 2022 · Example 3D CF 2018 · para 3.12 CF 2018 · para 3.13–3.14

Facts

  • Group Z holds many investment properties; each property sits in a separate subsidiary (S1–S50) with its own FS; Z also consolidates.
  • Z intends to list three subsidiaries (S1–S3) in an IPO of a new REIT; shares transfer to a newco.
  • Management builds the combined pack from the component subsidiaries’ FS (bottom-up), rather than extracting a clothing-style carve from one consolidated line.

Bottom-up flow

  • SourcesS1–S3 stand-alone FS (investment-property subsidiaries)
  • BuildCombine component information under REIT reporting entity
  • LabelCombined FS when not all parent–subsidiary-linked for the pack RE
  • CF still doesPrimary-user / FR boundary tests + binding element through the period

Assessment

  • Bottom-up: start from S1–S3 stand-alone information and combine under the REIT reporting entity.
  • CF label: combined FS when entities are not all parent–subsidiary-linked for the pack’s RE.
  • Still need primary-user / FR boundary tests and a binding element through the period.

Conclusion

  • Bottom-up is the peer firm approach to top-down — choice is facts/jurisdiction/records quality, not a CF flowchart.
  • Contrast Illus 9.5.6: assembling unrelated groups’ carve-outs is not the same as combining commonly controlled / fit-for-purpose components.

  • Modifies the clothing IPO top-down shape in Illustration 9.6.1: Group X will prepare clothing carve-out FS applying IFRS 1 paragraph D16(a).
  • X has previously issued IFRS consolidated FS that include the clothing operations.
  • Clothing division in Jurisdiction J was acquired in 2019; PPA recognised goodwill in X’s consolidated FS.

Assessment

  • D16(a) path: carrying amounts in the carve-out match those in X’s consolidated FS for clothing.
  • Exclude consolidation procedures and the goodwill that arose when X acquired the clothing division (D16(a) shape as illustrated).
  • IFRS 1 presentation: include IFRS 1 presentation/disclosures except no previous-GAAP reconciliation (firm 3.1.10).

Facts

  • Continues the REIT bottom-up shape in Illustration 9.6.2: Group Z prepares combined FS for S1–S3 applying IFRS 1 paragraph D16(b).
  • None of S1–S3 previously prepared IFRS FS — all applied local GAAP.

D16(b) own-transition path

StepWhat happens
1Each of S1–S3 prepares IFRS information YE 2021; date of transition 1 Jan 2020

Steps

  • Step 1: each of S1, S2, S3 prepares IFRS-compliant financial information for YE 2021 with DoT 1 Jan 2020 — creates base data (this step alone does not make each a first-time adopter without a complete set + explicit IFRS statement distributed externally).
  • Step 2: combine that IFRS information for S1–S3 into the combined pack.
  • Policy alignment: example assumes consistent policies; otherwise align when combining.
  • D16(b) essence: determine IFRS 1-compliant amounts using the combined RE’s own transition date — without simply taking the larger RE’s carrying amounts (contrast D16(a) / Illus 9.6.3).

Conclusion

  • Bottom-up + own DoT is a firm FTA path when components never issued IFRS.
  • Include IFRS 1 presentation/disclosures except previous-GAAP reconciliation (firm practice).
  • Third observed path (extract including goodwill/FV without D16) is flagged as inconsistent with IFRS 1 for generic FS — jurisdiction research.

Continuity design

  • Carved S1/S2 + portion of S3 (1 Jan 2020 – 31 Dec 2021): book values from underlying FS of S1–S3.
  • Newco consolidated (1 Jan 2021 – 31 Dec 2022): book values from underlying FS; Newco formed 1 Jan 2021 when S1, S2 and portion of S3 transfer — book-value accounting / comparatives per Insights 5.13.190 family.
  • Ideal case: pre-IPO carve-out track and post-IPO Newco FS connect on overlapping 2021 information so investors see continuous history.

Conclusion

9.7Historical or pro forma, presentation and disclosuretop

Combined statements that claim IFRS compliance rest on historical data. Historical financial information is past information prepared under statutory requirements and accounting standards, drawn from distinct or separate accounting records, and auditable. Pro forma information is hypothetical: it illustrates an outcome on an “as-if” basis, including effects of events that did not occur or occurred at a different date. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.63–A2.64

Decision — historical information or pro forma?

Test

Do the amounts and adjustments rest on actual past amounts — including actual group expenses allocated on a rational basis — rather than “as-if” effects of events that did not occur? PwC MOA 2020 · A2.64PwC MOA 2020 · A2.67–A2.68

Historic

Outcome · Historical

IFRS compliance can be claimed if the other requirements are met. Adjustments and allocations for items the new reporting entity needs are frequent; deciding what is historical is a key judgement. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.63PwC MOA 2020 · A2.66–A2.68

As-if

Outcome · As-if

Pro forma — does not comply with IFRS. Some jurisdictions regard any allocation as a pro forma adjustment. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.64PwC MOA 2020 · A2.67PwC MOA 2020 · A2.69

A neat sub-group is rare, and comparative periods are presented without hindsight.

  • A neat sub-group is rare. A new reporting entity is rarely a sub-group whose consolidated statements already present all and only its activities — including debt, taxes, share-based payments, share of corporate assets, management services and charges; where it is, it can present consolidated statements. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.65
  • No hindsight. Comparative periods stay closed and the business is presented as management ran it at the time — accruals for a lawsuit settled before the combined statements are prepared are not trued up. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.132

Facts

  • Group X intends to dispose of sub-group Y together with subsidiary Z, another directly owned subsidiary.
  • X acquired Y four years ago; its purchase price allocation recognised goodwill in X’s IFRS consolidated statements.
  • Management prepares combined statements for Y and Z using the amounts in X’s consolidated statements.

Assessment

  • The goodwill and other PPA adjustments in X’s consolidated statements are included in the sub-group financial statements.
  • The combined statements are based on financial statements prepared using only historical information.

Conclusion

  • It might be possible to claim compliance with IFRS.

Illustration: 9.7.2 — Metals demerger presented as a stand-alone listed group — pro formaPwC MOA 2020 · Exhibit A2.8 PwC MOA 2020 · A2.64PwC MOA 2020 · A2.69

Facts

  • Group X has metals and coal operations and plans a demerger creating two listed entities, A Metals and A Coal; each shareholder’s investment is divided between them.
  • A Metals combined statements are prepared from historical information extracted from X’s audited statements and underlying records.
  • Income statements and cash flows are presented as if the demerger had occurred and A Metals had been a stand-alone group for the three-year track record the listing authority requires.

Assessment

  • Coal activities in the same legal entities as metals in some jurisdictions are excluded.
  • Impairments recognised during the track record are reversed against opening retained earnings.
  • Additional costs are recorded to present A Metals as a separate listed entity, without regard to X’s actual expenses.

Conclusion

  • The information is prepared on a pro forma basis and is not in accordance with IFRS.

Goodwill and purchase-price adjustments should accompany the business they relate to. The predecessor method for common-control combinations generally uses carrying values at the highest level of common control, and the current parent is unlikely to be part of the new reporting entity. Parents rarely allocate goodwill in separate IFRS statements — IFRS has no push-down requirement, and company law often makes push-down difficult. Many users and regulators would see the track record as incomplete without the cost of the business to the current parent, so goodwill and related adjustments might be included rather than stranded in the parent. A carve-out goodwill note presents allocated goodwill by segment as if the group had acquired the business at the acquisition date. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.74–A2.78PwC MOA 2020 · Exhibit A2.9 Whether to present NCI turns on factors such as whether it survives the planned transaction, and the treatment chosen must be useful and comply with IFRS.

  • Non-controlling interest — practice varies. Whether to present it depends on factors including whether the NCI survives the planned transaction; choose a treatment that is useful and complies with IFRS. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.52

Businesses that will leave the new legal group are presented on a carve-out basis or a disposal group basis. The track-record entity may include businesses that will not continue after a re-organisation near the transaction. Regulators may prefer one approach — US regulators would generally expect the disposal group basis — and users often prefer information on the continuing business only. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.53–A2.56

Decision — carve-out basis or disposal group basis?

Test

Is the business being disposed of dissimilar to the rest of the new reporting entity, separately managed, and supported by separate historical books and records? PwC MOA 2020 · A2.56

All met

Outcome · All criteria met

Carve-out basis is reasonable — exclude all businesses and activity that will not form part of the new legal group. The criteria are not defined in IFRS; UK and US regulators have more explicit guidance sharing similar concepts. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.54PwC MOA 2020 · A2.56

Not met

Outcome · Not met

Disposal group basis — include those businesses up to disposal or discontinuance, presented separately from the continuing business. The disposal group is similar to IFRS 5’s, need not be a discontinued operation, and applies only to balance sheets where IFRS 5’s requirements were met at that date. PwC MOA 2020 · A2.57–A2.61

Clear disclosure is fundamental. The notes should address at least: PwC MOA 2020 · A2.139–A2.140

  • How the new reporting entity was identified and the significant judgements — the boundary described in the basis of preparation, explaining which entities are included — and a list of combining entities PwC MOA 2020 · A2.140CF 2018 · para 3.14
  • Whether prepared in accordance with IFRS or a basis of preparation PwC MOA 2020 · A2.140
  • Principal accounting policies
  • How assets, liabilities, income and expenses were allocated
  • How inter-company transactions were reflected PwC MOA 2020 · A2.140
  • How EPS was calculated, if applicable
  • Critical judgements and estimates PwC MOA 2020 · A2.140
  • Disclosure requirements of all applicable IFRSs, if IFRS compliance is claimed PwC MOA 2020 · A2.140
