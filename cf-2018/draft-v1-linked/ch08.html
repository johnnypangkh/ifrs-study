Chapter 8 — Concepts of capital

Concept Map

8.0Scope of this chaptertop

Concepts of capital and capital maintenance define the capital an entity seeks to maintain, and so the point from which profit is measured. They are important to financial reporting and influence the definitions of income and expenses, the selection of measurement bases, and presentation and disclosure decisions. The Framework does not override Standards. CF 2018 · para 8.4CF 2018 · para BC8.2CF 2018 · para SP1.2EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 11

Pitfall: Reading “capital” as share capital — under a financial concept capital is the net assets or equity of the entity; under a physical concept it is the entity’s productive capacity. CF 2018 · para 8.1EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 11.1

The chapter was carried forward unchanged rather than rewritten in 2018, no concept of wealth or capital was chosen to drive measurement, and hyperinflation was left to Standards.

  • Carried forward unchanged, not rewritten in 2018. Updating the discussion was not feasible when the Board developed the 2018 Framework and could have delayed its completion significantly; leaving the discussion out altogether would have been inappropriate. The material is therefore unchanged from the 2010 Framework and originally appeared in the 1989 Framework. The Board may revisit the concepts in the future if it considers a revision necessary. CF 2018 · para BC8.1–BC8.4CF 2018 · para BC0.16DTT A2 2022 · 11
  • No concept of wealth or capital was chosen to drive measurement. Had the Board identified a concept of wealth or capital that meets users’ information needs, a single measurement basis would have been required to measure it; the Board decided not to update the discussion of capital and capital maintenance and not to seek to identify such a concept. See also 6.1 Measurement bases CF 2018 · para BC6.6

In developing the 2018 Framework the Board did not address restatement of the measuring unit in hyperinflation, concluding that such issues would best be dealt with if it were to carry out projects to consider revising Standards on those topics. 8.1Concepts of capital — financial and physicaltop

Two concepts of capital. Under a financial concept — such as invested money or invested purchasing power — capital is synonymous with the net assets or equity of the entity. Under a physical concept — such as operating capability — capital is the productive capacity of the entity, based on, for example, units of output per day. CF 2018 · para 8.1PwC MOA 2020 · 1.91EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 11.1

Financial concept — invested money or purchasing power

Capital is the net assets or equity of the entity, measured in nominal monetary units or in units of constant purchasing power. CF 2018 · para 8.1CF 2018 · para 8.3(a)EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 11.1

Physical concept — operating capability

Capital is the productive capacity of the entity — or the resources or funds needed to achieve that capacity — for example units of output per day. CF 2018 · para 8.1CF 2018 · para 8.3(b)EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 11.2

Most entities adopt a financial concept of capital, and the Framework does not prefer either concept.

  • Most entities adopt a financial concept. A financial concept of capital is adopted by most entities in preparing their financial statements; it concentrates on maintaining the financial capital the owners have invested and is the most widely adopted capital concept. CF 2018 · para 8.1PwC MOA 2020 · 1.90PwC MOA 2020 · 1.91EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 11
  • The Framework does not prefer either concept. It describes both concepts of capital maintenance without indicating a preference; the choice is left to the needs of users. CF 2018 · para 8.2DTT A2 2022 · 11

Selection follows the needs of users of the financial statements. The concept chosen indicates the goal to be attained in determining profit, even though there may be some measurement difficulties in making the concept operational. CF 2018 · para 8.2PwC MOA 2020 · 1.90EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 11

8.2Capital maintenance and the determination of profittop

Capital maintenance links the concept of capital to profit. It is concerned with how an entity defines the capital it seeks to maintain, and provides the point of reference by which profit is measured. Profit is the residual that remains after expenses — including capital maintenance adjustments, where appropriate — have been deducted from income; if expenses exceed income, the residual is a loss. CF 2018 · para 8.3–8.4PwC MOA 2020 · 1.92EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 11

Return on capital — profit

Only inflows of assets in excess of the amounts needed to maintain capital may be regarded as profit, and therefore as a return on capital. CF 2018 · para 8.4PwC MOA 2020 · 1.92EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 11

Return of capital — maintaining what was there

Amounts needed to maintain the capital at the beginning of the period are not profit. Capital maintenance is the prerequisite for telling the two apart. CF 2018 · para 8.4CF 2018 · para 8.6EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 11

The principal difference between the two is how price changes are treated. In general terms, an entity has maintained its capital if it has as much capital at the end of the period as at the beginning; any amount over and above that is profit. CF 2018 · para 8.6EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 11

Financial — nominal monetary units

Profit is the increase in nominal money capital. Increases in the prices of assets held — holding gains — are, conceptually, profits, though they may not be recognised as such until the assets are disposed of in an exchange transaction. CF 2018 · para 8.7EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 11.1

Financial — constant purchasing power

Profit is the increase in invested purchasing power. Only the part of a price increase that exceeds the increase in the general level of prices is profit; the rest is a capital maintenance adjustment and part of equity. CF 2018 · para 8.7EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 11.1

Physical — productive capacity

Profit is the increase in physical productive capacity. All price changes affecting assets and liabilities are capital maintenance adjustments that are part of equity, not profit. CF 2018 · para 8.8EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 11.2

8.3Measurement bases and capital maintenance — the accounting modeltop

The measurement bases and the concept of capital maintenance selected together determine the accounting model. Different accounting models exhibit different degrees of relevance and reliability, and management must seek a balance between them. The Framework is applicable to a range of accounting models and provides guidance on preparing and presenting financial statements constructed under the chosen model. CF 2018 · para 8.9PwC MOA 2020 · 1.93DTT A2 2022 · 11EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 11 Physical capital maintenance requires current cost, financial capital maintenance leaves the basis open, and no particular model is prescribed except in exceptional circumstances.

  • Physical capital maintenance requires current cost. The maintenance concept constrains the measurement basis. CF 2018 · para 8.5EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 11.2
  • Financial capital maintenance leaves the basis open. The basis depends on whether the entity seeks to maintain nominal money capital or invested purchasing power. CF 2018 · para 8.5EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 11.1
  • No particular model is prescribed except in exceptional circumstances. At present it is not the intention of the Board to prescribe a particular model other than in exceptional circumstances, such as for entities reporting in the currency of a hyperinflationary economy; that intention will be reviewed in the light of world developments. CF 2018 · para 8.9PwC MOA 2020 · 1.90PwC MOA 2020 · 1.93EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 11

8.4Capital maintenance adjustments and incometop

Revaluations and restatements meet the definitions of income and expenses, but may stay out of the income statement. Revaluing or restating assets and liabilities increases or decreases equity. Under certain concepts of capital maintenance those increases or decreases are not included in the income statement; they are included in equity as capital maintenance adjustments or revaluation reserves. CF 2018 · para 8.10EY iGAAP 2026 Ch2 · section 11

The same price movement can be profit under one concept and an equity adjustment under another. That follows from the different price-change treatments of nominal, constant purchasing power and physical capital maintenance. CF 2018 · para 8.6–8.8PwC MOA 2020 · FAQ 1.93.1

Facts

  • A sole trader starts a business buying and selling second-hand cars.
  • In his first year he buys one car for C1,000 and sells it for C2,000.
  • When he sells it, an equivalent car costs C1,200.
  • General inflation between the dates of buying and selling is 10%.

Monetary capital

  • Profit C1,000 — the amount in excess of the business’s original capital.
  • Takes no account of the effects of inflation; the most commonly used basis.

General purchasing power

  • Profit C900 — measured only after preserving the opening capital of C1,000 in terms of its general purchasing power.
  • The C100 inflation adjustment is the effect of the general increase in prices on opening capital; the profit leaves capital of C1,100 in the business to maintain its purchasing power.

Operating capital maintenance

  • Profit C800 — after preserving the trader’s ability to replace the car sold.
  • Capital of C1,200 stays in the business, enough to buy a car to begin the next period’s trade.

The illustration’s columns map to the Framework’s concepts. Monetary capital is financial capital maintenance in nominal monetary units; general purchasing power is financial capital maintenance in units of constant purchasing power; operating capital maintenance is physical capital maintenance, where capital is operating capability.
