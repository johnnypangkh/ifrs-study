## Teaching sections

### 5.0 Scope of this chapter
This chapter is the site home for official CF **Chapter 5 — Recognition and derecognition**. Cites stay **§5.x** / **BC5.x**. Recognition is the process of capturing an item that meets an element definition into the statement of financial position or statement(s) of financial performance — in words and as a monetary amount, included in totals. Derecognition is the reverse move when a recognised asset or liability leaves the statement of financial position. **§5.1**, **§5.26**

Meeting a definition (Elements / site Ch4) is **not** the same as recognition. Definitions answer “what *is* an asset / liability / …?” Recognition answers whether capturing that defined item provides useful information. Existence ≠ depiction. **§5.6–5.7**, **§4.1**

**Contrast card — Definition met vs Recognised**

| What it is — definition met | Common trap — “defined → booked” |
|---|---|
| The item satisfies an element definition (asset, liability, equity, income or expense). Existence of the resource or claim is established — a candidate for recognition, not yet depicted. **§5.6**, **§4.1** | Treating a met definition as automatic recognition. Recognition still needs relevance and faithful-representation filters. Existence ≠ depiction. **§5.6–5.7** |

> **Pitfall:** Collapsing “meets the definition” into “must book it.” CF deliberately separates the two decisions. **§5.6–5.7**

### 5.1 Recognition process — capture, link, no matching objective

Recognition captures a defined element for inclusion in the structured summaries of financial position and performance. The amount at which an asset, liability or equity is recognised is its **carrying amount**. Those structured summaries make recognised amounts comparable and understandable by linking items into totals and subtotals. **§5.1–5.2**

Recognition is what ties the elements to the two statement families. At the beginning and end of the period, assets minus liabilities equal equity. Recognised equity changes during the period comprise income minus expenses, plus contributions from holders of equity claims minus distributions to them. Recognising one item (or changing its carrying amount) requires recognising, derecognising, or changing one or more other items — income arrives with an asset increase or liability decrease; expenses arrive with a liability increase or asset decrease. **§5.3–5.4**

Simultaneous recognition of income and related expenses (for example a cash sale of goods) can look like “matching.” Application of CF concepts produces that pairing when it arises from recognising changes in assets and liabilities — but **matching of costs with income is not a CF objective**. CF does not allow recognising statement-of-financial-position items that fail the asset, liability or equity definitions. **§5.5**

> **Pitfall:** Inventing a deferred “matching asset” or “matching liability” so costs line up with income when no element definition is met. **§5.5**

### 5.2 Recognition criteria — CF’s own gates (§5.6–5.7)

Read the chapter visual as CF’s own recognition structure — not a Content-invented 1→N checklist. CF’s gate order is already stated:

- **Definition gate:** only items that meet an element definition are candidates for recognition — and not all defined items are recognised. **§5.6**
- **Usefulness gates:** an asset or liability is recognised only if recognition (and any resulting income, expenses or equity changes) provides **relevant** information **and** a **faithful representation**. **§5.7**

Those usefulness gates are the same fundamental qualitative characteristics taught in the Qualitative chapter, applied to the recognition decision — not a third “recognition QC.” Non-recognition makes the statements less complete and can exclude useful information; forced recognition that fails the filters is worse. **§5.7**, **§2.4–2.5**

**Peer card row — Usefulness gates (equal-rank filters)**

| Relevance gate | Faithful representation gate | Cost constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Recognition (and any resulting income, expenses or equity changes) must provide **relevant** information. Existence uncertainty and low probability are factors here — not automatic kill switches. **§5.7**, **§5.12–5.17** | Recognition must also provide a **faithful representation** of the asset or liability and related income/expenses/equity changes — including measurement. **§5.7**, **§5.18–5.25** | Preparer and user costs must be justified by the benefits of recognition. Consider what information appears if the item is *not* recognised; notes may still be needed. **§5.8–5.11** |

**Cost constraint.** Cost constrains recognition as it constrains other reporting decisions: preparer measurement costs and user analysis costs must be justified by the benefits of recognition. There is no precise formula for “useful at justifiable cost” — judgement, facts, and Standards-level choices decide. Always consider what information would appear *if* the item were not recognised (for example expenditure booked as expense and the trends that creates). Even when not recognised, notes may still need visible information about the item. **§5.8–5.11**

**Why no “recognise everything” presumption?** The Board considered — and rejected — an overarching principle that every defined asset or liability is recognised (with Standards carving exceptions). It expects cases where recognition would not provide useful information, or costs would exceed benefits. CF needs guidance for setting recognition requirements, not a rigid “always recognise” rule. **BC5.5–BC5.6**

> **Pitfall:** Skipping the definition gate and jumping to “is it relevant?” Or treating disclosure as a substitute that rewrites failed recognition filters. Notes can compensate visibility — they do not replace the gates. **§5.6–5.7**, **§5.11**

### 5.3 Why the 2018 shape — probability and “reliable measurement” removed

Pre-2018 recognition criteria taught many candidates a two-gate memory: **probable** future flow + **reliable** cost or value. That package created real problems. Standards used inconsistent probability thresholds (“probable,” “more likely than not,” “virtually certain,” “reasonably possible”). Applying a probability filter could exclude useful items (for example some derivatives) or produce an immediate gain when cash was received for a low-probability liability that was then not recognised. “Reliable measurement” was unclear once reliability was no longer a QC label, and was often read as a hard ban on high measurement uncertainty — even when recognition would still be useful. **BC5.1–BC5.2**

The 2018 CF keeps the same usefulness goal but changes the means: it points **directly** to relevance and faithful representation, then gives guidance on when recognition may lack them — including (but not limited to) many cases the old probability / reliability filters would have caught. The Board did **not** include a probability threshold. Low probability is an **indicator** that recognition may lack relevance in some cases — not an automatic kill switch. High measurement uncertainty is discussed as a factor that may affect faithful representation, not as a single cross-cutting reliability threshold. The Board also emphasised it did not aim to increase or decrease the range of recognised assets and liabilities. **BC5.3–BC5.4**, **BC5.10**, **BC5.19**, **BC5.21**

| Older teaching memory | 2018 CF | Cite |
|-----------------------|---------|------|
| Probability threshold as recognition gate | **Removed** — low probability is a relevance *indicator*, not a threshold | **BC5.19**, **§5.15–5.17** |
| “Reliable measurement” as hard kill | Absorbed into FR discussion — high uncertainty ≠ automatic non-recognition | **BC5.2(c)**, **BC5.21**, **§5.19–5.22** |
| 2010 practical filters (probable + reliable) | Same usefulness goal; 2018 points directly to QCs + supporting guidance | **BC5.3–BC5.4**, **§5.6–5.7** |
| “Expected” flows inside some definitions | Low probability can still meet the definition; recognition may still fail relevance | **§4.14–4.15**, **§4.37–4.38**, **§5.12–5.17** |

**Contrast card — Older probable+reliable memory vs 2018 relevance/FR**

| What it is — 2018 relevance + FR | Common trap — “probable + reliable” memory |
|---|---|
| Point directly to relevance and faithful representation. Low probability is a relevance *indicator*; high measurement uncertainty is an FR factor — neither is an automatic CF kill switch. **§5.6–5.7**, **BC5.3–BC5.4**, **BC5.19**, **BC5.21** | Re-importing a probability threshold or “reliable measurement” hard ban as if they were the 2018 CF gates. Standards may still use probability language — CF itself does not. **BC5.1–BC5.2**, **BC5.19**, **SP1.2** |

> **[Deloitte 2022]** 2018 removed the probability and reliable-measurement thresholds; existence uncertainty or low probability of flow are circumstances when recognition *might* not provide relevant information · CF **§5.12–5.17**; **BC5.19**, **BC5.21**

> **Pitfall:** Re-importing IAS 37-style “probable” language as if it were the CF 2018 gate. Standards may still use probability thresholds — CF itself does not. CF also does not override Standards (**SP1.2**). **BC5.11**, **BC5.19**

### 5.4 Relevance — existence uncertainty and low probability as factors

Information about assets, liabilities, equity, income and expenses is relevant in general — but recognition of a **particular** item may still fail to provide relevant information. CF highlights two example strains: **existence uncertainty** (is there an asset/liability at all?) and **low probability** of inflow or outflow even though the item exists. Presence of one or both does **not** automatically mean recognition lacks relevance; other facts may matter; often a **combination** decides. Teach these as relationships among factors — not as a mandatory numbered pipeline beyond what CF structures. **§5.12–5.13**, **BC5.12**

**Existence uncertainty** is conceptually separate from outcome uncertainty and measurement uncertainty, even though it can feed both. Distinguishing the types makes it easier to decide what information is most relevant and how to depict it faithfully. Combined with low probability and an exceptionally wide outcome range, a single-amount recognition may not provide relevant information. Whether recognised or not, explanatory uncertainty information may still be needed. CF does not give detailed existence-uncertainty mechanics — facts and circumstances (and Standards) decide. **§5.14**, **BC5.13–BC5.14**, **§4.13**, **§4.35**

**Low probability of inflow/outflow.** An asset or liability can exist even if the probability of flow is low (link back to Elements). When probability is low, the most relevant information may be magnitude, timing, and factors affecting probability — typically in the **notes**. Recognition may still add relevant information beyond notes. Market-exchange acquisitions often already price low probability into cost, so recognition (and avoiding an immediate expense/income dump) can stay relevant. Pure non-exchange origins plus low probability income/expense may look less relevant to users. **§5.15–5.17**, **§4.15**, **§4.38**

#### 5.4-ex Worked-example strip — defined but not recognised (low-probability non-exchange) `{#s-5-4-ex}`

| Facts | Concept / gate | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| One-off contingent claim from an alleged product defect. Assume the liability definition is met, but probability of cash outflow is very low. No exchange created the claim; recognising it would create an expense and a single-amount liability. | **Definition:** low probability of outflow can still meet the liability definition (**§4.37–4.38**, **§5.15**). **Relevance:** for a non-exchange origin with low probability, users may not regard recognising the liability and expense as providing relevant information; notes on magnitude, timing and probability factors may be the most relevant package (**§5.16–5.17(b)**). Contrast: a *purchased* low-probability exposure (cost already prices it) can still support recognising that cost as an asset (**§5.17(a)**, **§5.25(a)**). | **Definition met ≠ recognised.** Low probability is a relevance indicator, not an automatic CF threshold. **§5.6–5.7**, **§5.13**, **BC5.19** |

> **Pitfall:** “Low probability → never recognise.” CF treats low probability as a relevance indicator; exchange vs non-exchange and other facts can tip the other way. **§5.13**, **§5.15–5.17**

### 5.5 Faithful representation — measurement uncertainty and related depiction

Recognition is appropriate only if it also provides a **faithful representation** of the asset or liability and of any resulting income, expenses or equity changes. Recognition always requires measurement. Reasonable estimates are essential and do not undermine usefulness if clearly described and explained; even a high level of measurement uncertainty does not necessarily prevent an estimate from providing useful information. **§5.18–5.19**, **§2.19**, **BC5.21**

Uncertainty can become so high that a faithful representation is questionable — especially when the only available measure uses cash-flow techniques plus exceptionally wide outcomes / hard probabilities, extreme sensitivity (tiny probability × huge magnitude), or exceptionally difficult/subjective cash-flow allocations. CF then describes case outcomes (teach as CF guidance on useful depiction — not a Content-invented mandatory A→B→C Board procedure):

| CF case outcome | Teaching paraphrase | Cite |
|-----------------|---------------------|------|
| Uncertain estimate + explanation | Most useful may still be the highly uncertain measure with clear description — especially if it is the most relevant measure | **§5.21** |
| Next-best lower-uncertainty measure | Slightly less relevant but better FR | **§5.21** |
| No useful measure | Limited cases: no available relevant measure is useful even with explanation → do not recognise | **§5.22** |

Whether recognised or not, faithful representation may still need explanatory information about existence, measurement, or outcome uncertainty. FR of a recognised item involves measurement, presentation and disclosure — not only the line amount. When assessing recognition, also weigh: (a) resulting income/expenses/equity depiction (not recognising an acquired asset can dump an expense and look like deterioration while the entity still holds the resource); (b) whether related assets/liabilities are recognised (one-sided recognition can create an accounting mismatch); (c) presentation and disclosure needed for a complete depiction. **§5.23–5.25**

#### 5.5-ex Worked-example strip — not recognising an acquired resource distorts performance `{#s-5-5-ex}`

| Facts | Concept / gate | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Entity pays cash for a specialised right that meets the asset definition. Measurement is estimable but noisy. If the right is not recognised, the cash outlay is booked as expense and profit/equity fall — even though the entity still controls the resource and has not consumed it. | CF flags that result as a potential misleading representation of deterioration (**§5.25(a)**). High measurement uncertainty alone does not force non-recognition; weigh whether a clearly explained estimate (or a slightly less relevant but lower-uncertainty measure) can still provide useful FR (**§5.19–5.21**). Only limited cases refuse recognition because no available relevant measure is useful even with explanation (**§5.22**). | **“Hard to measure” ≠ automatic non-recognition.** Measurement uncertainty is an FR factor; only limited cases refuse recognition. **§5.19–5.22**, **BC5.21** |

> **Pitfall:** Equating “hard to measure precisely” with automatic non-recognition. Measurement uncertainty is a FR factor; only limited cases refuse recognition. **§5.19–5.22**, **BC5.21**

### 5.6 Derecognition — twin aims and conflict cases

**Derecognition** is the removal of all or part of a recognised asset or liability from the statement of financial position. It normally occurs when the item no longer meets the definition — for an asset, when the entity loses control of all or part; for a liability, when it no longer has the present obligation for all or part. **§5.26**

Accounting requirements for derecognition aim to faithfully represent **both**:

1. any assets and liabilities **retained** after the transaction or other event (including any acquired, incurred or created as part of it); and
2. the **change** in the entity’s assets and liabilities as a result of that transaction or other event.

Both aims are required. CF does not invent an extra process spine beyond considering those aims and the supporting procedures it describes. **§5.27**, **BC5.26**

Normally the aims are achieved by: derecognising transferred / expired / consumed / collected / fulfilled components and recognising resulting income/expenses; continuing to recognise any **retained component** as a separate unit of account (generally no income/expense on the retained piece solely from derecognising the transferred piece); and, if needed, separate presentation and/or explanatory information. **§5.28**

**When the aims conflict.** Sometimes an entity appears to transfer an asset or liability but still has it (significant retained exposure to variations can indicate continued control; transfer to an agent leaves control with the transferor) — derecognition is then not appropriate because it would achieve neither aim. In other cases the entity truly no longer has the transferred component, so derecognition faithfully represents that fact — yet may still overstate how much the financial position changed (for example transfer plus a present right/obligation to reacquire via forward, written put or purchased call; or retained exposure to significant variations in a component the entity no longer controls). If derecognition plus presentation/explanation is still not enough for both aims, continuing to recognise the transferred component (treating proceeds as a loan, with separate presentation / explanation) is discussed as a last-resort depiction. **§5.29–5.32**, **§4.24–4.25**

**Why shaped this way?** Legacy debates contrasted a control approach (mirror of recognition — focus on what is retained) with a risks-and-rewards approach (focus on how much exposure changed). Full transfers with no retained exposure reconcile both. Partial transfers and retained exposures can make the aims conflict; the Board views **both** aims as valid and did not pick one labelled approach. Keep teaching labels CF-neutral: retained assets/liabilities vs change depiction. **BC5.24–BC5.29**

> **[Deloitte 2022]** Twin aims are akin to control vs risks-and-rewards approaches; the Board chose not to specify either because it views both as valid · CF **§5.27**; **BC5.27–BC5.28**

#### 5.6-ex Worked-example strip — transfer with retained exposure / reacquire right `{#s-5-6-ex}`

| Facts | Concept / gate | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Entity “sells” receivables for cash but writes a put giving the buyer a present right to put them back, and retains exposure to significant credit losses if defaults spike. Legal form says “sold.” | **Retained side:** significant exposure to variations can indicate continued control; if the asset remains the entity’s, derecognition achieves neither twin aim (**§5.29–5.30**, **§4.24–4.25**). **Change side:** even if control has passed, a present reacquire obligation (written put) or retained exposure can mean plain derecognition overstates how much position changed (**§5.31**). Depiction toolkit: derecognise only what transferred; keep retained components; presentation/explanation; last-resort continued recognition of transferred component (**§5.28**, **§5.32**, **SP1.2**). | **“Sold = off books” is not CF’s test** — both twin aims (retained items *and* change) are required. **§5.27**, **§5.31** |

> **Pitfall:** Achieving only one aim (e.g. “it’s gone from our books”) while overstating how much the financial position changed. Both aims are required. **§5.27**, **§5.31**

### 5.8 Optional teaching prompts *(not a CF procedure)*

> **Label:** CF gives definition gate + relevance/FR filters + derecognition twin aims and supporting procedures. It does **not** prescribe a fixed diagnostic sequence for preparers beyond that structure. Bullet order below is Content pedagogy, not Board order.

- **Definition ≠ recognition:** Does the item meet an element definition? If no → not that element. If yes → still run usefulness filters. **§5.6–5.7**
- **Relevance cue:** Existence uncertainty? Low-probability indicator? Exchange vs non-exchange? Combination of factors — no automatic kill. **§5.12–5.17**
- **FR cue:** Can it be measured usefully (estimate + explanation; or next-best measure; limited no-measure cases)? Related income/expense depiction? Recognition inconsistency? **§5.18–5.25**
- **Cost cue:** Do benefits of recognition justify costs? Consider the information if not recognised; notes visibility if still undefined recognition. **§5.8–5.11**
- **Derecognition twin aims:** Faithful depiction of retained assets/liabilities *and* of the change? Partial transfer / retained exposure / reacquire rights? **§5.26–5.32**
- **Standards reminder:** CF does not override recognition/derecognition requirements in Standards. **SP1.2**, **BC5.11**
