## Teaching sections

### 7.0 Scope of this chapter
This chapter is the site home for official CF **Chapter 7 — Presentation and disclosure**. Cites stay **§7.x** / **BC7.x**. A reporting entity communicates information about its assets, liabilities, equity, income and expenses by **presenting and disclosing** information in its financial statements. **§7.1**

Recognition (site Ch5) decides whether to capture a defined item; Measurement (site Ch6) decides how to quantify what is captured. This chapter sits after those decisions: how to **communicate** recognised and measured amounts so the information is more relevant and a faithful representation, and so understandability and comparability are enhanced. CF does not override Standards. **§7.2**, **SP1.2**

Operational statement structure, categories, and required totals/subtotals live in **IFRS 18** — this chapter stays at Conceptual Framework level with a one-line see-also only. Do **not** draft IFRS 18 body here.

> **Pitfall:** Treating presentation as “formatting after the numbers are done.” Communication choices are part of relevance and faithful representation — not cosmetics. **§7.2**

**Contrast card — Communication for relevance/FR vs formatting cosmetics**

| What it is — communication for relevance / FR | Common trap — “formatting cosmetics” |
|---|---|
| Presenting and disclosing so information about assets, liabilities, equity, income and expenses is more relevant and a faithful representation — and so understandability and comparability are enhanced. Communication is part of usefulness, not a post-numbers polish step. **§7.1–7.2** | Treating presentation and disclosure as typography after the numbers are done. That drops the link to relevance, FR, understandability and comparability. **§7.2** |

### 7.1 Concept structure — communication tools, not a pipeline

Read the chapter visual as a **concept structure**, not a decision pipeline. CF links three communication ideas under one goal — **effective communication**:

1. Focus on presentation and disclosure **objectives and principles** rather than rules alone. **§7.2(a)**
2. **Classify** so similar items are grouped and dissimilar items are separated. **§7.2(b)**
3. **Aggregate** so information is not obscured by unnecessary detail **or** by excessive aggregation. **§7.2(c)**

**Peer card row — Communication tools (relationships, not a pipeline)**

| Objectives & principles | Classification | Aggregation | Presentation vs disclosure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Focus on presentation/disclosure objectives and principles rather than rules alone — guide how to communicate usefully. **§7.2(a)**, **§7.4–7.5** | Sort on shared characteristics — similar together, dissimilar apart (nature / role / measurement). **§7.2(b)**, **§7.7–7.8** | Summarise shared-characteristic items without obscuring — neither clutter nor excessive bundling. **§7.2(c)**, **§7.20–7.22** | Face amounts and note detail are complementary communication locations — different aggregation levels, same usefulness goal. Not a pipeline; both serve relevance / FR. **§7.1**, **§7.21–7.22** |

Cost constrains presentation and disclosure as it constrains other reporting decisions: benefits to users must justify costs of providing and using the information. **§7.3**

**Why shaped this way — first time in 2018.** The 2010 Conceptual Framework did not address presentation and disclosure. Agenda consultation feedback made the topic a priority — especially information about financial performance, including use of OCI. The 2018 CF therefore introduces, for the first time: (a) concepts describing how information should be presented and disclosed (guiding the Board when setting Standards, and possibly guiding entities); (b) Board guidance on classifying income and expenses between profit or loss and OCI; and (c) Board guidance on whether and when OCI items are subsequently reclassified into profit or loss. **BC7.1–BC7.2**

Those concepts sit alongside (and do not replace) Standards-level work that builds on them. Teach relationships among objectives, classification and aggregation — do not invent a numbered CF communication procedure. **BC7.2–BC7.3**, **§7.2**

> **[Deloitte 2022]** High-level concepts on how information should be presented and disclosed; high-level principles on OCI · CF **§7.1–7.6**, **§7.15–7.19**

> **Pitfall:** Teaching a Content “1→N presentation checklist” as if the Board published a preparer diagnostic sequence. CF describes communication relationships and Board-level OCI guidance — it does not hand out a fixed procedure. **§7.2**, **BC7.2**

### 7.2 Objectives and principles

When developing presentation and disclosure requirements in Standards, a balance is needed between (a) giving entities **flexibility** to provide relevant information that faithfully represents the entity’s assets, liabilities, equity, income and expenses, and (b) requiring information that is **comparable** period-to-period and across entities. **§7.4**

Including presentation and disclosure **objectives** in Standards supports effective communication: objectives help entities identify useful information and decide **how** to communicate it most effectively — not only a checklist of line items. **§7.5**

| Principle | Teaching paraphrase | Cite |
|-----------|---------------------|------|
| Objectives over pure rules | Focus on presentation/disclosure objectives and principles | **§7.2(a)**, **§7.4–7.5** |
| Classify for usefulness | Classify so the result is relevant and a faithful representation | **§7.4**, **§7.7** |
| Aggregate without obscuring | Aggregate so information is not obscured and not unnecessarily detailed | **§7.2(c)**, **§7.20–7.22** |
| Entity-specific > boilerplate | Entity-specific information is more useful than standardised “boilerplate” descriptions | **§7.6(a)** |
| Avoid pointless duplication | Duplicating the same information in multiple FS locations usually hurts understandability | **§7.6(b)** |

> **[Deloitte 2022]** Including presentation/disclosure objectives in Standards supports effective communication; Board balances entity flexibility with comparability · CF **§7.4–7.5**

> **Pitfall:** Copy-pasting boilerplate note text when entity-specific facts would better serve FR and understandability. **§7.6(a)**

### 7.3 Classification

**Classification** is the sorting of assets, liabilities, equity, income or expenses on the basis of **shared characteristics** for presentation and disclosure. Characteristics include — but are not limited to — the nature of the item, its role (or function) within the business activities, and how it is measured. **§7.7**

Classifying **dissimilar** items together can obscure relevant information, reduce understandability and comparability, and may not provide a faithful representation of what it purports to represent. **§7.8**

Classification is applied to the **unit of account** selected for an asset or liability. Sometimes it is appropriate to separate an asset or liability into components with different characteristics and classify those components separately — for example current and non-current — when separate classification enhances usefulness. **§7.9**, **§4.48–4.55**

| Focus | Teaching paraphrase | Cite |
|-------|---------------------|------|
| What it is | Sorting on shared characteristics | **§7.7** |
| Assets & liabilities | Applied to unit of account; may split components (e.g. current / non-current) | **§7.9** |
| **Offsetting** | Recognising A and L as separate units of account but presenting a single net amount — generally **not** appropriate (groups dissimilar items); ≠ treating rights/obligations as one unit of account | **§7.10–7.11** |
| Equity | Separate equity claims / components when characteristics differ (e.g. distributable reserves under local law) | **§7.12–7.13**, **BC7.4** |
| Income & expenses | Apply to income/expenses from the unit of account, or to components with different characteristics (e.g. separate interest accrual from other current-value changes) | **§7.14** |

**Offsetting.** Offsetting occurs when an entity recognises and measures both an asset and a liability as separate units of account, but groups them into a single net amount in the statement of financial position. Because that classifies dissimilar items together, offsetting is generally not appropriate. Offsetting differs from treating a set of rights and obligations as a **single unit of account**. **§7.10–7.11**

**Contrast card — Offsetting / netting cleaner vs generally inappropriate**

| What it is — separate units; netting generally inappropriate | Common trap — “netting looks cleaner” |
|---|---|
| When A and L are recognised and measured as **separate** units of account, presenting one net amount groups dissimilar items (a right to receive and an obligation to pay). Offsetting is generally not appropriate under CF — and differs from selecting a single unit of account. **§7.10–7.11**, **§7.8** | Using “cleaner exposure” as a CF classification virtue. Standards may specify when a faithful net presentation is required — CF does not hand preparers a free “net when convenient” licence. **§7.10–7.11**, **SP1.2** |

#### 7.3-ex Worked-example strip — classification / netting that misleads `{#s-7-3-ex}`

| Facts | Concept / gate | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| CU80 trade receivable from Customer A and CU50 trade payable to Customer A — both recognised and measured as **separate** units of account. Management proposes a single “net receivable CU30” on the face “because netting looks cleaner.” | **Separate units:** asset and liability recognised/measured separately (**§7.10**). **Offsetting:** one net amount groups dissimilar items — generally not appropriate (**§7.10**, **§7.8**). **≠ unit of account:** treating rights/obligations as one unit is a different decision from recognising two units then netting for presentation (**§7.11**, **§4.48–4.55**). | **“Cleaner net” ≠ CF classification virtue** when it obscures resources and claims. Standards may require faithful net presentation — CF does not authorise net-when-convenient. **SP1.2** |

> **Pitfall:** “Netting looks cleaner.” Offsetting is generally inappropriate under CF unless a Standard specifies a faithful net presentation. Do not confuse offsetting with selecting a single unit of account. **§7.10–7.11**

### 7.4 Aggregation

**Aggregation** is the adding together of assets, liabilities, equity, income or expenses that have **shared characteristics** and are included in the **same classification**. **§7.20**

Aggregation makes information more useful by summarising a large volume of detail — but it also **conceals** some of that detail. A balance is needed so relevant information is not obscured either by a large amount of insignificant detail **or** by excessive aggregation. Different parts of the financial statements may need different aggregation levels: typically the statement of financial position and the statement(s) of financial performance provide summarised information, with more detail in the notes. **§7.21–7.22**

#### 7.4-ex Worked-example strip — aggregation that hides `{#s-7-4-ex}`

| Facts | Concept / gate | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Face “Other income/(expense)” nets three dissimilar items: (A) recurring FX on operating payables; (B) one-off gain on disposal of a major associate; (C) impairment of a specialised intangible. Net ≈ zero — face readers see “nothing happened.” | **Shared characteristics?** Nature, role/function and measurement story diverge — bundling classifies dissimilar items and aggregates beyond shared characteristics (**§7.7–7.8**, **§7.20**). **Obscure both ways:** quiet net conceals; line-by-line clutter also obscures (**§7.21**). **Face vs notes:** summarised face still needs enough disaggregation that users are not misled (**§7.22**). | **Quiet net from dissimilar drivers fails communication** — separate when characteristics diverge. Same “omit / misstate / obscure” instinct as relevance (**§2.11**), applied to communication design. |

> **Pitfall / callout — obscure works both ways:** Too much detail **and** too much aggregation can each obscure. **§7.21**, **§2.11**

### 7.5 P&L vs OCI (CF Board-guidance — keep short)

Income and expenses are classified and included either in the **statement of profit or loss** or outside it in **other comprehensive income (OCI)**. **§7.15**

The statement of profit or loss is the **primary source** of information about an entity’s financial performance for the reporting period. It contains a total for profit or loss that provides a highly summarised depiction. Many users incorporate that total as a starting point or as the main indicator for the period. Nevertheless, understanding performance still requires analysing **all** recognised income and expenses — including those in OCI — and other information in the financial statements. **§7.16**, **BC7.15–BC7.16**

**Light statement schematic — where amounts land (P&L vs OCI)**

| Profit or loss (schematic) | OCI (schematic) |
|---|---|
| **Primary source** of period performance information — default location for income and expenses; highly summarised total as users’ common starting point. **§7.15–7.16** | Income/expenses **outside** P&L only in exceptional Board/Standard decisions (typically current-value changes) when that improves P&L relevance/FR — not a preparer parking lot. **§7.15**, **§7.17** |

| CF teaching cue | Limit | Cite |
|-----------------|--------|------|
| P&L = primary source of performance information for the period | CF guides the **Board** when writing Standards | **§7.16–7.17**, **BC7.15** |
| Presumption: all income/expenses in P&L; OCI only in **exceptional** Board decisions (typically current-value changes) when that makes P&L more relevant / better FR for the period | **Cannot** use CF alone to park an item in OCI if a Standard requires P&L | **§7.17**, **BC7.24–BC7.25**; **SP1.2** |
| Historical-cost income/expenses → P&L (including when separately identified inside a current-value change, e.g. interest) | Standards specify the split | **§7.18** |
| Presumption of reclassification OCI → P&L in a future period when that improves P&L relevance/FR; Board may decide no reclassification if no clear basis for timing/amount | IAS 1 / IFRS 18 requirements prevail | **§7.19**, **BC7.29–BC7.32** |

**Why shaped this way — high-level Board guidance, not a preparer OCI menu.** Pre-2018 Standards put various items outside profit or loss for particular project reasons, not one consistently applied conceptual reason; the 1989/2010 Frameworks said nothing about OCI. The Board concluded a robust conceptual **definition** of profit or loss (or of OCI), or a complete prescriptive list of P&L categories, was not possible — no single shared characteristic separates all P&L items from all OCI items. It still closed the gap by describing P&L as the primary source and setting principles: default location is P&L; OCI only in exceptional Board decisions; presumption of later reclassification into P&L unless there is no clear basis. Entities cannot take the OCI decision — that is Standards territory. **BC7.9–BC7.14**, **BC7.17–BC7.25**, **BC7.26–BC7.33**

> **[Deloitte 2022]** P&L is primary; all income/expenses presumed in P&L; OCI only in exceptional Board decisions for current-value changes; reclassification presumed unless no clear basis; CF discussions guide the Board only — CF cannot justify recognising an item in OCI · CF **§7.16–7.19**; IAS 1.88

**Contrast card — OCI as Board/Standard exceptional decision vs preparer free choice**

| What it is — Board / Standard exceptional decision | Common trap — preparer free choice via CF |
|---|---|
| Presumption: all income/expenses in P&L. OCI only when the Board, writing a Standard, decides exceptionally (typically current-value changes) that excluding the item from P&L makes P&L more relevant / better FR for the period. Reclassification into P&L later is also Board/Standard territory. **§7.17–7.19**, **BC7.24–BC7.25** | “CF says I can park this current-value change in OCI.” Entities cannot take the OCI decision from CF alone — Standards prevail; CF cannot justify recognising an item in OCI against a Standard. **§7.17**, **BC7.25**, **SP1.2** |

> **Pitfall:** “CF says I can put this in OCI because it is a current-value change.” OCI placement is a **Board/Standard** decision under exceptional circumstances — not a preparer free choice via CF. **§7.17**, **BC7.25**, **SP1.2**

### 7.7 Optional teaching prompts *(not a CF procedure)*

> **Label:** CF describes communication concepts (objectives, classification, aggregation) and Board-level P&L/OCI guidance. It does **not** prescribe a fixed diagnostic sequence for preparers (**§7.2**, **BC7.2**). Bullet order below is Content pedagogy, not Board order.

- **Communication cue:** Will this presentation/disclosure make information more relevant and support FR — and enhance understandability/comparability? **§7.2**
- **Objectives cue:** Am I chasing a rules checklist, or the Standard’s presentation/disclosure objective? Entity-specific over boilerplate? Avoid pointless duplication? **§7.4–7.6**
- **Classification cue:** Similar items grouped / dissimilar separated (nature / function / measurement)? Inappropriate offsetting avoided? Unit of account vs components? **§7.7–7.11**
- **Aggregation cue:** Balance right — neither clutter nor buried totals? Face vs notes detail levels appropriate? **§7.20–7.22**
- **P&L vs OCI cue:** Follow the Standard; do not invent OCI via CF alone. Reclassification follows Standards. **§7.15–7.19**, **SP1.2**
- **Cost cue:** Benefits to users justify costs of providing and using the information? **§7.3**
- **Standards reminder:** CF does not override presentation/disclosure requirements in Standards (incl. IFRS 18 / IAS 1 territory). **SP1.2**
