## Teaching sections

### 1.0 Scope of this chapter
This chapter is the site home for official CF **Chapter 1 — The objective of general purpose financial reporting**. Cites stay **§1.x** / **BC1.x**. The objective forms the foundation of the Conceptual Framework. Qualitative characteristics, the reporting entity concept, elements, recognition and derecognition, measurement, and presentation and disclosure all flow logically from it. **§1.1**

Teach so a technical accountant can state the objective, name the **primary users**, explain the decisions and assessments those users make, and separate accrual vs cash lenses — without inventing a Board “decision tree.” **§1.2–1.5**, **§1.17**, **§1.20**

> **Pitfall:** Treating “objective of GPFR” as a slogan and skipping *which decisions* and *which assessments*. The chain is relationships among concepts — not a Content-invented flowchart. **§1.2–1.4**

### 1.1 Concept structure — relationships, not a decision path

Read the chapter visual as a **concept structure**, not a Board-ordered decision pipeline. CF links four ideas:

- **Objective:** useful financial information about the reporting entity for decisions about providing resources. **§1.2**
- **Whose decisions:** existing and potential investors, lenders and other creditors (primary users). **§1.2**, **§1.5**
- **Which decisions:** buy/sell/hold equity or debt; provide or settle credit; vote on or otherwise influence management’s actions that affect use of economic resources. **§1.2(a)–(c)**
- **Which assessments → which information:** prospects for future net cash inflows **and** management’s stewardship of economic resources → information about resources, claims and changes, plus how efficiently and effectively management discharged its responsibilities. **§1.3–1.4**, **§1.12–1.23**

Why this shape earns teaching time: every later CF chapter is justified by usefulness for these resource-provision decisions. Collapsing the structure into “FS exist because IAS 1 / IFRS 18 require them” loses the *why*. Collapsing it into a numbered Board procedure invents an order CF does not prescribe. **§1.1–1.2**, **BC1.37**

> **Pitfall:** Teaching a Content “decision path” as if the Board published a 1→N flowchart for preparers. CF describes relationships among objective, users, decisions, assessments and information needs — it does not hand out a diagnostic sequence. **§1.2–1.4**

**Peer card row — Objective structure (relationships, not a path)**

| Objective | Primary users | Decisions | Assessments → information |
|---|---|---|---|
| Useful financial information about the reporting entity for decisions about providing resources. **§1.2** | Existing and potential investors, lenders and other creditors who must rely on GPFR. **§1.2**, **§1.5** | Buy/sell/hold; provide or settle credit; vote or otherwise influence management’s use of resources. **§1.2(a)–(c)** | Cash-flow prospects **and** stewardship → resources, claims, changes, and how management used resources. **§1.3–1.4**, **§1.12–1.23** |


### 1.2 Objective — useful information for resource-provision decisions

The objective of general purpose financial reporting is to provide financial information about the **reporting entity** that is useful to existing and potential **investors, lenders and other creditors** in making decisions relating to providing resources to the entity. Those decisions involve: (a) buying, selling or holding equity and debt instruments; (b) providing or settling loans and other forms of credit; or (c) exercising rights to vote on, or otherwise influence, management’s actions that affect the use of the entity’s economic resources. **§1.2**

Those decisions depend on the returns users expect (dividends, principal and interest, market-price increases). Expectations about returns depend on assessment of the **amount, timing and uncertainty** of (prospects for) future net cash inflows to the entity **and** on assessment of management’s **stewardship** of the entity’s economic resources. Users need information to help them make both assessments. **§1.3**

**Why shaped this way — expand “resource allocation,” don’t shrink it.** Feedback on the 2015 Exposure Draft showed some readers treated “resource allocation decisions” as buy/sell/hold only — excluding decisions made while holding an investment (reappoint or replace management; assess remuneration; approve strategy). The Board did not intend that narrow reading. The 2018 CF therefore states the three decision types explicitly, and notes that users need *both* cash-flow-prospect and stewardship assessments for *any* of them. **BC1.36–BC1.37**, **§1.2(a)–(c)**


**Contrast card — Full resource-provision decisions vs buy/sell-only trap**

| What it is — full resource-provision decisions | Common trap — “buy/sell only” |
|---|---|
| Buy/sell/hold equity or debt; provide or settle credit; **and** vote on or otherwise influence management’s actions that affect use of economic resources. All three sit under one objective. **§1.2(a)–(c)**, **BC1.36–BC1.37** | Shrinking “resource allocation” to buy/sell/hold and dropping the vote/influence branch. That narrow reading excludes decisions made while holding an investment. **§1.2(c)**, **BC1.37** |

> **Pitfall:** Dropping the vote/influence branch (**§1.2(c)**) and teaching only buy/sell/lend. Stewardship information supports that branch too — and cash-flow prospects still matter for voting decisions. **§1.2(c)**, **BC1.37**, **§1.22**

> **Pitfall:** Collapsing the objective to “help value the entity.” GPFR help users *estimate* value — they are **not designed to show** the value of the reporting entity. **§1.7**

### 1.3 Primary users

Many existing and potential investors, lenders and other creditors cannot require reporting entities to provide information directly to them and must rely on general purpose financial reports for much of the financial information they need. Consequently, they are the **primary users** to whom GPFR are directed. Throughout the CF, “primary users” and “users” refer to that group. **§1.5**

| Point | Teaching paraphrase | Cite |
|-------|---------------------|------|
| Who | Existing & potential investors, lenders, other creditors | **§1.2**, **§1.5** |
| Why “primary” | Cannot require custom reports → rely on GPFR; most critical and immediate need; capital-market participants | **§1.5**, **BC1.16** |
| Not primary | Management (obtains info internally); regulators / public may find GPFR useful but reports are not primarily directed to them | **§1.9–1.10** |
| Common needs | Board seeks the information set that meets the needs of the maximum number of primary users | **§1.8** |
| Subset OK | Focusing on common needs does **not** stop an entity adding information most useful to a particular subset | **§1.8**, **BC1.19** |

**Why shaped this way?** Without a defined primary-user group, the Framework risks becoming unduly abstract or vague. The Board considered narrowing to existing shareholders (or residual equity holders) and widening to employees, customers, regulators and others — and kept the 2010 description. Information that meets these primary users’ needs is likely to meet needs under both shareholder-centric and broader stakeholder governance models. Expanding the objective to include maintaining financial stability was rejected: conflicts could deprive capital-market participants of information they need. **BC1.14**, **BC1.16**, **BC1.18–BC1.19**, **BC1.26**

#### 1.3-ex Worked-example strip — primary user vs CFO vs regulator `{#s-1-3-ex}`

| Facts | Concept / gate | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Entity X prepares IFRS financial statements. Three audiences look at them: (1) a potential bond purchaser deciding whether to lend; (2) the CFO building an internal dashboard; (3) a sector regulator monitoring capital adequacy. | **(1) Bond purchaser:** existing/potential lender who cannot require custom reports → primary user; GPFR are directed here (**§1.5**, **§1.2(b)**). **(2) CFO:** management obtains information internally → not a primary user (**§1.9**, **BC1.22**). **(3) Regulator:** may find GPFR useful; reports are **not primarily directed** to regulators (**§1.10**, **BC1.23–BC1.26**). | **“Useful to someone” ≠ “primary user.”** Designing GPFR for management’s dashboard or a regulator’s capital model mis-aims the Framework audience. Common needs still allow subset extras. **§1.8** |

> **Pitfall:** Designing GPFR for management’s internal needs, or treating regulators as the primary audience. “User” in CF is not “anyone who opens the PDF.” **§1.5**, **§1.9–1.10**

### 1.4 Management accountability (stewardship) — input, not a rival objective

To make the assessments in **§1.3**, users need information about (a) economic resources, claims and changes, and (b) how efficiently and effectively the entity’s management and governing board have discharged their responsibilities to use those resources. Information about efficient and effective use helps users assess management’s stewardship; it also helps predict future use and therefore prospects for future net cash inflows. Examples of management’s responsibilities include protecting resources from unfavourable economic factors and ensuring compliance with laws, regulations and contractual provisions. **§1.4**, **§1.22–1.23**

**Locked teaching label:** primary label = **management accountability**; CF wording = *stewardship* (**§1.3**, **§1.13**, **§1.22**). Teach stewardship as the CF synonym, not a rival tag. The Board’s use matches ordinary meaning: careful and responsible management of something entrusted to one’s care. **BC1.41**

**Why the 2018 shape — prominence without a second objective.** After 2010, some stakeholders read removal of the word “stewardship” as neglecting accountability information. The Board had not intended that; the 2010 wording was simply not clear enough. The 2018 CF reintroduces the term and gives more prominence to information needed to assess stewardship — identifying it as possibly partly separate from information needed for cash-flow prospects — while keeping **one** overall objective: information useful for resource-provision decisions. The Board rejected making stewardship part of the objective, or an additional equally prominent objective, because (a) assessing stewardship is an **input** to resource-allocation decisions, not an end in itself (e.g. unsatisfactory stewardship may lead to replacing management to increase future returns), and (b) a second objective could confuse. **BC1.32–BC1.35**


**Contrast card — Stewardship as input vs rival objective**

| What it is — stewardship as input | Common trap — “second / rival objective” |
|---|---|
| Assessing management’s stewardship (management accountability) is an **input** to resource-provision decisions under **one** objective. Information may be partly separate from cash-flow-prospect info — still under the same umbrella. **§1.3–1.4**, **BC1.34–BC1.35** | Fighting a cash-flow objective vs stewardship objective war, or treating stewardship as an equally prominent second objective. The Board rejected that shape because a second objective could confuse. **BC1.32–BC1.35** |

> **[Deloitte 2022]** 2018 reintroduces ‘stewardship’; users need information to assess management’s stewardship so they can hold management to account for resources entrusted to their care — that assessment helps resource-provision decisions · CF **§1.3–1.4**, **§1.22**; **BC1.41**

> **Pitfall:** Fighting a “cash-flow objective vs stewardship objective” war. CF has one objective; stewardship assessment is an input — sometimes partly separate information, always under the resource-provision umbrella. **BC1.34–BC1.35**, **§1.3**

### 1.5 Information about resources, claims and changes

GPFR provide information about **financial position** (economic resources and claims against the entity) and about the effects of transactions and other events that change those resources and claims. Both types of information provide useful input for resource-provision decisions. **§1.12**

| Layer | What users need | Cite |
|-------|-----------------|------|
| Economic resources & claims | Nature/amounts → strengths/weaknesses; liquidity/solvency; financing needs; claim priorities; also helps stewardship assessment | **§1.12–1.14** |
| Different resource types | Some cash flows map to single resources (e.g. receivables); others come from using several resources together — users still need nature/amounts available for operations | **§1.14** |
| Changes from performance vs other | Identify performance changes vs other changes (e.g. issuing debt/equity) — needed for both cash-flow prospects and stewardship | **§1.15–1.16**, **§1.21** |
| Accrual performance | Effects in periods they occur — better basis for assessing past/future performance than cash alone; capacity generated through operations (not only by raising capital) | **§1.17–1.19** |
| Past cash flows | How entity obtains/spends cash; borrowing/repayment; distributions; liquidity/solvency; interpret other performance info | **§1.20** |
| Use of resources | How efficiently & effectively management discharged responsibilities; helps predict future use and cash-flow prospects | **§1.22–1.23** |

Without separating **performance changes** from **financing/other changes**, users misread returns and stewardship. Accrual performance shows capacity generated through operations; market-price or rate changes can also move resources and claims and still be performance-relevant. Non-performance changes (issuing debt/equity; distributions) complete the picture of *why* resources and claims moved. **§1.15–1.16**, **§1.18–1.19**, **§1.21**

> **Pitfall:** Teaching only the statement of financial position and ignoring changes, cash flows, or use-of-resources. The information stack is multi-layer. **§1.12–1.23**

### 1.6 Accrual vs cash

**Accrual accounting** depicts the effects of transactions and other events and circumstances on a reporting entity’s economic resources and claims in the periods in which those effects occur, even if the resulting cash receipts and payments occur in a different period. Information about resources, claims and changes during a period provides a **better basis** for assessing past and future performance than information solely about cash receipts and payments during that period. **§1.17**

**Cash flows** remain a necessary complementary lens: how the entity obtains and spends cash; borrowing and repayment; distributions; liquidity and solvency; interpreting other performance information — and assessing both future net cash inflows and management’s stewardship. Accrual ≠ “ignore cash.” **§1.20**, **§1.15**, **§1.22**

#### 1.6-ex Worked-example strip — accrual vs cash-only `{#s-1-6-ex}`

| Facts | Concept / gate | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Entity Y sells goods on credit in Year 1 (customers pay in Year 2) and buys inventory for cash in Year 1 to sell in Year 2. A cash-only Year 1 report shows a large inventory outflow and almost no customer inflow — looking like a weak year. Accrual recognises the receivable and sale effect in Year 1. | **Cash-flow prospects:** users need Year 1’s collectible claims and locked-in inventory — cash alone hides that capacity (**§1.3**, **§1.17–1.18**). **Stewardship:** cash-only can make management look worse/better solely from payment timing (**§1.22**, **§1.16**). **Complement:** cash-flow statement still needed for liquidity and to interpret accrual performance (**§1.20**). | **Cash-only FS fail the objective’s information needs.** Accrual provides a better basis for assessing performance — cash still complements. **§1.17**, **§1.20** |

> **Pitfall:** “Cash is king → accrual is optional colour.” CF says accrual information provides a better basis than cash alone for assessing performance — cash still complements for liquidity, financing and stewardship. **§1.17**, **§1.20**

### 1.7 Limitations of GPFR

> **GPFS ≠ entity valuation:** General purpose financial reports are **not designed to show the value of a reporting entity**; they provide information to help users estimate that value. **§1.7**

| Limitation | Teaching paraphrase | Cite |
|------------|---------------------|------|
| Not all information | GPFR cannot provide all information users need — other sources (economy, politics, industry and company outlooks) matter | **§1.6** |
| Not a valuation | Not designed to show entity value; help users estimate it | **§1.7** |
| Estimates / models | Based largely on estimates, judgements and models — CF sets the concepts those aim toward | **§1.11** |
| Ideal not instant | Ideal financial reporting is a goal unlikely to be achieved in full in the short term | **§1.11** |

Limitations stop overselling GPFS as a complete decision pack or as a ready-made fair value of the entity. Technology that might let different users assemble custom reports does not, for now, make GPFR obsolete — custom packs would be expensive and often demand more accounting expertise than users have. **§1.6–1.7**, **§1.11**, **BC1.5–BC1.6**

> **Pitfall:** “GPFS omitted X → Framework failed.” Users must bring other sources; GPFS are necessary but not sufficient. **§1.6**

### 1.9 Optional teaching prompts *(not a CF procedure)*

> **Label:** CF describes the objective, primary users, decisions, assessments and information needs. It does **not** prescribe a fixed diagnostic sequence or “decision path” for preparers. Bullet order below is Content pedagogy, not Board order.

- **Objective cue:** Useful financial information about the reporting entity for resource-provision decisions? **§1.2**
- **Whose decisions:** Existing/potential investors, lenders, other creditors who must rely on GPFR? **§1.5**
- **Which decisions:** Buy/sell/hold; provide/settle credit; vote/influence resource use — not buy/sell only. **§1.2(a)–(c)**, **BC1.37**
- **Which assessments:** Future net cash inflows (amount/timing/uncertainty) **and** management accountability (CF *stewardship*) — one objective, two assessment inputs. **§1.3–1.4**, **BC1.34–BC1.35**
- **Information stack:** Resources & claims → performance vs other changes → accrual + cash → use of resources. **§1.12–1.23**
- **Audience cue:** Primary user ≠ management ≠ regulator/public. Common needs; subset extras OK. **§1.8–1.10**
- **Limitations cue:** Not a valuation; not all info; estimates/judgements apply. **§1.6–1.7**, **§1.11**
