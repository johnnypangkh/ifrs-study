## Teaching sections

### 9.0 Scope of this chapter
This chapter is the site home for official CF **Status and purpose** (**SP1.1–SP1.5**) and the related Basis for Conclusions on purpose and status (**BC0.18–BC0.28**). Cites stay **SP1.x** / **BC0.x**, plus **IAS 8** where the Framework and BC point to the preparer hierarchy.

Status is the **gate** for the whole site: before Objective, QC, Elements or Recognition, a technical accountant must know that the Conceptual Framework is **not a Standard**, does **not override** any Standard or requirement in a Standard, and enters preparer work mainly when **IAS 8** points to it for gap-filling or policy choice. **SP1.1–SP1.2**, **SP1.4**

> **Locked (2026-09-27):** Full **IAS 8 gap-fill path** lives here. Map Status band owns the CF ≠ Standard cue; do **not** invent a second orientation chapter or duplicate the IAS 8 path onto Map.

> **Pitfall:** Opening Elements or Recognition first and “CF-shopping” around a clear Standard. Status gate first. **SP1.2**

### 9.1 Concept structure — purpose triad + status (not a shadow Standard)

Read the chapter visual as a **concept structure**: who the CF assists, and what its status is relative to Standards. SP streamlines three main uses — the **purpose triad**:

1. **Board / standard-setter** — assist the HKICPA to develop Standards (and Accounting Guidelines) based on **consistent concepts**. Primary design tool for the Board. **SP1.1(a)**
2. **Preparers** — assist preparers to develop **consistent accounting policies** when **no** Standard or Accounting Guideline applies to a particular transaction or other event, **or** when a Standard / Guideline **allows a choice** of accounting policy. Gate = gap or choice — not disagreement with a clear Standard. **SP1.1(b)**
3. **All parties (auditors, users, others)** — assist all parties to **understand and interpret** the Standards and Accounting Guidelines. Shared vocabulary for reading requirements — interpretation ≠ override. **SP1.1(c)**

**Peer card row — Purpose triad (who CF assists)**

| Assist Board | Assist preparers | Assist users / others |
|---|---|---|
| Develop Standards (and Accounting Guidelines) based on **consistent concepts**. Primary design tool for the Board. **SP1.1(a)** | Develop consistent policies when **no** Standard/Guideline applies, **or** a Standard/Guideline allows a policy choice. Gate = gap or choice — not disagreement with a clear Standard. **SP1.1(b)** | Help all parties **understand and interpret** Standards and Guidelines — shared vocabulary. Interpretation ≠ override. **SP1.1(c)** |

> **Status note:** CF is **not a Standard** (nor an Accounting Guideline). Nothing in the CF overrides any Standard, any requirement in a Standard, or an Accounting Guideline. **SP1.2**

**Status next to purpose:** The Conceptual Framework is **not a Standard** nor an Accounting Guideline. Nothing in the CF overrides any Standard, any requirement in a Standard, or an Accounting Guideline. **SP1.2**

**Why shaped this way — triad kept, not Board-only.** The 2010 Framework listed many possible uses. In 2018 the Board streamlined to three main uses. It considered stating that the *primary* purpose would be only to assist the Board, and **rejected** that approach: acknowledging assistance to other parties does not prevent the Board from developing focused, consistent concepts. A few aspects of the CF can only be applied by the Board (the CF then says so — e.g. some Ch7 Board guidance). **BC0.18–BC0.20**

**Why status is reconfirmed.** The 1989 Framework and 2010 CF already said CF is not a Standard and does not override specific Standards. The 2018 CF **reconfirmed** that status. Explicit non-override wording prevents entities from attempting to override inappropriately Standards they might view as contradicting the CF. The status has worked well in practice. **BC0.21–BC0.22**

> **[Deloitte 2022]** Purpose triad (Board / preparers / all parties); CF is not a Standard; concepts do **not** override IFRS requirements and cannot justify a treatment that contravenes a Standard · **SP1.1–SP1.2**

> **Pitfall:** Expanding “assist preparers” into “preparers may depart from Standards using CF concepts.” That collapses **SP1.1(b)** into a violation of **SP1.2**.

### 9.2 Hierarchy — CF informs Standards; Standards prevail

**Contrast card — CF informs / assists vs CF overrides Standard**

| What it is — CF informs / assists | Common trap — CF overrides a Standard |
|---|---|
| CF describes the objective of, and concepts for, GPFR. It assists the Board, preparers (gap/choice), and all parties interpreting Standards — foundation rhetoric under **SP1.5** explains why Standards based on CF matter. **SP1.1**, **SP1.5** | Headline **SP1.2**: nothing in the CF overrides any Standard or requirement in a Standard. “CF principles say otherwise” is not a departure right for preparers. **SP1.2**, **BC0.21–BC0.22** |

| Layer | Teaching paraphrase | Cite |
|-------|---------------------|------|
| **Standards / Interpretations** | If a Standard (or Interpretation) applies, apply it. CF cannot trump a clear requirement | **SP1.2** |
| **Conceptual Framework** | Describes the objective of, and concepts for, GPFR — informs Standards; does not replace them | **SP1.1**, **SP1.2** |
| **Foundation rhetoric** | CF underpins Standards that contribute to transparency, accountability and economic efficiency — foundation ≠ override | **SP1.5**, **SP1.2** |

Teach the hierarchy as **authority order**, not as “CF is less important.” The CF is the conceptual foundation for Standards (**SP1.5**); reading that as elevating CF *above* Standards inverts the status sentence. **SP1.2**, **SP1.5**

> **Pitfall:** “The 2018 CF changed recognition / definitions, so I can ignore the probability threshold in IAS 37.” CF revision does **not** auto-amend Standards (**SP1.4**); CF does not override (**SP1.2**). See also unamended-wording cue in §9.4 / worked cue §9.5.

### 9.3 IAS 8 gap-fill path — ordered hierarchy (prescribed; lives on Status)

> **Locked:** Status owns the full IAS 8 gap-fill path + visual. Map Status band carries CF ≠ Standard; no Overview teaching body.

**When CF enters preparer work.** Only under the preparer limb of the purpose triad: no Standard / Guideline applies, **or** a Standard / Guideline allows a policy choice. Even then, CF is not the first stop. **SP1.1(b)**

**Prescribed order (IAS 8 / SP — not Content invention).** IAS 8 requires management to use judgement to develop and apply an accounting policy that results in relevant and reliable information when no Standard specifically applies. In making that judgement, **IAS 8.11** requires management to consider, **in this order**:

1. **Requirements and guidance in Standards and Interpretations dealing with similar and related issues** — first.  
2. **Definitions, recognition criteria and measurement concepts** for assets, liabilities, income and expenses in the **Conceptual Framework** — second.  

**Hierarchy stack — IAS 8.11 ordered path (prescribed; not Content invention)**

1. **Similar / related Standards & Interpretations** — requirements and guidance dealing with similar and related issues (**IAS 8.11** first).
2. **CF definitions / recognition / measurement concepts** — second (**IAS 8.11**, **SP1.1(b)**).

**SP1.1(b)** states the preparer purpose; **IAS 8.11** (as cited in CF BC footnote 6 / DTT) operationalises the order. Teaching this ordered path is teaching the Standard/SP hierarchy — **not** inventing an extra Content procedure. Do **not** add invented steps beyond that hierarchy. **SP1.1(b)**, **IAS 8.11**, **BC0.28** n.6

**May also consider (IAS 8.12 — not a skip past 8.11).** In making the **IAS 8.11** judgement, management **may also consider** the most recent pronouncements of other standard-setting bodies that use a similar conceptual framework, other accounting literature and accepted industry practices — **to the extent** these do not conflict with the sources in **IAS 8.11**. That “may also consider” layer sits **after** similar Standards and CF concepts in the prescribed hierarchy; it is not a free first stop. If a Design visual shows this layer, it must match this order. **IAS 8.12**, **IAS 8.11**, **SP1.1(b)**

**Peer / box — IAS 8.12 may also consider (keep separate from 8.11)**

| IAS 8.12 — may also consider (separate) |
|---|
| Most recent pronouncements of other bodies with a similar framework, other accounting literature, accepted industry practices — **only to the extent** they do not conflict with **IAS 8.11** sources. Do **not** collapse 8.12 into 8.11 or use it as a free first stop. **IAS 8.12** |


**Policy choice vs gap.** If a Standard *permits* a choice of accounting policy, entities select a policy subject to the overall fair-presentation requirement in **IAS 1**; the link between fair presentation and CF concepts is described in IAS 1.15 (BC footnote). Choice ≠ licence to ignore other requirements in that Standard. **BC0.28** n.6, **SP1.2**

> **[Deloitte 2022]** Absent a specifically applicable Standard/Interpretation, IAS 8 requires judgement for a relevant and reliable policy; IAS 8.11 — first similar/related Standards, **second** CF definitions / recognition / measurement concepts · **SP1.1(b)**, **IAS 8.11**

> **Pitfall:** Jumping to CF concepts before checking whether a Standard (or a similar-issue Standard) already answers the question. Order in the flowchart is mandatory teaching discipline. **SP1.1(b)**, **IAS 8.11**

### 9.4 Departures, revisions, reference updates

| Point | Teaching paraphrase | Cite |
|-------|---------------------|------|
| **Board departures** | To meet the objective of GPFR, the HKICPA / Board may sometimes specify requirements that **depart** from aspects of the CF — explained in that Standard’s **Basis for Conclusions** | **SP1.3**, **BC0.23** |
| **Who may depart** | Only the **Board** writing a Standard. Preparers do **not** get a parallel departure right | **SP1.3**, **SP1.2** |
| **Revisions ≠ auto-amend** | Revising the CF does **not** automatically lead to changes to Standards. Amending a Standard requires due process (agenda project + amendment) | **SP1.4**, **BC0.25**, **BC0.28** |
| **Foundation role** | CF underpins Standards that contribute to **transparency** (comparability / quality), **accountability** (reduce information gap; hold management to account; useful to regulators), and **economic efficiency** (identify opportunities/risks; lower cost of capital / reporting costs via a trusted language) | **SP1.5** |
| **2018 reference updates** | Board issued *Amendments to References to the Conceptual Framework in IFRS Standards* (2018) so Standards refer to the 2018 CF where appropriate — **without** rewriting every element definition in every Standard | **BC0.28**, DTT §12 / intro |

**Why Board departures are acknowledged.** Some stakeholders argued the Board should *never* develop Standards that depart from the CF. The Board disagreed: in some circumstances it might need to depart. Acknowledging that, and limiting departures to what is needed to meet the GPFR objective, is deliberate. Need might arise because conceptual thinking or the economic environment changes. **BC0.23**

**Why revisions don’t auto-change Standards.** Amending a Standard is not an automatic consequence of revising the CF. Changes to Standards address deficiencies in financial reporting; inconsistencies highlighted by new concepts are considered against other work-plan priorities. For most entities, CF changes have **no immediate effect** on the financial statements — preparers are directly affected mainly when they must use the CF under IAS 8 (gap or choice). **BC0.25**, **BC0.28**, **SP1.4**

**Unamended wording still prevails.** References in Standards were updated to the 2018 CF; the Board did **not** make consequential amendments reflecting every definition change (e.g. asset definition in **IAS 38**, liability definition in **IAS 37** may still use older wording until separately amended). Consequence for application: the **Standard’s own wording** still governs until that Standard is amended. CF vocabulary helps interpretation (**SP1.1(c)**) but does not silently rewrite the Standard. **SP1.2**, **SP1.4**, DTT intro / §12

> **Pitfall:** Reading SP1.5’s “foundation” rhetoric as elevating CF above Standards. Foundation ≠ override. **SP1.2**, **SP1.5**

### 9.7 Optional teaching prompts *(IAS 8 order is prescribed; do not invent extra steps)*

> **Label:** The **IAS 8.11** similar-Standards → CF concepts order **is** prescribed by IAS 8 / operationalised from **SP1.1(b)** — teach that ordered path with cites. Do **not** invent extra Content steps, mini-procedures, or Board departure rights beyond **SP1.1–SP1.5** / **IAS 8**. Bullet cues below are pedagogy around that hierarchy, not a new Board checklist.

- **Status cue:** Is anyone arguing CF overrides a Standard? If yes → stop; apply the Standard. **SP1.2**, **BC0.21–BC0.22**
- **Purpose cue:** Which triad limb am I on — Board design, preparer gap/choice, or all-parties interpretation? **SP1.1(a)–(c)**
- **Gate cue:** No Standard applies, **or** a Standard allows a choice — otherwise CF is not the preparer entry point. **SP1.1(b)**
- **IAS 8 order cue:** Similar/related Standards first; CF definitions / recognition / measurement concepts second. No invented third Content layer. **IAS 8.11**, **SP1.1(b)**
- **Departure cue:** Seeing Standard ≠ CF? Look for Board explanation in that Standard’s BC — do not self-author a departure. **SP1.3**, **BC0.23**
- **Revision cue:** Did the CF just get revised? Revisions do not auto-change Standards. **SP1.4**, **BC0.25**, **BC0.28**
- **Unamended cue:** Does the Standard still carry older definition/recognition wording? That wording prevails until amended. **SP1.2**, **SP1.4**
- **Foundation cue:** Transparency / accountability / efficiency explain *why Standards based on CF matter* — they do not elevate CF above Standards. **SP1.5**, **SP1.2**
