## Teaching sections

### 2.0 Scope of this chapter
This chapter is the site home for official CF **Chapter 2 — Qualitative characteristics of useful financial information**. Cites stay **§2.x** / **BC2.x**. QCs identify the types of information likely to be most useful to primary users for resource-provision decisions about the reporting entity. **§2.1**, **§1.5**

They apply to financial information in financial statements **and** to financial information provided in other ways. Cost applies similarly. Considerations in applying QCs and the cost constraint may differ by information type — e.g. forward-looking explanations vs information about existing resources, claims and changes. **§2.2–2.3**

Teach so a technical accountant can state the two fundamentals, place materiality / substance / prudence as 組成 inside the right fundamental (containment, not dashed hang), distinguish enhancers from fundamentals, and weigh cost — without inventing a Board “QC decision tree.” **§2.4–2.5**, **§2.23**, **§2.39**

> **Pitfall:** Treating QCs as “FS footnotes only.” Forward-looking explanations still sit under the same QC lens — applied with different practical considerations. **§2.2–2.3**

### 2.1 Concept structure — relationships, not a shopping list

Read the visual above as a **concept structure**, not a Board-ordered pipeline. CF links three layers:

- **Fundamentals (both required):** relevance **and** faithful representation. Useful information must have both. **§2.4–2.5**, **§2.20**
- **Enhancers:** comparability, verifiability, timeliness, understandability — improve information that is already relevant and faithfully represented; also help choose between two equally relevant / equally faithful depictions. **§2.4**, **§2.23**
- **Cost constraint (pervasive):** costs of reporting must be justified by benefits — not a third fundamental QC. **§2.39**

**Hierarchy mini-stack — Fundamentals → Enhancers → Cost**

| Layer | Teaching cue | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| Fundamental QCs (both required) | Relevance **and** faithful representation — useful information must have both. | **§2.4–2.5**, **§2.20** |
| Enhancing QCs | Comparability · Verifiability · Timeliness · Understandability — polish information that is already relevant and faithfully represented; cannot create usefulness from scratch. | **§2.23**, **§2.37** |
| Cost constraint | Pervasive constraint — costs of reporting must be justified by benefits; not a third fundamental QC. | **§2.39** |


Why this shape earns teaching time: without the fundamental/enhancing distinction, students treat every QC as equal weight and polish irrelevant or unfaithful information until it “looks professional.” The Board drew the distinction because information lacking relevance or FR **cannot** be made useful by being more comparable, verifiable, timely or understandable — while relevant + FR information can still be useful even without every enhancer. Collapsing the structure into a numbered preparer checklist invents an order CF does not prescribe for enhancers (enhancer application is iterative, no prescribed order). **BC2.9–BC2.11**, **§2.37–2.38**

> **Pitfall:** Ranking an enhancer (e.g. comparability) above a missing fundamental. Enhancers polish; they do not create usefulness from scratch. **§2.37**, **BC2.11**


**Peer card row — Fundamental QCs (equal-rank peers)**

| Relevance | Faithful representation |
|---|---|
| Capable of making a difference in users’ decisions — predictive and/or confirmatory value; materiality is the entity-specific aspect. **§2.6–2.11** | Faithfully represents the substance of the phenomena — maximise complete, neutral and free from error. **§2.12–2.13** |


### 2.2 Fundamental QC — Relevance

Relevant financial information is **capable of making a difference** in the decisions made by users. Capability holds even if some users choose not to take advantage of it or already know it from other sources. Usefulness starts with decision-capability — not with whether users actually open the note. **§2.6**, **BC2.12–BC2.14**

**Predictive and confirmatory value.** Information is capable of making a difference if it has predictive value, confirmatory value, or both. Predictive value = usable as an **input** to users’ own processes for predicting future outcomes — the information need **not** itself be a prediction or forecast. Confirmatory value = feedback that confirms or changes previous evaluations. The two are interrelated: information with predictive value often also has confirmatory value. Official cue: current-year revenue can be an input to predict future revenues **and** can be compared with past predictions for the current year — feedback that improves users’ earlier prediction processes. **§2.7–2.10**, **BC2.15–BC2.17**

**Materiality (entity-specific aspect of relevance).** Information is material if omitting, misstating or **obscuring** it could reasonably be expected to influence decisions that the **primary users** of GPFR make on the basis of those reports about that specific entity. Materiality turns on **nature and/or magnitude** of the items in the context of an individual entity’s financial report. The Board cannot specify a uniform quantitative threshold or predetermine what is material in a particular situation. The 2018 clarification emphasises primary users (not any other group); the later “obscuring” / “reasonably be expected” wording aligns with IAS 1 amendments. **§2.11**, **§1.5**, **BC2.18–BC2.20A**

**Containment cue (body):** Relevance outer — predictive/confirmatory + Materiality as 組成 **inside** Relevance (`.contain` / `.contain-grid`). Not a dashed hang under Relevance. **§2.6–2.11**

| Predictive / confirmatory (inside Relevance) | Materiality (inside Relevance) |
|---|---|
| Capable of making a difference. **§2.6–2.10** | Entity-specific nature and/or magnitude; omit / misstate / obscure. **§2.11** |

> **See also (optional, one-line):** Materiality application guidance → **IAS 1** / IFRS Practice Statement *Making Materiality Judgements* (body keeps materiality as 組成 **inside** relevance — containment, not hang).

| Facts | Concept / gate | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Entity Z discloses a CU5,000 related-party loan to a director (tiny vs group assets). Separately, Z has a large inventory balance already known to analysts from industry data feeds. | **(1) Related-party loan:** nature can still make omit/obscure reasonably expected to influence primary users → material as entity-specific aspect of relevance (**§2.11**, **BC2.20**). **(2) Inventory known elsewhere:** still capable of making a difference → relevant even if some users already know it (**§2.6**). | **Relevance = decision-capability; materiality = entity-specific filter.** “Users already know” ≠ irrelevant; “below 5% of profit” ≠ automatically immaterial. **§2.6**, **§2.11** |

> **Pitfall:** Demanding that “relevant” = management’s own forecast line. Predictive value is about **user inputs**, not necessarily presenting a forecast. **§2.8**

### 2.3 Fundamental QC — Faithful representation

Financial reports represent economic phenomena in words and numbers. To be useful, information must not only represent relevant phenomena — it must also faithfully represent the **substance** of the phenomena it purports to represent. In many circumstances substance and legal form are the same; when they differ, form-only depiction fails FR. Cross-ref unit-of-account / contractual rights discussion **§4.59–4.62**. **§2.12**

A perfectly faithful depiction would be **complete**, **neutral** and **free from error**. Perfection is seldom achievable — the objective is to **maximise** those qualities to the extent possible. **§2.13**

| Quality | Teaching paraphrase | Cite |
|---------|---------------------|------|
| Complete | All information necessary to understand the phenomenon, including necessary descriptions and explanations (e.g. nature of a group of assets; numerical depiction; what the number represents — HC vs FV; sometimes quality/process explanations) | **§2.14** |
| Neutral | Without bias in selection or presentation — not slanted, weighted, emphasised or de-emphasised to push favourable/unfavourable reception. Neutral ≠ purposeless: relevant info is, by definition, capable of making a difference | **§2.15** |
| Free from error | No errors/omissions in the **description**, and process selected/applied without process errors. Free from error ≠ perfectly accurate — especially for estimates of unobservable prices/values: describe as estimate, explain nature/limits, apply process without error | **§2.18** |

**Prudence supports neutrality.** Prudence = exercise of **caution** when making judgements under uncertainty. It means assets and income are not overstated and liabilities and expenses are not understated — **and**, equally, assets/income are not understated and liabilities/expenses are not overstated. Misstatements today can reverse into over-/understatement of income/expenses in future periods. **§2.16**

**Asymmetry is not a QC doctrine.** Prudence does **not** imply a systematic need for more persuasive evidence to recognise assets/income than liabilities/expenses. Such asymmetry is **not** itself a QC of useful financial information. **Nevertheless**, particular Standards **may** contain asymmetric requirements as a **consequence** of selecting the most relevant information that faithfully represents the phenomenon — not as CF “biased prudence.” **§2.17**, **BC2.42–BC2.45**

> **[Deloitte 2022]** IAS 37’s higher recognition threshold for contingent assets than contingent liabilities ≠ CF asymmetric-prudence doctrine — Board views it as relevance + FR choices for that phenomenon. · CF **§2.17**; **BC2.45**

**Measurement uncertainty.** When amounts cannot be observed directly and must be estimated, measurement uncertainty arises. Reasonable estimates are **essential** — they do not automatically undermine usefulness if clearly and accurately described and explained. Even a **high** level of measurement uncertainty does not necessarily prevent an estimate from providing useful information. **§2.19**


**Contrast card — Prudence as caution vs cookie-jar understatement**

| What it is — caution under neutrality | Common trap — cookie-jar understatement |
|---|---|
| Prudence = exercise of **caution** when making judgements under uncertainty. Supports neutrality: do not overstate assets/income or understate liabilities/expenses — **and** equally the reverse. **§2.16**, **BC2.39–BC2.40** | Systematically understating assets / overstating liabilities “just in case.” That introduces bias, can overstate future income when the reserve reverses, and is **not** CF prudence. **§2.16**, **BC2.35** |

| Facts | Concept / gate | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Finance team proposes to “be prudent”: systematically understate inventory and overstate a warranty provision “just in case,” building a cookie-jar reserve for future years. | **Caution under uncertainty** supports neutrality (**§2.16**, **BC2.39–BC2.40**). **Systematic understatement of assets / overstatement of liabilities** is not CF prudence — it introduces bias and can overstate future income when the reserve reverses (**§2.16**, **BC2.35**). Neutrality bans bias, not usefulness (**§2.15**). | **Prudence = two-way caution under uncertainty.** Cookie-jar asymmetry fails neutrality. Standards may set asymmetric recognition thresholds for relevance + FR — that is not a licence for preparer bias. **§2.16–2.17** |

> **Pitfall:** “Legal form checklist = faithful representation.” Form alone is not enough when substance differs. **§2.12**
>
> **Pitfall:** Equating “neutral” with “bland / decision-irrelevant.” Neutrality bans **bias**, not **usefulness**. **§2.15**
>
> **Pitfall:** Reading prudence as a licence to systematically understate assets / overstate liabilities. That is **not** CF prudence. **§2.16**, **BC2.35**
>
> **Pitfall:** “High uncertainty → automatically useless / never recognise.” CF rejects automatic kill — see trade-offs **§2.22**. **§2.19**

### 2.4 Why the 2010 → 2018 QC shape (FR family)

The Board did **not** fundamentally rewrite the QC chapter in 2018 — it made limited clarifications after stakeholders asked for them. The teaching-critical moves sit inside the **faithful-representation family**:

| Topic | 2010 teaching memory | 2018 CF | Cite cue |
|-------|----------------------|---------|----------|
| Label | Some still said “reliability” | Keep **faithful representation** (avoids confusion with “tolerable measurement uncertainty”) | **BC2.28–BC2.30** |
| Substance over form | Explicit reference removed (Board thought it redundant inside FR) | **Reinstated** in **§2.12** — form-only when substance differs fails FR | **BC2.32–BC2.33** |
| Prudence | Removed (feared conflict with neutrality) | **Reintroduced** as caution under uncertainty supporting neutrality — **not** asymmetric/cookie-jar prudence | **BC2.34–BC2.40**, **§2.16–2.17** |
| Measurement uncertainty | Easy to overlook under FR | Explicit discussion under FR; trade-off with relevance clarified | **§2.19**, **§2.22**, **BC2.46–BC2.49** |
| Materiality | Aspect of relevance | Kept; clarified **primary users**; later “obscuring” / “reasonably be expected” wording | **BC2.20–BC2.20A**, **§2.11** |

**Insight:** after reinstating substance and (cautious) prudence, the 2018 description of FR is substantially aligned with what the 1989 Framework called “reliability” — without bringing back the confusing label. Verifiability stays an **enhancing** QC (not a piece of FR). **BC2.21**, **BC2.31**, **BC2.60–BC2.62**

> **[Deloitte 2022]** Chapter 2 largely unchanged from 2010, but Board reintroduces explicit ‘substance over form’ and ‘prudence’; prudence supports neutrality and is not biased toward fewer assets / more liabilities. · CF **§2.12**, **§2.16**; **BC2.33**, **BC2.40**

### 2.5 Both fundamentals required — and when they trade off

Information must **both** be relevant and provide a faithful representation of what it purports to represent. Neither a faithful depiction of an **irrelevant** phenomenon nor an unfaithful depiction of a relevant one helps users make good decisions. **§2.20**

CF illustrates applying the fundamentals (subject to enhancers and cost, which the illustration sets aside): identify an economic phenomenon that could be useful to users → identify the type of information about it that would be **most relevant** → ask whether that information is available and can **faithfully represent** the phenomenon; if not, repeat with the **next most relevant** type. That is CF’s own illustration — not a Content-invented diagnostic sequence for every preparer judgement. **§2.21**, **BC2.52–BC2.57**

**Trade-off when the most relevant information is a highly uncertain estimate (§2.22):**

| Pathway | Teaching outcome | Cite |
|---------|------------------|------|
| A — Uncertain estimate **+ disclosure** | Description of the estimate + explanation of uncertainties may still be sufficiently useful | **§2.22**, **§2.19** |
| B — **Slightly less relevant / lower uncertainty** measure | Prefer next-best relevant info that **can** be faithfully represented | **§2.22**, **§2.21** |
| C — **No useful estimate** (limited cases) | Provide information that does **not** rely on an estimate | **§2.22** |

Teaching outcome: the trade-off is **QC-driven** (relevance ↔ FR under uncertainty), not a third fundamental QC. Deeper recognition filters live in the Recognition chapter. Measurement uncertainty affects whether a measure can be **faithfully represented** — it does not automatically strip relevance from the underlying phenomenon. **BC2.48–BC2.49**

> **Pitfall:** Skipping from “highly uncertain” straight to silence without testing pathways A→B→C. **§2.22**

### 2.6 Enhancing QCs

Comparability, verifiability, timeliness and understandability **enhance** information that is already relevant and a faithful representation. They may also help choose between two depictions that are equally relevant and equally faithful. Maximise them to the extent possible — but they **cannot**, individually or as a group, make irrelevant or unfaithful information useful. Application is **iterative**; no prescribed order; sometimes diminish one enhancer to maximise another (e.g. temporary comparability hit from **prospective** application of a new Standard may be worthwhile for longer-term relevance/FR; disclosures may partially compensate). **§2.23**, **§2.37–2.38**

| Enhancer | Teaching spine | Cite |
|----------|----------------|------|
| **Comparability** | Identify similarities and differences among items (needs ≥2 items). Consistency (same methods) **helps** but ≠ comparability. Comparability ≠ uniformity — like things alike **and** different things different. Alternative methods for the same phenomenon diminish comparability | **§2.24–2.29** |
| **Verifiability** | Knowledgeable independent observers could reach **consensus** (not necessarily complete agreement). Quantified info need not be a single point — a range + probabilities can be verified. Direct (observe) vs indirect (recalculate model outputs). Forward-looking info may need disclosed assumptions/methods | **§2.30–2.32** |
| **Timeliness** | Available in time to influence decisions. Older generally less useful; some info stays timely for trend assessment | **§2.33** |
| **Understandability** | Classify, characterise and present clearly and concisely. Inherently complex phenomena: excluding them can make reports incomplete and possibly misleading. Users = reasonable knowledge + diligent review; even they may need an adviser | **§2.34–2.36** |

#### 2.6-ex Worked-example strip — enhancers cannot rescue irrelevant info `{#s-2-6-ex}`

| Facts | Concept / gate | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Entity publishes a beautifully formatted, timely, cross-entity-comparable note on staff birthdays each month — verified by HR, explained plainly. Separately it buries a material contingent claim in boilerplate. | **Birthday note:** enhancers strong but not capable of making a difference to resource-provision decisions → fails relevance; enhancers cannot rescue (**§2.6**, **§2.37**, **BC2.11**). **Contingent claim:** omit/misstate/**obscure** may influence primary users — polishing unrelated notes does not fix the gap (**§2.11**, **§2.20**). | **Fundamentals first.** Enhancers polish useful information; they do not create usefulness from scratch. **§2.20**, **§2.37** |

> **Pitfall:** Forcing unlike items into the same accounting “bucket” in the name of comparability — that **reduces** comparability. **§2.27**
>
> **Pitfall:** Treating “verifiable” as unanimous agreement on a single point estimate. Consensus ≠ complete agreement; ranges OK. **§2.30**
>
> **Pitfall:** Omitting complex items “for understandability.” That can make reports incomplete/misleading. **§2.35**
>
> **Pitfall:** Using an enhancer (e.g. “everyone does it this way”) to justify reporting that fails relevance or FR. **§2.37**


**Contrast card — Useful information (fundamentals met) vs cost/enhancer traps**

| What it is — fundamentals met | Common trap — cost alone kills / enhancers rescue |
|---|---|
| Useful financial information is **relevant and faithfully represented**. Enhancers then maximise usefulness; cost constrains what can be provided. Enhancers cannot create usefulness from scratch. **§2.20**, **§2.37**, **§2.39** | Treating “too costly for us” as a preparer opt-out from a clear Standard, or polishing irrelevant/unfaithful information with comparability, timeliness or understandability until it “looks useful.” **§2.37**, **§2.39–2.43**, **SP1.2** |


### 2.7 Cost constraint

Cost is a **pervasive** constraint on the information financial reporting can provide — costs of reporting must be **justified by benefits**. Provider costs (collect, process, verify, disseminate) are largely borne by users via reduced returns; users also incur analysis costs — and extra costs if needed information is missing and must be obtained or estimated elsewhere. Relevant + FR information supports more confident decisions → more efficient capital markets / lower cost of capital economy-wide; individual users benefit from better-informed decisions — but GPFR cannot provide **all** information every user finds relevant. **§2.39–2.41**

The Board assesses whether benefits of particular information are likely to justify costs; seeks input from providers, users, auditors, academics and others; usually mixes quantitative and qualitative evidence. Assessments relate to financial reporting **generally**, not only individual entities — yet differences by size, capital-raising (public/private), user needs, etc. may still be appropriate. **§2.42–2.43**

> **Callout — Cost ≠ override:** “Too costly for us” is **not** a preparer opt-out from a clear Standard. Cost thinking informs Board standard-setting and gap-filling discipline — it does **not** trump **SP1.2**. **§2.39–2.43**, **SP1.2**

### 2.8 Optional teaching prompts *(not a CF procedure)*

> **Label:** CF describes qualitative characteristics, relationships and the cost constraint. It does **not** prescribe a fixed diagnostic sequence for preparers. Enhancer application has **no prescribed order** (**§2.38**). Bullet order below is Content pedagogy, not Board order. **§2.21** is CF’s own illustration of applying the fundamentals — not a licence to invent a numbered QC procedure.

- **Hierarchy cue:** Relevant + faithfully represented first; then maximise enhancers; weigh cost. Enhancers cannot rescue irrelevant or unfaithful info. **§2.4**, **§2.20**, **§2.37**, **§2.39**
- **Relevance cue:** Capable of making a difference (predictive and/or confirmatory)? Unused / known-elsewhere ≠ irrelevant. **§2.6–2.7**
- **Materiality cue:** Entity-specific nature and/or magnitude; omit / misstate / **obscure**; primary users — no uniform % threshold. **§2.11**, **§1.5**
- **FR cue:** Substance (not only legal form when they differ); maximise complete / neutral / free from error. **§2.12–2.15**, **§2.18**
- **Prudence cue:** Caution under uncertainty supporting neutrality — not cookie-jar understatement of assets / overstatement of liabilities. **§2.16–2.17**, **BC2.39–BC2.40**
- **Uncertainty cue:** High measurement uncertainty ≠ automatic kill; test §2.22 pathways A→B→C. **§2.19**, **§2.22**
- **Enhancer cue:** Comparability / verifiability / timeliness / understandability — maximise iteratively; no prescribed order. **§2.23**, **§2.38**
- **Cost cue:** Benefits justify costs in Board design — does **not** override a Standard. **§2.39–2.43**, **SP1.2**
