## Teaching sections

### 3.0 Scope of this chapter
This chapter is the site home for official CF **Chapter 3 — Financial statements and the reporting entity**. Cites stay **§3.x** / **BC3.x**. Chapters 1–2 of the CF (site Ch1–Ch2) discuss information in general purpose financial reports; Chapters 3–8 discuss information in general purpose **financial statements** — a particular form of GPFR. Financial statements provide information about economic resources, claims and changes that meet the definitions of the elements. **§3.1**, **BC3.1–BC3.2**

Teach so a technical accountant can state the **objective of FS**, apply the **entity perspective**, test the **going-concern** assumption, and set (or critique) a reporting-entity **boundary** — without loading IFRS 10 consolidation mechanics or inventing a Board “boundary checklist.” Stay CF-pure; Standards are thin dashed see-also only. **§3.2**, **§3.8–3.18**

> **Pitfall:** Loading IFRS 10 control/consolidation mechanics into this chapter. CF gives the conceptual link (parent control of subsidiary) and usefulness of consolidated vs unconsolidated information — not the Standard’s detailed control model. **§3.11**, **§3.15–3.18**

### 3.1 Concept structure — whose view? · continuing? · who is in?

Read the visual as a **concept structure**, not a Board-ordered decision pipeline. CF links three ideas under financial statements / the reporting entity:

- **Perspective:** transactions and events viewed from the **reporting entity as a whole** — not from any particular investor, lender or creditor group. **§3.8**
- **Going concern:** FS are normally prepared assuming the entity will continue in operation for the foreseeable future; if intention or need to liquidate / cease trading exists, a **different basis** may be needed and must be described. **§3.9**
- **Reporting entity / boundary:** who (or what set of activities) the statements are about — a single entity, a portion of an entity, or more than one entity; not necessarily a legal entity. Parent–subsidiary **control** is the conceptual link that produces consolidated FS; parent-alone produces unconsolidated FS; entities not all linked by parent–subsidiary produce combined FS. **§3.10–3.12**, **§3.15–3.18**

**Peer card row — Whose view · Continuing · Who is in (relationships, not a checklist)**

| Whose view? | Continuing? | Who is in? |
|---|---|---|
| **Entity perspective** — transactions and events viewed from the reporting entity as a whole, not from any particular investor/lender/creditor group. **§3.8** | **Going concern** — normally assume continue for the foreseeable future; if intention/need to liquidate or cease trading, a different basis may be needed and must be described. **§3.9** | **Boundary** — single entity, portion, or more than one; not necessarily a legal entity. Parent control of subsidiary → consolidated; parent alone → unconsolidated. **§3.10–3.12**, **§3.15–3.18** |


Why this shape earns teaching time: the Objective chapter asks for useful information about *the reporting entity*; this chapter answers **which entity**, from **whose view**, and on **what continuation assumption**. Collapsing the structure into “pick legal company X” loses hard boundaries. Collapsing it into a numbered Board procedure invents an order CF does not prescribe. **§3.1–3.2**, **§3.10**, **BC3.12–BC3.13**

**Teaching figure:** Boundary **contains** Consolidated ∥ Unconsolidated (**§3.10–3.12**, **§3.15–3.18**). The Concept Map org plate is the single consol/unconsol figure (parent–sub fork; Consol FS = group view; Unconsol FS = parent alone). Combined FS stays a chapter containment note only — not a third peer. **§3.12**, **BC3.20–BC3.21**

> **Pitfall:** Teaching a Content “1→N boundary checklist” as if the Board published a diagnostic sequence. CF describes relationships among FS objective, perspective, going concern and boundary — it does not hand out a fixed preparer flowchart. **§3.2**, **§3.8–3.14**

### 3.2 Objective & scope of financial statements

The objective of financial statements is to provide financial information about the reporting entity’s **assets, liabilities, equity, income and expenses** that is useful to users in assessing prospects for future net cash inflows to the reporting entity **and** in assessing management’s stewardship of the entity’s economic resources. That links back to the Objective chapter’s assessments, but scopes the depiction to the **elements**. **§3.2**, **§1.3**

That information appears: (a) in the statement of financial position — recognising assets, liabilities and equity; (b) in the statement(s) of financial performance — recognising income and expenses; and (c) in other statements and notes — recognised and unrecognised items (including risks), cash flows, equity contributions/distributions, and methods/assumptions/judgements. **§3.3**

**Why shaped this way?** The Board based the FS objective on the GPFR objective and on IAS 1’s objective wording, then adjusted: refer to **elements** (A/L/E and income/expenses) instead of only “position/performance”; omit cash flows from the *objective* sentence because cash flows are not elements (they still appear in other statements/notes); and expand usefulness into the dual assessments (cash-flow prospects + stewardship). Referring only to the SOFP and performance statement(s) as places where **recognition** of elements occurs does **not** make cash-flow or equity-change information secondary — those items are simply not elements. Risk information about recognised and unrecognised items contributes to the FS objective. **BC3.3–BC3.8**, **§3.3(c)**

**Reporting period.** FS are prepared for a specified period: end-of-period / during-period position and period income/expenses. Comparatives for at least one preceding period help users see changes and trends. Forward-looking information belongs in FS only when it relates to existing (or period) elements **and** is useful — not typically management’s strategy narrative. Post-period-end events are included when necessary to meet the FS objective. **§3.4–3.7**

> **Pitfall:** Expecting FS to carry full management strategy / outlook narrative. CF limits forward-looking content inside FS to element-linked useful info. **§3.6**

> **Pitfall:** Reading the SOFP / performance-statement focus as “cash flows don’t matter.” Cash-flow information is important; it is just not an *element*, so recognition language centres on A/L/E/income/expenses. **BC3.4–BC3.6**, **§3.3(c)(iii)**

### 3.3 Entity perspective

Financial statements provide information about transactions and other events viewed from the perspective of the **reporting entity as a whole**, not from the perspective of any particular group of the entity’s existing or potential investors, lenders or other creditors. **§3.8**

**Why shaped this way?** The Board adopted the entity perspective because it is consistent with the GPFR objective: useful information for the primary-user group as a whole, not a particular subset. Directing information at one subset could force different FS packs for each subset — costly and confusing — and treats the reporting entity as inseparable from its capital providers. The reporting entity is separate from its investors, lenders and other creditors. **BC3.9–BC3.10**, **§1.2**, **§1.5**

Teaching hook: under the entity-as-a-whole lens, non-controlling interests (NCI) in a group have the characteristics of **equity of the reporting entity** — not “outside” the entity view. That is a perspective consequence, not an IFRS 10 deep dive. **§3.8**, **§3.15** · **[Deloitte 2022]**


**Contrast card — Entity perspective vs proprietary / primary-user-as-viewpoint trap**

| What it is — entity perspective | Common trap — proprietary / primary-user-as-viewpoint |
|---|---|
| FS view transactions and events from the **reporting entity as a whole**. Primary users drive usefulness; the **viewpoint** is the entity — consistent with useful information for the primary-user group as a whole. **§3.8**, **BC3.9–BC3.10** | Treating a “parent-shareholders only” proprietary lens as the CF default, or conflating primary users (who the information is *for*) with the reporting viewpoint (the entity as a whole). **§3.8**, **BC3.9–BC3.10** |


> **Pitfall:** Switching the lens mid-pack (entity view in the statements, then proprietary carve-outs as if they were the CF default). Primary users still drive *usefulness*; the **viewpoint** is the entity. **§3.8**, **BC3.9–BC3.10**

### 3.4 Going concern

Financial statements are normally prepared on the assumption that the reporting entity is a **going concern** and will continue in operation for the foreseeable future — neither intention nor need to enter liquidation or to cease trading. If such an intention or need exists, the financial statements may have to be prepared on a **different basis**. If so, the financial statements **describe** the basis used. **§3.9**

**Why shaped this way?** The description is brought forward from the 2010 Conceptual Framework largely unchanged, except “cease trading” replaces “curtail materially the scale of its operations” — aligning more closely with IAS 1 / IAS 10 wording. **BC3.11**

Why it matters for later chapters: measurement, classification and “practical ability to avoid” analyses often presume continuation. On a going-concern basis, an entity cannot claim it has practical ability to avoid a transfer solely by liquidating or ceasing trade (→ Elements liability linker). **§3.9**, **§4.33**


**Contrast card — Going-concern assumption vs silent always-on trap**

| What it is — going-concern assumption | Common trap — silent always-on switch |
|---|---|
| FS are normally prepared assuming the entity will continue in operation for the foreseeable future. If intention or need to liquidate or cease trading exists, a **different basis** may be needed and must be **described**. **§3.9** | Leaving going-concern measurement and classification silently in place when the assumption fails, with no description of a different basis. Going concern is not automatic with zero disclosure when the basis changes. **§3.9**, **BC3.11** |


#### 3.4-ex Worked-example strip — going concern vs liquidation-basis pointer `{#s-3-4-ex}`

| Facts | Concept / gate | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Entity L’s board has resolved to wind up trading within six months; a liquidator will realise assets and settle claims. Management still drafts “normal” going-concern IFRS statements and plans no note about the basis. | **Intention/need to liquidate or cease trading exists** → FS may need a **different basis**; if so, the statements must **describe** the basis used (**§3.9**). Leaving going-concern measurement silently in place when the assumption fails misleads users. | **Going concern is not a silent always-on switch.** When the assumption fails, change (and describe) the basis — detailed liquidation measurement lives in Standards / practice, not in a CF procedure. **§3.9**, **BC3.11** |

> **Pitfall:** Treating going concern as automatic with no disclosure when a different basis is used. If the basis changes, **describe it**. **§3.9**

### 3.5 Reporting entity & boundary

A **reporting entity** is an entity that is required, or chooses, to prepare financial statements. It can be a single entity, a portion of an entity, or more than one entity. It is **not necessarily a legal entity**. **§3.10**

Possible shapes CF names:

| Shape | Conceptual cue | Cite |
|-------|----------------|------|
| Single entity | Often a legal entity — boundary usually straightforward | **§3.10**, **BC3.19** |
| Parent + subsidiaries | Parent has **control** over subsidiary → **consolidated** FS of the combined reporting entity | **§3.11**, **§3.15** |
| Parent alone | **Unconsolidated** FS (CF term) | **§3.11**, **§3.17** |
| Two+ entities not all linked by parent–subsidiary | **Combined** FS | **§3.12** |
| Portion of an entity | Possible (e.g. branch / defined activities) — hard-boundary rules apply | **§3.10**, **§3.13–3.14** |

**Why shaped this way?** The 2010 Conceptual Framework did not discuss what a reporting entity is. The 2018 CF gives a **general description**, not a list of who must prepare GPFS — the Board has no authority to decide who must, should or could prepare such statements. Vague 2010 Exposure Draft phrases (“circumscribed area,” “objectively distinguished”) were dropped as unclear. **BC3.12–BC3.15**, **BC3.13**

**Hard boundaries.** Determining the appropriate boundary can be difficult if the reporting entity (a) is not a legal entity **and** (b) does not comprise only legal entities linked by a parent–subsidiary relationship. In such cases the boundary is driven by **primary users’ information needs**: relevant information that **faithfully represents** what it purports to represent. Faithful representation requires that: (a) the boundary does not contain an **arbitrary or incomplete** set of economic activities; (b) including that set results in **neutral** information; and (c) a **description** is provided of how the boundary was determined and of what constitutes the reporting entity. Completeness and neutrality are especially important here. When the entity *is* a legal entity (or only legal entities linked by parent–subsidiary), the legal/control boundary normally meets users’ needs. **§3.13–3.14**, **BC3.16–BC3.19**

Control as link (high level only): CF uses parent **control** of a subsidiary as the conceptual reason a multi-entity set becomes one consolidated reporting entity. Detailed control tests → thin see-also **IFRS 10**. Joint control and significant influence are deliberately **not** embedded in the CF. **§3.11**, **BC3.26**


**Contrast card — CF boundary tests vs invent-IFRS-10 / cherry-pick trap**

| What it is — CF boundary | Common trap — invent IFRS 10 / cherry-pick |
|---|---|
| Reporting entity need not be a legal entity. Hard cases: primary users’ needs → relevant + faithfully represented set (not arbitrary/incomplete; neutral; boundary **described**). Parent **control** of a subsidiary is the conceptual link for consolidated FS. **§3.10**, **§3.13–3.14**, **§3.11** | Loading IFRS 10’s detailed control model into CF, or drawing a boundary for presentation convenience (exclude awkward activities) so the set is arbitrary or incomplete. Detailed control tests stay in Standards. **§3.14**, **BC3.18**, **BC3.26** |


#### 3.5-ex Worked-example strip — wrong boundary misleads `{#s-3-5-ex}`

| Facts | Concept / gate | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| HoldCo wants “branch B” FS for a lend-out pitch. Draft includes B’s external revenue and directly attributable assets, but **excludes** B’s share of group overheads and shared-service costs B consumes — cleaner numbers for the lender. | Portion of an entity is possible (**§3.10**), but this is not a legal entity / parent–subsidiary set → **§3.13–3.14**. Boundary must not be arbitrary/incomplete; inclusion must yield **neutral** information; FS must **describe** how the boundary was set (**§3.14**, **BC3.17–BC3.18**). | **Wrong boundary misleads** — even if every line item inside the cherry-picked set is “accurate.” Describe the boundary; do not draw it for presentation convenience. **§3.14**, **BC3.17–BC3.18** |

> **Pitfall:** Drawing a boundary for presentation convenience (exclude awkward activities) so the set is arbitrary or incomplete — fails faithful representation. Skipping the description of how the boundary was determined also fails **§3.14(c)**. **§3.14**, **BC3.18**

### 3.6 Consolidated vs unconsolidated

> **Figure:** Consol ∥ Unconsol structure lives on the Concept Map org plate — body keeps usefulness + substitute rule only (no second compare plate). **§3.15–3.18**

**Contrast — usefulness (not a second org plate)**

| Consolidated FS | Unconsolidated FS (CF term) |
|---|---|
| Parent **and** subsidiaries as **one** reporting entity. Useful for parent’s investors/lenders — parent cash inflows depend on subsidiary cash inflows. Not designed as separate info about any one subsidiary. **§3.11**, **§3.15–3.16**, **BC3.22–BC3.25** | Parent **alone**. Can matter (claim vs parent ≠ claim vs subs; distributable amounts may track parent reserves). When consolidated FS are required, unconsolidated are typically **not sufficient** as a substitute. **§3.17–3.18**, **BC3.24** |


> **Callout — CF “unconsolidated” vs IAS 27 “separate”:** The CF uses **unconsolidated** financial statements; IAS 27 uses **separate** financial statements for a similar parent-alone view — treat as a **label difference**, not a second CF concept. **§3.17–3.18** · see also **IAS 27**. · **[Deloitte 2022]**

> **See also (thin dashed only):** → **IFRS 10** (control / consolidated) · → **IAS 27** (separate FS). Do **not** expand those Standards here.

> **Pitfall:** Treating unconsolidated / separate FS as enough for primary users of the parent when consolidated FS are required. CF says typically **not sufficient** as a substitute. **§3.18**

### 3.7 Combined FS (containment note inside Boundary — not a map box)

If a reporting entity comprises two or more entities that are **not** all linked by a parent–subsidiary relationship, the FS are **combined financial statements**. **§3.12**

**Why acknowledged but undeveloped?** Combined FS can provide useful information in some circumstances (e.g. sibling entities). The 2018 CF acknowledges the concept but does **not** discuss when or how to prepare them — that would be best developed if the Board later decides on a Standard. Keep as a **chapter nest only** — do **not** elevate to a third L2 map box beside consolidated/unconsolidated. **BC3.20–BC3.21** · **[Deloitte 2022]**

> **Pitfall:** Elevating combined FS to a peer map box or inventing CF “when/how” rules for combination. Locked: containment note inside Boundary; Standards-level if ever developed. **§3.12**, **BC3.21**

### 3.8 Optional teaching prompts *(not a CF procedure)*

> **Label:** CF describes the FS objective, entity perspective, going-concern assumption, reporting-entity concept and boundary factors. It does **not** prescribe a fixed diagnostic sequence or “boundary checklist” for preparers. Bullet order below is Content pedagogy, not Board order.

- **FS objective cue:** Elements info useful for cash-flow prospects **and** stewardship? **§3.2**
- **Period / forward-looking cue:** Specified period + comparatives; forward-looking only if element-linked and useful? **§3.4–3.7**
- **Perspective cue:** Viewed as a whole — not one claimant class? **§3.8**, **BC3.9–BC3.10**
- **Going concern cue:** Continuing assumption appropriate? If not → different basis, **described**? **§3.9**
- **Boundary cue:** Legal / parent–sub / portion / combined? If hard case → primary-user needs + relevant + complete + neutral + described? **§3.10–3.14**, **BC3.18**
- **Statement-type cue:** Consolidated (parent+subs as one) vs unconsolidated (parent alone) — unconsolidated ≠ substitute when consolidated required? **§3.15–3.18**
- **Combined cue:** Not all linked by parent–subsidiary → combined concept only; no CF when/how? **§3.12**, **BC3.21**
- **CF-pure reminder:** Control/consolidation mechanics → IFRS 10; separate FS label → IAS 27 — not expanded here.
