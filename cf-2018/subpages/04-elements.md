## Teaching sections

### 4.0 Scope of this chapter
This chapter defines the **elements** of financial statements: assets, liabilities and equity (financial position) and income and expenses (financial performance). Those definitions link back to the economic resources, claims and changes discussed in the Objective chapter. Meeting a definition is not the same as recognition — recognition filters live in the Recognition chapter. **§4.1–4.2**, **§1.12–1.21**, **§5.6–5.7**

Position elements answer “what resources and claims exist **now**.” Performance elements answer “what changed equity this period **other than** owner contributions/distributions.” Definitions come before recognition so the Board can decide whether a defined item is useful to capture — existence ≠ depiction. **§4.1–4.2**, **§4.68–4.70**, **§5.6**

**Contrast card — Definition met vs Recognised**

| What it is — definition met | Common trap — “defined → booked” |
|---|---|
| The item satisfies an element definition (asset, liability, equity, income or expense). Existence of the resource or claim is established. **§4.1–4.2**, **§4.3**, **§4.26** | Treating a met definition as automatic recognition. Recognition still needs relevance and faithful-representation filters (Recognition chapter). Definition ≠ depiction. **§5.6–5.7** |

> **Pitfall:** Blurring definition tests into recognition gates. This chapter defines; Recognition decides whether to capture. **§4.1**, **§5.6**

### 4.1 Concept structure (Asset ∥ Liability → Equity residual; Income/Expenses inside Equity)

Read the visual as a **concept structure**, not a decision pipeline. Asset and Liability are structural peers: each stands on its own three criteria. Equity is residual after liabilities. Income and expenses are the performance composition **inside** that residual story — equity-changing movements in assets and liabilities (excluding owner contributions and distributions). Teach them as **組成 inside Equity**, not as a demoted fourth position peer hung below Equity on a dashed stem. Unit of account is a grouping choice for recognition and measurement — not a fifth element. **§4.1–4.2**, **§4.48**, **§4.63**, **§4.68–4.69**

Why this order earns teaching time: weak A/L definitions poison both residual equity and performance depiction, because equity and income/expenses are defined *from* A/L. A sequential “first prove asset, then prove liability” pipeline invents an order CF does not require. **§4.3**, **§4.26–4.27**, **§4.63**, **§4.68–4.71**

**Containment cue (body):** Equity (residual) outer — Income ∥ Expenses solid inner peers as 組成 inside Equity. Not a dashed hang under Equity. **§4.63**, **§4.68–4.71**

| Income (inside Equity story) | Expenses (inside Equity story) |
|---|---|
| Increases in equity from A/L movements other than contributions. **§4.68** | Decreases in equity from A/L movements other than distributions. **§4.69** |

> **Pitfall:** Teaching Elements as “Asset checklist → then Liability checklist → then stop,” or hanging Income/Expenses under Equity with a dashed stem. Peers first; residual Equity next; Income/Expenses as performance composition **inside** Equity. **§4.1–4.2**, **§4.63**, **§4.68–4.70**

### 4.1.1 Asset ∥ Liability symmetry — and why the linkers differ

Asset and Liability share one mirrored skeleton: a present claim-side object (right ↔ obligation), a directional potential (benefits **in** ↔ transfer **out**), and past events. Probability was removed from the existence hurdle on both sides — low chance can still meet the definition; recognition and measurement absorb much of the uncertainty. **§4.3–4.5**, **§4.14–4.15**, **§4.26–4.27**, **§4.37–4.38**, **§5.12**

The Board deliberately separated “economic resource” from the asset/liability definitions so that an asset (or liability) is the resource or obligation itself — not the ultimate inflow or outflow it may produce. That structural change also makes the A ∥ L parallel visible. **BC4.6–BC4.7**, **§4.4**

| Lens | Asset | Liability | Cite |
|------|-------|-----------|------|
| Right ↔ Obligation | present **right** (economic resource) | present **obligation** | **§4.3–4.4**, **§4.26–4.28** |
| Direction of benefits | potential to produce benefits **in** | potential to require transfer **out** | **§4.14**, **§4.36–4.37** |
| Linking test | **control** (direct use + obtain benefits; prevent others) | **no practical ability to avoid** | **§4.19–4.20**, **§4.28–4.29** |
| Timing | as a result of **past events** | as a result of **past events** | **§4.3**, **§4.26**, **§4.42–4.43** |
| Probability in definition | can be **low** and still meet definition | can be **low** and still meet definition | **§4.14–4.15**, **§4.37–4.38** |

The linkers are deliberately asymmetric. **Control** asks which entity obtains the benefits from a present right (and can prevent others). **No practical ability to avoid** asks whether a duty binds the entity in substance — even when a theoretical exit still exists. The Board chose “practical ability” language partly because it mirrors “practical ability / present ability” used when assessing control of an asset: both tests ask what the entity can actually do, not what labels say. **§4.19–4.20**, **§4.29**, **BC4.40**, **BC4.57**

**Contrast card — Control vs no practical ability to avoid** (peer linkers; solid peer frames)

| Control (asset linker) | No practical ability to avoid (liability linker) |
|---|---|
| Present ability to direct use of the right and obtain its benefits, including preventing others. Links the economic resource to the entity. **§4.19–4.20**, **BC4.40** | The duty binds in substance even if a theoretical exit exists. Intention or high likelihood alone is not enough — do not swap this linker for “control.” **§4.29**, **§4.34**, **BC4.57** |

> **Teaching move:** same “practical ability” family of language on both sides — but the questions differ (who gets the benefits vs whether the duty can be avoided). Do not swap the linkers. **BC4.40**, **BC4.57**

> **[Deloitte 2022]** Removal of “expected flow” from **both** asset and liability definitions lowers the existence hurdle and pushes uncertainty toward recognition/measurement. · CF **§4.14–4.15**, **§4.37–4.38**; **BC4.8–BC4.9**

> **Pitfall:** Memorising two separate shopping lists and forgetting the mirror (right↔obligation; in↔out; control↔practical ability). Definition success still ≠ recognition. **§4.3**, **§4.26**, **§5.6–5.7**

### 4.2 Asset — present right, potential, control

An asset is a present economic resource controlled by the entity as a result of past events. An economic resource is a right that has the potential to produce economic benefits. The three supporting ideas — right, potential, control — explain that definition; they are not a process checklist you tick in a fixed order before moving to Liability. **§4.3–4.5**

**Why the 2018 shape?** Pre-2018 wording put “expected” flows inside the asset definition. Some readers treated “expected” as a probability gate, which blurred the resource itself with the cash it might eventually produce, and wrongly excluded clear assets such as out-of-the-money options. The Board therefore: (1) defined the economic resource separately as a **present right** with potential; (2) deleted “expected flow” from the existence test; and (3) left low-probability concerns to recognition and measurement. **§4.3–4.4**, **§4.14–4.17**, **BC4.3**, **BC4.8–BC4.9**, **BC4.14**, **BC4.25**

**Contrast card — Present right / potential vs “expected flow” trap**

| What it is — present right with potential | Common trap — “expected flow” gate |
|---|---|
| The economic resource is a **present right** that has the potential to produce benefits. Potential need not be certain or even likely. **§4.4**, **§4.14–4.15**, **BC4.8–BC4.9** | Reading “expected” as a probability hurdle inside the definition, and excluding low-probability rights (e.g. OTM options). Uncertainty belongs mainly in recognition/measurement. **BC4.9**, **BC4.14** · **[Deloitte 2022]** |

**Right.** Rights come from contract or legislation — receive cash, exchange on favourable terms, use PPE or IP — and also from other means (secret know-how; constructive rights arising from another party’s practices). Not every right is an asset: it needs potential benefits beyond those available to all other parties, and control. Public rights of way and public-domain know-how typically fail. An entity cannot have a right to obtain benefits from itself (treasury shares; intra-group holdings inside one reporting entity). Existence uncertainty (e.g. a disputed right) means it is uncertain whether an asset exists — recognition of that uncertainty is discussed at **§5.14**. **§4.6–4.10**, **§4.13**, **BC4.28**

**Potential.** Potential need not be certain or even likely. The right must already exist and, in at least one circumstance, produce benefits beyond those available to all others. Low probability can still meet the definition; it may still block or shape recognition or measurement. The economic resource is the present right that contains the potential — not the future benefits themselves. Incurring expenditure is neither necessary nor sufficient proof of an asset. **§4.14–4.18**, **BC4.11**

**Control.** Control links the resource to the entity: present ability to direct use and obtain the benefits (including preventing others). Generally one party controls a given resource at a time; an entity may control a share without controlling the whole. Control usually arises from enforceable legal rights, but can arise otherwise (secret know-how). Exposure to significant variation in benefits may indicate control — one factor, not conclusive. An agent holding a principal’s resource does not recognise that resource as its own asset. **§4.19–4.25**, **BC4.40**

### 4.3 Liability — obligation, transfer, past events

A liability is a present obligation of the entity to transfer an economic resource as a result of past events. All three criteria must be satisfied: obligation · transfer · past events. Mirror the asset rewrite: focus on a present obligation with **potential** to require a transfer — not on whether an outflow is “expected.” **§4.26–4.27**, **§4.37–4.38**, **BC4.44**

**Why the linker is “no practical ability to avoid.”** Problems arose when an entity had some limited ability to avoid a transfer — constructive practices, or requirements conditional on the entity’s own future action. The Board considered stricter “no ability even in theory” and looser “may have to transfer if further conditions are met,” and adopted the middle view: **no practical ability to avoid**. Intention and high likelihood are not enough; a probability-of-outflow threshold was also rejected as the definition of obligation. **§4.29**, **§4.34**, **BC4.47–BC4.53**

**Obligation.** Always owed to another party (identity need not be known). Legally enforceable **or** constructive (customary practices / published policies / specific statements where the entity has no practical ability to act inconsistently). Conditional on a future action the entity may take → still an obligation if it has no practical ability to avoid taking that action. Going-concern basis → cannot “avoid” only by liquidating or ceasing trade. An avoidance path with economic consequences significantly more adverse than the transfer itself may still leave an obligation. **§4.28–4.34**, **§3.9**, **BC4.55**

**Transfer.** Potential to require a transfer is enough — need not be certain or likely. Low probability can still meet the definition; it may affect recognition or measurement. Examples include paying cash, delivering goods/services, unfavourable exchange, contingent transfer, or issuing an instrument that will oblige a transfer. Until settled, transferred or replaced, the obligation to transfer remains. **§4.36–4.41**

**Past events.** A present obligation exists only if the entity has already obtained economic benefits or taken an action **and**, as a consequence, will or may have to transfer a resource it would not otherwise have had to transfer. New legislation or a constructive policy alone is not enough — need the benefits obtained or action taken to which it applies. The obligation can exist now even if enforcement or performance is only in the future. **§4.42–4.46**

| Facts | Concept / gate | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Retailer has a published, long-standing full cash-refund policy within 30 days (not legally required). Customers expect refunds; withdrawing would cause significantly more adverse commercial damage than honouring it. Goods already sold this period (past event / benefits obtained). | **Obligation:** constructive — customary practice / published policy; no practical ability to act inconsistently (**§4.28–4.29**, **§4.31**). Practical ability ≠ intention (**§4.34**, **BC4.55**). **Transfer + past events:** potential to pay cash; sales already occurred (**§4.37**, **§4.43**, **§4.45**). | **Liability definition can be met.** Recognition and measurement still follow the Recognition chapter / Standards (e.g. IAS 37 territory for amount) — definition ≠ recognition. **§5.6–5.7** |

**Contrast inside the teaching:** under an executory salary contract, there is no present obligation to pay salary until the employee’s services have been received — equally unperformed means the past-events criterion for that one-sided pay obligation has not yet been met. **§4.47**

> **Pitfall:** Treating “management intends to refund” or “refunds are likely” as the practical-ability test — likelihood and intention alone are insufficient. **§4.34**, **BC4.53**

### 4.4 Equity — residual after liabilities

Equity is the residual interest in the assets of the entity after deducting all its liabilities. CF does not start from instrument labels (“called a share → equity”). It starts from the liability definition; anything left is equity. That keeps Equity consistent with the Asset ∥ Liability pair. Claims that fail the liability definition sit in equity — the residual does the classification work conceptually. **§4.63–4.64**, **BC4.89**

**Contrast card — Instrument label vs residual equity test**

| What it is — residual equity test | Common trap — instrument label |
|---|---|
| Run the liability definition first. If the claim fails that definition, residual interest after liabilities → equity. The residual does the classification work. **§4.63–4.64**, **BC4.89** | Jumping from “share” / “preferred” straight to equity without testing liability. Labels do not decide under CF. Instrument puzzles (puttables, convertibles) are Standards / FICE territory. **§4.64–4.67**, **BC4.92** |

The Board kept a binary liability/equity split and declined to invent a third claim element: another class would complicate classification and force a new answer for whether its changes are income or expenses. Instrument-level puzzles (puttables, convertibles) are Standards / FICE research territory — not a second CF equity definition here. **BC4.90–BC4.92**, **§4.64–4.67**

Equity claims may include various shares and some obligations to issue another equity claim (to the extent they are not liabilities). Different classes can carry different rights. Legal or regulatory limits on distributable reserves can affect components of equity — the residual definition still applies. **§4.64–4.67**

> **Out of scope / FICE banner:** Distinguishing **equity vs liability instruments** (e.g. puttable instruments, convertible debt classification) is **Standards / FICE** territory — the CF residual definition does **not** rewrite that instrument distinction here. **§4.64–4.67**, **BC4.45**, **BC4.92**

|---|---|---|

Run liability tests first; if they fail → residual. | **Equity (residual), not liability** — the label does not decide. **§4.63–4.64** |

> **Pitfall:** Jumping from the word “share” or “preferred” straight to equity without testing the liability definition. **§4.63–4.64**

### 4.5 Income / Expenses — derived from A/L, excluding owners

Income is increases in assets, or decreases in liabilities, that result in increases in equity, other than those relating to contributions from holders of equity claims. Expenses are the mirror: decreases in assets, or increases in liabilities, that result in decreases in equity, other than distributions to holders of equity claims. **§4.68–4.69**

Why define performance via A/L changes? It ties performance to the same economic resources and claims used for position — one coherent model. Owner contributions and distributions are carved out so financing with equity-claim holders is not painted as performance. Defining performance via A/L does **not** make the performance statement secondary: users need both position and performance, and the Board has not designated one as primary. Income and expenses are performance elements — the performance composition **inside** the Equity residual story. They are not a fourth term in the position equation, and they are not hung under Equity as a demoted peer on the formula board. **§4.70–4.71**, **BC4.93–BC4.94**

Different transactions generate income and expenses with different characteristics; separate presentation can help (→ Presentation **§7.14–7.19**). **§4.72**

#### 4.5-ex Worked-example strip — owner cash vs inventory sale `{#s-4-5-ex}`

| Facts | Concept / gate | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| **(A)** Equity-claim holder injects cash: assets ↑ and equity ↑. **(B)** Sell inventory for cash: one asset ↑ (cash), another ↓ (inventory); equity ↑ from the net effect. | Income/expenses = equity-changing A/L movements **other than** contributions/distributions (**§4.68–4.70**). Same A/L mechanics; the owner carve-out decides whether the equity change is performance. | **(A)** contribution → **not income**. **(B)** → **income path**, with expense for inventory consumed. **§4.68–4.69**, **§4.70** |

> **Pitfall:** Booking owner cash injections as revenue/income. CF excludes contributions and distributions by definition. **§4.70**

### 4.6 Unit of account

Unit of account is the right or group of rights, the obligation or group of obligations, or the group of rights and obligations, to which recognition criteria and measurement concepts are applied. It does not define a new element — it decides how to group rights and obligations already in play. The same phenomenon can use different units for recognition versus measurement; the Board sets this at Standards level. You still apply Asset / Liability / Equity / Income–Expenses definitions; unit of account only chooses the grouping. **§4.48–4.51**

Select the unit to provide useful information (relevant + faithful). Rights are often bundled for understandability (a set of ownership rights described as the physical object). Cost constrains unit selection — finer units generally cost more. **§4.11–4.12**, **§4.51–4.54**

> **Pitfall:** Treating unit of account as a fifth element beside Asset / Liability / Equity / Income–Expenses. It only decides the grouping used when applying those definitions. **§4.48**

### 4.7 Executory contracts — one-contract A ∥ L mirror

An executory contract (or portion) is equally unperformed — neither party has fulfilled any of its obligations, or both have partially fulfilled to an equal extent. It establishes a **combined** right and obligation to exchange economic resources. The right and obligation are interdependent and inseparable, so they form a single asset or liability. Favourable terms → asset; unfavourable → liability. Recognition and measurement (including onerous tests) still apply. When one party performs first: if the reporting entity performs first → right to receive (asset); if the other party performs first → obligation to transfer (liability). **§4.56–4.58**

Teaching point: executory contracts show Asset ∥ Liability symmetry **inside one contract** — combined right/obligation until performance tips the mirror — not a separate process spine. That is why the salary contrast in **§4.47** fits here: equally unperformed → no one-sided present salary liability yet.

> **Executory contracts:** Equally unperformed rights & obligations → usually analysed as a combined right/obligation (often net depiction until performance, subject to recognition/measurement/onerous). **§4.56–4.58**

### 4.8 Substance of contractual rights/obligations

Report the substance of contractual rights and obligations (link faithful representation **§2.12**). All terms — explicit or implicit — that have substance are considered; terms with no discernible economic effect are disregarded. A group or series of contracts may need one unit of account, or one contract may need splitting, to depict substance. **§4.59–4.62**

**Short form-vs-substance cue:** a contract labelled “lease of equipment” that, in substance, transfers control of the underlying asset and leaves the lessor with only a financing residual is analysed by the rights and obligations that have economic effect — not by the lease label alone. Rights and obligations are identified by economic substance, then slotted into the Asset ∥ Liability mirror. **§4.59**, **§2.12**

> **Substance of contractual rights/obligations:** Look through legal form when needed. **§4.59–4.62**, **§2.12**

### 4.9 Optional teaching prompts *(not a CF procedure)*

> **Label:** CF gives definitions and factors; it does **not** prescribe a fixed diagnostic sequence. These prompts are optional memory cues only. Asset ∥ Liability stay peers — not steps. Bullet order below is Content pedagogy, not Board order.

- **Symmetry cue (peers, not steps):** Right ↔ obligation; benefits **in** ↔ transfer **out**; control ↔ no practical ability to avoid; both need past events; both allow low probability. **§4.3**, **§4.26**, **4.1.1** *(page section — not a CF paragraph)*
- **Asset definition tests:** Present economic resource (= right with potential benefits) **controlled** as a result of past events? **§4.3–4.4**, **§4.14**, **§4.19**
- **Liability definition tests (parallel):** Present obligation to transfer an economic resource as a result of past events — obligation / transfer / past events? **§4.26–4.27**
- **Practical ability cue:** No practical ability to avoid — not mere intention/likelihood? Going-concern link? **§4.29**, **§4.33–4.34**
- **Equity residual cue:** After liabilities, what residual interest remains? (Instrument classification → FICE/Standards, not here.) **§4.63–4.64**
- **Income/Expenses cue:** Equity-changing A/L movements, excluding contributions/distributions? **§4.68–4.70**
- **Unit of account cue:** Is the grouping chosen so recognition/measurement stay relevant and faithfully represented? **§4.48**, **§4.51**
- **Recognition reminder:** Definition met ≠ recognised — see Recognition chapter filters. **§5.6–5.7**
