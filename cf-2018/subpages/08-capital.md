## Teaching sections

### 8.0 Scope of this chapter
This chapter is the site home for official CF **Chapter 8 — Concepts of capital and capital maintenance**. Cites stay **§8.x** / **BC8.x**. The material was carried forward **unchanged** from the 2010 Conceptual Framework (and originally from the 1989 Framework). **§8.1–8.10**; carry-forward note before **§8.1**; **BC8.1–BC8.3**

Capital concepts sit after recognition, measurement and presentation in the site sequence — but they are not a residual “appendix.” They influence how profit is defined (return *on* capital vs return *of* capital), how price changes are treated, and how measurement bases and capital maintenance combine into an accounting model. CF does not override Standards. **BC8.2**, **SP1.2**

> **Pitfall:** Treating “capital” here as the share-capital line in equity. In CF Ch8, capital is a **maintenance concept** (what the entity seeks to preserve) — not a single equity caption. **§8.1**, **§8.4**

### 8.1 Concept structure — two capital concepts, not a pipeline

Read the chapter visual as a **concept structure**, not a decision pipeline. CF describes **two concepts of capital**:

1. **Financial concept** — capital as invested money or invested purchasing power; synonymous with the **net assets or equity** of the entity. Most entities adopt a financial concept when preparing financial statements. **§8.1**
2. **Physical concept** — capital as **operating capability / productive capacity** (for example units of output per day). **§8.1**

**Contrast card — Financial capital vs physical capital (two concepts, not a pipeline)**

| Financial — what is preserved / what counts as profit | Physical — what is preserved / what counts as profit |
|---|---|
| Preserves invested money or purchasing power (net assets / equity). Profit when end financial amount exceeds beginning after owner transactions — “did financial capital grow?” Most entities adopt this concept. **§8.1**, **§8.3** | Preserves operating capability / productive capacity. Profit when end physical capacity exceeds beginning after owner transactions — “can we still operate at the same capacity?” Equal-rank alternative — not a next step after financial. **§8.1**, **§8.3** |

Selection should follow **user needs**: financial if users are primarily concerned with maintaining nominal invested capital or the purchasing power of invested capital; physical if the main concern is operating capability. The concept chosen indicates the **goal** to be attained in determining profit — even when measurement is difficult. **§8.2**

**Why shaped this way — carried forward, not rewritten in 2018.** Updating capital and capital maintenance was not feasible when the Board developed the 2018 Conceptual Framework and could have delayed completion significantly. Excluding the discussion altogether would have been inappropriate: those concepts are important to financial reporting and influence the definitions of income and expenses, the selection of measurement bases, and presentation and disclosure decisions. The Board therefore carried Chapter 8 forward unchanged from 2010 (originally 1989) and may revisit it later if needed. **BC8.1–BC8.4**

Teach relationships among capital concept → capital maintenance → profit / adjustments — do **not** invent a numbered CF “choose capital model” procedure or mini flowchart as Board process. **§8.3–8.4**, **§8.9**, **BC8.2**

> **[Deloitte 2022]** Two capital-maintenance concepts identified; CF does **not** indicate a preference for either; selection of concept (and measurement basis) determines the accounting model · CF **§8.3**, **§8.9**

> **Pitfall:** Teaching a Content “1→N capital checklist” or “mini flowchart” as if the Board published a preparer diagnostic sequence. CF describes concepts and relationships; it does not hand out a fixed capital-model procedure. **§8.2**, **§8.9**, **BC8.1–BC8.3**

### 8.2 Capital maintenance and the determination of profit

The concepts of capital in **§8.1** give rise to corresponding **concepts of capital maintenance**. Capital maintenance is how an entity defines the capital it seeks to maintain — the link between capital concept and profit, and the prerequisite for distinguishing **return on capital** from **return of capital**. Only inflows of assets in excess of amounts needed to maintain capital may be regarded as profit (a return *on* capital). Profit is the residual after expenses (including capital-maintenance adjustments where appropriate) have been deducted from income; if expenses exceed income, the residual is a loss. **§8.3–8.4**

| | **Financial capital maintenance** | **Physical capital maintenance** | Cite |
|--|-----------------------------------|----------------------------------|------|
| Capital idea | Money / financial net assets — measured in **nominal monetary units** *or* **units of constant purchasing power** | Physical productive capacity / operating capability (or the resources or funds needed to achieve that capacity) | **§8.3** |
| Profit earned when… | End-of-period financial (money) amount of net assets **exceeds** beginning, after excluding distributions to and contributions from owners | End-of-period physical productive capacity **exceeds** beginning, after excluding owner transactions | **§8.3** |
| Price-change treatment | **Nominal:** holding gains are, conceptually, profits (may await disposal before recognition). **Constant PP:** only the part of a price increase that **exceeds** the general price-level rise is profit; the rest = capital-maintenance adjustment in equity | **All** price changes affecting assets and liabilities are viewed as changes in measuring physical productive capacity → capital-maintenance adjustments in equity, **not** profit | **§8.6–8.8** |
| Measurement link | Does **not** require a particular measurement basis — selection depends on the type of financial capital being maintained | **Requires** the current-cost basis of measurement | **§8.5** |
| Teaching cue | “Did financial capital grow?” | “Can we still operate at the same capacity?” | **§8.3–8.4** |

In general terms, an entity has maintained its capital if it has as much capital at the end of the period as at the beginning. Any amount over and above that required to maintain beginning capital is profit. The **principal difference** between the two maintenance concepts is the treatment of the effects of **changes in the prices** of assets and liabilities. **§8.6**

**Peer card row — Price-change landing (profit vs capital-maintenance adjustment)**

| Financial — nominal | Financial — constant PP | Physical — holding gains |
|---|---|---|
| Holding gains are, conceptually, **profit** (recognition as profit may await disposal under the concept’s teaching). **§8.7** | Only the excess of a specific price rise over the general price-level rise is profit; the inflation layer = **capital-maintenance adjustment** in equity. **§8.7** | **All** price changes affecting A/L are capital-maintenance adjustments in equity — **not** profit — even if they meet the income definition. **§8.8**, **§8.10** |

> **Pitfall:** Booking holding gains as “profit” under a physical-capital concept. Under physical maintenance they are equity capital-maintenance adjustments, not profit. **§8.8**

### 8.3 Measurement bases × capital concept → accounting model

Selection of the **measurement bases** and the **concept of capital maintenance** together determine the accounting model used in preparing the financial statements. Different models exhibit different degrees of relevance and reliability; as elsewhere, management must seek a balance. This Conceptual Framework applies to a **range** of accounting models and provides guidance on preparing and presenting statements constructed under the chosen model. **§8.9**

**Relationships to teach (not a Board flowchart):**

- **Physical capital maintenance** requires **current cost** measurement — the maintenance concept constrains the basis. **§8.5**
- **Financial capital maintenance** does **not** require a particular basis; the basis depends on whether the entity is seeking to maintain nominal money capital or constant purchasing power. **§8.5**
- At present, it is **not** the intention of the HKICPA / Board to prescribe a particular model **other than in exceptional circumstances** — such as entities reporting in the currency of a **hyperinflationary** economy. That intention is subject to review in light of world developments. **§8.9**

> **[Deloitte 2022]** Selection of capital-maintenance concept and measurement basis determines the accounting model; different models exhibit different degrees of relevance and reliability; preparers should seek a balance · CF **§8.9**

**Contrast card — CF describes models across IFRS vs “CF picks one capital model”**

| What it is — CF describes a range of models | Common trap — “CF picks one capital model” |
|---|---|
| Measurement bases × capital-maintenance concept jointly determine the accounting model. CF applies across that range and does **not** prescribe one model other than in exceptional circumstances (e.g. hyperinflationary reporting). **§8.9**, **BC8.2** | Reading **§8.9** as a Board choice of one IFRS-wide capital model. Selection follows user needs and Standards; CF does not override. **§8.2**, **§8.9**, **SP1.2** |

> **Pitfall:** Reading §8.9 as “CF picks one capital model for IFRS.” CF applies across models and does not prescribe one except in exceptional cases (e.g. hyperinflationary reporting). Standards remain authoritative. **§8.9**, **SP1.2**

### 8.4 Capital-maintenance adjustments vs income

Revaluation or restatement of assets and liabilities gives rise to increases or decreases in equity. Those increases or decreases may meet the **definitions** of income and expenses, yet under certain concepts of capital maintenance they are **not** included in the income statement — instead they are included in equity as **capital maintenance adjustments** or revaluation reserves. **§8.10**

That is the presentation consequence of the price-change treatments in **§8.6–8.8**: same economic price movement can be profit under one capital concept and an equity adjustment under another.

#### 8.4-ex1 Worked-example strip — nominal money vs constant purchasing power `{#s-8-4-ex1}`

| Facts | Concept / gate | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Inventory acquired CU100. General prices rise 10%; specific inventory price rises to CU115. No owner transactions. Do not invent a Standards revaluation rule here. | **Nominal:** CU15 holding gain is, conceptually, profit (recognition may await disposal) (**§8.7**). **Constant PP:** CU10 general rise = capital-maintenance adjustment in equity; only excess CU5 (115−110) is profit (**§8.7**). | **Same asset, same CU15 rise — different profit** under the two financial sub-concepts. The CU10 inflation layer is equity maintenance under constant PP, not return on capital. |

#### 8.4-ex2 Worked-example strip — physical capital holding gains `{#s-8-4-ex2}`

| Facts | Concept / gate | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Same inventory CU100 → CU115; assume current-cost measurement as physical maintenance requires. **§8.5** | Under **physical** maintenance, **all** price changes affecting A/L are capital-maintenance adjustments in equity — not profit — even if the increase meets the income definition (**§8.8**, **§8.10**). | **“Holding gain = profit” is concept-dependent.** Physical maintenance consumes the full CU15 into equity first; only capacity growth beyond that is profit. **§8.4**, **§8.8** |

> **Pitfall:** Calling every equity revaluation reserve “OCI per IFRS” without checking which capital concept and which Standard drive the presentation. CF Ch8 explains capital-maintenance adjustments conceptually; Standards (and site Presentation / OCI Board guidance) govern where amounts appear in practice. **§8.10**, **SP1.2**

### 8.5 Practice note and cross-links (thin)

> **Practice note:** IFRS practice typically aligns with **financial capital** maintenance — most entities adopt a financial concept (**§8.1**). Physical capital stays for conceptual contrast and for understanding why some models pair with current cost. Do not invent a physical-capital P&L without a Standard. **§8.1**, **SP1.2**

> **Cross-link (thin):** Physical capital maintenance **requires** current-cost measurement — → Measurement · current cost. **§8.5**, **§6.21–6.22**

> **See also (one-line):** Hyperinflation / restatement mechanics → **IAS 29**. No IAS 29 mechanics in this chapter. **§8.9**

### 8.6 Optional teaching prompts *(not a CF procedure)*

> **Label:** CF describes capital concepts, capital maintenance, and how they interact with measurement and profit. It does **not** prescribe a fixed diagnostic sequence or “mini flowchart” for preparers (**§8.2**, **§8.9**, **BC8.1–BC8.3**). Bullet order below is Content pedagogy, not Board order.

- **User-needs cue:** Are users primarily concerned with nominal / purchasing-power invested capital, or with operating capability? **§8.1–8.2**
- **Maintenance cue:** Has beginning capital been maintained before any amount is called profit? Owner contributions/distributions excluded? **§8.3–8.4**
- **Return cue:** Is this inflow a return *on* capital or a return *of* capital? **§8.4**
- **Price-change cue:** Under this concept, is the price movement profit, or a capital-maintenance adjustment in equity? Nominal vs constant PP vs physical differ. **§8.6–8.8**
- **Measurement pairing cue:** Physical → current cost required; financial → basis depends on what financial capital is being maintained. **§8.5**
- **Model cue:** Measurement bases + capital-maintenance concept jointly determine the accounting model; CF does not prescribe one model except exceptional cases. **§8.9**
- **Standards reminder:** CF does not override recognition, measurement or presentation requirements in Standards. **SP1.2**
