## Teaching sections

### 6.0 Scope of this chapter
This chapter is the site home for official CF **Chapter 6 — Measurement**. Cites stay **§6.x** / **BC6.x**. Recognised elements are quantified in monetary terms. That requires selecting a **measurement basis** — an identified feature of the item being measured (for example historical cost, fair value or fulfilment value). Applying a basis creates a measure for the asset or liability **and** for related income and expenses. **§6.1**

Recognition (site Ch5) decides whether to capture a defined item. Measurement decides *how* to quantify what is captured. A Standard may still need to describe how to implement the chosen basis — techniques, simplified approaches, or modifications. CF does not override Standards. **§6.3**, **SP1.2**

**Contrast card — Recognition (whether) vs Measurement (how)**

| What it is — recognition vs measurement | Common trap — “always FV” / conflating the gates |
|---|---|
| **Recognition** = whether to capture. **Measurement** = how to quantify (select a basis). Separate decisions. **§6.1**, **§6.3**, **§5.6–5.7** | **Measurement ≠ recognition gate**; CF does not favour FV for everything. **§6.2**, **BC6.10–BC6.11** |

### 6.1 Concept structure — two families, not a pipeline

Read the chapter visual as a **concept structure**, not a decision pipeline. CF identifies **two categories** of measurement bases: **historical cost** and **current value**. Current value is a family with siblings — fair value, value in use / fulfilment value, and current cost — not a synonym for fair value alone. **§6.4**, **§6.10–6.11**, **BC6.12**

**Peer card row — Two measurement-basis families**

| Historical cost | Current value |
|---|---|
| Monetary information derived, at least in part, from the price of the transaction or other event that gave rise to the item. Does not reflect value changes except impairment / onerous. **§6.4** | Monetary information updated to reflect conditions at the measurement date — not derived from the originating transaction price. A family, not “FV only.” **§6.10–6.11** |

**Peer card row — Current-value siblings** (equal-rank peers)

| Fair value | VIU · fulfilment | Current cost |
|---|---|---|
| Exit price to sell an asset / transfer a liability between market participants. **§6.12–6.16** | Entity-specific present value of continuing use / disposal (VIU) or of fulfilling a liability (fulfilment). **§6.17–6.20** | Entry cost of an equivalent asset (or consideration for an equivalent liability) at the measurement date. **§6.21–6.22** |

**Why more than one basis?** Different bases provide different information about cash-flow amount, timing and uncertainty, and about how an item contributes to future cash flows. Consideration of the qualitative characteristics and the cost constraint is likely to result in **different** bases for different assets, liabilities, income and expenses. The Board considered — and rejected — advocating a single measurement basis for everything. **§6.2**, **§6.23**, **BC6.5–BC6.11**, **BC6.17**

Cash-flow-based techniques (DCF and similar) are **not** a third measurement-basis category. They estimate a measure when applying a specified basis. Name the basis; describe the technique. **§6.91**, **BC6.13**

> **[Deloitte 2022]** Two families; both can provide predictive and confirmatory value; CF does not favour one over the other · CF **§6.2**, **§6.10–6.11**; **BC6.10–BC6.11**, **BC6.17**

> **Pitfall:** Skipping “what information does this basis provide?” and jumping straight to “pick FV.” Information-provided discussion feeds selection — it is not optional colour. **§6.23**, **BC6.16**

### 6.2 Historical cost

Historical cost measures provide monetary information about assets, liabilities and related income and expenses using information derived, at least in part, from the **price of the transaction or other event** that gave rise to them. Unlike current value, historical cost does **not** reflect changes in values — except impairment of an asset or a liability becoming onerous. **§6.4**, **BC6.21**

**Initial.** Asset historical cost = consideration paid to acquire or create + transaction costs. Liability historical cost = consideration received to incur or take on − transaction costs. When there is no market-terms transaction (or the price is not faithful), a current value may be used as **deemed cost** on initial recognition, then used as the starting point for subsequent historical-cost measurement. **§6.5–6.6**

**Subsequent (still HC family).** Asset carrying amount updates for consumption (depreciation/amortisation), collections, impairment, and interest accrual where a financing component exists. Liability updates for fulfilment, onerous increases, and interest accrual. Those updates stay inside the historical-cost family when anchored to the original transaction price — they are not automatic “current value.” **§6.7–6.8**, **BC6.20–BC6.21**

**Amortised cost** is one way to apply historical cost to financial assets and financial liabilities: future cash flows discounted at a rate determined at initial recognition (updated only for variable-rate instruments), with subsequent updates for interest, impairment and receipts/payments. CF categorises amortised cost as a form of historical cost. **§6.9**, **BC6.22**

> **Pitfall:** Calling every “updated” carrying amount current value. Amortisation and impairment can still sit inside the historical-cost family. **§6.4**, **§6.7–6.9**

### 6.3 Current value — FV, VIU / fulfilment, current cost

Current value measures provide monetary information using information **updated to reflect conditions at the measurement date**. Unlike historical cost, a current value is not derived, even in part, from the price of the originating transaction. **§6.10**

> **Matrix (visual):** Compare HC · FV · VIU · Fulfilment · **Current cost** on the Design card-grid above — perspective, entry/exit, teaching cues. Body keeps siblings as unordered peers; do not rebuild a second textbook matrix here. **§6.11**, **§6.12–6.22**
>
> *(Body peer-row for CV siblings still names current cost — Map CV kids currently FV/VIU/Fulfilment only; Design may add current cost on Map later.)*

**Fair value.** Market-participant assumptions, not entity-specific. May be observed in an active market or estimated (cash-flow techniques reflecting estimated flows, variations, time value, risk premium, liquidity, including counterparty / own credit risk). FV is not increased by acquisition transaction costs and does not reflect disposal/transfer transaction costs. **§6.13–6.16**

**VIU / fulfilment.** Same factor family as FV in **§6.14**, but from an entity-specific perspective. Cannot be observed directly. Include present value of expected disposal/fulfilment transaction costs; do not include acquisition transaction costs. CF identifies VIU as a **separate** measurement basis even though some Standards use it inside recoverable-amount / impairment tests — it differs conceptually from historical cost, and the Board may choose entity-specific current value instead of FV in some cases. **§6.18–6.20**, **BC6.25–BC6.26**

**Current cost.** Entry value like historical cost, but reflecting conditions at the measurement date — unlike FV / VIU / fulfilment, which are exit values. Often estimated indirectly (adjust new-asset prices for age/condition). **§6.21–6.22**

> **See also:** → **IFRS 13** *Fair Value Measurement* for the fair-value hierarchy and inputs; this chapter stays at Conceptual Framework level. **BC6.24**

> **Pitfall:** Equating “current value” with “fair value only.” VIU, fulfilment value and current cost are siblings in the family. **§6.11**

### 6.4 Information different bases provide

When selecting a basis, consider the nature of the information it will produce in **both** the statement of financial position and the statement(s) of financial performance. Relative weight of those statements depends on facts and circumstances — CF does not crown one statement primary. **§6.23**, **§6.43**, **BC6.36**

### 6.5 Selecting a basis — factors CF discusses *(not a numbered Board procedure)*

Select a measurement basis for an asset or liability **and** for related income and expenses so that the information is useful: relevant and faithfully representational, enhanced as far as possible, subject to cost. In most cases **no single factor** decides; relative importance depends on facts and circumstances. **§6.43–6.45**

**Why shaped this way — no mandatory 1→5 checklist.** The Board considered whether to prescribe an order of factors (hierarchy or decision tree) and concluded that would not be possible or desirable — several factors often matter together. CF therefore **describes bases and discusses factors**; it does not hand preparers a fixed selection sequence. Teach the factors below as CF relationships, not as Content-invented Board steps. **BC6.1**, **BC6.35**

Fundamental-then-enhancer process echoes Qualitative **§2.21**: identify the most relevant information; if it cannot be provided with faithful representation, consider the next most relevant. Enhancers and cost constrain; they do not rescue a basis that fails the fundamentals. **§6.46**, **§6.45**

Four factor clusters CF discusses — **unordered** peers (Board rejected a factor hierarchy — **BC6.35**):

**Peer card row — Selection factor clusters (unordered)**

| Relevance — characteristics | Relevance — cash-flow contribution | Faithful representation | Enhancers + cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash-flow variability; sensitivity to market / other risks. Market-sensitive items: HC may diverge sharply and delay depicting changes (e.g. amortised cost unlikely for a derivative). **§6.49–6.53** | Used in combination for goods/services → HC or current cost often relevant (margins). Produce cash directly / saleable → current value often most relevant. Contractual cash-flow management → amortised cost may fit. **§6.54–6.57** | Related-item measurement mismatches. High measurement uncertainty does **not** automatically bar a relevant basis — extreme uncertainty may tip toward another relevant basis. Distinguish measurement vs outcome vs existence uncertainty. **§6.58–6.62** | Comparability, verifiability, understandability; benefits vs costs. More bases → often less understandable totals — sometimes still justified. **§6.63–6.68** |

### 6.6 Initial vs subsequent — high-level quirks

Choose the basis considering **both** initial and subsequent measurement. If the initial basis is inconsistent with the subsequent basis, income or expenses might appear at the first subsequent measurement solely because the basis changed — depicting a transaction that did not occur. **§6.48**, **§6.78**

On market terms, cost at initial recognition is often similar to fair value at that date (unless transaction costs are significant). Still **name** which basis is used. If subsequent measurement will be historical cost, that basis is normally also appropriate initially; if subsequent will be a current value, that current value is normally also appropriate initially. **§6.78**

**Non-market / special origins.** Related-party or distressed prices, government grants / donations, liabilities imposed by legislation, or liabilities from wrongdoing may make historical cost a poor faithful representation. Deemed cost (a current value used as starting HC) may be appropriate; any difference from consideration given/received is recognised as income or expenses at initial recognition. Identify all related aspects (other assets/liabilities, contributions/distributions) so substance is depicted. **§6.6**, **§6.80–6.82**

### 6.7 Equity measurement and cash-flow techniques — containment notes (inside Measurement)

**Equity.** Total equity is **not measured directly** — it equals recognised assets minus recognised liabilities (residual). General purpose financial statements are not designed to show the value of the entity as a whole; total equity ≠ market capitalisation, sale-as-a-whole price, or asset-by-asset liquidation. Some individual equity classes or components may be measured directly; at least one cannot be (the residual). Total equity (or a class) can be negative. **§6.87–6.90**, **BC6.52–BC6.55**

**Cash-flow-based techniques.** Used to **estimate** a measure when it cannot be observed directly — not themselves measurement bases. The same technique family can support FV, fulfilment, VIU, etc., depending on which factors and whose assumptions are included. Central estimates (expected value / median-like / mode) give different information; none alone captures the price for bearing uncertainty; users may still need range information. **§6.91–6.95**, **BC6.13**

> **Pitfall:** Reading equity carrying amount as “what the company is worth,” or calling “DCF” a measurement basis. **§6.88**, **§6.91**

### 6.8 More than one basis / P&L vs OCI — thin see-also

Sometimes more than one measurement basis is needed to provide relevant information that faithfully represents both position and performance. In most cases the most understandable approach is one basis in the statements with the other in the notes — or, in some cases, a current value in the statement of financial position with a different basis for related income/expenses in the performance statement(s). Those communication choices live mainly in Presentation. **§6.83–6.86**

> **See also (one-line):** Where current-value changes appear in **P&L vs OCI** → **Presentation & disclosure** chapter. Do not expand here. **§6.85–6.86**, **§7.17–7.19**

### 6.9 Optional teaching prompts *(not a CF procedure)*

> **Label:** CF describes measurement bases and discusses factors to consider. It does **not** prescribe a fixed selection sequence, hierarchy, or decision tree for preparers (**BC6.1**, **BC6.35**). Bullet order below is Content pedagogy, not Board order. Factors are unordered teaching prompts.

- **Family cue:** Historical cost vs current value (FV · VIU/fulfilment · current cost) — not “FV only.” Techniques ≠ bases. **§6.4**, **§6.10–6.11**, **§6.91**
- **Information cue:** What would this basis say in SoFP *and* in performance? **§6.23**, **§6.43**
- **Relevance — characteristics:** Market-sensitive? Cash-flow variability? Would HC delay depicting changes users need? **§6.50–6.53**
- **Relevance — contribution:** Used in combination (margins) vs produces cash directly / saleable vs managed for contractual cash flows? **§6.54–6.57**
- **FR cue:** Related-item mismatch? Measurement uncertainty so high that another relevant basis is needed? Outcome/existence ≠ measurement uncertainty. **§6.58–6.62**
- **Enhancers + cost:** Comparability, verifiability, understandability; benefits vs costs. **§6.63–6.68**
- **Initial/subsequent cue:** Same basis family across day 1 and later? Deemed cost for non-market origins? Avoid day-2 P&L from basis flip. **§6.48**, **§6.78–6.82**
- **Standards reminder:** CF does not override measurement requirements in Standards. **SP1.2**
