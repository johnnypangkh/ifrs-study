<strong>5.0 Scope — analysis inside the operating category</strong>
<p><strong>Inside the operating category, classify and present expenses so the operating band remains a useful structured summary — by nature, by function, or mixed.</strong> IFRS 18 requires that analysis on the face of the statement of profit or loss. Any function line triggers a cost-of-sales line (when the entity has that function), a qualitative nature description per function line, and a <strong>single-note</strong> package of specified nature totals. <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para 78–85</span><span class="cite cite-comma cite-official">IFRS 18 · para B80–B85</span><span class="cite cite-comma cite-official">IFRS 18 · para IN17</span> <strong>Analysis inside the operating category sits within a wider structure and brings two major changes from IAS 1 plus the IN package. Nature and function both matter, there is no mirror package for nature presenters, and how much practice changes depends on the starting point.</strong></p>
<ul>
 <li><strong>Where this sits.</strong> Ch3 locked the five P&amp;L categories; Ch4 locked required totals and subtotals. This chapter is the analysis <em>inside</em> the operating band — after those frames exist. <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para 47</span><span class="cite cite-comma cite-official">IFRS 18 · para 69</span><span class="cite cite-comma cite-official">IFRS 18 · para 78</span></li>
 <li><strong>Two major changes from IAS 1.</strong>
 <ul>
 <li><strong>Face location.</strong> The analysis of operating expenses must be presented on the face of the income statement — the former option to disclose the analysis only in the notes is removed. <span class="cite cite-kpmg"><span class="cite-house">KPMG</span> FI 2024 · section 2.2</span></li>
 <li><strong>Mixed explicitly allowed.</strong> Entities may present by nature, by function, or a mixture of both on the face. <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para 79</span><span class="cite cite-comma cite-official">IFRS 18 · para BC252</span> <span class="cite cite-kpmg"><span class="cite-house">KPMG</span> FI 2024 · section 2.2</span> <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> Closer look 2026 · section 3.4.2 / section 3.4.2.E</span> <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 3.4.2 / section 3.4.2.E</span></li>
 </ul>
 </li>
 <li><strong>IN package.</strong> <q>HKFRS 18 requires an entity to present the operating category expenses based on their nature or their function. Entities that present expenses classified by function are required to disclose in the notes the amount of depreciation, amortisation, employee benefits, impairment losses and write-downs of inventories included in each line item in the operating category</q>. <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para IN17</span></li>
 <li><strong>Why nature and function both matter.</strong> Nature information helps users analyse components and forecast future operating expenses; function aggregation facilitates some performance metrics (including margins). In some cases, classifying some expenses by function and others by nature provides the most useful structured summary. <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para BC246–BC247</span> <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> Closer look 2026 · section 3.4.2</span> <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 3.4.2</span></li>
 <li><strong>No mirror package for nature presenters.</strong> The Board considered requiring nature presenters to disclose function information in the notes but decided not to: users showed little demand, so expected costs would exceed expected benefits. <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para BC249</span></li>
 <li><strong>How much practice changes depends on the starting point.</strong> Under IAS 1, practice varied by industry and geography, shaped by legacy GAAP, regulatory requirements and industry practice. Some entities may have been prohibited from a mixed presentation; others presented the analysis only in the notes. <span class="cite cite-kpmg"><span class="cite-house">KPMG</span> FI 2024 · section 2.2</span></li>
</ul>

<strong>5.1 Choosing nature, function or mixed — most useful structured summary</strong>
<p><strong>In the operating category, classify and present expenses in line items in a way that provides the most useful structured summary, using one or both of nature and function.</strong> <q>an entity shall classify and present expenses in line items in a way that provides the most useful structured summary of its expenses, using one or both of these characteristics</q>: (a) the nature of expenses; or (b) the function of the expenses within the entity. <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para 78</span> <strong>Each line uses one characteristic, with mixing allowed across the band, and nature expenses (paragraph 80) and function expenses (paragraph 81) are defined separately. The method is not a free choice, so entities weigh the B80 selection factors, consider when mixed may be most useful (B81) and how to label it (B82), keep consistency (B83 and paragraph 30), treat a change of method as a policy change, set the aggregation level (B85), and work without a definition of “function”.</strong></p>
<ul>
 <li><strong>One characteristic per line; mixed across the band.</strong> <q>Any individual line item shall comprise operating expenses aggregated on the basis of only one of these characteristics, but the same characteristic does not have to be used as the aggregation basis for all line items</q>. <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para 79</span><span class="cite cite-comma cite-official">IFRS 18 · para B81</span></li>
 <li><strong>Nature expenses (paragraph 80).</strong> Information about operating expenses related to the nature of the economic resources consumed to accomplish the entity’s activities <strong>without</strong> reference to the activities in relation to which those resources were consumed — e.g. raw material expense, employee benefit expense, depreciation and amortisation. <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para 80</span></li>
 <li><strong>Function expenses (paragraph 81).</strong> Allocate and aggregate operating expenses according to the activity to which the consumed resource relates. Cost of sales is a function line that may combine several natures (raw materials, employee benefits, depreciation, amortisation). Under function classification an entity might:
 <ol type="a">
 <li>Allocate expenses of the <strong>same nature</strong> (e.g. employee benefits) across <strong>several</strong> function lines (e.g. cost of sales and research and development); and</li>
 <li>Include in a <strong>single</strong> function line an allocation of expenses of <strong>several natures</strong>.</li>
 </ol>
 <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para 81</span><span class="cite cite-comma cite-official">IFRS 18 · para BC254</span>
 </li>
 <li><strong>Not a free choice.</strong> Selection must provide the most useful structured summary and may require judgement. IFRS 18 does not weight the B80 factors when they point in different directions. <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para 78</span><span class="cite cite-comma cite-official">IFRS 18 · para B80</span> <span class="cite cite-kpmg"><span class="cite-house">KPMG</span> FI 2024 · section 2.2.1</span> <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> Closer look 2026 · section 3.4.2.D / section 3.4.2.E</span> <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 3.4.2.D / section 3.4.2.E</span></li>
 <li><strong>B80 selection factors (consider all).</strong>
 <ol type="a">
 <li><strong>Main components / drivers of profitability</strong> — e.g. cost of sales may be a main driver for a retailer; nature items such as employee benefits may be more relevant for some service entities.</li>
 <li><strong>How the business is managed and reported internally</strong> — e.g. a manufacturer managed by major functions vs an entity with a single predominant function (such as providing financing to customers) that may report by nature.</li>
 <li><strong>Standard industry practice</strong> — same-industry classification aids comparability.</li>
 <li><strong>Arbitrary allocation to functions</strong> — if allocation would be arbitrary to the extent that the line items would not faithfully represent the functions, classify those expenses by nature.</li>
 </ol>
 <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para B80</span> <span class="cite cite-kpmg"><span class="cite-house">KPMG</span> FI 2024 · section 2.2.1</span> <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> Closer look 2026 · section 3.4.2.D</span> <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 3.4.2.D</span>
 </li>
 <li><strong>When mixed may be most useful (B81).</strong>
 <ul>
 <li>Function would otherwise provide the most useful summary, except for particular expenses whose allocation to functions would be arbitrary (B80(d)).</li>
 <li>An entity with two different types of main business activities may classify some expenses by function and others by nature to depict the main drivers of profitability for each activity.</li>
 </ul>
 <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para B81</span><span class="cite cite-comma cite-official">IFRS 18 · para BC250–BC252</span>
 </li>
 <li><strong>Mixed labelling (B82).</strong> If some expenses are by nature and others by function, label line items so users can clearly identify what is included. Example: if some employee benefits sit in a function line and others in a nature line, the nature label must make clear it does not include all employee benefits (e.g. “employee benefits other than those included in cost of sales”). <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para B82</span> <span class="cite cite-kpmg"><span class="cite-house">KPMG</span> FI 2024 · section 2.2.1</span> <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> Closer look 2026 · section 3.4.2.E</span> <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 3.4.2.E</span></li>
 <li><strong>Consistency (B83 and paragraph 30).</strong> Classify and present expenses consistently from period to period unless paragraph 30(a) or 30(b) applies. A goodwill impairment presented as a nature line in one period stays a nature line in subsequent periods (unless paragraph 30 applies); a nil amount in a later period is not a change in classification. <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para B83</span><span class="cite cite-comma cite-official">IFRS 18 · para 30</span> <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> Closer look 2026 · section 3.4.2</span> <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 3.4.2</span></li>
 <li><strong>Changing the method is a policy change.</strong> KPMG reads a change in the classification and presentation of expenses in the income statement as a change in accounting policy — e.g. goodwill impairment presented as a nature line stays a nature line unless the entity changes its accounting policy. <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para 30</span><span class="cite cite-comma cite-official">IFRS 18 · para B83</span> <span class="cite cite-kpmg"><span class="cite-house">KPMG</span> FI 2024 · section 2.2.3</span></li>
 <li><strong>Aggregation level (B85).</strong> Consider what level of aggregation provides the most useful structured summary — e.g. administrative activities (HR, IT, legal, accounting) may share a characteristic and be labelled “administrative expenses”; selling expenses may be sufficiently dissimilar to need a separate line. EY adds that the general aggregation requirements still apply, so some items allocated to different functions or natures may need to be aggregated (<a href="ch02.html#s-2-3-5">Ch2 2.3.5</a>). <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para B85</span><span class="cite cite-comma cite-official">IFRS 18 · para BC254</span> <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> Closer look 2026 · section 3.4.2.D</span> <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 3.4.2.D</span></li>
 <li><strong>No definition of “function”.</strong> IFRS 18 does not define “function”; the concept is carried from IAS 1 and the entity applies its own definition consistently. The Board judged a practicable definition and allocation guidance too difficult to develop; EY calls function a longstanding concept that is widely understood. Guidance focuses on aggregation level (B85) rather than a dictionary definition. <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para BC253</span><span class="cite cite-comma cite-official">IFRS 18 · para B85</span> <span class="cite cite-kpmg"><span class="cite-house">KPMG</span> FI 2024 · section 2.2.2</span> <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> Closer look 2026 · section 3.4.2.B</span></li>
</ul>

<strong>5.2 Function path — cost of sales + qualitative description</strong>
<p><strong>If any operating line presents expenses classified by function, apply the paragraph 82 face and qualitative package.</strong> <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para 82</span> <strong>Function presenters show a separate cost-of-sales line when applicable, although “cost of sales” has no definition. The floor is not costly, scope stays judgemental, each function line needs a qualitative description of natures, the path links to gross profit, and the Big4 align on it.</strong></p>
<ul>
 <li><strong>Separate cost-of-sales line (when applicable).</strong> Present a separate line item for cost of sales if the entity classifies operating expenses in functions that include a cost-of-sales function. That line item shall include the total of inventory expense described in IAS 2.38. The requirement builds on IAS 1; cost of sales is kept apart from other function lines because it facilitates metrics such as gross margins. <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para 82(a)</span><span class="cite cite-comma cite-official">IFRS 18 · para BC255</span><span class="cite cite-comma cite-official">IFRS 18 · para BC257</span></li>
 <li><strong>No definition of “cost of sales”.</strong> The Board acknowledged diversity in what entities put in cost of sales but decided not to define the term: it could not be done within a reasonable time frame, the topic ranked low in the Third Agenda Consultation, and a definition could have unintended IAS 2 consequences. The IAS 2.38 inventory-expense inclusion is the comparability floor — some entities had previously excluded components of inventory expense from cost of sales. <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para BC256–BC257</span> <span class="cite cite-kpmg"><span class="cite-house">KPMG</span> FI 2024 · section 2.2</span> <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> Closer look 2026 · section 3.4.2.C</span> <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 3.4.2.C</span></li>
 <li><strong>Why the floor is not costly.</strong> IAS 2 already requires an entity to recognise inventories as an expense when the related revenue is recognised and to disclose the amount so recognised. <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para BC257(a)–(b)</span></li>
 <li><strong>Scope stays judgemental.</strong> EY view: without a definition, entities still consider facts and circumstances for items such as shipping and handling costs and write-offs of inventory and PPE. <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> Closer look 2026 · section 3.4.2.C</span> <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 3.4.2.C</span></li>
 <li><strong>Qualitative description of natures in each function line.</strong> Disclose a qualitative description of the nature of expenses included in each function line item — to enhance understandability of composition and help users assess comparability of cost of sales across entities. Nature lines need no such description because their composition is typically clear, except “other” expenses (<a href="ch02.html#s-2-4">Ch2 2.4</a>). <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para 82(b)</span><span class="cite cite-comma cite-official">IFRS 18 · para BC258</span> <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> Closer look 2026 · section 3.4.2.B</span> <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 3.4.2.B</span></li>
 <li><strong>Gross profit link.</strong> A cost-of-sales line supports the gross profit subtotal, which paragraph 118 lists as not an MPM (<a href="ch04.html#s-4-4-2">Ch4 4.4.2</a>). <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para 118(a)</span></li>
 <li><strong>Big4 alignment.</strong> KPMG and EY both stress: any function (or mixed-with-function) face presentation requires separate cost of sales when that function exists, plus qualitative natures per function line; cost of sales is undefined beyond the IAS 2.38 inventory-expense inclusion. <span class="cite cite-kpmg"><span class="cite-house">KPMG</span> FI 2024 · section 2.2 / section 2.2.2</span> <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> Closer look 2026 · section 3.4.2.B / section 3.4.2.C</span></li>
</ul>
<aside class="point-chip warn"><strong>Pitfall:</strong> Treating cost of sales as an optional label when the entity’s functions include a cost-of-sales function. Paragraph 82(a) requires a separate line in that case, and that line must include IAS 2.38 inventory expense. <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para 82(a)</span><span class="cite cite-comma cite-official">IFRS 18 · para BC255</span><span class="cite cite-comma cite-official">IFRS 18 · para BC257</span></aside>

<strong>5.3 Function path — para 83 single-note nature totals</strong>
<p><strong>An entity that presents one or more function line items in the operating category shall also disclose, in a single note, specified nature totals and how they relate to operating (and any non-operating) lines.</strong> Function on the face does not hide the nature lens investors need. <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para 83</span><span class="cite cite-comma cite-official">IFRS 18 · para IN17</span><span class="cite cite-comma cite-official">IFRS 18 · para BC259–BC260</span> <strong>The paragraph 83 note exists because function presentation aggregates natures that respond differently to the economic environment and users need cash-flow links, and it requires five specified nature totals (paragraph 83(a)) with set disclosures for each (paragraph 83(b)). Users gain from it, the five were chosen because they are generally useful and not too costly, it is a single note, amounts need not equal period expense (B84), the five reflect a cost–benefit design, systems are affected, a pure nature face needs no paragraph 83 note, the Big4 align, and function lines required by another Standard are covered.</strong></p>
<ul>
 <li><strong>Why the note exists.</strong> Function presentation aggregates natures that respond differently to the economic environment, which can impair forecasting. Users also need links to cash-flow information. IAS 1 already required some nature disclosures for function presenters, but practice was thin — IFRS 18 strengthens the package. <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para BC260–BC261</span></li>
 <li><strong>Five specified nature totals (paragraph 83(a)).</strong> Disclose the total for each of:
 <ol type="i">
 <li><strong>Depreciation</strong> — amounts required by IAS 16.73(e)(vii), IAS 40.79(d)(iv) and IFRS 16.53(a);</li>
 <li><strong>Amortisation</strong> — amount required by IAS 38.118(e)(vi);</li>
 <li><strong>Employee benefits</strong> — amounts under IAS 19 plus services received from employees under IFRS 2;</li>
 <li><strong>Impairment losses and reversals</strong> — amounts required by IAS 36.126(a)–(b); and</li>
 <li><strong>Write-downs and reversals of inventories</strong> — amounts required by IAS 2.36(e)–(f).</li>
 </ol>
 <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para 83(a)</span>
 </li>
 <li><strong>For each of those totals (paragraph 83(b)).</strong>
 <ol type="i">
 <li>The amount related to each line item in the operating category (see B84); and</li>
 <li>A list of any line items <strong>outside</strong> the operating category that also include amounts relating to the total — explaining why the total differs from the operating-category amounts.</li>
 </ol>
 <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para 83(b)</span><span class="cite cite-comma cite-official">IFRS 18 · para BC268</span>
 </li>
 <li><strong>What users gain.</strong> The split shows what sits in each operating line (useful for margin analysis), how the primary statements relate to the notes, and how non-cash items in operating lines relate to the statement of cash flows (<a href="ch07.html#s-7-1">Ch7 7.1</a>). <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para BC265(b)(i)–(iii)</span></li>
 <li><strong>Why these five.</strong> Users said employee benefits, depreciation and amortisation by line are generally useful in any industry; impairments and inventory write-downs are useful partly because they are non-cash. Preparers said these would not be too costly. <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para BC266</span></li>
 <li><strong>Single note.</strong> Put the package in one note so users can locate and analyse operating-expense nature information together. <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para 83</span><span class="cite cite-comma cite-official">IFRS 18 · para BC268(a)</span> <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> Closer look 2026 · section 3.4.2.B</span> <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 3.4.2.B</span></li>
 <li><strong>Amounts need not equal period expense (B84).</strong> Presented or disclosed nature amounts need not be the amounts recognised as expense in the period — they may include amounts recognised as part of an asset’s carrying amount. If paragraph 83(b) disclosures include capitalised amounts, give a qualitative explanation identifying the assets involved. (If nature amounts are presented on the face and are not period expense, also present an additional line for the change in carrying amount of the affected assets — e.g. changes in inventories of finished goods and work in progress under IAS 2.39; <a href="#s-5-1-ie10">Illustration 5.1.4</a>.) <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para B84</span><span class="cite cite-comma cite-official">IFRS 18 · para BC269–BC271</span> <span class="cite cite-kpmg"><span class="cite-house">KPMG</span> FI 2024 · section 2.2.2</span> <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> Closer look 2026 · section 3.4.2.A / section 3.4.2.B</span> <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 3.4.2.A / section 3.4.2.B</span></li>
 <li><strong>Cost–benefit design of the five.</strong> The Board rejected full function-line-by-nature analyses and full total-opex-by-nature analyses as too costly for many preparers; the five specified natures struck the cost–benefit balance users and preparers described. Adding further natures would tip that balance (<a href="#s-5-3-3">5.3.3</a>). <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para BC262–BC267</span></li>
 <li><strong>Systems impact.</strong> EY view: the five-item requirement could require added system functionality. IAS 1.104 and current practice already require depreciation, amortisation and employee benefits, but IFRS 18 adds specifically identified expenses and, importantly, the function line in which each sits. <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> Closer look 2026 · section 3.4.2.B</span></li>
 <li><strong>Pure nature face → no paragraph 83 note.</strong> If all operating expense lines are presented by nature, the natures are already on the face and the paragraph 83 single-note package is not required. <span class="cite cite-kpmg"><span class="cite-house">KPMG</span> FI 2024 · section 2.2</span></li>
 <li><strong>Big4 alignment.</strong> KPMG and EY both map the same five natures, the operating-line split, the outside-operating list, the single-note location, and the B84 capitalisation qualitative. <span class="cite cite-kpmg"><span class="cite-house">KPMG</span> FI 2024 · section 2.2.2</span> <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> Closer look 2026 · section 3.4.2.B</span></li>
 <li><strong>Function lines required by another Standard.</strong> Paragraph 83 applies to all operating expenses presented by function, including when another IFRS requires a function line (e.g. IFRS 17 insurance service expenses; <a href="ch03.html#s-3-5-insurers">Ch3 3.5.19</a>). The IFRS Interpretations Committee (March 2026) observed that paragraph 83 has no exception based on why the expense is classified by function. <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> Closer look 2026 · section 3.4.2.B · Q3-43</span></li>
</ul>

<strong>5.4 Para 84 exemption — narrow relief, not a general waiver</strong>
<p><strong>Applying paragraph 83 exempts certain further nature↔function disaggregations beyond the paragraph 83 amounts — but does not waive other specific disclosure requirements in IFRS Accounting Standards, and does not erase paragraph 41 materiality generally outside that narrow package.</strong> <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para 84–85</span><span class="cite cite-comma cite-official">IFRS 18 · para 41</span> <strong>Paragraph 84 grants a narrow exemption, which the Board gave to protect the cost–benefit balance of the five specified natures, with no threshold and in all circumstances. Other IFRS disclosures still apply under paragraph 85, voluntary extra natures are still allowed, and the Big4 align.</strong></p>
<ul>
 <li><strong>What paragraph 84 exempts.</strong> <q>Paragraph 41 requires an entity to disaggregate items to provide material information. However, an entity that applies paragraph 83 is exempt from disclosing</q>:
 <ol type="a">
 <li>In relation to function line items in the operating category — disaggregated information about the amounts of nature expenses included in each line item, <strong>beyond</strong> the amounts specified in paragraph 83; and</li>
 <li>In relation to nature expenses specifically required by an IFRS Accounting Standard to be disclosed in the notes — disaggregated information about the amounts of those expenses included in each function line item in the operating category, <strong>beyond</strong> the amounts specified in paragraph 83.</li>
 </ol>
 <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para 84</span>
 </li>
 <li><strong>Why the Board gave the exemption.</strong> Without it, the general disaggregation requirement would push entities toward disclosing further material natures inside function lines, upsetting the cost–benefit balance struck for the five specified natures. The Board deliberately did not insert an “undue cost or effort” threshold, because a threshold might not change outcomes and might be difficult to apply. <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para BC272–BC274</span></li>
 <li><strong>No threshold, all circumstances.</strong> EY view: limiting the requirement to five natures may appear to conflict with paragraph 41, since other natures inside function lines could be individually material; but the exemption has no threshold and applies in all circumstances. <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> Closer look 2026 · section 3.4.2.B</span></li>
 <li><strong>Paragraph 85 — other IFRS disclosures still apply.</strong> <q>The exemption in paragraph 84 relates to disaggregation of operating expenses. However, it does not exempt an entity from applying specific disclosure requirements relating to those expenses in HKFRS Accounting Standards.</q> <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para 85</span></li>
 <li><strong>Voluntary extra natures still OK.</strong> The Board did not intend to prevent entities that already disclose more natures than the five from continuing to do so. <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para BC275</span></li>
 <li><strong>Big4 alignment.</strong> KPMG and EY both describe paragraph 84 as a narrow exemption tied to the paragraph 83 five-nature package: function presenters are not forced to disaggregate every other nature inside function lines, but other Standards’ specific disclosures still bind. <span class="cite cite-kpmg"><span class="cite-house">KPMG</span> FI 2024 · section 2.2.2</span> <span class="cite cite-ey"><span class="cite-house">EY</span> Closer look 2026 · section 3.4.2.B</span></li>
</ul>

<p class="purpose">Densify from PwC Manual of Accounting 2026 Chapter 18 (Presentation and disclosure in financial statements). Short paraphrase + cite; bodies from SoT FAQ/EX PDFs. <span class="cite cite-pwc"><span class="cite-house">PwC</span> MOA 2026 Ch18</span></p>

<h4 id="s-5-pwc-faq-18941"><strong>5.5.1 — Can interest income earned on a receivable be presented as revenue?</strong></h4>
<p><strong>Can interest income earned on a receivable be presented as revenue?</strong> <span class="cite cite-pwc"><span class="cite-house">PwC</span> MOA 2026 Ch18 · FAQ 18.94.1</span> <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para 75</span> <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 15 · para 65</span></p>
<ul>
  <li>Interest income should be included within ‘revenue’ where earning interest income is part of the entity’s ordinary activities, rather than an incidental benefit.</li>
  <li>Paragraph 75 of IFRS 18 requires an entity to present revenue, presenting separately interest revenue calculated using the effective interest method.</li>
  <li>However, separate presentation is only necessary where this provides a useful structured summary.</li>
  <li>If the entity aggregates interest revenue with revenue from contracts with customers, it would not be appropriate to label the line item ‘Revenue from contracts with customers’.</li>
  <li>IFRS 15 specifically states that an entity should present the effects of financing (interest revenue or interest expense) separately from revenue from contracts with customers in the statement of comprehensive income.</li>
  <li>If separate presentation is not necessary for a useful structured summary, the interest income should be separately disclosed in the notes (if material).</li>
  <li>If interest income is incidental and not presented as revenue, it could be presented separately or aggregated with other items with similar characteristics (see para 8.40).</li>
</ul>

<h4 id="s-5-pwc-faq-18942"><strong>5.5.2 — How should negative interest be presented?</strong></h4>
<p><strong>How should negative interest be presented?</strong> <span class="cite cite-pwc"><span class="cite-house">PwC</span> MOA 2026 Ch18 · FAQ 18.94.2</span> <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · paras 58, 61, 65, 75, para B45</span></p>
<ul>
  <li>The classification considerations are the same, whether the return or expense is positive or negative.</li>
  <li>An entity’s classification of negative effective interest on a financial asset in either the investing or operating category depends on whether the financial asset generates a return individually and largely independently of the entity’s other resources, and whether the entity invests in the financial asset as a main business activity.</li>
  <li>An entity‘s classification of negative effective interest arising on a financial liability in either the financing or operating category depends on whether the entity provides financing to customers as a main business activity, whether the financial liability arises from a transaction that only involves the raising of finance, and whether the financial liability relates to providing financing to customers.</li>
  <li>Negative interest income or expense could be presented separately on the face of the statement of profit or loss if it is necessary to provide a useful structured summary (see para 18.31) or, if material, it should be disclosed separately in the notes to the financial statements (see para 18.32).</li>
</ul>

<h4 id="s-5-pwc-faq-18971"><strong>5.5.3 — Is the decision to present line items based on their function, nature, or a mix of function and…</strong></h4>
<p><strong>Is the decision to present line items based on their function, nature, or a mix of function and…</strong> <span class="cite cite-pwc"><span class="cite-house">PwC</span> MOA 2026 Ch18 · FAQ 18.97.1</span> <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · paras 78, 79</span> <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para B80</span></p>
<ul>
  <li>The choice of whether line items in the operating category are presented based on their nature or function is not a free choice.</li>
  <li>An entity needs to consider what characteristic results in line items in the operating category that provide the most useful structured summary.</li>
  <li>Examples of considerations related to achieving the most useful structured summary are set out below: a.</li>
  <li>Information about the main components or drivers of the entity’s profitability For a retailer, presenting operating expenses by function might be more relevant, since the line item that provides the most useful information about the main components or drivers might be the cost of sales.</li>
  <li>The cost of sales line item provides relevant information about whether the revenue generated from the sale of goods covers mainly direct costs, and by what margin.</li>
  <li>For some service entities, presenting operating expenses by nature might be more relevant to users of financial statements, since these are the main drivers of profitability.</li>
  <li>For these entities, the link between revenue and costs might be less direct, and therefore cost of sales is unlikely to provide relevant information about the important components or drivers of profitability. b.</li>
  <li>Close representation of the way the business is managed and how management reports internally For a manufacturing entity, presenting operating expenses by function might closely represent the way in which the business is managed based on major functions for internal reporting purposes.</li>
  <li>However, for entities that provide financing to customers that have a single predominant function, presenting operating expenses by nature might provide the most useful information for internal reporting purposes.</li>
</ul>

<h4 id="s-5-pwc-faq-18992"><strong>5.5.4 — What types of expenses are included in cost of sales, distribution costs, administrative expenses…</strong></h4>
<p><strong>What types of expenses are included in cost of sales, distribution costs, administrative expenses…</strong> <span class="cite cite-pwc"><span class="cite-house">PwC</span> MOA 2026 Ch18 · FAQ 18.99.2</span> <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · paras 81, 82(a)</span> <span class="cite cite-official">IAS 2 · paras 34, 38</span></p>
<ul>
  <li>Paragraph 81 of IFRS 18 provides guidance when presenting certain expenses by function in the operating category of the statement of profit and loss.</li>
  <li>In this case, IFRS 18 requires operating expenses to be aggregated according to the activity (that is, the process) that consumed the economic resources.</li>
  <li>IFRS 18 does not define what a ‘function’ is.</li>
  <li>Examples of typical functions include sales, distribution, marketing, administration, or research and development.</li>
  <li>The functional costs would then be the costs relating to those activities or processes which consumed the economic resources (for example, cost of sales for revenue-generating activities).</li>
  <li>An entity will need to apply judgement in defining the ‘functions’ appropriate to its business and in ensuring that the objective of providing a useful structured summary to users of financial statements will be achieved.</li>
  <li>Some examples of what might be included in cost of sales, distribution costs, administrative expenses and sales and marketing expenses are included below.</li>
  <li>Cost of sales IFRS 18 does not provide a definition of cost of sales, but it requires, at a minimum, the total inventory expense described in IAS 2 to be included in cost of sales.</li>
  <li>IFRS 18 sets out an example that states that ‘cost of sales’ is a function line item that combines expenses relating to an entity’s production or other revenue-generating activities, such as raw materials, employee benefit expense, depreciation and amortisation.</li>
  <li>Cost of sales must include the following: The total inventory expense described in paragraph 38 of IAS 2.</li>
  <li>This includes unallocated production overheads and abnormal amounts of production costs of inventories.</li>
  <li>Costs directly associated with fulfilling performance obligations under IFRS 15: if these costs have not been previously included in the measurement of inventory (for example, for an entity that provides services): direct labour; direct materials; other external charges (such as the hire of equipment or the cost of casual labour required to deliver services); and all direct overheads, including depreciation of property, plant and equipment, and indirect overheads that can reasonably be allocated to satisfying the performance obligation; depending on the circumstances of the entity, other amounts, such as distribution costs, might also be included in ‘cost of sales’; for example, transport costs where the company has concluded that part of its performance obligation is the physical transportation of goods to the customer, or where it has concluded that the delivery services are a separate performance obligation; and advertising costs related to delivering advertising services to a customer in accordance with IFRS 15.</li>
</ul>

<h4 id="s-5-pwc-faq-181001"><strong>5.5.5 — For entities that classify operating expenses by function, does the disclosure of the nature of…</strong></h4>
<p><strong>For entities that classify operating expenses by function, does the disclosure of the nature of…</strong> <span class="cite cite-pwc"><span class="cite-house">PwC</span> MOA 2026 Ch18 · FAQ 18.100.1</span> <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · paras 83(b), BC270, para B84</span></p>

  <li>An entity can disclose either the cost of the nature item consumed by the function during the period or the amount of the nature item that was recognised as an expense in profit or loss during the period.</li>

<h4 id="s-5-pwc-faq-181021"><strong>5.5.6 — In what circumstances can an entity present some expenses by function and others by nature?</strong></h4>
<p><strong>In what circumstances can an entity present some expenses by function and others by nature?</strong> <span class="cite cite-pwc"><span class="cite-house">PwC</span> MOA 2026 Ch18 · FAQ 18.102.1</span> <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · paras BC251, paras B80, B82</span></p>
<ul>
  <li>Entities are not prohibited from mixing the ‘by function’ and ‘by nature’ classifications of expenses if doing so provides the most useful structured summary of the statement of profit or loss.</li>
  <li>However, this is not a free choice – an entity will need to present line items that provide the most useful structured summary for users.</li>
  <li>Where an entity is considering mixing function and nature, judgement will often be required to ensure that the outcome provides the most useful structured summary of the statement of profit or loss.</li>
  <li>Examples of the circumstances in which the mixed presentation might be appropriate include: a.</li>
  <li>An entity has two different types of main business activities and determines that this presentation provides information about the main drivers of its profitability. b.</li>
  <li>A manufacturing entity that recognised a substantive impairment of property, plant and equipment might present the impairment expense separately in the statement of profit or loss even if this impairment related to the production activities of the entity. c.</li>
  <li>An entity that classifies its expenses by function and that has operating expenses that are difficult to allocate to functions in a non-arbitrary way, such as a substantive impairment of goodwill which does not clearly support a single activity or process.</li>
  <li>Entities using the mixed presentation should disclose the breakdown of expenses by nature in the notes (see para 18.100).</li>
  <li>The presentation should be applied consistently and explained in the accounting policies.</li>
</ul>

<h4 id="s-5-pwc-faq-181042"><strong>5.5.7 — How should an entity disclose costs for directors and key management that do not have employment…</strong></h4>
<p><strong>How should an entity disclose costs for directors and key management that do not have employment…</strong> <span class="cite cite-pwc"><span class="cite-house">PwC</span> MOA 2026 Ch18 · FAQ 18.104.2</span> <span class="cite cite-official">IFRS 18 · para 82</span> <span class="cite cite-official">IAS 24 · para 17</span></p>
<ul>
  <li>Directors and other key management personnel (as defined in IAS 24) might not have employment contracts with the entity.</li>
  <li>This might be the case where, for example, the entity engages a non-executive director and pays their ‘service entity’ for their services, the director providing similar services to a number of companies.</li>
  <li>Employee benefits are defined in IAS 19; for the purposes of IAS 19, employees include directors and other management personnel. [IAS 19 para 7].</li>
  <li>This could be interpreted as requiring all costs of engaging directors and key management, whether under employment contracts or otherwise, to be included in employee benefits expense.</li>
  <li>The most important consideration is the transparency of the disclosure and its consistency from year to year.</li>
  <li>Paragraphs 17 and 18 of IAS 24 require additional disclosure in respect of directors and key management.</li>
  <li>Payments made to directors and key management should be disclosed in the notes, regardless of the disclosure within employee benefits expense.</li>
</ul>
