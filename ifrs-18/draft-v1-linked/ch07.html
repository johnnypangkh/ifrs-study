7.0 Scope — consequential amendments beyond the statement of profit or losstop

IFRS 18 makes limited changes to the statement of cash flows (through IAS 7) and the statement of financial position, amends IAS 33 and IAS 34, and carries the capital disclosures forward from IAS 1. Figure 1 places the statement of cash flows and the statement of financial position under “limited changes to specific requirements” and the statement presenting comprehensive income and the statement of changes in equity under “no changes”.   • Amendments apply with IFRS 18. 7.1 Statement of cash flows — operating profit start and interest and dividend classificationtop

Amended IAS 7 requires all entities using the indirect method to start operating cash flows from the operating profit or loss subtotal (IAS 7 paragraph 18(b)). The Board wanted to reduce diversity in practice: a common starting point makes statements of cash flows more consistent, helps investors compare operating cash flows, and removes some reconciling items used today. IAS 7 as amended IFRS 18 · para 70 EY Closer look 2026 · section 5.1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 5.1 KPMG FI 2024 · section 5.1 The points below are Standard bridge., Impact on most entities., Interest and dividends., P&L categories are not IAS 7 activities., and IE coverage.

  • Standard bridge. Operating profit (paragraphs 69–70) is the defined operations subtotal, so opening the statement of cash flows from it matches the P&L operating band. IFRS 18 · para 69–70
  • Impact on most entities. Pre-amendment IAS 7 required “profit or loss” as the starting point without defining it, so entities start from profit before tax or profit after tax. EY’s view: the change will affect most, if not all, entities. EY Closer look 2026 · section 5.1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 5.1
  • Interest and dividends. The amendments remove the classification options for interest and dividend cash flows, because today’s mixed classifications do not necessarily show the role of interest and dividends in an entity’s business. Dividends paid are financing for all entities, as a cost of obtaining financing (IAS 7 paragraph 33A); see 7.1.1 and 7.1.2. KPMG FI 2024 · section 5.1 EY Closer look 2026 · section 5.1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 5.1 KPMG FI 2024 · section 5.1.1
  • P&L categories are not IAS 7 activities. The IFRS 18 categories and the IAS 7 activities have different definitions: the Board put each statement’s objective ahead of aligning them. For example, cash from selling property, plant and equipment is an investing cash flow, but the gain or loss is in the operating category because the asset is used with the entity’s other resources and does not generate a return individually. IFRS 18 · para BC86 KPMG FI 2024 · section 2.1.2.2 EY Closer look 2026 · section 5.1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 5.1
  • IE coverage. IE Part I does not illustrate a statement of cash flows; the worked statement in Illustration 7.1.3 is EY’s, based on Illustrative Example A accompanying amended IAS 7. IFRS 18 · para IE6(a) EY Closer look 2026 · section 5.1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 5.1

7.1.2 — Specified main business activities — one activity per cash flow type

The points below are Follow the P&L., Assessed separately., Income or expense split across P&L categories., and Dividends paid.

  • Follow the P&L. An entity that invests in assets or provides financing to customers as a main business activity classifies the total of interest paid, the total of interest received and the total of dividends received each in a single activity, matching how the related expense or income is classified in the P&L (IAS 7 paragraphs 34B–34C). EY Closer look 2026 · section 5.1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 5.1 KPMG FI 2024 · section 5.1.1
  • Assessed separately. The three flows do not have to land in the same activity: an entity investing in financial assets can classify all dividends received in operating (matching dividend income) and all interest paid in financing (matching interest expense). 7.2 Statement of financial position — limited changes and the structured-summary lenstop

Current and non-current classification and the minimum line items are largely carried forward from IAS 1; IFRS 18 adds a goodwill line item and requires nature and function to drive SFP aggregation. Paragraph 96 requires separate current and non-current classifications unless a liquidity-based presentation gives a more useful structured summary, in which case all assets and liabilities are presented in order of liquidity.   • XYZ conclusion (IE7). A statement of financial position distinguishing current from non-current items provides the most useful structured summary of its assets and liabilities. IFRS 18 · para IE7(c) IFRS 18 · para 96

Assets and liabilities are aggregated and disaggregated using nature and function; other characteristics (duration, liquidity, measurement basis, type, tax effects) help identify them. 7.3 EPS — permitted numerators for additional amounts per sharetop

Amended IAS 33 still permits additional amounts per share, but only when the numerator is a total or subtotal identified in IFRS 18 (paragraphs 69, 86 and 118) or an MPM, attributable to ordinary equity holders of the parent (IAS 33 paragraph 73B). The IASB specified the numerators so that users get the same information about additional per-share amounts as they get about MPMs. IAS 33 as amended EY Closer look 2026 · section 5.3 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 5.3 KPMG FI 2024 · section 5.3 The points below are Same denominator, equal prominence., Notes only., MPM numerator brings MPM disclosures., Non-compliant numerator., Local law or regulation., and What changed.

  • Same denominator, equal prominence. Additional basic and diluted amounts use the weighted-average number of ordinary shares determined under IAS 33 and are disclosed with equal prominence (IAS 33 paragraph 73C(a)–(b)). EY Closer look 2026 · section 5.3 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 5.3 KPMG FI 2024 · section 5.3
  • Notes only. Additional amounts per share cannot be presented in a primary financial statement (IAS 33 paragraph 73C(c)), because they need extra information and reconciliations to the presented measures to be understood. EY Closer look 2026 · section 5.3 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 5.3 KPMG FI 2024 · section 5.3
  • MPM numerator brings MPM disclosures. If the numerator is an MPM, the paragraph 121–125 disclosures apply to it (IAS 33 paragraph 73C(d)); see Ch6 6.4. IFRS 18 · para 121–125 EY Closer look 2026 · section 5.3 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 5.3

IFRS 18 does not apply to condensed interim financial statements under IAS 34, except that paragraphs 41–45 (aggregation) and 117–125 (MPMs) apply to them. First-year interim headings, subtotals and reconciliations are in Ch8 8.4. 7.5 Capital disclosures — paragraphs 126–129 and IE Part IIItop

An entity discloses information that enables users to evaluate its objectives, policies and processes for managing capital, based on information provided internally to key management personnel. These requirements are carried forward from IAS 1. The IASB put them in IAS 1 rather than IFRS 7 because capital does not relate solely to financial instruments; they are illustrated by EE Group and FF Group in IE Part III. IFRS 18 · para IE14–IE17 IFRS 18 · para 126–128 IFRS 18 · para BCZ394 The points below are All entities., What paragraph 127 requires., Capital need not equal equity., Separate requirements., and Discussion first.

  • All entities. Capital information is useful for all entities, not only those with external requirements: the level of capital and how it is managed affect the risk profile, resilience to unexpected adverse events and the ability to pay dividends.   • Capital need not equal equity. Some entities include some financial liabilities (such as subordinated debt) in capital; others exclude some equity components (such as cash flow hedge amounts). IFRS 18 · para 127 IFRS 18 · para BCZ397

Illustration: 7.5.2 — EE Group — debt-to-adjusted capital ratio IFRS 18 · para IE14 IFRS 18 · para IE15 IFRS 18 · para 126–128

Facts

  • EE Group (IE14–IE15) is not a financial institution and is not subject to externally imposed capital requirements. It manufactures and sells cars and has a finance subsidiary that finances customers, mainly through leases.
  • Objectives: safeguard the ability to continue as a going concern, so it can keep providing returns for shareholders and benefits for other stakeholders; and provide an adequate return to shareholders by pricing products and services in line with risk.
  • Policy: capital is set in proportion to risk. To maintain or adjust it, EE may change dividends, return capital, issue new shares or sell assets to reduce debt.
  • Metric (consistent with others in the industry): debt-to-adjusted capital = net debt ÷ adjusted capital. Net debt = total debt (per the SFP) minus cash and cash equivalents. Adjusted capital = all equity components (share capital, share premium, NCI, retained earnings, revaluation surplus) other than cash flow hedge amounts, plus some subordinated debt.
  • Strategy, unchanged from 20X1: keep the ratio at the lower end of 6:1 to 7:1 to secure finance at reasonable cost by maintaining a BB credit rating.
  • The ratio fell in 20X2 mainly because net debt dropped on the sale of Subsidiary X. With improved profitability and lower managed receivables, the dividend rose to CU2.8 million (20X1: CU2.5 million).

Assessment

  • The paragraphs 126–128 package covers objectives, policies and processes; summary quantitative data; changes from the prior period; and compliance with any externally imposed requirements.
  • EE decides how much detail to give in its circumstances and also considers the IAS 7 paragraphs 44A–44E disclosure requirements. Another entity might monitor capital differently.
  • The table shows each component of net debt and adjusted capital, so users can see what EE manages as capital.
  • For the paragraph 127(e) non-compliance disclosure, see Illustration 7.5.3 (FF Group).

Decision

  • Disclose objectives, policy, metric definition, the unchanged strategy, the ratio table (6.6 at 20X2 vs 6.9 at 20X1) and the reasons for the change.
  • The IE disclosure has no paragraph 127(d)–(e) compliance statement; EE is not subject to externally imposed capital requirements.

Illustration: 7.5.3 — FF Group — non-compliance with externally imposed capital requirements IFRS 18 · para IE16 IFRS 18 · para IE17 IFRS 18 · para 127(e) IFRS 18 · para 126–128

Facts

  • IE16–IE17 illustrate paragraph 127(e) only (non-compliance with externally imposed capital requirements during the period); the other paragraphs 126–128 disclosures are not shown.
  • FF Group provides financing to its customers and is subject to capital requirements imposed by Regulator X. It did not comply during the year ended 31 December 20X2.
  • FF filed its quarterly regulatory capital return for 30 September 20X2 on 20 October 20X2; at that date its regulatory capital was CU1.0 million below the requirement.
  • FF had to submit a plan to Regulator X: sell part of its investment portfolio (carrying amount CU11.5 million) in Q4 20X2. In Q4 it sold its fixed-interest investment portfolio for CU12.6 million and met the requirement.

Assessment

  • Paragraph 127(e) requires disclosure of the consequences of non-compliance; here, the requirement to submit a remediation plan to Regulator X.
  • Restoring compliance in Q4 does not remove the disclosure: there is no exemption for temporary non-compliance during the year. IFRS 18 · para BCZ403
  • The disclosure is factual (shortfall, regulator response, plan, remediation) and does not replace the full paragraphs 126–128 package.

Decision

  • In the 20X2 financial statements, disclose the CU1.0 million shortfall at the 20 October filing, the required plan, the planned sale (CU11.5 million carrying amount) and the actual sale (CU12.6 million) that restored compliance.
