4.0 Scope — required totals vs para 118-listed vs MPMtop

Once the five P&L categories exist (Ch3), lock the required totals and subtotals on the statement of profit or loss — and keep them separate from paragraph 118-listed (non-MPM) subtotals and from MPMs (Ch6). IFRS 18 requires presentation of operating profit or loss, profit or loss before financing and income taxes (subject to paragraph 73), and profit or loss. Separately, paragraph 118 lists commonly used income-and-expense subtotals that are not management-defined performance measures. MPMs follow a different disclosure regime. IFRS 18 · para 69–74IFRS 18 · para 117–118IFRS 18 · para IN8IFRS 18 · para IN14

  • First decision — which required anchors? Present the three paragraph 69 measures on the face (with the paragraph 73 exception for the middle subtotal). IFRS 18 · para 69–72
  • Second decision — paragraph 118-listed or MPM? A useful subtotal of income and expenses may be IFRS-recognised under paragraph 118 (not an MPM) or may meet the paragraph 117 MPM definition and trigger the single-note reconciliation regime (Ch6). IFRS 18 · para 117–118
  • Third decision — additional face subtotals? Additional PFS subtotals are allowed only when necessary for a useful structured summary and must stay compatible with the category structure and required anchors. IFRS 18 · para 24IFRS 18 · para B9
  • Fourth decision — which line items? Paragraph 75 lists the line items to present, filtered by the useful-structured-summary test; the same listed line item can appear in more than one category (4.3). IFRS 18 · para 75IFRS 18 · para B77
  • Four building blocks. KPMG frames the topic as two new required profit subtotals, enhanced guidance on additional subtotals, the commonly presented subtotals listed in paragraph 118, and MPMs that are subtotals of income and expenses. KPMG FI 2024 · section 2.3
Pitfall: Equating “gross profit” (or similar) with an MPM. Gross profit or loss and the other paragraph 118 items are listed as not management-defined performance measures. IFRS 18 · para 118(a)IFRS 18 · para B123

4.1 Required presentation — three anchorstop

Present three totals/subtotals in the statement of profit or loss. An entity shall present totals and subtotals in the statement of profit or loss for: (a) operating profit or loss …; (b) profit or loss before financing and income taxes …, subject to paragraph 73; and (c) profit or loss. 4.1.2 — Alternatives the Board rejected

Alternative consideredBoard’s response
Define operating profit directly (by what it contains)Rejected: a direct definition suitable for all entities would be difficult, if not impossible; it would need more judgement and cost; and it could leave some operating expenses out (e.g. an expense not allocated to a function line). Operating stays the default category (Ch3 3.2.2). IFRS 18 · para BC91(a)–(c)
Narrow ‘core’ or ‘normalised’ earningsRejected: views on operating profit vary, and a narrow definition applied consistently would be hard to develop. Users wanted a consistent, comparable starting point; other tools (additional subtotals, disaggregation, MPMs) carry management’s view. IFRS 18 · para BC92–BC93
Merge investing and financing; drop the middle subtotalSuggested because it would remove classification issues (e.g. cash and cash equivalents), investing may hold few items, and the extra subtotal might clutter. Rejected: the merged band would mix items with different characteristics that are analysed separately, and almost all users supported the subtotal as a comparable anchor. IFRS 18 · para BC150(a)–(c)IFRS 18 · para BC151
Label the middle subtotal EBITRejected: EBIT would rarely be faithful — it might imply all interest (including interest revenue) is excluded, or that only interest and tax are excluded. IFRS 18 · para BC149
Present one subtotal when the two are equalRejected: both subtotals are presented, because identifying each is useful even when equal, particularly for digital access. IFRS 18 · para BC152
Relabel the categories or require a subtotal per categoryNot required: no category labels and no per-category subtotals (e.g. investing alone), so a different label such as ‘non-operating’ would not change presentation. IFRS 18 · para BC87

4.2 The para 73 policy-exception traptop

Do not present the paragraph 69(b) “profit or loss before financing and income taxes” subtotal if the entity applies the paragraph 65(a)(ii) accounting policy. That policy classifies in the operating category income and expenses from raising-finance liabilities that do not relate to providing financing to customers. Presenting a “before financing” subtotal in that case would be misleading because most financing expenses would sit inside it. IFRS 18 · para 73IFRS 18 · para 65(a)(ii)IFRS 18 · para BC189–BC190

  • Standard prohibition. An entity shall not apply paragraph 69(b) if it applies the accounting policy set out in paragraph 65(a)(ii) of classifying in operating the income and expenses from liabilities that do not relate to the provision of financing to customers. IFRS 18 · para 73
  • Who can reach this path. Only an entity that provides financing to customers as a main business activity has the paragraph 65 policy choice for unrelated Type 1 liabilities (Ch3 3.5.11, 3.5.15; bancassurers in 3.5.21). IFRS 18 · para 65(a)(ii) EY Closer look 2026 · section 3.3.3.G EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 3.3.3.A
  • Still test for an additional subtotal. Such an entity shall apply paragraph 24 to determine whether to present an additional subtotal after operating profit and before the financing category — e.g. operating profit plus equity-method investment results, if that is necessary for a useful structured summary. IFRS 18 · para 73IFRS 18 · para 24IFRS 18 · para BC190
  • Faithful labelling only. If that additional subtotal comprises operating profit plus all investing-category amounts, it shall not label the subtotal in a way that implies the subtotal excludes financing amounts, such as ‘profit before financing’. Label so the description faithfully represents the amounts included (paragraph 43). IFRS 18 · para 74IFRS 18 · para 43
  • Big4 alignment. KPMG and EY both flag the same trap: entities that pull the relevant raising-finance amounts into operating under the paragraph 65(a)(ii) path are not permitted to present the required middle subtotal; any extra post-operating / pre-financing subtotal must be faithfully labelled and cannot imply financing is excluded. KPMG FI 2024 · section 2.3.1 EY Closer look 2026 · section 3.2.1.H / section 4.2.4
  • Paragraph 118 bridge for the paragraph 73 entity. For an entity that applies paragraph 73, a subtotal comprising operating profit or loss and all investing-category income and expenses is listed in paragraph 118(d) as not an MPM. IFRS 18 · para 118(d)

Main path — present para 69(b)

Most entities: operating profit → + investing → “profit before financing and income taxes” → financing → tax → discontinued → profit or loss. IFRS 18 · para 69–72

Special-entity path — para 73

4.4 Required vs para 118-listed vs MPM — three-column matrixtop

Not every useful subtotal is an MPM. An MPM is a subtotal of income and expenses that an entity uses in public communications outside financial statements to communicate management’s view of an aspect of performance of the entity as a whole, and that is not listed in paragraph 118 or specifically required by IFRS Accounting Standards. IFRS 18 · para 117IFRS 18 · para IN14

  • Paragraph 118 list — not MPMs. Subtotals of income and expenses that are not management-defined performance measures are:
    1. Gross profit or loss (revenue minus cost of sales) and similar subtotals (see B123);
    2. Operating profit or loss before depreciation, amortisation and impairments within the scope of IAS 36;
    3. Operating profit or loss and income and expenses from all investments accounted for using the equity method;
    4. For a paragraph 73 entity: a subtotal comprising operating profit or loss and all income and expenses classified in the investing category;
    5. Profit or loss before income taxes; and
    6. Profit or loss from continuing operations.
    IFRS 18 · para 118
  • Similar to gross profit (B123). A subtotal is similar to gross profit when it depicts the difference between a type of revenue and directly related expenses incurred in generating that revenue. Examples include net interest income, net fee and commission income, insurance service result, net financial result (investment income minus insurance finance income and expenses), and net rental income:
    • Net interest income
    • Net fee and commission income
    • Insurance service result
    • Net financial result (investment income minus insurance finance income and expenses)
    • Net rental income
    IFRS 18 · para B123
  • Why listed items are carved out. The Board’s view is that these measures are commonly understood and their relationship to IFRS totals/subtotals is usually apparent from the face of the statement, so the MPM disclosure package would not provide useful incremental information. 4.4.1 — Additional subtotals can be MPMs

  • Overlapping groups. KPMG maps four groups of income-and-expense subtotals: required, common (paragraph 118-listed), additional and MPMs. An MPM is neither required nor listed, but both a listed subtotal (e.g. gross profit) and an MPM (e.g. operating profit before research and development expenditure) can be presented as additional subtotals on the face. KPMG FI 2024 · section 3.1.3KPMG FI 2024 · section 2.3.2

EY view: a paragraph 24 subtotal is required only generally — paragraph 24(d) sets it apart from required totals — so it is an MPM if it meets the rest of the definition. 4.5 Additional subtotals (compatibility)top

Additional subtotals on the PFS must remain compatible with the category structure and required totals — and must be faithfully labelled. They do not replace the paragraph 69 anchors. The general rule is in Ch2 2.2.2. IFRS 18 · para 24IFRS 18 · para 43

  • When to add. Present additional line items and subtotals if necessary for a primary financial statement to provide a useful structured summary. IFRS 18 · para 24
  • Four constraints (paragraph 24 / B9).
    1. Comprise amounts recognised and measured in accordance with IFRS Accounting Standards;
    2. Be compatible with the statement structure created by the requirements listed in paragraph 22 (for P&L: including paragraphs 47, 69, 76 and 78);
    3. Be consistent from period to period (paragraph 30); and
    4. Be displayed no more prominently than the totals and subtotals required by IFRS Accounting Standards.
    IFRS 18 · para 24IFRS 18 · para 22IFRS 18 · para B9
  • No IAS 1-style face reconciliation requirement. IFRS 18 removes the former IAS 1 requirement to reconcile additional subtotals to required totals/subtotals on the face; compatibility with the structured summary means the relationship is apparent from the statement itself. KPMG FI 2024 · section 2.3.2
  • Investing-category extras. There is no requirement to insert an “investing” heading; additional subtotals within investing are permitted when necessary for a useful structured summary. EY view: this lets entities show how relevant different investments are to their business (Ch3 3.3.9). IFRS 18 · para BC120 EY Closer look 2026 · section 3.2.1.G
  • No cap on number. EY view: the Standard requires precise labels but does not restrict how many subtotals are presented, provided each contributes to a useful structured summary. EY Closer look 2026 · section 3.3.3.G EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 3.3.3.A
  • Big4 alignment. KPMG and EY both stress the same paragraph 24 constraints and that paragraph 118-listed common subtotals can appear on the face only if those criteria are met — face presentation of a paragraph 118 item does not turn it into an MPM, and an MPM may exist even if it is not presented on the face. Facts

  • Entity I is a short-term insurer holding insurance-related associates (equity method) plus other equity-accounted investments unconnected to that insurance business.
  • I concludes an additional subtotal is needed to present separately the returns from its insurance-related associates.
  • Extract shape (EY Ill 3-12): Operating profit → Share of profit of insurance-related associates → Operating profit and share of profit of insurance-related associates → other investing items. The extract table is in Illustration 3.5.20.

Assessment

  • Paragraph 24 test. Additional subtotals must comprise IFRS-recognised amounts, be compatible with the category/totals structure, be consistent period-to-period, and be displayed no more prominently than required totals/subtotals.
  • Presenting insurance-related associate returns next to operating profit can be necessary when those associates are tightly linked to the insurance business (BC120(b)).
  • Listed paragraph 118 subtotals are not MPMs. A custom additional subtotal that is not paragraph 118-listed and that meets paragraph 117 can be an MPM → Ch6.
  • Additional face subtotals and MPM classification are separate tests — compatibility under paragraph 24 first; MPM only if paragraph 117 also bites.

Decision

  • Present the subtotal inside investing, after operating profit; profit before financing and income taxes still follows.
  • It covers only some equity-accounted investees, so it is not the paragraph 118(c) subtotal; if used in public communications it can be an MPM (BC367; EY section 4.2.4.B).
