Chapter 5 — Operating expenses

Concept Map

5.0 Scope — analysis inside the operating categorytop

Inside the operating category, classify and present expenses so the operating band remains a useful structured summary — by nature, by function, or mixed. IFRS 18 requires that analysis on the face of the statement of profit or loss. Any function line triggers a cost-of-sales line (when the entity has that function), a qualitative nature description per function line, and a single-note package of specified nature totals. IFRS 18 · para 78–85, IFRS 18 · para B80–B85, IFRS 18 · para IN17 The points below are Where this sits., Two major changes from IAS 1., IN package., Why nature and function both matter., No mirror package for nature presenters., and How much practice changes depends on the starting point.

  • Where this sits. Ch3 locked the five P&L categories; Ch4 locked required totals and subtotals. This chapter is the analysis inside the operating band — after those frames exist. IFRS 18 · para 47, IFRS 18 · para 69, IFRS 18 · para 78
  • Two major changes from IAS 1.
    • Face location. The analysis of operating expenses must be presented on the face of the income statement — the former option to disclose the analysis only in the notes is removed. KPMG FI 2024 · section 2.2
    • Mixed explicitly allowed. Entities may present by nature, by function, or a mixture of both on the face. IFRS 18 · para 79, IFRS 18 · para BC252 KPMG FI 2024 · section 2.2 EY Closer look 2026 · section 3.4.2 / section 3.4.2.E EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 3.4.2 / section 3.4.2.E
  • IN package. HKFRS 18 requires an entity to present the operating category expenses based on their nature or their function. Entities that present expenses classified by function are required to disclose in the notes the amount of depreciation, amortisation, employee benefits, impairment losses and write-downs of inventories included in each line item in the operating category. IFRS 18 · para IN17
  • Why nature and function both matter. Nature information helps users analyse components and forecast future operating expenses; function aggregation facilitates some performance metrics (including margins). In some cases, classifying some expenses by function and others by nature provides the most useful structured summary. IFRS 18 · para BC246–BC247 EY Closer look 2026 · section 3.4.2 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 3.4.2
  • No mirror package for nature presenters. The Board considered requiring nature presenters to disclose function information in the notes but decided not to: users showed little demand, so expected costs would exceed expected benefits. IFRS 18 · para BC249
  • How much practice changes depends on the starting point. Under IAS 1, practice varied by industry and geography, shaped by legacy GAAP, regulatory requirements and industry practice. Some entities may have been prohibited from a mixed presentation; others presented the analysis only in the notes. KPMG FI 2024 · section 2.2

5.1 Choosing nature, function or mixed — most useful structured summarytop

In the operating category, classify and present expenses in line items in a way that provides the most useful structured summary, using one or both of nature and function. an entity shall classify and present expenses in line items in a way that provides the most useful structured summary of its expenses, using one or both of these characteristics: (a) the nature of expenses; or (b) the function of the expenses within the entity. Any individual line item shall comprise operating expenses aggregated on the basis of only one of these characteristics, but the same characteristic does not have to be used as the aggregation basis for all line items.   • Nature expenses (paragraph 80). Information about operating expenses related to the nature of the economic resources consumed to accomplish the entity’s activities without reference to the activities in relation to which those resources were consumed — e.g. raw material expense, employee benefit expense, depreciation and amortisation. IFRS 18 · para 80
  • Function expenses (paragraph 81). Allocate and aggregate operating expenses according to the activity to which the consumed resource relates. Cost of sales is a function line that may combine several natures (raw materials, employee benefits, depreciation, amortisation). Under function classification an entity might:
    1. Allocate expenses of the same nature (e.g. employee benefits) across several function lines (e.g. cost of sales and research and development); and
    2. Include in a single function line an allocation of expenses of several natures.

  • Not a free choice. Selection must provide the most useful structured summary and may require judgement. IFRS 18 does not weight the B80 factors when they point in different directions. Facts

  • XYZ Group (IE7) concludes the most useful structured summary presents some expenses by function and other expenses by nature in the operating category.
  • Function presentation most closely represents how the business is managed and how management reports internally, and is standard practice in its industry.
  • XYZ also presents additional face subtotals: gross profit, profit before income taxes, and profit from continuing operations (Ch4 Illustration 4.1.1).

Assessment

  • Mixed presentation is permitted under paragraph 78 and B80–B85 when that provides the most useful structured summary — not a forced pure-nature or pure-function choice.

  • Mixed is a deliberate usefulness conclusion — document why function lines fit management and industry practice and why selected nature lines stay separate.

Decision

  • Four function lines (cost of sales, selling, research and development, general and administrative) plus a goodwill impairment line by nature.
  • Because function lines exist, disclose specified expenses by nature in a single note (Illustration 5.3.1, paragraph 83).

Facts

  • Other operating expenses include HR, legal, accounting, commissions, and marketing.

  • XYZ Note 1 (IE Part I) — specified expenses by nature under paragraph 83: totals of depreciation, amortisation, employee benefits, impairment losses and inventory write-downs, and amounts related to each operating-category line item.

Assessment

  • Nature totals may need allocation across function lines (B82) — e.g. employee benefits in cost of sales vs selling vs administrative.
  • Amounts disclosed are expenses for the year except depreciation (the IAS 16 charge) and employee benefits (IAS 19 costs, including pension costs); both include amounts capitalised into inventory carrying amount at period-end.

Decision

  • When any operating expenses are presented by function, disclose the paragraph 83 nature totals in a single note, with the capitalisation explanation for depreciation and employee benefits.
  • Function face + nature note is one package — do not skip nature totals when function is used.

5.4 Para 84 exemption — narrow relief, not a general waivertop

Applying paragraph 83 exempts certain further nature↔function disaggregations beyond the paragraph 83 amounts — but does not waive other specific disclosure requirements in IFRS Accounting Standards, and does not erase paragraph 41 materiality generally outside that narrow package. IFRS 18 · para 84–85, IFRS 18 · para 41 The points below are What paragraph 84 exempts., Why the Board gave the exemption., No threshold, all circumstances., Paragraph 85 — other IFRS disclosures still apply., Voluntary extra natures still OK., and Big4 alignment.

  • What paragraph 84 exempts. Paragraph 41 requires an entity to disaggregate items to provide material information. However, an entity that applies paragraph 83 is exempt from disclosing:
    1. In relation to function line items in the operating category — disaggregated information about the amounts of nature expenses included in each line item, beyond the amounts specified in paragraph 83; and
    2. In relation to nature expenses specifically required by an IFRS Accounting Standard to be disclosed in the notes — disaggregated information about the amounts of those expenses included in each function line item in the operating category, beyond the amounts specified in paragraph 83.
    IFRS 18 · para 84
  • Why the Board gave the exemption. Without it, the general disaggregation requirement would push entities toward disclosing further material natures inside function lines, upsetting the cost–benefit balance struck for the five specified natures. The Board deliberately did not insert an “undue cost or effort” threshold, because a threshold might not change outcomes and might be difficult to apply. IFRS 18 · para BC272–BC274
  • No threshold, all circumstances. EY view: limiting the requirement to five natures may appear to conflict with paragraph 41, since other natures inside function lines could be individually material; but the exemption has no threshold and applies in all circumstances. EY Closer look 2026 · section 3.4.2.B
  • Paragraph 85 — other IFRS disclosures still apply. The exemption in paragraph 84 relates to disaggregation of operating expenses. However, it does not exempt an entity from applying specific disclosure requirements relating to those expenses in HKFRS Accounting Standards. IFRS 18 · para 85
  • Voluntary extra natures still OK. The Board did not intend to prevent entities that already disclose more natures than the five from continuing to do so. IFRS 18 · para BC275
  • Big4 alignment. KPMG and EY both describe paragraph 84 as a narrow exemption tied to the paragraph 83 five-nature package: function presenters are not forced to disaggregate every other nature inside function lines, but other Standards’ specific disclosures still bind. KPMG FI 2024 · section 2.2.2 EY Closer look 2026 · section 3.4.2.B
