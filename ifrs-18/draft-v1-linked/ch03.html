3.0 Scope — five P&L categoriestop

Classify every profit-or-loss amount into one of five categories. IFRS 18 requires an entity to classify income and expenses included in the statement of profit or loss in the operating, investing, financing, income taxes or discontinued operations category. Operating is the residual/default. Investing and financing follow specified asset and liability logic, with overrides when investing in assets or providing financing to customers is a specified main business activity. Income taxes and discontinued operations close the set. Foreign exchange differences, the net monetary position, derivatives and derecognition follow the cross-category rules in 3.7. , (selective) IFRS 18 · para 47–68 IFRS 18 · para B29–B72 Classifying an amount across the five P&L categories takes three decisions (which band, whether there is a specified MBA, and whether the amount is residual operating), and general and specific requirements apply together.

  • First decision — which band? Place each P&L amount in exactly one of the five categories; do not invent a sixth “non-recurring” band. IFRS 18 · para 47IFRS 18 · para B29
  • Second decision — specified MBA? Assess whether investing in assets or providing financing to customers is a main business activity before applying the investing and financing tests. If yes, some amounts that would otherwise sit in investing or financing are classified in operating instead. IFRS 18 · para 49–51IFRS 18 · para 55–58IFRS 18 · para 65–66
  • Third decision — residual operating? Whatever is not classified in investing, financing, income taxes or discontinued operations lands in operating — including volatile or non-recurring items. IFRS 18 · para 52IFRS 18 · para IN9IFRS 18 · para B42
  • General and specific requirements together. Every entity applies the general classification requirements. An entity with a specified main business activity also applies the specific requirements, but only to the income and expenses they cover; the rest of its income and expenses stay under the general requirements. Specific requirements also exist for issuers of insurance contracts and of investment contracts with participation features. 3.2 Operating — the default categorytop

Operating is residual by design. An entity shall classify in the operating category all income and expenses included in the statement of profit or loss that are not classified in investing, financing, income taxes or discontinued operations. IFRS 18 · para 52 Operating is the default category and gives a complete operations picture, as App B confirms, with operations including supporting activities. The category is residual rather than directly defined because a direct definition would be hard to apply consistently, the Big4 align on it, and it links to operating profit.

  • Complete operations picture. The Introduction states that the operating category is the default and provides a complete picture of an entity’s operations for the reporting period; it includes, but is not limited to, income and expenses from main business activities regardless of whether they are volatile or non-recurring. IFRS 18 · para IN9
  • App B confirmation. B42 repeats that operating includes all income and expenses not classified in the other categories applying paragraphs 53–68, including such income or expenses that are volatile or non-recurring, and that main-business results land in operating except equity-accounted investments. IFRS 18 · para B42
  • Operations include supporting activities. In the Board’s view all income and expenses not relating to another category arise from operations, and operations are not limited to main business activities: supporting activities that do not generate revenue directly also give rise to operating income and expenses. Volatile, unusual or non-recurring items are included because faithful representation of the period’s operations needs them — omitting either the depreciation or the impairment of an item of equipment would not depict the decision to buy and deploy it. IFRS 18 · para BC89 EY Closer look 2026 · section 3.2.3 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 3.2.3
  • Why residual, not a direct definition. BC91 explains that a direct definition of operating profit would be difficult to apply consistently for all entities; classifying investing and financing first, then treating operating as default, is expected to be simpler and more consistent. IFRS 18 · para BC91
  • Big4 alignment. KPMG describes operating as a ‘default’ or ‘residual’ category and stresses that volatile, unusual or non-recurring items are not excluded; EY likewise positions operating as residual so that main-business items (and supporting activities) stay inside operating, including volatile items. Equity-accounted results remain in investing even if management views them as main-business. KPMG FI 2024 · section 2.1.2.1 EY Closer look 2026 · section 3.2.3 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 3.2.3
  • Operating profit link. Operating profit or loss comprises all income and expenses classified in the operating category (presentation detail in Ch4). IFRS 18 · para 70

Facts

  • Entity A receives a government grant to purchase a new rolling mill, which it uses in combination with other assets to produce goods. It recognises deferred income and releases it over the mill’s useful life; its policy presents grant income gross.
  • Entity B receives a government grant to reimburse the interest expense on its loan with Bank X. The grant is received in full when the loan is taken out, recognised as deferred income and released as the loan interest is paid; its policy also presents grant income gross.

Assessment

  • Entity A. The income is the release of deferred income, not a return from the mill (an asset used with others). It does not arise from a Type 1 liability and is not interest identified on a Type 2 liability.
  • Entity B. The income is not from a stand-alone-return asset; it is not part of the initial or subsequent amortised-cost accounting for the Bank X loan under IFRS 9, so not Type 1 income; and it is not identified interest on a Type 2 liability.

Decision

  • Gross policy — both entities: classify the grant income in operating.
  • Net policy — Entity A: the grant reduces depreciation on the mill, which stays in operating.
  • Net policy — Entity B: the grant reduces interest expense on the Type 1 Bank X loan, which is classified in financing.
  • Gross policy in practice: EY sees gross grant income as generally operating, but practice may develop and should be monitored.

3.3 Investing categorytop

Investing separates stand-alone returns from operations. Except as required by paragraphs 55–58 for an entity that has a specified main business activity, classify in investing the paragraph 54 income and expenses from the assets listed in paragraph 53. IFRS 18 · para 53 The investing category captures three asset sets (paragraph 53) and moves specified amounts (paragraph 54), distinguishing stand-alone from combination assets. The band exists so investors can analyse stand-alone investment returns separately from operations, some assets keep their own rules, and the Big4 align on the approach.

  • Three asset sets (paragraph 53).
    1. Associates, joint ventures and unconsolidated subsidiaries (B43–B44).
    2. Cash and cash equivalents.
    3. Other assets that generate a return individually and largely independently of the entity’s other resources (B45–B49).
    IFRS 18 · para 53
  • What amounts move (paragraph 54). For those assets, classify in investing:
    • (a) The income generated by the assets;
    • (b) Income and expenses from initial and subsequent measurement, including on derecognition; and
    • (c) Incremental expenses directly attributable to acquiring and disposing of the assets (e.g. transaction costs, costs to sell)
    IFRS 18 · para 54IFRS 18 · para B47

3.5.3 — Unit of assessment, timing and disclosure

A specified MBA is assessed for individual assets or groups, with no assessment for cash or equity-accounted investments. The assessment is made at a point in time and applied prospectively, and the disclosure serves a stated purpose.

  • Individual assets or groups. Assess an individual asset or a group with shared characteristics.
