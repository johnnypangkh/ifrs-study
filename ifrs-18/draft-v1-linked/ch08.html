8.0 Scope — effective date, transition and first-year dutiestop

Appendix C sets when IFRS 18 applies and how to move to it: effective date, retrospective application, comparative P&L reconciliations in the first annual and interim financial statements, the IAS 28 fair value election and the withdrawal of IAS 1. IFRS 18 · para C1–C8 The Big4 align on effective date and transition, the examples follow one set calendar, and the consequential amendments apply from the same date.

  • Big4 alignment. KPMG and EY agree on the date, retrospective application, the reconciliations and the first-year interim duties; both follow Appendix C closely. KPMG FI 2024 · section 7 EY Closer look 2026 · section 6 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 6

8.1 Effective datetop

Apply IFRS 18 for annual reporting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027. Earlier application is permitted, and an entity that applies it early discloses that fact in the notes. IFRS 18 · para C1 KPMG FI 2024 · section 7.1 EY Closer look 2026 · Overview / section 6 IFRS 18 supersedes IAS 1, and the Board chose 2027 to balance preparers’ implementation time, jurisdictional incorporation and users’ wish for the information soon. Implementation takes effort, entities should plan ahead, a phased transition can work, and some questions remain pending.

  • Supersession. IFRS 18 supersedes IAS 1; the consequential amendments to other Standards apply when IFRS 18 is applied. IFRS 18 · para C8 EY Closer look 2026 · section 6 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 6
  • Why 2027. The IASB weighed preparers’ implementation time, time for jurisdictions to incorporate the requirements into law, and users’ wish to have the information soon. Feedback indicated that some entities might need at least two years; after considering endorsement, translation and regulatory changes, the Board judged 1 January 2027 enough. IFRS 18 · para BC414 IFRS 18 · para BC416
  • What implementation involves. The greatest effect is on the statement of profit or loss. Entities may need changes to data collection and IT systems and discussions with lenders, auditors and regulators, including renegotiating remuneration policies and debt covenants linked to P&L metrics and agreeing MPM disclosures, since alternative performance measures are often unaudited. IFRS 18 · para BC415
  • Plan ahead. EY’s view: transition may take considerable time and combine resources from financial reporting, legal and investor relations; information systems and the close process may change, remuneration agreements and covenants may be affected, and entities should monitor industry practice. Preparation actions are also in Ch1 1.2.1. EY Closer look 2026 · section 6 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 6
  • Phased transition. EY’s view: because IFRS 18 carries over most of IAS 1, and much of the new aggregation guidance and the MPM disclosures are largely permissible under or do not conflict with IAS 1, some entities might phase changes in before the mandatory date, justified under the IAS 1 and IAS 8 guidance on changes in presentation. Phased versus all-at-once depends on facts and circumstances. EY Closer look 2026 · section 6 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 6
  • Pending questions. EY (April 2026) reports IFRS Interpretations Committee submissions not yet discussed: two on the MPM definition, one on labelling subtotals and one on the specified main business activity concept. EY Closer look 2026 · section 7

8.2 Retrospective applicationtop

Apply IFRS 18 retrospectively under IAS 8, but without the IAS 8 paragraph 28(f) quantitative information, which is the adjustment for each line item affected and the effect on basic and diluted EPS for the current and each prior period. An entity applying IFRS 19 is likewise not required to give the paragraph 178(f) information of IFRS 19. IFRS 18 · para C2 KPMG FI 2024 · section 7.2 EY Closer look 2026 · section 6 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 6 Retrospective application is required because unrestated comparatives could mislead given the extensive P&L changes, and it uses a reconciliation in place of paragraph 28(f). Additional comparatives and MPMs in the year of adoption need specific treatment.

  • Why retrospective. IFRS 18 is expected to change the statement of profit or loss extensively, and unrestated comparatives could mislead. Because the requirements affect only presentation and disclosure, no period before the start of the earliest comparative period needs to be considered. IFRS 18 · para BC417
  • Reconciliation instead of paragraph 28(f). The paragraph C3 reconciliation replaces the IAS 8 paragraph 28(f) line-item adjustment disclosure (8.3). IFRS 18 · para BC418
  • Additional comparatives. An entity that presents (or is required by local law to present) additional comparative periods gets no relief: IFRS 18 applies retrospectively to the immediately preceding period and to every additional comparative period presented. KPMG FI 2024 · section 7.2 IFRS 18 · para B14–B15 IFRS 18 · para C2
  • MPMs in the year of adoption. For changes to MPM disclosures on first application, see Ch6 6.5. IFRS 18 · para 124–125

8.3 Annual reconciliations (first IFRS 18 annual)top

In the first annual FS under IFRS 18, disclose for the comparative period immediately preceding first application a reconciliation for each P&L line item between: IFRS 18 · para C3

  • Restated amounts applying IFRS 18; and
  • Amounts previously presented applying IAS 1.

First IFRS 18 annual reconciliations are required so users can bridge to pre-IFRS 18 amounts and analyse trends, may include optional extras, can be shaped by local regulation, and affect systems.

  • Why. The new P&L structure would make it hard for users to analyse trends without a bridge to the amounts presented before IFRS 18. IFRS 18 · para BC418
  • Optional extras. Reconciliations for the current period or earlier comparatives are permitted but not required. Requiring one for the year of first application would mean running IAS 1 systems for that whole year, at extra cost and with little benefit given retrospective application. IFRS 18 · para C6 IFRS 18 · para BC419–BC420
  • Local regulation. Where local rules require two comparative years, they may also require a reconciliation for every period presented. EY Closer look 2026 · section 6 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 6
  • Systems. Comparative-period data must be capturable in IFRS 18 categories and subtotals, while the IAS 1 amounts previously presented form the other side of the reconciliation. KPMG FI 2024 · section 7.2 EY Closer look 2026 · section 6 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 6

8.3.1 — Paragraph C3 reconciliation — elements

Paragraph C3 text. In its annual financial statements an entity shall disclose, for the comparative period immediately preceding the period in which this Standard is first applied, a reconciliation for each line item in the statement of profit or loss between (a) the restated amounts presented applying this Standard and (b) the amounts previously presented applying IAS 1. 8.4 Interim — first year of applicationtop

In the first year, condensed interim statements present each heading the entity expects to use under IFRS 18 and the paragraphs 69–74 subtotals, despite IAS 34 paragraph 10, and disclose reconciliations for the comparative and cumulative comparative periods. IAS 34 paragraph 10 (same headings and subtotals as the most recent annual statements) is set aside for headings and subtotals until the first annual IFRS 18 financial statements are issued. IFRS 18 · para C4–C5 EY Closer look 2026 · section 6.1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 6.1 Interim headings change mid-year because the latest IAS 1 annual may carry different subtotals from those IFRS 18 requires, both periods are reconciled, the reconciliation has a set location, and the ongoing interim requirements are taught in Ch7.

  • Why headings change mid-year. The most recent annual statements, still under IAS 1, may have different subtotals from those IFRS 18 requires; presenting the IFRS 18 subtotals gives users useful information. IFRS 18 · para BC421
  • Why both periods. Without reconciliations for both the comparative and the cumulative comparative period, users would struggle to understand interim comparatives and analyse trends; the Board judged the benefit worth the cost. IFRS 18 · para BC422
  • Where the reconciliation sits. It forms part of the IAS 34 paragraph 16A(a) disclosure (nature and effect of changes in accounting policies since the last annual period). EY Closer look 2026 · section 6.1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 6.1 IFRS 18 · para C5
  • Ongoing interim requirements. Aggregation and MPM disclosures in interim statements after the first year are in Ch7 7.4. IFRS 18 · para 5

8.5 IAS 28 fair value election on initial applicationtop

At the date of initial application, an entity eligible under IAS 28 paragraph 18 may change its election for an associate or joint venture from the equity method to fair value through profit or loss under IFRS 9, retrospectively under IAS 8, and must make the same change in its separate financial statements (IAS 27 paragraph 11). IFRS 18 · para C7 EY Closer look 2026 · section 6 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 6 The IAS 28 fair value election on initial application is open to eligible entities, exists as relief because some eligible entities chose the equity method before knowing how IFRS 18 would classify the income, affects operating profit, and comes with an eligibility clarification.

  • Who is eligible. IAS 28 paragraph 18 covers investments held by, or indirectly through, venture capital organisations, mutual funds, unit trusts and similar entities, including investment-linked insurance funds. KPMG gives venture capital organisations, mutual funds and some insurers as examples. EY Closer look 2026 · section 3.3.5.A EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 3.3.5.A KPMG FI 2024 · section 7.2
  • Why the relief. The IAS 28 election is made per investment at initial recognition. Some eligible entities may have chosen the equity method before they knew how IFRS 18 would classify the income, so IFRS 18 lets them elect again on initial application. IFRS 18 · para BC423 EY Closer look 2026 · section 3.3.5.A EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 3.3.5.A
  • Effect on operating profit. For an entity that invests in assets as a main business activity, income and expenses from associates and joint ventures at fair value are operating; under the equity method they are always investing. Electing fair value brings them into operating profit (Ch3 3.3.2). KPMG FI 2024 · section 7.2 IFRS 18 · para BC423
  • Eligibility clarification. In February 2026 the IASB published ED/2026/1, Amendments to the Fair Value Option for Investments in Associates and Joint Ventures, proposing to clarify which entities are eligible. EY Closer look 2026 · section 3.3.5.A
