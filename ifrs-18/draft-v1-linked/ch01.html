1.0 Scope of this chaptertop

Chapter 1 sets out what IFRS 18 changes relative to IAS 1 — why the IASB issued it, the three new requirement sets, what IAS 1 material was carried forward or moved, the new terminology, and the effective date. Quotations are taken from HKFRS 18, whose text is the same as IFRS 18; the pack labels everything IFRS 18. Detailed requirements on the primary financial statements and aggregation (Ch2), categories (Ch3), totals and subtotals (Ch4), operating expenses (Ch5), MPMs (Ch6), other statements (Ch7) and transition (Ch8) are taught in later chapters. IFRS 18 is the presentation and disclosure spine for general purpose financial statements. Its objective is relevant information that faithfully represents assets, liabilities, equity, income and expenses, matching IAS 1 for every IFRS preparer, while other Standards still set recognition and measurement.

1.1 Why IFRS 18 — investor demand for comparable performancetop

The IASB’s case for change is three linked gaps — where to classify income and expenses, which subtotals to present above profit or loss, and how to group information between the primary financial statements and the notes. The gaps produced diversity, diversity left investors unable to compare, and IFRS 18 responds. The case for IFRS 18 runs from a gap (no detailed classification, subtotal or grouping requirements) to its consequence (diversity that makes comparison hard), then to the IASB’s response, the three stakeholder concerns in the Basis for Conclusions, and investors’ demand met through more structure, disaggregation and audited MPMs.

  • Gap — no detailed classification, subtotal or grouping requirements:
    • HKFRS Accounting Standards do not have detailed requirements on where to classify income and expenses in the statement of profit or loss; what subtotals to present above “profit or loss” in the statement of profit or loss; or how to group the information to be presented in the primary financial statements or disclosed in the notes. IFRS 18 · para IN4
  • Consequence — diversity makes comparison hard:
    • This lack of detailed requirements lead to diversity in practice. Entities define their own subtotals and performance measures and group items in their own ways. Investors commented that this diversity makes it difficult to analyse and compare entities’ performance. IFRS 18 · para IN5
  • Response
    • To respond to investors’ demands for better information about entities’ financial performance, the IASB … [issued] IFRS 18 which aims to give investors more transparent and comparable information about entities’ financial performance, thereby enabling better investment decisions. IFRS 18 · para IN6
  • Three stakeholder concerns in the Basis for Conclusions:
    • Strong user demand: the IASB developed IFRS 18 in response to strong demand from stakeholders, particularly from users of financial statements, for improvements to financial performance reporting. IFRS 18 · para BC2
    • Subtotals not comparable: IAS 1 required an entity to present profit or loss, but no specific subtotals, leading to diversity in the presentation and calculation of subtotals even among entities in the same industry… Such diversity made it difficult for users… to understand and compare information. Entities often used the same label for subtotals containing varying income and expenses; comparability matters most to buy-side investors who analyse many entities across industries. IFRS 18 · para BC3(a)
    • Aggregation and disaggregation poorly applied: IAS 1 aggregation/disaggregation requirements were sometimes not understood or applied well in practice, leading to diversity…; users struggled to find and understand relevant information, and entities sometimes disclosed large amounts as other expenses with no composition. IFRS 18 · para BC3(b)
    • Management-defined measures hard to find and unassured: users find some of these measures (also called alternative performance measures or non-GAAP measures) useful for analysing performance and forecasting, but information about why they are used and how they are calculated can be difficult to find and understand; they are typically reported outside the financial statements, where they are often not subject to assurance. IFRS 18 · para BC3(c)
  • More structure, disaggregation and audited MPMs — investors’ demand met:
    • Investors want more relevant, transparent, comparable performance information and more consistency in how particular financial measures are communicated; IFRS 18 responds with a more structured income statement, greater disaggregation, and MPMs brought into the audited financial statements for the first time, giving certain key performance indicators more credibility and helping companies connect their reporting. Much of IAS 1 is carried forward with only limited wording changes. KPMG FI 2024 · Foreword EY Closer look 2026 · Overview
    • Primary Financial Statements project (from 2014) — targeted improvements, not a rewrite: IFRS 18 is the outcome of the IASB’s Primary Financial Statements project, whose objective was to improve communication in financial statements. The IASB focused on targeted improvements to the three user concerns (comparability of subtotals; transparency of non-GAAP measures; aggregation and disaggregation applied inconsistently, omitting needed information and obscuring material information), so many IAS 1 requirements carry forward substantially unchanged. 1.2 Three new requirement setstop

IFRS 18 adds three sets of new requirements — teach the change as these three sets rather than as a list of line moves. IFRS 18’s three new requirement sets give a better basis for analysing and comparing entities. They are a structured income statement, a single MPM note and enhanced aggregation and disaggregation, and their impact is pervasive but greatest on the statement of profit or loss.

  • Better basis for analysing and comparing entities: HKFRS 18 introduces the following three sets of new requirements to improve entities’ reporting of financial performance and give investors a better basis for analysing and comparing entities. IFRS 18 · para IN7
  • Structured income statement · MPM single note · enhanced aggregation and disaggregation: five categories with two new mandatory subtotals; MPM disclosures in a single note; enhanced aggregation and disaggregation guidance across the primary financial statements and the notes, including the location of information and informative labels. KPMG FI 2024 · section 1.1 EY Closer look 2026 · Overview EY Closer look 2026 · section 1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 1
  • Pervasive impact, greatest on the statement of profit or loss: IFRS 18 changes presentation and disclosure throughout the financial statements, with the greatest effect on the statement of profit or loss; many aspects of presentation and disclosure are affected and most, if not all, entities are expected to be affected. 1.2.1 — Implementation effects and preparation actions

Implementing IFRS 18 touches data, systems and stakeholder discussions as well as the close process and cross-team collaboration, so preparers should plan ahead because transition takes time and resources, and apply the detailed requirements rather than the illustrations.

  • Data, systems and stakeholder discussions: entities are likely to need changes to data collection processes and IT systems, and discussions with internal and external stakeholders including lenders, auditors and regulators — for example, renegotiating remuneration policies and debt covenants linked to statement-of-profit-or-loss metrics, and discussing MPM disclosures with auditors and regulators because alternative performance measures and non-GAAP measures are often not subject to audit. IFRS 18 · para BC415 EY Closer look 2026 · Overview
  • Close process and cross-team collaboration: the financial statement close process and information systems will need updating; identifying MPMs and preparing their disclosures will need more collaboration between financial reporting, legal and investor relations teams than is currently the norm; MPMs will generally be audited and face more regulatory scrutiny. EY Closer look 2026 · Overview EY Closer look 2026 · section 1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 1

1.3 What carries forward and what movestop

Much of IAS 1 continues — carried forward into IFRS 18 or moved to IAS 8 and IFRS 7 without substantive change. Those topics are mapped at paragraph level below rather than taught again. The Board focused on the statement of profit or loss rather than a full rewrite of IAS 1, and IAS 8, renamed Basis of Preparation of Financial Statements, absorbs IAS 1 requirements.

1.3.5 — Net profit unchanged — the statement of profit or loss changes shape

Net profit keeps the same bottom line under a new structure, and comparability builds over time because there is no live benchmark. The change comes through presentation rather than recognition or measurement, and a familiar net profit does not mean the teaching of the statement of profit or loss is unchanged.

  • Same bottom line, new structure: Although companies’ net profit will remain unchanged, many will see changes to the structure of their income statement. For some, the changes will be significant, depending on their current presentation practice… KPMG FI 2024 · Foreword
  • No live benchmark — comparability builds over time: IFRS 18 will affect all companies across different industries; with no live benchmark yet, comparability will build over time. KPMG FI 2024 · Foreword
  • Achieved through presentation, not recognition or measurement: …more transparent and comparable information about entities’ financial performance… comes from categories, defined subtotals, grouping discipline and the MPM note, not from changing recognition or measurement. IFRS 18 · para IN6–IN7IFRS 18 · para 4IFRS 18 · para 69–72
  • Familiar net profit ≠ unchanged teaching of the statement of profit or loss: study the shape — category bands, defined subtotals and the MPM bridge — not only the total. IFRS 18 · para IN7–IN8IFRS 18 · para IN15

1.4 Effective date and transitiontop

IFRS 18 applies to annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027, with earlier application permitted and disclosed, and is applied retrospectively with first-year reconciliations.

  • Annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027; earlier application permitted and disclosed:
    • Effective date: An entity shall apply this Standard for annual reporting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027. Earlier application is permitted. If an entity applies this Standard for an earlier period, it shall disclose that fact in the notes. IFRS 18 · para C1 IFRS 18 · para IN2 KPMG FI 2024 · section 7.1
    • Why 1 January 2027: the IASB weighed implementation time (including for jurisdictions to incorporate the requirements into law) against users’ need for the information as soon as possible; some entities said they need at least two years, and endorsement, translation and regulatory changes also take time. IFRS 18 · para BC414 IFRS 18 · para BC416
    • Consequential amendments at the same time: IFRS 18 and the consequential amendments to other Standards must be adopted together. EY Closer look 2026 · Overview
  • Retrospective application with first-year reconciliations:
    • Retrospective under IAS 8, with relief: apply retrospectively applying IAS 8; the quantitative information in IAS 8 para 28(f) is not required. Retrospective restatement avoids misleading comparatives given extensive changes to the statement of profit or loss; because only presentation and disclosure change, periods before the earliest comparative period need not be considered. IFRS 18 · para C2 IFRS 18 · para BC417
    • Annual reconciliation: for the comparative period immediately preceding first application, reconcile each line item in the statement of profit or loss between the restated IFRS 18 amounts and the amounts previously presented under IAS 1. IFRS 18 · para C3
    • First-year interim: condensed interim financial statements present each heading the entity expects to use and the subtotals required by paras 69–74, despite IAS 34 para 10, until the first IFRS 18 annual financial statements are issued; interim reports also reconcile each statement of profit or loss line item for the comparative and cumulative comparative periods. Reconciliations for the current or earlier periods are permitted, not required. IFRS 18 · para C4–C5 IFRS 18 · para C6
