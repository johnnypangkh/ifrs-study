2.0 Scope — where information livestop

Start with location, not category mechanics. IFRS 18 gives the PFS and the notes distinct but complementary roles. The PFS provide useful structured summaries; the notes provide material detail and supplementation. Aggregation, disaggregation and faithful labelling are the controls that make those roles work. IFRS 18 · para 15–18IFRS 18 · para 41–45IFRS 18 · para B16–B26 Four decisions settle where information lives. The first asks whether the information is material, the second where it does its job, the third whether the grouping obscures, and the fourth whether the label tells users what is inside.

  • First decision — is the information material? An entity need only present or disclose material information. A specific Standard requirement can be omitted when the resulting information is not material, but additional disclosure may still be needed when the specific requirements are insufficient for users to understand the effects of transactions, events or conditions. Materiality requirements apply equally to every presentation and disclosure requirement: they set out whether information is provided, not where in the financial statements it is provided. IFRS 18 · para 19–20IFRS 18 · para BC53 KPMG FI 2024 · section 4.1 EY Closer look 2026 · section 2 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 2
  • Second decision — where does the information do its job? Present summarised information in the PFS when it contributes to an understandable overview, comparisons, or a cue to seek more information; disclose material detail in the notes when users need to understand a PFS line or when the information supplements the PFS. IFRS 18 · para 16–17 KPMG FI 2024 · section 4.1 EY Closer look 2026 · section 2.1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 2.1
  • Third decision — does the grouping obscure? Aggregate items with shared characteristics, disaggregate items with characteristics that are not shared, and do not obscure material information. IFRS 18 · para 41–42 KPMG FI 2024 · section 4.2 EY Closer look 2026 · section 2.2 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 2.2
  • Fourth decision — does the label tell users what is inside? Label and describe totals, subtotals, line items and note items so they faithfully represent the item’s characteristics, and use ‘other’ only when no more informative label exists. 2.0.1 — Materiality — definition, obscuring and the four-step process

Information is material if omitting, misstating or obscuring it could reasonably be expected to influence decisions that primary users of general purpose financial statements make on the basis of those statements, which provide financial information about a specific reporting entity. IFRS 18 · para B1 Materiality depends on nature or magnitude, alone or in combination, is judged against primary users and their characteristics, and cuts both ways.

  • Nature or magnitude, alone or in combination: materiality depends on the nature or magnitude of information, or both, assessed individually or in combination with other information in the context of the financial statements as a whole. Because the definition already covers individual and collective assessment, paragraph 19 needs no separate “individually or collectively” wording. IFRS 18 · para B2IFRS 18 · para BCZ51
  • Primary users and their characteristics: the primary users are existing and potential investors, lenders and other creditors who cannot require reporting entities to provide information directly to them and must rely on general purpose financial statements. They have a reasonable knowledge of business and economic activities and review the information diligently, although even well-informed users may need an adviser for complex economic phenomena. The entity considers those user characteristics together with its own circumstances. IFRS 18 · para B4IFRS 18 · para B5
  • Materiality cuts both ways: it is not only about excluding information. The IASB amended IAS 1 to highlight that materiality applies to specifically required disclosures and that it also involves decisions to include additional information — now retained in paragraph 20 of IFRS 18. Facts

  • An entity undertakes a major restructuring during the reporting period and concludes that information about the restructuring is material.
  • Question: should the statement of profit or loss present a separate line item for ‘restructuring expenses’?
  • Materiality is already settled; the open question is location — does a separate line contribute to a useful structured summary under the PFS role?
  • Each of the three aspects of the PFS role is tested; any one of them can support separate presentation.

Assessment

  • (a) Understandable overview of the entity’s expenses for the period. Separate presentation could help users understand an increase in total operating expenses for the current period.
  • (b) Comparisons between entities or between reporting periods. Separately presenting restructuring expenses, because they lack persistence, could make other line items more comparable to the entity’s prior-period amounts and to amounts presented by other entities, aiding trend analysis.
  • (c) Items about which users may seek more information in the notes. When the notes include additional information about restructuring expenses, separate presentation on the face — with a cross-reference to the related note — could draw users’ attention to that information.

Decision

  • Present the line when it is necessary for the statement of profit or loss to provide a useful structured summary.
  • Otherwise disclose in the notes. Material information that is not presented on the face is disclosed in the notes.
  • Material does not mean an automatic face line. Presentation follows the defined PFS role; the notes carry the remaining material detail.
  • Cross-reference. A presented line is cross-referenced to the restructuring note; paragraph 114 also requires a note to identify the line item(s) that include its amounts.

2.1.2 — Why the roles exist — user attention, prominence and clutter

The PFS and notes roles exist because users rely more on the PFS, and the role descriptions serve two purposes. More prominent is not more important, digital access needs the same structure, not all material information fits on the face, and the Standard distinguishes item, line item and other material information.

  • Users rely more on the PFS: feedback and research showed that users pay more attention to information presented in the PFS, and use it more often, because the PFS
    • (a) Give an overview of financial performance, financial position, cash flows and changes in equity;
    • (b) Are more structured than the notes and can be easier to use to compare entities; and
    • (c) Can be used to identify items or areas to seek more information on. The roles were developed from the Conceptual Framework, that feedback and research on how users use financial statements
    IFRS 18 · para BC45 EY Closer look 2026 · section 2.1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 2.1
  • Two purposes for the descriptions: the roles help an entity judge whether to present information in the PFS or disclose it in the notes, and they will help the IASB when it develops new or revised presentation and disclosure requirements. IFRS 18 · para BC46 KPMG FI 2024 · section 4.1
  • More prominent is not more important: because each PFS provides a useful structured summary, PFS information is more prominent than note information — but greater prominence does not make the notes secondary to, or less important than, the PFS. The notes have a different role in both digital and paper-based reporting. IFRS 18 · para BC49 EY Closer look 2026 · section 2.1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 2.1
  • Digital access needs the same structure: a useful structured summary gives the context in which individually tagged items can be understood — for example, operating profit is identified as a subtotal that includes all income and expenses classified in the operating category, and a specific operating expense is identified as included in operating profit.   • Not all material information fits on the face: the role refers to an understandable overview (the Conceptual Framework’s understandability characteristic). Presenting many income and expense items as line items would clutter the statement and make the overview hard to obtain; the entity’s specific facts and circumstances determine what gives an understandable overview.   • Item, line item and other material information: an item is an asset, liability, equity instrument or reserve, income, expense or cash flow, or any aggregation or disaggregation of them; a line item is an item presented separately in the PFS; other material information about items is disclosed in the notes. 2.1.4 — Cross-references and systematic notes

Cross-references signpost in both directions, which is expected to improve transparency, and the notes follow a systematic order as far as practicable.

  • Signposting in both directions: as under IAS 1, each item in the PFS is cross-referenced to any related information in the notes. IFRS 18 adds the reverse link — if amounts disclosed in a note are included in one or more PFS line items, the note discloses the line item(s) in which they are included — because users said it is important to understand how note amounts relate to PFS line items. IFRS 18 · para 114IFRS 18 · para BC324 EY Closer look 2026 · section 2.1.3 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 2.1.3
  • Expected effect: the new note-to-line-item reference is expected to improve transparency and make it easier for users to link note amounts to the PFS. EY Closer look 2026 · section 2.1.3 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 2.1.3
  • Systematic manner, as far as practicable: in deciding the order of the notes, the entity considers understandability and comparability. There must be a system or reason behind the ordering and grouping — for example by importance to the entity, in line-item order, or a combination. Ordering for understandability can reduce comparability; consistency of an entity’s note order from period to period is important, and a change in order should generally result from a specific event or transaction, such as a change in business. 2.3 Aggregation and disaggregation principlestop

Group by characteristics, then test the roles. IFRS 18 requires an entity to classify and aggregate based on shared characteristics, disaggregate based on characteristics that are not shared, use the two roles to determine the destination, and ensure that the result does not obscure material information. IFRS 18 · para 41 Shared characteristics aggregate and non-shared characteristics disaggregate, aggregation must not obscure and specific requirements prevail, and items are disaggregated whenever the result is material. Detail should be neither too little nor too much, and PFS line items still contain dissimilar items.

  • Shared characteristics aggregate; non-shared characteristics disaggregate. Classify and aggregate … based on shared characteristics and disaggregate items based on characteristics that are not shared; the result must fulfil the PFS role or the notes role. IFRS 18 · para 41(a)–(d)
  • Do not obscure; specific requirements prevail. Aggregation and disaggregation must not obscure material information, and the principles apply unless doing so would override specific aggregation or disaggregation requirements in IFRS Accounting Standards. IFRS 18 · para 41(e)IFRS 18 · para B3 EY Closer look 2026 · section 2.2.1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 2.2.1
  • Disaggregate whenever the result is material. Disaggregate whenever the resulting information is material. If the PFS does not present material information while applying its role, disclose that information in the notes. IFRS 18 · para 42
  • Neither too little nor too much detail. KPMG describes the principles as a balance between useful detail and avoiding obscuring information; EY likewise says the entity must apply judgement to avoid both insufficient detail and excessive detail. Total assets or total expenses alone are too aggregated to be useful; information about individual transactions can be so detailed that it obscures material information. IFRS 18 · para BC73 KPMG FI 2024 · section 4.2 EY Closer look 2026 · section 2.2 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 2.2
  • PFS line items still contain dissimilar items. Because the PFS role is to summarise, line items are likely to aggregate items with sufficiently dissimilar characteristics that information about the disaggregated items is material — generally requiring further disaggregation in the notes. Facts

  • An entity presents its financial assets — equity investments and debt investments — separately from its non-financial assets in the statement of financial position.
  • The financial assets are dissimilar to each other because of their measurement bases: some are measured at fair value through profit or loss (FVTPL), others at amortised cost.
  • Equity investments and debt investments are also dissimilar because each exposes the entity to different risks.
  • Equity investments may have further dissimilar characteristics of their own.

Assessment

  • 1. Measurement-basis disaggregation on the face. The entity determines that line items based on measurement basis give a useful structured summary: (i) equity and debt investments at FVTPL; (ii) debt investments at amortised cost.
  • 2. Further nature/risk disaggregation? FVTPL equity and debt investments are still dissimilar (different risks), so the entity assesses whether splitting them on the face is needed for a useful structured summary.
  • 3. If not on the face → notes. If further face disaggregation is not necessary for the PFS role, the entity considers whether the disaggregated information is material; if so, it discloses equity investments separately from debt investments in the notes.
  • 4. Cascading characteristics. If the equity investments have other dissimilar characteristics, the entity further disaggregates them in the notes when the resulting information is material.

Outcome

  • Face: two financial-asset line items split by measurement basis (unless the risk split is also needed for the summary).
  • Notes: equity versus debt investments within the FVTPL line, if material; further equity disaggregation, if material.
  • Role test layered on a characteristics test: the face/notes split follows the PFS role, not “material → always face”.

Facts

  • Entity X enters into a reverse factoring (supplier finance) arrangement with Finance Provider Y for some of its trade payables — Y pays X’s suppliers; X pays Y on the same date or later.
  • Such arrangements typically give the entity extended payment terms compared with the invoice due date, or give the supplier the benefit of early payment.
  • X must decide statement of financial position presentation and note disclosure for those payables.
  • Characteristics considered against other trade payables: nature, function, measurement basis and size.

Assessment

  • Face aggregation acceptable. Characteristics are sufficiently similar to other trade payables, so a separate line item is not necessary for a useful structured summary of liabilities.
  • Judgement on X’s facts. X presents one trade-payables line item comprising payables subject to the arrangement and those that are not.
  • Notes still needed. Characteristics are also sufficiently dissimilar that note disclosure gives material information.

Outcome

  • Face: one aggregated trade-payables line item.
  • Notes: disaggregated amounts subject to the arrangement, the terms and conditions, the due dates (IAS 7 paragraphs 44F and 44H) and other relevant information.
  • Characteristics test, not habit: aggregation follows paragraphs 41–43 and B17, not “same caption as last year”; B111(d) names supplier finance payables as a candidate for separate disclosure.

2.3.5 — Operating expenses by function — level of aggregation

B85 applies the aggregation principles to operating expenses. Expenses for human resources, information technology, legal and accounting share a characteristic — resources consumed in administrative activities — and might be presented as ‘administrative expenses’. Selling expenses arise from resources consumed in selling activities, a dissimilar characteristic, so separate line items for selling and administrative expenses might be necessary for a useful structured summary. IFRS 18 · para B85IFRS 18 · para 78 One entity’s facts can support separate lines for operating expenses by function, yet a combined line can also be valid.

  • Separate lines on one entity’s facts: an entity presenting by function puts production costs into cost of sales, aggregates human resources, legal and accounting costs as administrative and commissions and marketing as selling, and concludes that a combined ‘selling and administrative expenses’ line is not appropriate in its circumstances — separate lines give a more understandable overview and may help peer comparison; the notes add the nature of the expenses. Full illustration: Ch5 Illustration 5.1.2. KPMG FI 2024 · Example 4 / section 4.2
  • A combined line can also be valid: despite the B85 example, selling and administrative expenses need not always be presented separately. They may share other characteristics; aggregating individually material items may be needed to avoid clutter, provided the aggregated material information is disclosed in the notes; and because the useful structured summary takes a user perspective, an entity might conclude users would not find separate lines important for comparisons or the overview. 2.4 Labelling and “other” disciplinetop

Faithful grouping needs a faithful label. Label and describe totals, subtotals, line items and note items so the description represents the item’s characteristics and gives users the explanations needed to understand how the item was aggregated or disaggregated. IFRS 18 · para 43IFRS 18 · para B24 Descriptions must be complete, and aggregation plays out differently for material plus material, material plus immaterial, and immaterial plus immaterial. ‘Other’ is permitted but discouraged.

  • Descriptions must be complete: some entities provided information about ‘unusual’ income or expenses without explaining why they were unusual. Faithful representation requires the descriptions and explanations a user needs — in some cases the meaning of the terms used and how items were aggregated or disaggregated; an entity describing items as ‘unusual’ explains its definition of ‘unusual’. IFRS 18 · para BC77–BC78 KPMG FI 2024 · section 4.3 EY Closer look 2026 · section 2.3 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 2.3
  • Material plus material. An entity may aggregate material items to summarise information, but must disclose information about each material item when required by the aggregation and disaggregation principles. IFRS 18 · para B24(a)
  • Material plus immaterial. Use a label that faithfully represents the material item; disclose disaggregated information about it if the immaterial information obscures the material information. IFRS 18 · para B24(b)
  • Immaterial plus immaterial. A group may complete a list or subtotal, but the entity still applies the “other” guidance if the aggregated amount could make users reasonably question whether material items are included. IFRS 18 · para B24(c)IFRS 18 · para B26(b)
  • ‘Other’ is permitted but discouraged. KPMG and EY both stress that “other” is discouraged: first look for a more informative label; if none exists, describe the aggregation as precisely as possible and provide further information when the amount could reasonably raise a materiality question. Users raised concerns about large ‘other expenses’ amounts with no information about what they include. 2.5 Offsettingtop

Offsetting is prohibited unless required or permitted, aggregation is not netting, and the prohibition rests on the harm offsetting does to users.

  • No offsetting unless required or permitted. Do not offset assets and liabilities or income and expenses unless an IFRS Accounting Standard requires or permits it; report both sides separately unless the offset reflects the substance of the transaction or other event. IFRS 18 · para 44–45
  • Aggregation is not netting. The aggregation/disaggregation principles concern how items are grouped and separated; they do not create a general permission to net unlike balances or flows. The offsetting requirements are substantially unchanged from IAS 1. EY Closer look 2026 · section 2.2.1
  • Why offsetting harms users: outside cases where it reflects substance, offsetting in the statement(s) of financial performance or the statement of financial position reduces users’ ability to understand the transactions, events and conditions that occurred and to assess future cash flows.
