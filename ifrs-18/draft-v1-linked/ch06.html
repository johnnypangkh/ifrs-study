Chapter 6 — Management-defined performance measures (MPMs)

Concept Map

6.0 Scope — MPMs inside the audited FStop

Management-defined performance measures (MPMs) bring management’s performance story inside the audited financial statements via a single note — with calculation, reconciliation to the most comparable IFRS or paragraph 118 measure, and tax and NCI effects for each reconciling item. This chapter is the definition test, the exclusions, and the disclosure build-up. IFRS 18 · para 117–125IFRS 18 · para B113–B142IFRS 18 · para IN14–IN15 The MPM regime sits inside the audited FS and comes with the IN package. The Board created it so management-defined measures sit under common disclosure requirements and likely audit rather than only outside the FS, and framed three design questions, consistency is required inside and outside the FS, MPMs are a subset of “non-GAAP” measures and APMs, an entity may have zero, one or many, and audit and scrutiny follow.

  • Where this sits. Ch4 locked required totals and subtotals and the paragraph 118-listed (non-MPM) list (Ch4 4.4.2). This chapter is the separate MPM regime for subtotals that pass paragraph 117 — not a different level of importance, a different disclosure path. IFRS 18 · para 69IFRS 18 · para 117–118
  • IN package. HKFRS 18 defines MPM as a subtotal of income and expenses other than those listed by HKFRS 18 or specifically required by HKFRS Accounting Standards, that an entity uses in public communications outside financial statements, and to communicate to investors management’s view of an aspect of the financial performance of the entity as a whole. The single-note package includes reconciliation (with tax and NCI effects), description of management’s view and calculation, explanation of changes, and a statement that the measure reflects management’s view and is not necessarily comparable with similarly labelled measures of other entities. IFRS 18 · para IN14IFRS 18 · para IN15
  • Why the Board created the regime. Users said information about management-defined measures is useful for insight into how the business is managed and the persistence of performance, but raised concerns about the quality of information provided outside the financial statements. Including MPMs in the FS brings them under common disclosure requirements, regardless of jurisdiction, and likely within audit scope. IFRS 18 · para BC325–BC326 KPMG FI 2024 · section 3 / section 3.1 EY Closer look 2026 · section 4 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 4
  • Three design questions. The Board worked through how to identify MPMs (6.1–6.3), where to put the information (6.4), and what to disclose, including changes (6.4–6.5). IFRS 18 · para BC327
  • Consistency inside and outside the FS. Only measures publicly communicated outside the FS can be MPMs; the Board’s view is that performance measures used outside the FS should be consistent with those disclosed in them. IFRS 18 · para BC334
  • MPMs are a subset of “non-GAAP” / APMs. Only subtotals of income and expenses can be MPMs. Free cash flow, ratios, balance-sheet combinations and non-financial KPIs sit outside the MPM definition (they may still exist under other regimes). IFRS 18 · para B116IFRS 18 · para BC331 KPMG FI 2024 · section 3.1.3 EY Closer look 2026 · section 4.1 / section 4.2.1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 4.1 / section 4.2.1
  • Zero, one, or many. An entity might have no MPMs, one, or more than one. Publicly communicating financial performance using only totals and subtotals required by IFRS Accounting Standards means the entity has no MPM. IFRS 18 · para B113
  • Audit and scrutiny. Because MPMs sit in the notes, they are subject to audit. EY view: the disclosures will likely raise the prominence of MPMs and increase regulator scrutiny. KPMG FI 2024 · section 3.4 EY Closer look 2026 · section 4.1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 4.1
Pitfall: Treating every non-GAAP label as an MPM — or treating paragraph 118 items as MPMs. Run the paragraph 117 definition; apply the paragraph 118 and IFRS-required exclusions. IFRS 18 · para 117–118

6.1 What is an MPM? — the para 117 teststop

A management-defined performance measure is a subtotal of income and expenses that meets all three limbs of paragraph 117. IFRS 18 · para 117IFRS 18 · para B113–B122 Paragraph 117 defines an MPM through four tests, which are a subtotal of income and expenses, public communications outside the FS, management’s view of entity-as-a-whole performance, and not being paragraph 118-listed or IFRS-required. Face versus notes does not decide MPM status, a replicated P&L in a presentation needs assessing, face presentation remains constrained by paragraph 24, face subtotals need reassessing, and the Big4 align.

  • Definition (Standard). A management-defined performance measure is a subtotal of income and expenses that (see paragraphs B113–B122): (a) an entity uses in public communications outside financial statements; (b) an entity uses to communicate to users of financial statements management’s view of an aspect of the financial performance of the entity as a whole; and (c) is not listed in paragraph 118, or specifically required to be presented or disclosed by HKFRS Accounting Standards. IFRS 18 · para 117
  • Test 1 — subtotal of income and expenses. Only measures that are subtotals of income and expenses can be MPMs. Stand-alone adjusted revenue, assets/liabilities/equity combinations, financial ratios, liquidity/cash-flow measures (e.g. free cash flow), and non-financial KPIs are not MPMs.
    • Ratio numerator / denominator exception. A financial ratio itself is not an MPM; but a subtotal that is the numerator or denominator is an MPM if that subtotal would meet paragraph 117 if it were not part of a ratio — and the paragraph 121–125 disclosures then apply to that subtotal (6.1.2). IFRS 18 · para B117 EY Closer look 2026 · section 4.2.1 · Q4-1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 4.2.1 · Q4-1
    • Why the definition stops at income and expenses. Each MPM is defined by management, so it is not a measure required by IFRS. Widening the definition to asset, liability or cash-flow measures could be useful, but would need new definitions, disclosures and guidance beyond the project’s focus on financial performance. 6.2 Public-communications presumption and rebuttaltop

Presume that a subtotal of income and expenses used in public communications outside the FS communicates management’s view of an aspect of entity-as-a-whole performance — unless the entity rebuts under paragraph 120 with reasonable and supportable information. IFRS 18 · para 119–120IFRS 18 · para B124–B131 A subtotal in public communications is presumed to communicate management’s view so the test stays objective, and the presumption can be rebutted on two limbs. Rebuttal draws on evidence that a subtotal does not communicate management’s view (B125) and other reasons for public use (B129 examples), cannot be item-by-item inside the subtotal, and needs no disclosure. “Reasonable and supportable” has a precedent, the Big4 expect rebuttal to be rare, and status can change over time (B131).

  • Presumption (Standard). An entity shall presume that a subtotal of income and expenses that it uses in public communications outside its financial statements communicates to users of financial statements management’s view of an aspect of the financial performance of the entity as a whole, unless, applying paragraph 120, the entity rebuts the presumption. IFRS 18 · para 119
  • Why a presumption. Stakeholders worried that management’s view would be hard to assess and that measures not reflecting it might be caught. The presumption gives an objective process and is not intended to produce disclosure of a large number of subtotals. IFRS 18 · para BC347–BC348
  • Two-limb rebuttal. Rebuttal is permitted only if reasonable and supportable information demonstrates both:
    1. The subtotal does not communicate management’s view of an aspect of entity-as-a-whole performance (B125–B128); and
    2. The entity has a reason for using the subtotal in public communications other than communicating that view (B129).
    IFRS 18 · para 120IFRS 18 · para B124IFRS 18 · para BC352
  • Evidence that it does not communicate management’s view (B125).
    • Without prominence — judgement on extent of references and the content of commentary (e.g. described as information provided only in response to frequent user requests vs used to support management analysis and period-to-period explanations, or compared to competitors / industry benchmarks). IFRS 18 · para B126
    • Not used internally to assess or monitor the entity’s financial performance. Internal use of a sufficiently similar subtotal supports that the public subtotal does communicate management’s view; internal-only use (never public) never creates an MPM. IFRS 18 · para B127–B128
  • Other reasons for public use (B129 examples). The subtotal is required by law or regulation; communicates performance under a non-IFRS accounting framework; satisfies a request from an external party; or is used to communicate information other than financial performance. EY view: the regulatory and investor-request reasons are the common ones; the other two are likely rarer. IFRS 18 · para B129 EY Closer look 2026 · section 4.2.3.C EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 4.2.3.C
  • Cannot rebut item-by-item inside the subtotal. Paragraph 120 applies to the subtotal, not to individual income/expense items inside it. IFRS 18 · para B130
  • No disclosure of rebuttal required. IFRS 18 does not require an entity to disclose when and why it rebutted the presumption for a public subtotal; a list of non-MPMs is not expected to be useful. IFRS 18 · para BC355 KPMG FI 2024 · section 3.1.2

Subtotals listed in paragraph 118 are not management-defined performance measures — even if used in public communications. 6.4 Single note — disclosure objective and contentstop

Disclose all MPMs in a single note so users understand which aspect of performance management thinks the MPM communicates, and how it compares with IFRS-defined measures. IFRS 18 · para 121–123IFRS 18 · para B132–B142 The MPM single note serves the objective in paragraph 121, pairs with a management statement (paragraph 122), and carries disclosures for each MPM (paragraph 123). Calculation is unrestricted but must be a faithful representation, MPMs differ from additional subtotals, labels must be clear (B134–B135), a reconciliation bridge is required (B136–B140), tax and NCI are given per item so users can build their own adjusted EPS from only the adjustments they accept, tax effect methods are set (B141–B142), comparatives apply, and the Big4 align.

  • Objective (paragraph 121). Provide information to help a user understand: (a) the aspect of financial performance that, in management’s view, is communicated by the MPM; and (b) how the MPM compares with the measures defined by IFRS Accounting Standards. Stating an MPM’s purpose and limitations is what makes it transparent. IFRS 18 · para 121IFRS 18 · para BC377
  • Single note + management statement (paragraph 122). Disclose information about all measures that meet paragraph 117 in a single note. That note shall include a statement that the MPMs provide management’s view of an aspect of the financial performance of the entity as a whole and are not necessarily comparable with measures sharing similar labels or descriptions provided by other entities.
    • Why one note, and no cross-referencing. One note keeps the measures with their calculations, explanations and reconciliations and makes them easy to find. The Board did not allow cross-referencing to other documents (it fragments information), but saw no need for an explicit prohibition. If reportable segment information includes an MPM, the entity may put the required MPM information in the same note as other segment information — provided either (a) that note covers all MPMs and clearly distinguishes paragraph 121–125 information from IFRS 8 information, or (b) a separate note covers all MPMs (including those also shown in segment information).   • For each MPM (paragraph 123). Label and describe each MPM in a clear and understandable manner that does not mislead users. Disclose:
    1. A description of the aspect of financial performance communicated (including why management thinks the MPM provides useful information);
    2. How the MPM is calculated;
    3. A reconciliation to the most directly comparable paragraph 118-listed subtotal or IFRS-required total/subtotal;
    4. The income-tax effect and the effect on non-controlling interests for each reconciling item; and
    5. A description of how the entity determined the income-tax effect (B141).
    IFRS 18 · para 123IFRS 18 · para IN15
  • No restrictions on calculation — but faithful representation. The Board placed no specific restrictions on how an MPM is calculated, so useful measures are not blocked. MPMs must still faithfully represent the aspect they purport to show (complete, neutral, free from error), meet aggregation rules for reconciling items, and carry comparatives. Faithful representation does not say whether a measure is “good” or “bad”. IFRS 18 · para BC357–BC361
  • MPMs versus additional subtotals. Additional subtotals always comprise IFRS-recognised and measured amounts; MPMs may use different or non-IFRS policies, provided the entity explains the calculation or terms. IFRS 18 · para 24IFRS 18 · para B134IFRS 18 · para BC357 KPMG FI 2024 · section 3.1.3
  • Clear labelling (B134–B135). Enable users to understand which income/expense items are included and excluded. Label so the measure faithfully represents its characteristics (e.g. use “operating profit before non-recurring expenses” only if the subtotal excludes all expenses the entity identifies as non-recurring) and explain entity-specific terms (e.g. how the entity defines “non-recurring”). If the calculation differs from the entity’s FS accounting policies — or additionally from policies required or permitted by IFRS — state that fact and explain. IFRS 18 · para B134–B135IFRS 18 · para BC357–BC358 KPMG FI 2024 · section 3.3 EY Closer look 2026 · section 4.3.2 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch04 · section 4.3.2
  • Reconciliation bridge (B136–B140).
    • Reconcile each MPM to the most directly comparable paragraph 118-listed or IFRS-required total/subtotal — e.g. adjusted operating profit or loss → operating profit or loss. Apply paragraph 41–43 aggregation/disaggregation to reconciling items. The reconciliation shows how the MPM relates to IFRS totals, helps compare it with other entities’ measures, and lets users judge whether to make their own adjustments. IFRS 18 · para B136IFRS 18 · para BC378
    • For each reconciling item disclose: (a) the amount(s) related to each line item in the statement(s) of financial performance; and (b) if needed for paragraph 123(a)–(b), a description of how the item is calculated and contributes to the MPM providing useful information. Fieldwork showed reconciliations varied with form and detail, so the line-item link was made mandatory. IFRS 18 · para B137IFRS 18 · para BC380IFRS 18 · para BC379–BC381
    • Different calculation methods / different usefulness contributions across items → distinguish which items drive which type of adjustment (B138). A single entity-specific definition (e.g. of “non-recurring”) may cover all reconciling items collectively (B139). IFRS 18 · para B138–B139
    • May reconcile to a paragraph 118 total/subtotal that is not presented on the face; then also reconcile that total/subtotal to the most directly comparable total/subtotal that is presented — without repeating tax/NCI effects for that second bridge. IFRS 18 · para B140
  • Why tax and NCI per item. Users need the tax and NCI amounts of each adjustment to build their own adjusted EPS from only the adjustments they accept (Ch7 7.3). KPMG adds that these effects are required even if they are not part of management’s assessment. IFRS 18 · para BC383–BC385 KPMG FI 2024 · section 3.3
  • Tax effect methods (B141–B142). Determine the income-tax effect of each reconciling item by calculating the tax effects of the underlying transaction(s):
    • (a) At the statutory tax rate(s) applicable in the jurisdiction(s) concerned;
    • (b) Based on a reasonable pro rata allocation of current and deferred tax in the jurisdiction(s) concerned; or
    • (c) By using another method that achieves a more appropriate allocation. If more than one method is used, disclose how the tax effect was determined for each reconciling item. The statutory-rate route answers preparers’ IAS 12 cost concerns; the alternatives mirror the IAS 12 approach for OCI tax

6.5 Changes to MPMstop

If calculation changes, a new MPM is added, a previous MPM ceases, or tax-effect methodology changes:

  • Explain the change and its effects
  • Give reasons
  • Restate comparatives unless impracticable. Selecting an MPM is not an accounting-policy choice — but IAS 8 impracticability ideas still apply to the restatement assessment

IFRS 18 · para 124–125 Changes to MPMs have triggers (paragraph 124) and require disclosure. Selecting an MPM is not an accounting-policy choice, although IAS 8 impracticability is still used, and specific rules apply if comparatives are omitted as impracticable. Entities restate so trend information remains usable, a zero adjustment is not a change, timing links back to identification, and ceasing an MPM and ceasing one in the year of adoption each need care.

  • Triggers (paragraph 124). Apply when the entity:
    1. Changes how it calculates an MPM;
    2. Adds a new MPM;
    3. Ceases using a previously disclosed MPM; or
    4. Changes how it determines the income-tax effects of reconciling items under paragraph 123(d).
    IFRS 18 · para 124
  • Disclose.
    1. An explanation that enables users to understand the change, addition or cessation and its effects;
    2. The reasons for the change, addition or cessation; and
    3. Restated comparative information to reflect the change, addition or cessation unless it is impracticable to do so.
    IFRS 18 · para 124(a)–(c)
  • Not an accounting-policy choice; IAS 8 impracticability still used. An entity’s selection of a management-defined performance measure is not an accounting policy choice. Nonetheless, in assessing whether restating the comparative information is impracticable, an entity shall apply the requirements in paragraphs 50–53 of HKAS 8. Unlike a policy, an MPM is defined to communicate performance outside the FS, not selected within the confines of IFRS; the disclosures borrow from IAS 8 but IAS 8 itself does not apply. IFRS 18 · para 124(c)IFRS 18 · para BC368IFRS 18 · para BC388
  • If comparatives omitted as impracticable. Disclose that fact. IFRS 18 · para 125
  • Why restate. Trend information matters to users, and management is generally expected to monitor what it needs for comparatives. IFRS 18 · para BC390
  • A zero adjustment is not a change. If an adjusted measure excludes restructuring and there is no restructuring this period, the nil adjustment does not change the calculation. IFRS 18 · para BC389
  • Link to identification timing. A prior-period MPM dropped because evidence shows it will not appear in current-period public communications is a change or cessation under paragraph 124; so is a measure dropped because it no longer communicates management’s view or is no longer permitted. IFRS 18 · para B122IFRS 18 · para BC338 EY Closer look 2026 · section 4.2.2 / section 4.3.5 KPMG FI 2024 · section 3.3
  • Ceasing an MPM. EY view: restating comparatives generally means the ceased MPM and its reconciliation drop out of the comparatives, but the explanation and reasons for ceasing are still disclosed. IFRS 18 · para 124(a)–(c) EY Closer look 2026 · section 4.3.5 · Q4-7
  • Ceasing in the year of adoption. EY view: no cessation disclosure is needed for an MPM dropped before the first IFRS 18 FS, because it was never a “previously disclosed” MPM (Ch8 8.2). IFRS 18 · para 124(c) EY Closer look 2026 · section 4.3.5 · Q4-8
Pitfall: Treating MPM selection as an IAS 8 accounting-policy change. It is not — but when restating comparatives for an MPM change, still use IAS 8.50–53 to assess impracticability, and disclose if comparatives are omitted. IFRS 18 · para 124(c)IFRS 18 · para 125

6.6 Identify, disclose, change — the MPM sequencetop

The MPM sequence runs identify, disclose and change, with face presentation as an optional path and interim reporting to follow.

  • Identify. Income/expense subtotal? → public communications (B119 include/exclude)? → management view of entity-as-a-whole (paragraph 119 presumption / paragraph 120 rebuttal)? → not paragraph 118 / not IFRS-required? → MPM. IFRS 18 · para 117–120IFRS 18 · para B116IFRS 18 · para B119
  • Disclose. If MPM → single note · management statement · describe aspect / usefulness · calculate · reconcile · tax & NCI · tax methodology. IFRS 18 · para 122–123
  • Change. If calculation / addition / cessation / tax methodology changes → explain · reasons · restate (or impracticable disclosure). IFRS 18 · para 124–125
  • Face (optional path). MPM status ≠ automatic face presentation — paragraph 24 additional-subtotal criteria still bind; failing them means notes-only disclosure, still as an MPM. IFRS 18 · para B118IFRS 18 · para 24
  • Interim. MPMs used in interim public communications need disclosure in the interim FS (Ch7 7.4). IFRS 18 · para BC337
