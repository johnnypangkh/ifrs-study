## 1. Frozen story (2–5 sentences) — ch02 only

The learner applies the **recognition principle** — probable future economic benefits **and** cost reliably measurable — to costs **when incurred**, whether those costs are **initial** or **subsequent** (IAS 16.7 · 10). **Yes** enters the **capitalise / asset** zone (continuing toward Initial measurement); **No** = muted **expense / do not capitalise as PPE**. The same principle then filters common subsequent applications without inventing R&M-vs-improvement labels: day-to-day servicing → expense; replacements → capitalise if criteria met **and** derecognise the replaced part; major inspections → capitalise as a replacement component (derecognise prior inspection CA); safety / environmental items may qualify because they enable benefits from related assets (then review combined carrying amount under IAS 36 — tip only). Spare parts / stand-by / servicing equipment nest as a definition test (PPE vs inventory); unit of measure and the component bridge stay lighter annotations. **What this is not:** a purchase flowchart, a subsequent-expenditure FAQ dump on the spine, a component-depreciation course (ch05), or an IAS 36 impairment course.

5. **Still inside the Yes plate — subsequent capitalise facets (not equal spine):**
