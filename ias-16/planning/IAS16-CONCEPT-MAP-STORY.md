## 1. Frozen story (2–5 sentences)

The entity decides whether a tangible item held for use over more than one period is **PPE in the scope of IAS 16** (definition and carve-outs). When the recognition criteria are met — probable future economic benefits and reliably measurable cost — the item is **recognised as an asset and measured initially at cost**, whose elements (purchase price, directly attributable costs, and qualifying dismantling/restoration obligations) sit **inside** that cost amount. After recognition the entity chooses, **by entire class**, either the **cost model** or the **revaluation model**; under either model it **systematically allocates the depreciable amount over useful life**, usually **part by significant part**. The carrying amount leaves when the item is disposed of or no future benefits are expected; **disclosure** packages the class roll-forward and policy story. **What this is not:** an IAS 36 impairment course, an IAS 40 investment-property course, an IFRS 16 lease-accounting course, or an invented “buy → install → operate → sell” process flowchart — those Standards appear only as **see-also** tips beside the PPE node they qualify.

## 2. Learner visit path (eye walk · L→R then T→B · 4–8 steps)

1. **Enter** at the **scope gate** (top-left): does this tangible item meet the PPE definition and stay in IAS 16? Muted / stop exits for IFRS 5 held-for-sale, non-bearer biological assets (IAS 41), exploration & evaluation (IFRS 6), mineral rights/reserves — dashed tips, not the continuing spine.  
2. **Branch — recognition Decision:** probable benefits **and** reliable cost? **No** = muted stop (expense / do not capitalise as PPE). **Yes** = labelled stem **into** the asset containment plate (continuing route stays in column 1).  
3. **Inside the Yes plate — initial cost containment:** open Cost → purchase price + directly attributable costs + initial dismantling/restoration (IAS 16.16); note exclusions and “capable of operating as intended” stop-capitalising rule; thin tip for proceeds before intended use (20A) and IAS 23 borrowing costs.  
4. **Next phase — subsequent models (peers):** Cost model ∥ Revaluation model as **same-format peer cards** under a spanning “policy by entire class” bar (IAS 16.29–31, 36). Both peers remain on the continuing spine (neither is a dead end).  
5. **Still on subsequent — depreciation formula / parts:** from either model, enter the **depreciation** zone: depreciable amount = amount substituted for cost − residual value; allocate over useful life; **significant parts** nest as components inside the asset (IAS 16.43–50). Idle ≠ stop depreciation (except IFRS 5 / derecognition).  
6. **Exit stem — derecognition:** disposal or no future benefits → remove carrying amount; gain/loss to P&L (not revenue). Optional thin side events: compensation receivable; replacement + derecognise replaced part.  
7. **Secondary (lighter, below):** disclosure package by class (IAS 16.73–79) — policies, reconciliations, revalued-asset extras.  
8. **Dashed see-also only** (near what they qualify): IAS 36 · IAS 40 · IAS 23 · IFRS 5 · IFRS 16 · IAS 2 · IAS 41 · IFRS 13 / IFRIC 1 as needed.

Geometry must force: enter gate → Yes into containment → peers under class rule → depreciation nested under carry → exit stem; disclosure lighter; exclusions muted and not feeding the main zone.

## 5. What is NOT the story (rival objectives)

- Not a **vertical TOC reprint** of every Official heading as equal process steps.  
- Not **impairment accounting** (IAS 36 owns that; IAS 16.63 is a pointer).  
- Not **investment property** fair-value / transfers deep dive (IAS 40).  
- Not **lease ROU** recognition/measurement (IFRS 16) — only depreciation / S&LB tips.  
- Not treating **revaluation** and **depreciation** as alternative “choose one” peers (both models depreciate).  
- Not promoting **subsequent expenditure** FAQs into the Map spine (Official keeps them under Recognition; Map points).  
- Not HKAS dual codes on the board.
