Chapter 4 — Subsequent measurement — models

Choose cost model or revaluation model as policy for an entire class; account for revaluation increases/decreases and class simultaneity.

4.0 After recognition — choose a modeltop

An entity shall choose either the cost model in paragraph 30 or the revaluation model in paragraph 31 as its accounting policy and shall apply that policy to an entire class of property, plant and equipment. IAS 16 · para 29 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.65 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 5.1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 5 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.300.10

  • Policy by class, not by asset. An entity may have one or more classes on the cost model and other classes on the revaluation model — but not mixed models inside one class. PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.65 IAS 16 · para 29
  • Depreciation still applies under both models. Choosing a model does not replace systematic allocation of depreciable amount — that is Chapter 5. This chapter stops at the measurement model, class simultaneity, revaluation mechanics, surplus transfers, and IFRIC 1 tips. IAS 16 · para 30 IAS 16 · para 31 IAS 16 · para 43
  • IAS 40 cost-model lock-in. An entity that uses the cost model for investment property under IAS 40 applies the cost model in IAS 16 for owned investment property. IAS 16 · para 5 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.10 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 5
  • Initial adoption of revaluation is treated as a revaluation, not an IAS 8 prior-period restatement. IAS 8 · para 17: changing from cost to revaluation is accounted for as a revaluation in the current period — comparatives are not restated. Changing from revaluation back to cost is a normal retrospective IAS 8 change. IAS 8 · para 17 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 6.1-1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 6.4 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.360.10

4.1 Policy by entire class — what is a class; simultaneitytop

If an item of PPE is revalued, the entire class of PPE to which that asset belongs shall be revalued. A class is a grouping of assets of a similar nature and use in an entity’s operations. Items within a class are revalued simultaneously to avoid selective revaluation and mixed cost/value dates; a rolling basis is allowed only if the class is completed within a short period and revaluations are kept up to date.   • Official examples of separate classes (para 37).
    • (a) land;
    • (b) land and buildings;
    • (c) machinery;
    • (d) ships;
    • (e) aircraft;
    • (f) motor vehicles;
    • (g) furniture and fixtures;
    • (h) office equipment; and
    • (i) bearer plants.
    IAS 16 · para 37 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · FAQ 22.66.1
  • Official list is illustrative, not exhaustive (DTT / EY). Classes may be combined or subdivided by nature and use (for example office buildings vs factories/hotels). Geography alone usually does not create a separate class. DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 6.5.2-1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 6 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.320.20
  • Simultaneous revaluation (para 38). Avoid reporting a mixture of costs and values as at different dates. Rolling revaluation of a class is permitted only if completed within a short period and kept up to date — DTT reads “short period” as typically within the same accounting period (and same interim period if interim FS are presented), shorter when fair values are volatile. IAS 16 · para 38 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 6.5.3-1 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.71 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 6
  • Land and building on that land are separate assets. Fair-value movements on land and on the building are recognised separately. KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.300.20 IAS 16 · para 58
  • Assets under construction — policy choice (KPMG). IAS 16 does not preclude revaluing PPE under construction; reliability of fair value is a judgement. In KPMG’s view, assets under construction may be treated as a separate class (not yet used in operations), so an entity may consistently either revalue them with the completed class or keep them at cost until complete. KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.330 IAS 16 · para 31
  • Fair value not reliably measurable. Only assets whose fair value can be measured reliably are carried at revalued amount. When the exception applies, account for those assets under the cost model; DTT prefers adopting cost for the whole specialised class rather than mixing cost and valuations. IAS 16 · para 31 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 6.6-1

Illustration: 4.1.1 — Office buildings revaluation model; industrial buildings cost modelPwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · FAQ 22.71.1 IAS 16 · para 37 IAS 16 · para 29

Facts

  • Entity A is a large manufacturing group.
  • It owns industrial buildings (factories and warehouses) in industrial zones, and office buildings in central business districts of several capital cities.
  • Management wants the revaluation model for office buildings and the historical cost model for industrial buildings.

Assessment

  • IAS 16 permits the policy choice on a class-by-class basis (para 29 / 36–37).
  • Office buildings can be clearly distinguished from industrial buildings by function, nature and general location.
  • Those differences support treating them as different classes of PPE.
  • Once office buildings are a class on revaluation, all properties in that class must be carried at revalued amount; separate disclosure of the two classes is required.

Conclusion

  • Acceptable: revaluation model for the office-building class; cost model for the industrial-building class.
  • Not acceptable: revaluing only some office buildings while leaving others in the same class at cost.

Illustration: 4.1.2 — Same class despite different geographic locationsKPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · Example 17 IAS 16 · para 36–37 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 6

Facts

  • Company T has office buildings in Europe and Asia.
  • Buildings in both regions are used for administrative purposes.
  • Question: may Europe be revalued without Asia?

Assessment

  • A class is assets of a similar nature and use in the entity’s operations (para 37).
  • KPMG: different geographic locations do not by themselves justify different classes.
  • EY: similar manufacturing facilities for similar products in two countries are likely one class; pharmaceuticals vs clothing in Europe could be different classes by nature and use — judgement on operations.
  • Para 36: if one item is revalued, the entire class must be revalued.

Conclusion

  • Europe and Asia administrative office buildings belong to the same class.
  • Do not revalue Europe without also revaluing Asia (simultaneous / rolling within a short period).

Illustration: 4.1.3 — Assets under construction — revalue with class or keep at costKPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · Example 18 IAS 16 · para 31 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.330

Facts

  • In 2019, Company X commissioned construction of a new office building that will become its corporate headquarters.
  • X has a policy of revaluing land and buildings.
  • Question: must / may the building under construction be revalued?

Assessment

  • IAS 16 does not preclude the revaluation model for assets under construction; ability to measure fair value reliably is a judgement.
  • KPMG view: assets under construction may be a separate class because they are not yet used in operations.
  • Accounting policy (applied consistently) is therefore a choice between two acceptable paths.

Conclusion

  • Option A: revalue the building under construction, consistent with the land-and-buildings revaluation policy.
  • Option B: account for the building under construction at cost until complete (separate class on cost), then bring it into the revalued completed class.
  • Apply the chosen policy consistently.

Class-policy bar — two equal-rank models. After the class is identified, apply one of the peer models below to that entire class.

Cost model

  • Carry at cost less any accumulated depreciation and any accumulated impairment losses.
  • Cost of the asset normally stays unchanged until derecognition (subsequent costs evaluated under recognition — Chapter 2).
  • Detail in 4.2.

Revaluation model

  • Carry at revalued amount = fair value at revaluation date less subsequent accumulated depreciation and subsequent accumulated impairment losses.
  • Revaluations with sufficient regularity so carrying amount does not differ materially from fair value at period-end.
  • Detail in 4.3.

4.2 Cost modeltop

After recognition as an asset, an item of PPE shall be carried at its cost less any accumulated depreciation and any accumulated impairment losses. IAS 16 · para 30 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.68 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 5.2 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 5 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.90

  • Cost remains the base. Under the cost model the historical cost is not rewritten for market movements — market declines may still trigger IAS 36 impairment (see 4.8). IAS 16 · para 30 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 5.2
  • Depreciation and impairment reduce carrying amount. Systematic depreciation (Chapter 5) and accumulated impairment losses are deducted from cost. IAS 16 · para 30
  • Voluntary FV disclosure. Entities on the cost model may disclose fair value when materially different (IAS 16 · para 79(d)); if disclosed, DTT expects current fair values for an entire class — not cherry-picking only assets above carrying amount. IAS 16 · para 79(d) DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 6.7-1
  • IFRIC 1 under cost model. Changes in existing decommissioning / restoration liabilities (other than unwinding of discount) generally adjust the cost of the related asset — see 4.9. IFRIC 1 · para 5

4.3 Revaluation modeltop

After recognition as an asset, an item of PPE whose fair value can be measured reliably shall be carried at a revalued amount, being its fair value at the date of the revaluation less any subsequent accumulated depreciation and subsequent accumulated impairment losses. Revaluations shall be made with sufficient regularity to ensure that the carrying amount does not differ materially from that which would be determined using fair value at the end of the reporting period. IAS 16 · para 31 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.69 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 5.3 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 6 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.300.10

  • Frequency depends on FV volatility (para 34). When fair value differs materially from carrying amount, a further revaluation is required. Significant and volatile FV changes may need annual revaluation; items with only insignificant FV changes may need revaluation only every three or five years. IAS 16 · para 34 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.70 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 6.3-1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 6
  • Estimate FV each period-end even if no adjustment. DTT: to meet para 31 the entity must estimate fair value at each reporting period end — that does not mean a journal every year unless the estimate differs significantly from carrying amount. DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 6.3-1 IAS 16 · para 31
  • Fair value = IFRS 13 exit price. Fair value is the price that would be received to sell an asset in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date. Measure under IFRS 13 (highest and best use, valuation premise, hierarchy). No requirement for an external professional valuer — but disclose whether an independent valuer was involved. IAS 16 · para 6 IFRS 13 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 6.2 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 6.1 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.73
  • Revaluation increases and decreases cannot be offset across assets — even within the same class; the surplus/deficit rules in 4.5 apply per asset. KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.300.10 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 6.2 IAS 16 · para 39–40
  • Deferred tax. Tax effects of revaluation are recognised and disclosed under IAS 12 (para 42) — thin tip only in this chapter; examples below ignore deferred tax as firm manuals do. IAS 16 · para 42 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.78 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 6.9 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 6.2

4.4 How to account for a revaluation mechanicallytop

When an item of PPE is revalued, the carrying amount of that asset is adjusted to the revalued amount. At the date of the revaluation, the asset is treated in one of two ways: (a) adjust the gross carrying amount consistently with the revaluation of the carrying amount (observable market data or proportionate restatement), and adjust accumulated depreciation to the difference; or (b) eliminate accumulated depreciation against the gross carrying amount. The adjustment of accumulated depreciation forms part of the increase or decrease accounted for under paragraphs 39 and 40. IAS 16 · para 35 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.74 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 6.4 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 6.2 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.340

  • Two mechanical methods — same revaluation gain/loss. Elimination (net) method zeroes accumulated depreciation so gross = net = revalued amount. Gross (restatement) method restates gross cost and accumulated depreciation so net equals revalued amount. Whichever method is selected, the amount recognised as the revaluation increase or decrease is the same. IAS 16 · para 35 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 6.4-1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · Illustration 6-3–6-5
  • Gross method sub-variants. Restate gross by reference to observable market data (e.g. as-new fair value), or restate gross proportionately to the change in carrying amount (useful when an index / depreciated replacement cost path is used). IAS 16 · para 35(a) EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · Illustration 6-4 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · Illustration 6-5 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · Example 19A
  • Elimination is the more common approach in practice (KPMG). Same approach is used when fully depreciated assets are revalued. KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.340

4.5 Revaluation increases / decreasestop

If an asset’s carrying amount is increased as a result of a revaluation, the increase shall be recognised in other comprehensive income and accumulated in equity under the heading of revaluation surplus — except that the increase shall be recognised in profit or loss to the extent that it reverses a revaluation decrease of the same asset previously recognised in profit or loss. If an asset’s carrying amount is decreased as a result of a revaluation, the decrease shall be recognised in profit or loss — except that the decrease shall be recognised in other comprehensive income to the extent of any credit balance existing in the revaluation surplus in respect of that asset. IAS 16 · para 39 IAS 16 · para 40 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.75 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.77 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 6.8.1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 6.2 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.300.10

  • Same asset only. Reversals through P&L or against surplus apply to the same asset — not netting across a class. A negative revaluation reserve cannot be created. IAS 16 · para 39–40 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.77 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 6.2
  • Increase does not reverse past depreciation through P&L (DTT). When a revaluation surplus is recognised, no amendment is made to profit or loss to reverse depreciation previously recognised — only a prior revaluation decrease of the same asset goes back through P&L. DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 6.8.1
  • Depreciating assets — restrict P&L reversal of a prior decrease. EY / DTT: if the asset was depreciated after a downward revaluation, the amount credited to profit or loss on a later upward revaluation should take account of the lower depreciation already charged (parallel to IAS 36 · para 117 ceiling thinking). See Illustration 4.5.4. EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · Illustration 6-6 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 6.8.3-4 IAS 36 · para 117
  • Impairment interaction. An impairment under IAS 36 on a revalued asset is treated as a revaluation decrease under IAS 16; only the excess over any revaluation surplus for that asset hits profit or loss. EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 6.2 IAS 16 · para 63

Illustration: 4.5.4 — Upward revaluation after a prior deficit — restrict P&L reversal for depreciation already takenDTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 6.8.3-4 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · Illustration 6-6 IAS 16 · para 39

Facts (DTT Entity D)

  • Property cost CU10 million; useful life 20 years; dep CU0.5 million/year.
  • End Year 5: CA CU7.5 million; FV CU6 million → deficit CU1.5 million to P&L; move to revaluation basis.
  • Years 6–9: no material FV difference; dep CU0.4 million/year (on CU6 million / 15 years).
  • End Year 10: CA CU4 million; FV rises to CU7 million (uplift CU3 million).

Assessment

  • Naive reading of para 39 might credit the full prior P&L deficit of CU1.5 million back to P&L.
  • Part of that deficit has already been “reversed” via lower depreciation in Years 6–10: (0.5 − 0.4) × 5 = CU0.5 million.
  • P&L credit on the Year-10 surplus should be CU1.5 − 0.5 = CU1.0 million; remaining CU2.0 million → OCI.
  • End result: revaluation reserve CU2 million = excess of CA CU7 million over what CA would have been had the property never been revalued (CU5 million).
  • EY Illustration 6-6 applies the same restriction idea on a larger numeric path (cost CU1,000,000; down to CU350,000 at Y3; up to CU500,000 at Y6).

Conclusion

  • Credit CU1 million to profit or loss; CU2 million to OCI / revaluation reserve.
  • Do not recycle the full historical P&L deficit without adjusting for lower depreciation already recognised.
  • Treatment is consistent with the IAS 36 impairment-reversal ceiling thinking.

4.6 Revaluation surplus transferstop

The revaluation surplus included in equity in respect of an item of PPE may be transferred directly to retained earnings when the asset is derecognised. Some of the surplus may be transferred as the asset is used — the amount transferred is the difference between depreciation based on the revalued carrying amount and depreciation based on the asset’s original cost. Transfers from revaluation surplus to retained earnings are not made through profit or loss. IAS 16 · para 41 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.76 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 6.8.4 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · Illustration 6-2 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.350

  • Optional policy (not mandated). DTT / KPMG read para 41 as permitting: (i) no transfer; (ii) transfer the whole surplus on disposal/retirement; or (iii) transfer as used (difference in depreciation) with the balance on disposal. Apply consistently. Leaving surplus permanently after full depreciation/disposal is generally unattractive. DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 6.8.4 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.350.20 IAS 16 · para 41
  • Never through P&L. Reserve transfers are equity-to-equity only. IAS 16 · para 41 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 6.2
  • Net of tax. Transfers should be considered on a net-of-tax basis; reducing surplus also reduces the amount available to absorb future revaluation deficits on that asset. DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 6.8.4 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · FAQ 22.77.1
  • Derecognition home. Full surplus transfer on disposal is also a Derecognition topic — thin cross-link; gain/loss on disposal still excludes recycling of OCI surplus through P&L. IAS 16 · para 41 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 7

  • Depreciation based on revalued amount exceeds depreciation based on original cost by the “realised” portion of the surplus each period.

  • Each remaining year: transfer CU50 (EY path) / CU2 (KPMG) / CU0.3 million (DTT) from surplus to RE.
  • Alternatively transfer the whole remaining surplus on retirement or disposal.
  • Depreciation expense in P&L stays on the revalued amount; the transfer does not change P&L.

4.9 Changes in decommissioning / IFRIC 1 under the cost modeltop

IFRIC 1 addresses how to account for changes in the measurement of existing decommissioning, restoration and similar liabilities. Under the cost model, changes other than the unwinding of the discount are added to or deducted from the cost of the related asset in the current period (subject to not reducing carrying amount below zero; consider IAS 36 if cost increases). Under the revaluation model, such changes generally adjust the revaluation surplus or deficit for that asset; a change in the liability is also an indicator that fair value may have changed. Illustration: 4.9.1 — Remeasurement of decommissioning provision under the cost modelKPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · Example 6A IFRIC 1 · para 5 IAS 16 · para 16(c)

Facts

  • Company X built a plant brought into use 1 January 2019; construction cost 1,500.
  • Estimated useful life 20 years; cost model.
  • Initial carrying amount included 100 for decommissioning (discount rate 10%).
  • At end of 2019, X remeasures the decommissioning provision to 130.

Assessment

  • Unwinding of the discount during the year is recognised in profit or loss (finance cost).
  • Other changes in the provision (estimate / rate) under the cost model are added to or deducted from the cost of the related asset (IFRIC 1 · para 5).
  • Amount deducted from the asset cannot exceed its carrying amount; any excess → P&L.
  • An increase in asset cost may require consideration of whether there is an IAS 36 impairment indicator.

Conclusion

  • Adjust plant cost for the remasurement change (other than unwinding); continue depreciating the adjusted depreciable amount over the remaining useful life.
  • Do not send the estimate/rate change through OCI under the cost model (contrast revaluation-model path in IFRIC 1 · para 6).

4.10 Compensation for impairment or loss — thin tiptop

Compensation is a separate event. Compensation from third parties for items of PPE that were impaired, lost or given up shall be included in profit or loss when the compensation becomes receivable. Impairments/losses, claims/payments of compensation, and subsequent purchase or construction of replacements are separate economic events and are accounted for separately (IAS 36 impairment; IAS 16 derecognition; compensation in P&L when receivable; replacement cost under IAS 16). Not the spine of this models chapter — thin cross-link toward Derecognition / impairment events.
