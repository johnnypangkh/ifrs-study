Chapter 1 — Scope

Decide whether a tangible item is PPE under IAS 16, or another Standard prevails / is carved out. Bearer plants are in; produce on bearer plants, IFRS 5 held-for-sale PPE, IFRS 6 E&E recognition/measurement, and mineral rights/reserves are out — with the develop-or-maintain carve-back. Cross-standards are see-also only.

Concept Map

1.0 Objectivetop

IAS 16 prescribes the accounting treatment for property, plant and equipment so users can discern the entity’s investment in PPE and changes in that investment. The principal issues are recognition of the assets, determination of their carrying amounts, and the depreciation charges and impairment losses to be recognised in relation to them. IAS 16 · para 1 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.1–22.2 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 1.1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 1

  • Arc of the Standard. Under IAS 16, PPE is initially measured at cost, subsequently measured using either the cost model or the revaluation model, and depreciated so that its depreciable amount is allocated on a systematic basis over its useful life. DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 1.1 IAS 16 · para 15 IAS 16 · para 29–31 IAS 16 · para 50
  • Impairment lives elsewhere. Impairment of PPE is addressed by IAS 36 — see-also only in this pack (not a leaf course). PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.1 IAS 16 · para 63
See also — IAS 36. IAS 16 points out to IAS 36 for determining whether an item of PPE is impaired. This pack does not teach IAS 36 as a chapter. IAS 16 · para 63 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.1

1.1 Definition of PPEtop

Property, plant and equipment are tangible items held for use in production or supply of goods or services, for rental to others, or for administrative purposes, and expected to be used during more than one period. IAS 16 · para 6 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.6 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 2.1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 2.1 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.10.10

  • Two-limb definition (Official). PPE are tangible items that:
    • are held for use in the production or supply of goods or services, for rental to others, or for administrative purposes; and
    • are expected to be used during more than one period.
    IAS 16 · para 6 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.6 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 2.1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 2.1 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.10.10
  • Not limited to revenue-generating plant. The definition is not restricted to assets with an explicit link to revenue-generating operations (for example manufacturing plant) or to administrative infrastructure (for example corporate headquarters). Assets acquired as part of research activities can meet the definition of PPE if they are expected to be used in more than one period — even though research costs are expensed as incurred under IAS 38. Such assets support the entity’s ongoing supply of goods or services in the longer term. KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.10.15 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 2.4 IAS 38 · para 54
  • Bearer plant (defined term). A bearer plant is a living plant that:
    • is used in the production or supply of agricultural produce;
    • is expected to bear produce for more than one period; and
    • has a remote likelihood of being sold as agricultural produce, except for incidental scrap sales.
    Paragraphs 5A–5B of IAS 41 elaborate on this definition. Bearer plants are within the scope of IAS 16; produce on bearer plants is not (see 1.5). IAS 16 · para 6 IAS 16 · para 3(b) KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.10.10 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 3.1.7 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 2.6.1
  • Software integral to hardware. Computer software that is an integral part of related hardware (for example an operating system) is treated as PPE. Software that is not an integral part of related hardware is treated as an intangible asset under IAS 38. PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.5 IAS 38 · para 4
  • Collectibles held for use, not trade. Accounting for collectibles (for example paintings or sculptures) depends on purpose. Items collected for administrative or aesthetic purposes and not traded in the ordinary course of business meet the IAS 16 definition of PPE. EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 2.1 IAS 16 · para 6
See also — IAS 38. When an asset incorporates both intangible and tangible elements, IAS 38.4 requires assessment of which element is more significant. Software that is not integral to hardware is intangible; mineral-rights classification judgements often use the same tangible-vs-intangible significance test (see Illustration 1.3.1). IAS 38 · para 4 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 2.1-1 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.5

1.2 When IAS 16 appliestop

Apply IAS 16 in accounting for PPE except when another Standard requires or permits a different accounting treatment. IAS 16 · para 2 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.7 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 2.1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 2.1 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.10.05

  • Default rule. All PPE is within the scope of IAS 16 unless an exclusion in para 3 applies, or another Standard requires or permits different treatment (for example IAS 40 for investment property at fair value). EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 2.1 IAS 16 · para 2 IAS 16 · para 3 IAS 16 · para 5
  • Cost-model investment property bridge. An entity using the cost model for investment property under IAS 40 shall use the cost model in IAS 16 for owned investment property. That is a measurement bridge into IAS 16’s cost model — not an IAS 40 chapter in this pack. IAS 16 · para 5 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.10 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 2.1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 2.1
See also — IAS 40. Investment property (including property being constructed or developed for future use as investment property) is within the scope of IAS 40. Owner-occupied property is PPE under IAS 16. When the cost model is elected for investment property, IAS 16’s cost model applies to owned investment property (IAS 16 · para 5). IAS 16 does not apply to four carved-out categories — but PPE used to develop or maintain the biological, E&E, and mineral assets stays in. IAS 16 · para 3 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.8 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 2.1 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 2.1

Illustration: 1.3.1 — Classification of mineral rights as tangible or intangibleDTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 2.1-1 IAS 16 · para 3 IAS 38 · para 4

Facts

  • The term mineral rights is not defined in IFRS Accounting Standards.
  • In practice it is often used to refer to both an intangible right to explore or mine and the tangible underlying mineral reserve.
  • IFRS 3 illustrative examples list use rights such as drilling rights as an example of a contract-based intangible asset recognised separately from goodwill in a business combination.
  • It is not always possible to distinguish the intangible right from the tangible element.

Assessment

  • Under IAS 38 · para 4, assess which element is more significant when an asset incorporates both intangible and tangible elements.
  • Assess whether the underlying reserve or the right to mine is more significant.
  • If the tangible resource/reserve is the more significant element, the combined mineral rights should be classified as tangible.

Factors

  • Whether the rights are granted for extraction of the mineral resources.
  • Whether the rights include ownership of the land on which the mineral resources are located.
  • Whether the rights are granted for exploration only.

1.4 Cost-model investment property (bridge)top

Owned investment property measured under the IAS 40 cost model uses IAS 16’s cost model — a bridge, not an IAS 40 chapter. IAS 16 · para 5 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.10 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 2.1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 2.1

  • Official bridge. An entity using the cost model for investment property in accordance with IAS 40 shall use the cost model in this Standard for owned investment property. IAS 16 · para 5
  • Fair-value IP stays in IAS 40. When another Standard requires or permits different treatment — for example IAS 40 for investment properties held at fair value — that Standard prevails. Investment properties held using the cost model under IAS 40 should use the cost model in IAS 16. EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 2.1 IAS 16 · para 2 IAS 16 · para 5
  • IP under construction. Investment property, including property being constructed or developed for future use as investment property, is within the scope of IAS 40. When the entity chooses the cost model under IAS 40, it uses the cost model as specified in IAS 16. DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 2.1

1.5 Bearer plants in scopetop

Bearer plants are PPE under IAS 16; agricultural produce growing on them stays under IAS 41. IAS 16 · para 3(b) IAS 16 · para 6 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 3.1.7 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 2.6.1 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.10.10

  • In IAS 16. Bearer plants related to agricultural activity are within the scope of IAS 16 and subject to all of its requirements, including the choice between the cost model and the revaluation model for subsequent measurement. Examples may include tea bushes, grape vines, oil palms and rubber trees. EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 3.1.7 IAS 16 · para 3(b) IAS 16 · para 6
  • Produce stays in IAS 41. Agricultural produce growing on bearer plants (for example fruit growing on a tree) remains within the scope of IAS 41. IAS 16 · para 3(b) EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 3.1.7 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 2.6.5
  • Not bearer plants (IAS 41 instead).
    • plants cultivated to be harvested as agricultural produce (for example trees grown for use as lumber);
    • plants cultivated to produce agricultural produce when there is more than a remote likelihood that the entity will also harvest and sell the plant as agricultural produce, other than as incidental scrap sales (for example trees cultivated both for fruit and lumber);
    • annual crops (for example maize and wheat).
    IAS 41 · para 5A EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 3.1.7 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 2.6.1
  • Incidental scrap sales. When bearer plants are no longer used to bear produce, they may be cut down and sold as scrap (for example firewood). Such incidental scrap sales do not prevent a plant from satisfying the definition of a bearer plant. IAS 41 · para 5B DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 2.6.1
  • Livestock is not in. The scope of IAS 16 extends only to bearer plants. Livestock held for breeding purposes only, with only a remote likelihood that it will ever be sold, is accounted for under IAS 41. DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 2.6.2 IAS 16 · para BC52
  • Immature bearer plants. Bearer plants that are not yet mature are accounted for under IAS 16 provided it is intended that they will only be used in the production or supply of agricultural produce — throughout their lives, even while they continue to undergo biological transformation. DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 2.6.4 IAS 16 · para BC70–BC72
  • Self-constructed analogy (tip → Initial measurement). Bearer plants are accounted for in the same way as self-constructed items of PPE before they are in the location and condition necessary to be capable of operating in the manner intended by management. References to “construction” cover cultivation activities necessary to bring bearer plants to that condition (typically when they are mature). Detail of cost build sits in Chapter 3 Initial measurement. IAS 16 · para 22A EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 3.1.7
See also — IAS 41. Non-bearer biological assets, produce growing on bearer plants, dual-use plants (fruit and lumber), and livestock sit in IAS 41 — not in this IAS 16 pack as chapters. IAS 16 · para 3(b) IAS 41 · para 5A–5C DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 2.6 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 3.1.7

1.6 Boundary examples (definition / classification)top

Named firm examples that test the PPE definition or the IAS 16 / IAS 40 boundary — without inventing fact patterns.

Illustration: 1.6.1 — Hotel properties: IAS 16 or IAS 40?DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 2.2-1 IAS 16 · para 6 IAS 40

Facts

  • Question often asked: classify a hotel as PPE under IAS 16 or as investment property under IAS 40?
  • Key determinant: whether the owner acts primarily as the hotel operator or as a landlord.
  • Management contracts with third parties vary widely — from passive investor to outsourcing day-to-day functions while retaining significant exposure to cash-flow variation.

IAS 16 path

  • Primary income depends on day-to-day or week-by-week occupancy of hotel rooms and usage of restaurants and other facilities.
  • The property owner provides services directly to hotel guests and diners.
  • The hotel is likely property held for use in the production of services → IAS 16.

IAS 40 path

  • Primary income comes from longer-term leases (months and years rather than days or weeks) → likely investment property / IAS 40.
  • That can hold even if the owner provides a relatively insignificant amount of ancillary services such as cleaning.
  • Not an accounting-policy choice — determination based on facts and circumstances.
  • If the owner retains significant exposure to variations in cash flows from hotel operations after outsourcing day-to-day functions, classification as investment property is not appropriate.

Does the owner act primarily as hotel operator (IAS 16) or as landlord (IAS 40)?

Illustration: 1.6.2 — Base stock of silverware and dishesDTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 2.3-1 IAS 16 · para 6

Facts

  • Restaurants and similar operations often maintain a base stock inventory of items such as silverware and dishes in an unchanging amount.
  • Additions to the stock are recognised in profit or loss.
  • On average, the turnover of the items is likely to exceed one year.

Definition test

  • These items are tangible assets.
  • Held for use in the supply of goods and services.
  • Expected to be used for more than one period.

Conclusion

  • Appropriately classified as property, plant and equipment.
  • Should not be included in current assets.

Illustration: 1.6.3 — PPE used in research activitiesDTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 2.4-1 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.10.15 IAS 16 · para 6 IAS 38 · para 54

Facts

  • Entity acquires tangible items for use in research activities.
  • IAS 38 · para 54 requires all expenditure on research to be recognised as an expense when incurred.
  • Question: must the PPE itself also be expensed on acquisition?

Assessment

  • PPE used in research activities is accounted for in the same way as other PPE under IAS 16.
  • IAS 38 · para 54 does not require expenditure on PPE used in research to be recognised in profit or loss when acquired.
  • The definition of PPE is not restricted to assets with an explicit link to revenue-generating operations — research PPE can meet the definition if expected to be used in more than one period.

Conclusion

  • Recognise the tangible item as PPE when it meets IAS 16 · para 6.
  • Depreciation of PPE used in research activities constitutes a research expense to which IAS 38 applies.

Illustration: 1.6.4 — Gold bullion held by a central bankDTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 2.5-1 IAS 16 · para 6 IAS 2 · para 6 IFRS 9 · IG.B.1

Facts

  • Central banks commonly hold significant gold reserves to support the national currency.
  • Such reserves are not traded and levels frequently do not change from one year to the next.

Not inventory / not a financial instrument

  • Not held for sale; not in process of production for sale; not to be consumed in a production process or the rendering of services → does not meet IAS 2 · para 6 inventories.
  • IFRS 9 IG.B.1: gold bullion is a commodity; there is no contractual right to receive cash or another financial asset inherent in bullion → outside IFRS 9.

Conclusion

  • Classify as PPE and account under IAS 16 (cost model or revaluation model).
  • Meets the definition: tangible item, held for use in the supply of services (stabilisation of exchange rates for the benefit of entities operating in the jurisdiction), expected to be used during more than one period.

1.7 Lessee right-of-use assets (tip)top

ROU assets are recognised and measured under IFRS 16; a lessee applies IAS 16’s depreciation requirements when depreciating the ROU asset — see-also only. PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.9 IFRS 16 · para 31 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 2.1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 8.4

  • Recognition / measurement under IFRS 16. Assets acquired under a lease are recognised and measured based on IFRS 16. Under IFRS 16, lessees recognise most leases as lease liabilities with corresponding right-of-use assets. Accounting for ROU assets is in accordance with IFRS 16 — not a ROU chapter in this pack. PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.9 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 2.1
  • Depreciation tip into IAS 16. A lessee applies the depreciation requirements in IAS 16 in depreciating the right-of-use asset. Detail of depreciation methods, useful life, and residual value sits in Chapter 5 Depreciation. PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.9 IFRS 16 · para 31
  • ROU depreciation as PPE cost (later chapter). Official notes that the cost of an item of PPE may include costs relating to leases of assets used to construct, add to, replace part of or service PPE — such as depreciation of right-of-use assets. That cost-element point belongs in Initial measurement / Recognition, not Scope. IAS 16 · para 10
See also — IFRS 16. ROU recognition and measurement live in IFRS 16. IAS 16 supplies depreciation requirements that lessees often apply to ROU assets (IFRS 16 · para 31). Sale-and-leaseback gain exceptions appear at derecognition (IAS 16 · para 68) — later chapter. PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.9 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 2.1 IFRS 16 · para 31

1.8 Spare parts — definition pointer onlytop

Spare parts, stand-by equipment and servicing equipment are PPE when they meet the PPE definition; otherwise inventory. Recognition criteria and subsequent-cost mechanics live in Chapter 2 Recognition. IAS 16 · para 8 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.10.20 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.15

  • Official placement. Items such as spare parts, stand-by equipment and servicing equipment are recognised in accordance with this Standard when they meet the definition of PPE. Otherwise, such items are classified as inventory. Official nests this under Recognition (para 8), immediately after the recognition principle (para 7). IAS 16 · para 8 IAS 16 · para 7
  • Definition limb KPMG emphasises. Such items are classified as PPE if they meet the definition, including the requirement to be used over more than one period. There is no requirement for such items to be used only in connection with an item of PPE. KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.10.20 IAS 16 · para 8 IAS 16 · para BC12A
  • Forward link. Capitalise-vs-expense for spare parts, safety/environmental items, replacements and major inspections → Chapter 2 Recognition. Unit of measure / aggregation of insignificant items (moulds, tools, dies) also sits with Recognition (IAS 16 · para 9). IAS 16 · para 8–9
See also — IAS 2. Spare parts that fail the PPE definition are classified as inventory under IAS 2. IAS 16 · para 8 IAS 2 · para 6
