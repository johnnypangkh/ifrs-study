Chapter 6 — Derecognition

Remove carrying amount on disposal or when no future benefits expected; recognise gain/loss in P&L (not revenue); treat compensation / replacement as separate events.

Concept Map

6.0 When to derecognisetop

The carrying amount of an item of property, plant and equipment shall be derecognised (a) on disposal; or (b) when no future economic benefits are expected from its use or disposal. IAS 16 · para 67 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.106 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 9.1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 7 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.380.10

  • Disposal takes many forms. Disposal may occur by sale, by entering into a finance lease, or by donation. The date of disposal is the date the recipient obtains control of the item under the IFRS 15 performance-obligation guidance. IFRS 16 applies to disposal by a sale and leaseback. IAS 16 · para 69 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.106 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 7 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.380.60 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 9.4.1
  • Permanent withdrawal from use. When an item is permanently withdrawn from use (retired) and no future benefits are expected from use or disposal, derecognise under para 67(b) even without a sale. Idle status alone does not trigger derecognition — depreciation continues until held for sale or derecognised (see Chapter 5). IAS 16 · para 67 IAS 16 · para 55 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.380.80
  • Part of an item. When part of an asset is disposed of or permanently withdrawn, the carrying amount of that part is derecognised (including when a replacement part is capitalised — see 6.5). IAS 16 · para 70 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.380.20
See also — Chapter 5 Depreciation · begin/cease. Depreciation ceases at the earlier of classification as held for sale (IFRS 5) or derecognition — idle / mothballed assets continue to be depreciated. IAS 16 · para 55

6.1 Gain or loss on derecognitiontop

The gain or loss arising from the derecognition of an item of property, plant and equipment shall be included in profit or loss when the item is derecognised (unless IFRS 16 requires otherwise on a sale and leaseback). Gains shall not be classified as revenue. IAS 16 · para 68 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.106 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 9.2 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 7 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.380.30

  • Amount of the gain or loss. The gain or loss is the difference between the net disposal proceeds, if any, and the carrying amount of the item. IAS 16 · para 71 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 9.2 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 7 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.380.10
  • Net proceeds — incremental disposal costs (KPMG view). Official does not define “net proceeds.” KPMG: deduct all directly attributable incremental costs of disposal (for example advertising, legal fees, stamp duty, agency fees and removal costs); also deduct amounts recognised as liabilities relating to the disposal (for example warranty claims under the sales agreement, or an agreed schedule of repairs at the current owner’s expense). KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.380.15 IAS 16 · para 71
  • Gains are not revenue (default). Except for the routine rental-fleet path in 6.3 (IAS 16 · para 68A), a gain on derecognition is not classified as revenue. IAS 16 · para 68 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 9.2
  • Revaluation surplus on disposal. Any attributable revaluation surplus may be transferred directly to retained earnings when the asset is derecognised — the transfer is not through profit or loss (and is not mandatory). Surplus-transfer mechanics live in Chapter 4. IAS 16 · para 41 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 9.2 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 7 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.380.35

Gain / loss on derecognition

  • Net disposal proceeds (if any)
  • minus carrying amount of the item
  • equals gain or loss → profit or loss (para 68 / 71)

What does not go through P&L

  • Revaluation surplus transfer to retained earnings (para 41) — optional, direct
  • Sale-and-leaseback outcomes required by IFRS 16 (para 68 carve-out) — see 6.4
  • Routine rental-fleet sales presented as IFRS 15 revenue (para 68A) — see 6.3

6.2 Consideration / IFRS 15 transfer tiptop

The amount of consideration to be included in the gain or loss arising from the derecognition of an item of property, plant and equipment is determined in accordance with the requirements for determining the transaction price in IFRS 15 · para 47–72. Subsequent changes to the estimated amount of the consideration included in the gain or loss shall be accounted for in accordance with the requirements for changes in the transaction price in IFRS 15. IAS 16 · para 72 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.107 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 9.3.1 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 7 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.380.10

  • Control date = disposal date. Para 69 ties the disposal date to when the recipient obtains control under IFRS 15’s performance-obligation guidance — the same transfer lens that drives when consideration is recognised. IAS 16 · para 69 IAS 16 · para 72
  • Variable / changing consideration. After initial inclusion in the gain or loss, changes in estimated consideration follow IFRS 15’s changes-in-transaction-price requirements — not a free rewrite of the PPE carrying amount. IAS 16 · para 72 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 9.3.1
See also — IFRS 15 Revenue from Contracts with Customers. Transaction price (para 47–72) and satisfaction of performance obligations drive both disposal date and consideration in the IAS 16 gain or loss. IFRS 15 IAS 16 · para 69 IAS 16 · para 72

6.3 Assets held for rental then soldtop

An entity that, in the course of its ordinary activities, routinely sells items of property, plant and equipment that it has held for rental to others shall transfer such assets to inventories at their carrying amount when they cease to be rented and become held for sale. The proceeds from the sale of such assets shall be recognised as revenue in accordance with IFRS 15. IFRS 5 does not apply when assets that are held for sale in the ordinary course of business are transferred to inventories. IAS 16 · para 68A PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.108 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 9.6 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 7.2 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.380.40

  • Gross revenue path, not net gain. For entities whose ordinary activities include renting then selling (for example car-rental fleets), presenting gross revenue and cost of sales better reflects those activities than a net PPE disposal gain. IAS 16 · para 68A EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 7.2
  • Transfer at carrying amount. Move from PPE to inventories at CA when renting ceases and the item becomes held for sale; IFRS 5 does not apply to that ordinary-course inventory path. IAS 16 · para 68A KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.380.40
  • Cash-flow presentation tip (IAS 7). Cash payments to manufacture or acquire assets held for rental and subsequently held for sale, and cash receipts from rentals and sales of such assets, are presented as operating cash flows — so purchase outflows are not investing while sale inflows are operating. IAS 7 · para 14 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 7.2
  • Residual / costs of disposal at transfer (EY practice note). IAS 16 does not define “estimated costs of disposal” for residual value. EY notes preparers may look to IAS 2, IFRS 5 or IAS 36 hierarchies with different incremental-vs-necessary outcomes — a potential loss on transfer from PPE to inventories if residual was set using a narrower cost-to-sell definition. Practice note only; residual review lives in Chapter 5. EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 7.2 IAS 16 · para 6

Illustration: 6.3.1 — Car-rental fleet sold after rental period — IFRS 15 revenue pathKPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · Example 22 IAS 16 · para 68A

Facts

  • Company Z operates a car rental business and also sells second-hand cars as part of its business model.
  • Z acquires new cars with the intention of renting them for three years and then selling them.
  • During the rental period the cars are recognised as property, plant and equipment.
  • When renting ceases and the cars become held for sale, Z transfers them to inventory at carrying amount.

Assessment

  • Para 68A applies because Z routinely sells items previously held for rental in the course of ordinary activities.
  • Transfer PPE → inventories at carrying amount when renting ceases and the cars are held for sale.
  • IFRS 5 does not apply to these ordinary-course transfers to inventories.
  • Sale proceeds are recognised as revenue under IFRS 15 (gross path), not as a net PPE disposal gain under para 68.

Conclusion

  • Carry as PPE while rented; transfer to inventory at CA when held for sale; recognise sale proceeds as IFRS 15 revenue.
  • Do not present the eventual sale as a para 68 PPE disposal gain classified outside revenue.
See also — IFRS 15 · IAS 2 · IFRS 5. Revenue recognition on sale; inventory measurement after transfer; IFRS 5 carve-out for ordinary-course held-for-sale transfers to inventories. IFRS 15 IAS 2 IFRS 5 IAS 16 · para 68A

6.4 Sale-and-leaseback exception tiptop

IFRS 16 overrides the default gain-in-P&L rule on a sale and leaseback. Para 68 includes gain or loss in profit or loss when the item is derecognised unless IFRS 16 requires otherwise on a sale and leaseback. An asset disposed of via sale and leaseback is accounted for under IFRS 16. Under IFRS 16, a seller-lessee derecognises the underlying asset only if the transfer leg satisfies IFRS 15’s requirements to be accounted for as a sale. Full sale-and-leaseback accounting lives in IFRS 16 — thin tip only. IAS 16 · para 68 IAS 16 · para 69 IFRS 16 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.109 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.380.18 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 7 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 9.2

6.5 Replacement + derecognise replaced parttop

If, under the recognition principle in paragraph 7, an entity recognises in the carrying amount of an item of property, plant and equipment the cost of a replacement for part of the item, then it derecognises the carrying amount of the replaced part regardless of whether the replaced part had been depreciated separately. If it is not practicable to determine the carrying amount of the replaced part, the entity may use the cost of the replacement as an indication of what the cost of the replaced part was at the time it was acquired or constructed. IAS 16 · para 70 IAS 16 · para 13 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 7 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.380.20 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 3.5.3

  • Bridge from Recognition — do not re-dump. Capitalise-the-replacement decision and day-to-day vs modification analysis live in Chapter 2 · 2.6 (and major inspections in 2.7). This section owns removing the replaced carrying amount and the resulting gain or loss (often a write-off when there are no disposal proceeds). IAS 16 · para 13 IAS 16 · para 70
  • Even if never separately depreciated. Derecognise the replaced part’s CA whether or not it had been identified as a component and depreciated separately — so both the old part and the new part are not carried as assets. IAS 16 · para 70 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.380.20
  • Estimating the replaced CA. When original cost of the replaced part is unknown, use replacement cost as an indication of original cost (often depreciated to the replacement date). Full numeric plate also in Illustration 2.6.2. IAS 16 · para 70 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · Illustration 3-3

Illustration: 6.5.1 — Replacement pump — derecognise estimated carrying amountEY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · Illustration 3-3 IAS 16 · para 70 IAS 16 · para 13

Facts

  • Machinery bought for CU10 million; estimated useful life ten years; contains two identical pumps assumed to share that useful life.
  • After seven years one pump fails and is replaced at a cost of CU200,000.
  • The entity had not identified the pumps as separate parts and does not know the original cost of the failed pump.
  • With supplier help, estimated original cost of one pump ≈ CU170,000; remaining carrying amount after seven years’ depreciation ≈ CU51,000.

Assessment

  • Para 7 / 13: capitalise the CU200,000 replacement cost in the carrying amount of the machine if recognition criteria are met.
  • Para 70: derecognise the replaced pump’s carrying amount even though it was never depreciated separately.
  • Use replacement-cost indication (CU170,000 original → CU51,000 remaining CA) because original pump cost is not practicable to determine from the books.
  • No disposal proceeds for the failed pump → the CU51,000 write-off is a loss on derecognition in profit or loss (para 68 / 71).

Conclusion

  • Derecognise CU51,000 (replaced pump CA) → P&L loss.
  • Capitalise CU200,000 replacement cost into the machine.
  • Recognition home for the capitalise decision: Illustration 2.6.2; this plate is the derecognition outcome.

6.6 Compensation for impairment / loss / giving uptop

Compensation from third parties for items of property, plant and equipment that were impaired, lost or given up shall be included in profit or loss when the compensation becomes receivable. Impairments or losses of items of PPE, related claims for or payments of compensation from third parties, and any subsequent purchase or construction of replacement assets are separate economic events and are accounted for separately. Insurance reimbursements after damage, theft or mishandling; government indemnities for expropriation or compulsory purchase; compensation for compulsory relocation of PPE; physical replacement in whole or in part of an impaired or lost asset. IAS 16 · para 65 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.104 DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 8.2

KPMG view: recognise compensation income when the damage giving rise to any loss or impairment has occurred and the entity has an unconditional contractual right to receive the compensation. Illustration: 6.6.1 — Plant destroyed by fire — insurance recovery as separate eventsKPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · Example 21 IAS 16 · para 65–66

Facts

  • Company W’s main operating plant is destroyed in a fire.
  • Carrying amount of the plant was 600.
  • Insurers pay out 1,000 — comprising 800 for rebuilding the plant and 200 for loss of profits.
  • Actual cost of rebuilding the plant is 900.

Assessment

  • Event 1 — loss / derecognition. Write off the destroyed plant’s carrying amount of 600 in profit or loss when the loss occurs (para 66(b) / 67).
  • Event 2 — compensation. Recognise compensation in profit or loss when receivable: 800 relates to the PPE; 200 relates to loss of profits (not a PPE carrying-amount recovery).
  • Event 3 — replacement cost. Capitalise the actual rebuild cost of 900 as a new item of PPE under IAS 16 (para 66(d)).
  • Do not net the insurance receipt against the rebuild cost, and do not delay recognising the write-off until cash is received.

Conclusion

  • Three (plus loss-of-profits) separate P&L / statement-of-financial-position effects — never a single net “insurance settles the plant” entry.
  • PPE path: derecognise 600; recognise compensation income when receivable; capitalise replacement at 900.

Illustration: 6.6.2 — New-for-old insured replacement — no cash compensationPwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · FAQ 22.104.1 IAS 16 · para 65–66

Facts

  • Entity A carried plant and machinery at C200,000.
  • The plant and machinery were destroyed in a fire.
  • Assets were insured “new for old” and replaced by the insurance company with new machines that cost C2 million.
  • The replacement machines were sourced by the insurer; Entity A did not receive the C2 million as cash compensation.

Assessment

  • Derecognise the destroyed plant and machinery — loss equal to carrying amount C200,000 in profit or loss.
  • Separately recognise a receivable and a gain in profit or loss from the insurance proceeds under IAS 37 once receipt is virtually certain.
  • Measure the receivable at the fair value of the assets that will be provided by the insurer (not at the old carrying amount).
  • Recognise the new machines as PPE at cost under IAS 16 when obtained — separate from the compensation gain.

Conclusion

  • Physical replacement by the insurer is still three separate events: derecognition loss, compensation income when receivable, and capitalisation of the new assets.
  • Absence of cash does not collapse the events into a single revaluation of the old CA to C2 million.

6.7 IFRS 5 held-for-sale see-alsotop

Items of property, plant and equipment classified as held for sale in accordance with IFRS 5 are outside the scope of IAS 16 for measurement. Once classified as held for sale (or as part of a disposal group held for sale), depreciation ceases; the asset (or disposal group) is measured at the lower of carrying amount and fair value less costs to sell. Classification requires the asset to be available for immediate sale in its present condition on usual and customary terms, and the sale to be highly probable. IFRS 5 IAS 16 · para 3 IAS 16 · para 55 PwC MOA 2020 Ch22 · 22.110 EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 7.1 KPMG Insights 2019/20 · 3.2 · 3.2.380.70

  • Exit from IAS 16 measurement — not from all disclosure. EY notes IAS 16 still requires certain disclosures about assets held for sale (see Disclosure chapter when unlocked). Full held-for-sale / discontinued-operations accounting lives in IFRS 5. EY iGAAP 2026 Ch19 · section 7.1 IFRS 5
  • Ordinary-course rental fleet ≠ IFRS 5. Para 68A transfers to inventories are outside IFRS 5 — see 6.3. IAS 16 · para 68A
  • In-period disposals (DTT). When material, apply IFRS 5 not only to items held for sale at the reporting date but also to in-period disposals (assets sold during the period that were not classified as held for sale at the previous reporting date) — see Illustration 6.7.1. DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 9.5-1

Illustration: 6.7.1 — In-period disposal of a revalued property under IFRS 5DTT iGAAP 2022 A7 · 9.5-1 IFRS 5 IAS 16 · para 55 IAS 16 · para 71

Facts

  • Entity R prepares annual financial statements to 31 December; no interim reports.
  • At 31 December 20X1, a property is carried at revalued amount CU2 million; depreciation CU60,000 per year.
  • In April 20X2 management decides to sell; by 30 April 20X2 the sale is highly probable. Fair value CU2.6 million; value in use CU2.8 million; costs of disposal CU100,000.
  • On 15 June 20X2 the property is sold for CU2.75 million.

Assessment

  • Apply IFRS 5 from the classification date (30 April 20X2) even though the asset was not held for sale at the prior year-end.
  • Depreciate to classification: CU60,000 × 4/12 = CU20,000.
  • Revalue to fair value CU2.6 million at classification if the difference is material — increase in OCI under IAS 16.
  • No IAS 36 impairment at classification: VIU CU2.8 million > FV less costs of disposal CU2.5 million.
  • Remeasure under IFRS 5 to FV less costs of disposal CU2.5 million → immediate P&L loss CU100,000 (costs of disposal).
  • On 15 June disposal: profit CU150,000 (net proceeds CU2.65 million − CA CU2.5 million). Remaining revaluation reserve is not recycled through P&L; may transfer to retained earnings.

Conclusion

  • With IFRS 5: depreciation CU20,000; net P&L gain CU50,000 (CU150,000 disposal profit − CU100,000 costs-of-disposal write-down); pre-classification valuation movement CU620,000 stays in OCI.
  • Without IFRS 5 (DTT contrast): depreciation ≈ CU27,500; net P&L gain would have been CU677,500 — different presentation and OCI vs P&L split.
  • Under the cost model, IFRS 5 may not change the period’s net P&L total but still changes IFRS 5 disclosures and any separately presented disposal gain/loss.
