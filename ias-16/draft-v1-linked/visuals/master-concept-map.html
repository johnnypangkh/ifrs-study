3.6Self-constructed asset

  • Same principles as an acquired asset.
  • Similar assets made for sale: usually the cost of constructing an asset for sale.
  • Internal profits are eliminated. Abnormal waste is excluded.
  • Bearer plants: same as a self-constructed asset until capable of operating as intended.

3.7Payment deferred beyond normal credit terms

  • Cost is the cash price equivalent.
  • The difference to total payment is interest over the credit period, unless it is capitalised under IAS 23.

3.8Exchange of non-monetary assets

  • Measured at fair value unless the exchange lacks commercial substance, or neither fair value is reliably measurable; then the carrying amount of the asset given up.
  • Commercial substance: the cash-flow configuration differs, or entity-specific value changes, and the difference is significant.
  • Fair value of the asset given up is used unless the asset received is more clearly evident.

Residual value

Residual value
  • The amount an entity would currently obtain from disposal, after deducting the estimated costs of disposal, if the asset were already of the age and in the condition expected at the end of its useful life.
  • If residual value rises to or above carrying amount, the depreciation charge is zero until it falls back below carrying amount.

In practice, residual value is often insignificant and immaterial to the depreciable amount.

5.2 Significant parts

Allocate the initial amount to significant parts and depreciate each separately.

Parts with the same useful life and the same method may be grouped.

The remainder is also depreciated separately.

An entity may choose to depreciate separately parts whose cost is not significant to the total cost of the item.

2.6Replacement or major inspection — capitalise the new part; derecognise the replaced carrying amount.

5.3 Useful life
Definition

Period over which an asset is expected to be available for use by an entity

OR

Number of production or similar units expected to be obtained from the asset by an entity

May be shorter than its economic life.

Factors
Expected usage

Assessed by reference to expected capacity or physical output

Expected physical wear and tear

Depends on shifts, repair and maintenance, and care while idle

Technical or commercial obsolescence

From changes in production, or in market demand for the output

Legal or similar limits

For example the expiry dates of related leases

Land and buildings
Land

Unlimited useful life — not depreciated (exceptions: quarries, landfill sites)

Buildings

Limited useful life — depreciable

Separable assets — accounted for separately, even when acquired together.

Site dismantlement, removal and restoration costs in land are depreciated over the period of benefit.

6.5Replaced part

  • The carrying amount of the replaced part is derecognised, whether or not the replaced part had been depreciated separately.
  • If it is not practicable to determine that carrying amount, the cost of the replacement may be used as an indication of what the cost of the replaced part was at the time it was acquired or constructed.

6.3Held for rental then sold

  • Transfer such assets to inventories at their carrying amount when they cease to be rented and become held for sale.
  • The proceeds from the sale of such assets are recognised as revenue in accordance with IFRS 15.
  • IFRS 5 does not apply when assets that are held for sale in the ordinary course of business are transferred to inventories.
