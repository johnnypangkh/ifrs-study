Residual value

Residual value
  • The amount an entity would currently obtain from disposal, after deducting the estimated costs of disposal, if the asset were already of the age and in the condition expected at the end of its useful life.
  • If residual value rises to or above carrying amount, the depreciation charge is zero until it falls back below carrying amount.

In practice, residual value is often insignificant and immaterial to the depreciable amount.

5.2 Significant parts

Allocate the initial amount to significant parts and depreciate each separately.

Parts with the same useful life and the same method may be grouped.

The remainder is also depreciated separately.

An entity may choose to depreciate separately parts whose cost is not significant to the total cost of the item.

6.5Replaced part

  • The carrying amount of the replaced part is derecognised, whether or not the replaced part had been depreciated separately.
  • If it is not practicable to determine that carrying amount, the cost of the replacement may be used as an indication of what the cost of the replaced part was at the time it was acquired or constructed.

6.3Held for rental then sold

  • Transfer such assets to inventories at their carrying amount when they cease to be rented and become held for sale.
  • The proceeds from the sale of such assets are recognised as revenue in accordance with IFRS 15.
  • IFRS 5 does not apply when assets that are held for sale in the ordinary course of business are transferred to inventories.
