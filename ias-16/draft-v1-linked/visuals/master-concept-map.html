Measurement after recognition

4.0Measurement after recognition

4.1Accounting policy for an entire class

  • Choose the cost model or the revaluation model as the accounting policy, and apply that policy to an entire class (similar nature and use).
  • If the revaluation model is used for an item, revalue the entire class.
  • Revalue the class simultaneously. A rolling basis is permitted if the class is revalued within a short period and kept up to date.

4.2Cost model

4.3Revaluation model

Carrying amount

  • Cost less any accumulated depreciation and any accumulated impairment losses.

1.4See also IAS 40

  • Carrying amount is the revalued amount: fair value less subsequent accumulated depreciation and subsequent accumulated impairment.
  • Sufficient regularity so the carrying amount does not differ materially from fair value.
  • Frequency depends on fair value changes: annual if significant and volatile; every three to five years if insignificant.

When fair value changes

  • Depreciation is still recognised even if fair value exceeds the carrying amount, as long as the residual value does not exceed the carrying amount.

4.4Restate the gross carrying amount consistently, or eliminate accumulated depreciation against it.

4.5An increase is recognised in other comprehensive income (revaluation surplus), and in profit or loss to the extent it reverses a past decrease. A decrease is recognised in profit or loss, and in other comprehensive income up to the surplus. Same asset only.

4.6The surplus may be transferred directly to retained earnings on derecognition, or as the asset is used. It is never transferred through profit or loss.

Fair value disclosure

  • 7.7Encouraged: disclose fair value when it is materially different from the carrying amount.
  • 7.6Required, in addition to IFRS 13.

5.1 Residual value

5.1Current amount, not forecast inflation. If residual value is greater than or equal to the carrying amount, the depreciation charge is zero.

5.7 Charge location

The charge is recognised in profit or loss, unless it is included in the carrying amount of another asset (inventories under IAS 2; intangible assets under IAS 38).

5.2 Significant parts

Allocate the initial amount to significant parts and depreciate each separately.

Parts with the same useful life and the same method may be grouped.

The remainder is also depreciated separately.

5.3 Useful life
  • The period the asset is available for use, or the number of production or similar units expected.
  • Useful life may be shorter than economic life.
  • Expected usage
  • Physical wear and tear
  • Technical or commercial obsolescence
  • Legal or similar limits on use
  • Land has an unlimited useful life, except quarries and landfill sites.
  • Land and buildings are separable assets.

5.5Reflect the pattern of consumption of future economic benefits

Diminishing balanceDecreasing charge

Units of productionBased on expected use or output

5.6A revenue-based method is not appropriate.

5.8Review residual value and useful life, and the method, at least at each financial year-end. A change is a change in accounting estimate (IAS 8).

Depreciate even if fair value exceeds carrying amount. Repair and maintenance do not remove the need to depreciate.

5.4Depreciation begins when the asset is available for use. It ceases at the earlier of the date the asset is classified as held for sale and the date it is derecognised. It continues when the asset is idle, unless the method is usage-based.
