### 1.1 What the Conceptual Framework is / is not

The Conceptual Framework describes the **objective** of, and the **concepts** for, general purpose financial reporting. Its purpose is to assist: (a) the **Board** to develop Standards based on consistent concepts; (b) **preparers** to develop consistent policies when no Standard applies, or when a Standard allows a choice; and (c) **all parties** to understand and interpret Standards. **SP1.1**

**What it is not:** The Conceptual Framework is **not a Standard**. Nothing in the CF overrides any Standard or any requirement in a Standard. **SP1.2**

Overview states that gate once, lightly. Full purpose triad, hierarchy, Board departures, revisions, and the **IAS 8 gap-fill path** live on **Status** (site Ch10). Overview only orients. **SP1.1–SP1.5**

**Contrast card — CF concepts / purpose helper vs CF overrides Standard**

| What it is — concepts / purpose helper | Common trap — CF is a Standard / overrides IFRS |
|---|---|
| CF describes the objective and concepts for GPFR. Purpose triad (Board / preparers / all parties) assists design, gap-or-choice policies, and interpretation — Overview points; Status owns the full triad. **SP1.1** | CF is **not a Standard**. Nothing in the CF overrides any Standard or any requirement in a Standard. Overview’s purpose pointer is not authority to ignore a clear Standard. **SP1.2** |

> **[Deloitte 2022]** Purpose triad (Board / preparers / all parties); CF is not a Standard; concepts do **not** override IFRS requirements · **SP1.1–SP1.2**

> **Pitfall:** Using Overview’s purpose pointer as authority to ignore a clear Standard. That fight belongs to Status — and CF loses. **SP1.2**

### 1.3 How chapters relate / suggested reading path

The objective of GPFR forms the **foundation** of the Conceptual Framework. Other aspects — qualitative characteristics and the cost constraint, a reporting entity concept, elements, recognition and derecognition, measurement, presentation and disclosure — **flow logically** from the objective. The suggested reading path mirrors that logic; it is Content pedagogy for studying this pack, **not** a Board-ordered diagnostic sequence. **§1.1**

**Suggested path (pack pedagogy — not a Board procedure):**

**Peer card row — Pack reading orientation (relationships, not a Board start procedure)**

| Status gate | Overview orient | Objective · QC peers | Then pipeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Know CF ≠ Standard and does not override — open Status first. **SP1.2** | This page — Status gate, numbering cue, cheat-grid jumps, then jump. Not a second Map. | *What info is for* and *what makes it useful* — equals, not a sequence. **§1.1–1.2**, **§2.1**, **§2.4–2.5** | Reporting entity → elements → recognition → measurement → presentation → capital — flows from the objective. **§1.1** |

**Peer cue (Map layout, not hierarchy):** Objective | QC sit side-by-side; Elements | Recognition sit side-by-side. Peers are equals — do not read Map left-to-right as “Overview above Status” or “Elements above Recognition.” Status is the gate. **SP1.2**, **§1.1**, **§4.1**, **§5.1**

Why this order earns teaching time: jumping straight to Elements or Measurement skips *why* definitions exist and *what* usefulness tests apply. Starting at Presentation because “that’s where the numbers show” leaves selection and communication without anchors. **§1.1**, **§2.1**, **§4.1**

> **Pitfall:** Reading Map as a Standards hierarchy, or starting at Measurement / Presentation because “numbers live there.” Foundation first. **§1.1**, **SP1.2**
